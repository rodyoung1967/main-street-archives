# Research Standard

## Purpose

This standard defines what must be completed before a research year in the 503/505 Main Street archive can be described as closed.

Research-work completeness is separate from historical certainty. A year may be complete at one of the levels below while a historical question remains unresolved, provided the relevant work has been performed and the unresolved result is stated accurately.

## Year closeout levels

Use these exact status labels:

### ONLINE COMPLETE

A year is **ONLINE COMPLETE** when the exhaustive online work defined below has been completed and every remaining task requiring physical access, restricted access, human assistance, or non-online records is listed as a manual-work callout.

This level:

- is sufficient for chronological progression to the next year;
- certifies completion of the defined online research pass;
- does **not** claim that manual work is complete;
- does **not** convert an inaccessible source or negative search result into proof of vacancy, demolition, closure, relocation, or non-use.

### ONLINE + MANUAL COMPLETE

A year is **ONLINE + MANUAL COMPLETE** when:

- it is already ONLINE COMPLETE;
- every manual-work callout has been completed and its result integrated, or the project steward has explicitly closed that callout after documenting why it cannot be completed;
- related source, evidence, timeline, building, business, people, map, and register files are synchronized.

This is the highest year-closeout level. It describes completion of the identified research work, not proof that every historical question has a surviving answer.

### IN PROGRESS

Use **IN PROGRESS** when the requirements for ONLINE COMPLETE have not yet been satisfied.

Do not use vague substitutes such as “complete enough,” “substantially complete,” or “closed” without also assigning one of the exact levels above.

## Exhaustive online-work requirement

For each year, the online pass must include all of the following:

1. Identify every relevant newspaper title available online for that year.
2. Build an issue inventory for each title, including issue dates and page/image counts where the archive exposes them.
3. Perform a **page-by-page visual review of every online-retrievable page of every relevant issue**.
4. Use OCR, keyword search, snippets, metadata, and text extraction for discovery only.
5. Make reasonable online recovery attempts for missing, misassociated, restricted, or non-rendering pages, including direct page files and credible alternate online derivatives when available.
6. Review other relevant online source classes identified for the year, including directories, maps, municipal records, property records, historic-resource records, photographs, and official databases where applicable.
7. Search the established target categories, including exact addresses, occupants, business names, ownership/agent references, sales, transfers, moves, vacancies, fires, construction, demolition, remodeling, public works, and neighboring address anchors.
8. Integrate verified findings, conflicts, OCR-only leads, retrieval gaps, and unresolved questions into the appropriate repository files.
9. Create a distinct manual-work callout list for everything that cannot be completed online.

A search that returns no result is a coverage statement only. It is never proof that an occupant, business, building, or event did not exist.

## Newspaper visual-review rules

### What counts

A newspaper page counts as visually reviewed only when the **actual scanned page image**—including PDF, JP2, JPEG, PNG, or another faithful image representation of the original page—has been visually inspected.

### What does not count

The following do not satisfy the visual-review requirement by themselves:

- OCR text;
- keyword search;
- archive index/search results;
- snippets;
- metadata;
- text extraction;
- search-engine results;
- a later source quoting or summarizing the page.

OCR may help locate material or decipher difficult print, but it must never substitute for viewing the original scanned page.

### Audit states

Use these page/source states consistently:

- **VISUALLY VERIFIED** — the actual scanned page or source image was inspected.
- **OCR/TEXT ONLY** — OCR or extracted text was reviewed, but the scan still requires visual inspection.
- **RETRIEVAL GAP** — a known online source/page could not be retrieved or viewed.
- **PENDING** — identified online work has not yet been reviewed.
- **MANUAL CALLOUT** — completion requires physical, restricted-access, institutional, user-assisted, or other non-online work.

## Retrieval gaps and manual callouts

A known online page or source that cannot be viewed remains a **RETRIEVAL GAP** during the online pass.

After reasonable online recovery attempts, the year may still become ONLINE COMPLETE only if the gap is:

- precisely documented;
- preserved as a retrieval gap rather than negative evidence;
- copied or cross-referenced into the year’s manual-work callout list with the next physical/restricted-access retrieval route.

An undocumented or unattempted online gap blocks ONLINE COMPLETE. A documented manual callout blocks ONLINE + MANUAL COMPLETE until it is completed or explicitly closed by the project steward with the reason preserved.

## What counts as manual work

Manual work includes, but is not limited to:

- visiting a library, archive, courthouse, historical society, FamilySearch Center, or affiliate library;
- viewing physical books, bound newspapers, microfilm, maps, deeds, permits, tax records, assessment rolls, licenses, or photographs;
- using a restricted database or account that requires human sign-in or on-site access;
- requesting scans or research assistance from an institution;
- inspecting user-held documents or physical artifacts not available online;
- resolving a visual ambiguity that requires a better scan, original document, or in-person examination.

Each manual callout must state:

1. the source or record sought;
2. the holding institution, collection, or likely access route;
3. the exact pages, addresses, names, dates, or questions to check;
4. why the task could not be completed online;
5. the result and repository updates when the work is completed.

## Required year-closeout record

Every annual closeout must include:

- the exact closeout level;
- online newspaper titles and issue/page coverage;
- other online source classes reviewed;
- visually verified findings;
- OCR/text-only leads;
- retrieval gaps;
- unresolved conflicts and historical questions;
- a numbered manual-work callout list;
- a statement that absence of evidence was not treated as evidence of absence;
- links to the relevant source captures, evidence entries, research leads, and registers.

## Certification rules

A year may be labeled **ONLINE COMPLETE** only when every requirement in the exhaustive online-work section has been satisfied and all remaining non-online work is captured as manual callouts.

A year may be labeled **ONLINE + MANUAL COMPLETE** only when no manual callout remains open, unless the project steward explicitly closes an impossible callout and preserves the reason.

Never describe a year simply as “complete” or “exhaustively reviewed.” Always use the exact applicable level.
