Purpose: resolve the project-steward family-memory hypothesis that **Raye LaFarlette / Raye Grisham worked at the Wheel Cafe in Morton, Washington; its owners later owned The Wheel at 503 Main Street, Oregon City; and Raye later bought the Oregon City business from them.** On 6 September Rodney Young's brother **Mitch Young independently recalled** that Morton was **before Coquille**, that Sheila “wasn't of school age,” and, as far as he knew, that the family **came to Morton from Chicago**. Rodney independently remembers the same basic sequence. The requests intentionally seek names and dates rather than assuming either the chronology or the ownership bridge is correct.

Sheila Wiitanen's published obituary reports that she was born **13 June 1944**. The visually rechecked 11 March 1954 *Coquille Valley Sentinel* profile says Sheila was 9 and gives Raye's own contemporary account as returning to Chicago after a 1944 Coos County vacation, selling the Chicago rooming-house business, and moving to Coquille without mentioning Morton. Floyd is independently documented in a southern Coos County business by **7 August 1947**. Accordingly, if the family recollection is substantially correct, the highest-value working Morton search bracket is **13 June 1944 through 7 August 1947**, especially **1945–7 August 1947** because *The Morton Journal* begins in 1945. This remains an **UNRESOLVED FAMILY / CONTEMPORARY-SOURCE CONFLICT**, not a documented Morton residence.

A separate property-control pass also found a public-record-derived but **unverified 1950 build-date lead for the surviving 145 Main building**. Therefore, do not assume that a pre-1950 Wheel Cafe, if found, had to occupy the present building or even the later 145 Main address.

## Request A — Washington State Library: early Morton / LaFarlette / Wheel Cafe lookup

### Recipient

Washington State Library Reference / Ask a Librarian  
Reference email: **askalibrarian@sos.wa.gov**  
Current route: https://www.sos.wa.gov/library/ask-librarian

### Subject

**Specific Morton, Washington lookup — LaFarlette family and Wheel Cafe, 1944–1947**

### Ready-to-send body

Hello,

I am researching a specific family-history question involving **Morton, Washington, between mid-1944 and August 1947**.

Two family members independently remember that **Floyd LaFarlette and Raye / Rae LaFarlette** spent time in Morton after Chicago and before settling in Coquille, Oregon. One specifically remembers that their daughter **Sheila was not yet school age** and that the family came to Morton from Chicago. Another family recollection says Raye may have worked at **The Wheel Cafe in Morton**.

I am trying to test this recollection rather than assume it is correct. Sheila's published obituary reports that she was born **13 June 1944**, and a contemporary 1954 Oregon newspaper profile instead describes the family's 1944 move as Chicago directly to Coquille. Floyd is independently in a Coos County business record by **7 August 1947**. That makes **13 June 1944 through 7 August 1947** the most useful current search bracket, with special emphasis on **1945–1947**.

Would you please help determine whether your **1946 Lewis County / Centralia-Chehalis directory** includes **Morton** or an outlying-community section? If it does, would you please check for:

- **Floyd LaFarlette / Floyd La Farlette**;
- **Raye LaFarlette / Rae LaFarlette / Raye La Farlette / Rae La Farlette**;
- daughters **Carolyn / Carol LaFarlette** and **Sheila LaFarlette** when useful as family-location controls;
- **Wheel Cafe / The Wheel Cafe**;
- **145 Main Street, Morton**, if that street address was in use then;
- any owner, proprietor, manager, employee, occupation, spouse, residence, or telephone notation associated with those entries.

Your county-directory holdings page lists Lewis County for **1946** and again for **1948–1950**, while your separately named Morton telephone-book holdings begin later. If the 1946 volume does not cover Morton, I would appreciate confirmation of that coverage limitation and any recommendation for a directory, telephone exchange book, tax list, or other source that did cover Morton in 1944–1947.

I am also trying to establish whether **The Wheel Cafe itself existed in Morton during this interval**. The Library of Congress catalog identifies *The Morton Journal* as a weekly Morton newspaper beginning in **1945** (LCCN `sn88085057`, OCLC `17315663`). If Washington State Library holds that microfilm, would you please advise the most practical route for a targeted **1945 through 7 August 1947** search for:

- `LaFarlette` / `La Farlette` / `Floyd` / `Raye` / `Rae`;
- `Carolyn` / `Carol` / `Sheila` when the family context is clear;
- `Wheel Cafe` / `The Wheel` / `Wheel`;
- `145 Main` only as one later-address control, not as a required pre-1950 location;
- `waitress`, `employee`, `cook`, `manager`, `proprietor`, `owner`;
- `new ownership`, `new management`, `sold`, `purchased`, `transferred`;
- arrival, departure, residence, visitor, club, school, or move notices.

One current public-record-derived property source suggests the **surviving building at 145 Main may date to about 1950**, although that year is not yet verified against the original assessor card. Please therefore do not exclude an earlier Wheel Cafe reference merely because it appears at another Morton address or does not give a street number.

A no-hit should be reported as a coverage result only; I am not assuming the family recollection is correct.

Thank you,

Rodney Young

A no-hit is not proof that Raye never lived or worked in Morton. If staff can only identify the microfilm/access route rather than perform the search, retain that response as an access finding and complete the page-by-page visual review separately.

## Request B — OLCC: 503 Main / Wheel Cafe licensee chain before Raye

### Recipient

Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission — Public Records  
Email: **OLCC.PublicRecords@olcc.oregon.gov**  
Current route: https://www.oregon.gov/olcc/pages/public-records-request.aspx

### Subject

**Historical licensee/trade-name lookup — Wheel Cafe, 503 Main Street, Oregon City, 1958–1967**

### Ready-to-send body

Hello,

I am researching the ownership/operator history of **The Wheel / Wheel Cafe / Wheel Cafe & Lounge at 503 Main Street, Oregon City** and am trying to identify the business owner or OLCC licensee immediately before **Raye LaFarlette / Raye Grisham** acquired the business.

Contemporary newspaper advertising places **Wheel Cafe & Lounge at 503 Main Street by 22 August 1958**, telephone **9106**. Oregon City Commission minutes later place **the Wheel at 503 Main Street on 7 June 1967** in connection with a liquor application, but the public minute text available to me does not identify the individual applicant.

Would you please determine whether OLCC still holds, has imaged/microfilmed, or transferred any legacy licensing records for:

- **The Wheel**
- **Wheel Cafe**
- **Wheel Cafe & Lounge**
- **503 Main Street, Oregon City, Oregon**
- date range **1958–1967**, extending later if necessary to capture the transfer into Raye LaFarlette / Raye Grisham's ownership

I am especially interested in any surviving **Licensee History Card, License Tradename Card, License File, application, renewal, transfer/change-of-ownership record, county/renewal-district licensee list, microfiche record, or predecessor series** that identifies:

1. the individual/corporate licensee or applicant;
2. the licensed trade name and address;
3. change-of-ownership or transfer dates;
4. predecessor and successor licensees;
5. the first record naming **Raye LaFarlette, Raye Grisham, Rae LaFarlette, or Rae Grisham**, if any.

I am testing, not assuming, a family recollection that the people who owned a **Wheel Cafe in Morton, Washington** may later have owned the Oregon City Wheel before selling it to Raye. For that reason, the actual pre-Raye licensee name is the key fact sought.

If records from this period are no longer held by OLCC, could you please provide any disposition, transfer, accession, microfilm/microfiche, or successor-custodian information for the relevant historical license series?

Thank you,

Rodney Young

### Handling note

Keep liquor-license identity distinct from business ownership, real-estate ownership, tenancy, and trade-name use unless a source explicitly bridges them. A custody/no-record response is a retrieval/survival finding, not proof that the Wheel lacked a license.
