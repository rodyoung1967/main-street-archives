## Evidence-handling note

This supplement follows `RESEARCH-STANDARD.md` and `AGENTS.md`. Primary newspaper and map claims are distinguished from catalog metadata, retrospective historic-resource research, strong inference, and unresolved hypotheses. OCR/search results are discovery aids only unless the underlying scan has been visually inspected.

The central unresolved question remains the **18 July 1922 Fifth/Main fire**: which modern Main Street storefront — **501, 503, or 505 Main** — contained the vacant former Alex Schieremann tailor shop where the fire began?

The current strongest working reconstruction is **503 predecessor = ignition room / 505 = adjoining pool hall**, but this is **not proven**. **501 remains a live alternative.**

---

## 1. 505 Main has a documented pre-1911 business history

### 1910 — George H. Young second-hand store at 505 Main

The Museum of the Oregon Territory / Clackamas County Historical Society photo-reference spreadsheet has been visually inspected at external catalog item **MOT:P-391**. The catalog identifies:

- **Young 2nd hand store**;
- **505 Main Street, Oregon City**;
- **1910**;
- a note reading **“same location as pool hall is now”**;
- G. H. Young among the men identified in the photograph.

Evidence state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED CATALOG METADATA**. The underlying historic photograph has not yet been directly inspected, and the date represented by the word `now` in the annotation has not been established. Therefore this record supports a remembered later pool-hall use at 505 but does **not** identify the July 1922 adjoining pool hall.

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1910-young-second-hand-store-505-main-p391.md`

A separate catalog item, **MOT:P-372**, describes a different 1907 Young second-hand-store photograph as being “across alley from 1st National Bank.” Earlier search snippets blended the two entries; they must remain separate.

### 1911 — Chicago Store moves to 505 Main

Primary newspaper advertisements establish:

- **3 Feb 1911:** The Chicago Store at **405 Main**, Electric Hotel Building, with **Wolf & Nat, proprietors** — visually verified.
- **31 Mar 1911:** The Chicago Store at **505 Main** — visually verified.

The 505 advertisement does not itself repeat the Wolf & Nat proprietor line, so their proprietorship must not automatically be carried forward after the move without another source.

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1911-chicago-store-wolf-nat-405-to-505.md`

This adds an earlier **Wolf-named business thread** to the 505 history but does not prove any identity connection among:

1. Wolf & Nat / Chicago Store, 1911;
2. Wolf & Miller / 505 Main, 1918;
3. S. Wolf of Portland, owner of the 1922 fire-origin building;
4. the Wolf family later reported as owning 503 Main from about 1922 to 1972.

### George H. Young moves nearby

On 20 Apr 1911 George H. Young advertised that he had moved into a **“new store”**, but the advertisement does not print its address. By **5 Jan 1912**, an original *Oregon City Courier* business page visually verifies **G. H. Young, The Curio Man, at 507 Main Street**.

The supported sequence is therefore:

- 1910: Young second-hand store at **505 Main** — museum catalog metadata;
- by Apr 1911: Young says he has moved into a new store — exact location unstated;
- 5 Jan 1912: Young explicitly at **507 Main** — visually verified primary newspaper.

A spring-1911 move from 505 into the nearby/new premises is a strong reconstruction, but the exact move date and exact first address of the “new store” remain unproven.

## 2. 503 Main had a proven pool-hall use in 1912

The **5 Jan 1912 Oregon City Courier** business page has been visually inspected and places a pool room under the name **H. N. Smith at 503 Main**. A 24 Mar 1912 legal notice signed **H. H. Smith** applies for a pool-hall license at **503 Main Street**, and a July report says H. H. Smith was leaving Oregon City after conducting a pool hall at **Fifth and Main**.

The H. N. / H. H. initials discrepancy is a real source conflict and remains unresolved; the archive does not silently merge the initials.

Evidence:
- `../../evidence/E-089-1912-smith-pool-room-503-main.md`
- `../../evidence/E-092-1912-main-address-ladder-young-harding.md`

This matters to the 1922 reconstruction because **both 503 and 505 have independent pool-hall/billiards associations at different dates**. The mere fact that a source says “pool hall” cannot by itself identify the 1922 adjoining premises.

## 3. Wolf & Miller / Gilbert Miller chronology is stronger, with a preserved source conflict

### 1918 — Wolf & Miller at 505 Main

The **10 Oct 1918 Oregon City Courier** directly places **Wolf & Miller at 505 Main**. The firm sold shoes, clothing/men's furnishings and maintained a shoe-repair operation.

A local **22 Aug 1918 Springfield News** item says **Gilbert Miller** had left Springfield for Oregon City. However, a **20 Aug 1918 Eugene Daily Guard** item genuinely prints **“C. E. Miller”** in its account of the same Wolf & Miller move.

This is a **contemporary-source conflict**, not simply an OCR error. Current evidence strongly favors Gilbert Miller as the working identification of the Miller partner, but the `C. E. Miller` wording must be preserved.

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1918-1922-wolf-miller-followup.md`

The October 1918 dissolution notice says one Wolf & Miller partner would go to Idaho while the other would restock and continue the business. Because Gilbert is independently documented moving to Oregon City, the natural reading is that Gilbert was probably the partner who stayed and the Wolf partner may have been the Idaho-bound partner. This remains an **inference**, not explicit source wording.

## 4. The fire-period Post Office was on the east/even side of Main

Post Office evidence now requires three distinct chronological locations rather than one continuous site:

1. an earlier circa-1912 Main Street location, probably on the odd-numbered side; exact bay unresolved;
2. by 1913/14 through 1918, a location at **Eighth & Main**;
3. after September 1918, a leased location in the **M. D. Latourette building on the east/even side of Main between Fifth and Sixth**, within the later 504–514 range.

Two June 1922 spatial anchors have been visually verified:

- **Dr. Freeze — 505½ Main — “Opposite Postoffice”**;
- **Neal, Mc and Rose — 522 Main — “Near Post Office.”**

The exact post-office bay within the even-side frontage remains unresolved.

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1918-1922-post-office-east-main-fifth-sixth-anchor.md`

This correction is important when interpreting pre-fire advertisements such as Miller's Shoe Store “Opposite Post Office.”

## 5. Miller's Shoe Store strongly tracks Gilbert Miller, but the pre-fire street number is still missing

Original *Banner-Courier* scans visually verify **Miller's/Millers Shoe Store** on **Main Street — Opposite Post Office** in February and May 1922. The store advertised shoes and expert shoe repairing.

The 20 Jul 1922 fire report says **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop** and shoe stock were destroyed. On **21 Sep 1922**, a signed Gilbert Miller advertisement says his shoe machinery is set up again for the same high-class repairing he provided before the fire and places him at **209 Fifth Street**. On **26 Oct 1922**, a visually inspected advertisement places **MILLER'S SHOE STORE at 419 Main Street**.

This produces a strong business-continuity inference that the pre-fire Miller's Shoe Store was Gilbert Miller's operation. No recovered pre-fire advertisement yet explicitly says “Gilbert Miller, proprietor,” and no exact pre-fire street number has been found.

Do **not** backdate the post-fire **419 Main** address.

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1922-millers-shoe-store-gilbert-miller-continuity-lead.md`

## 6. July 1922 Fifth/Main fire — current reconstruction

The **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** reports that the 18 July fire began in a **vacant Main Street store room formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop**. The building was owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**. A **pool hall adjoined** the former tailor premises.

The fire destroyed or affected businesses including the Oregon City Laundry, Fifth Street restaurant, Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop, May & Washburn's garage, and the Ketchner home/confectionery. A separate notice in the same issue explicitly describes **H. Ketchner's confectionery as “next to the laundry,”** adding another independent adjacency inside the fire cluster.

An independent Eugene-area report says the fire originated in an unoccupied Main Street building recently occupied by a cleaning/dyeing business. That description conflicts with the local former-tailor wording and remains preserved rather than harmonized away.

### Five-day pre-fire property sale and separate Gates corner site

The visually inspected **13 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** now supplies a direct property sequence. It reports that George A. Harding's Main Street property group—including the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller's shoe store, an adjoining barber shop, and the Red Front Clothing Store—was sold to **S. Wolf and C. Gates**, both of Portland. Its wording assigns the **restaurant location to Gates** and the **remainder to Wolf** (`E-096` / `S-111`).

The 20 July fire report then names S. Wolf as owner of the ignition building. Read together, the two sources strongly link the ignition building to the newly reported Wolf remainder, while the exact deed, legal boundary, and address remain unverified.

Two more visually inspected reports document the aftermath. On **17–18 Aug 1922**, C. E. Gates was identified as owner of the Fifth/Main corner site where R. A. Junken was constructing a two-story hollow-tile replacement for the fire-destroyed restaurant building. The first floor was planned as two storerooms, one already leased to a barber (`E-097` / `S-112`–`S-113`).

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1922-harding-wolf-gates-sale-and-rebuild.md`

### Current storefront assessment

The 1925 Sanborn visually shows:

- **501** — separate store frontage;
- **503** — restaurant;
- **505** — billiards/cigars;
- Oregon City Laundry behind the 501/503 portion of the frontage.

This geometry is compatible with a reconstruction in which the **503 predecessor contained the vacant ignition room and the 505 premises was the adjoining pool hall**. It remains the project's strongest working hypothesis, but the Gates evidence requires two explicit alternatives:

- if the two Gates rooms were one Main-facing room plus a Fifth-facing or internal/rear room, the corner property could map principally to 501 and the Wolf remainder could begin at 503;
- if both Gates rooms faced Main and became the 1925 501+503 pair, the Wolf remainder could begin around 505.

It is **not proven**. No recovered 1922 exact-address, parcel, license, tax, deed, directory, or equivalent contemporary source yet maps the former Schieremann tailor shop or the adjoining pool hall directly to 503 or 505. **501 remains a live alternative.**

Detailed evidence: `../../evidence/source-captures/1922-fire-s-wolf-main-street-property-lead.md`

## 7. The identity of the adjoining 1922 pool hall remains unresolved

At least several distinct pool/billiards businesses must be kept separate.

### Timms Pool Hall

**Timms Pool Hall** appears by name in the visually reviewed **8 Jun 1922 Buyers' Carnival** merchant roster. No exact address has yet been recovered.

### Leland & Little

The actual **25 Feb 1922 Morning Oregonian** scan lists **Leland & Little** among Oregon City cigar dealers. The **17 Aug 1922 Banner-Courier** visually identifies **LELAND AND LITTLE, Billiards**.

This makes Leland & Little a particularly interesting functional match to the **1925 505 Main = billiards/cigars** Sanborn use. It is still only a **strong lead**, not an address bridge.

### Other halls should not be folded into the fire candidate

- **Ed Wheeler's pool room** is directly placed at **Sixth & Main** in Nov 1921 and is still referred to after the July 1922 fire.
- **Huerth & Sandergaard's pool room** is visually verified in 1917 in the Commercial Club building, whose location was at **Eighth & Main**. No later move to Fifth/Main has been proven.

Therefore Timms, Leland & Little, Wheeler, and Huerth & Sandergaard must remain separate business streams until exact-address evidence proves otherwise.

## 9. S. Wolf acquisition sequence is reported; identity and exact parcel remain open

The 1922 fire source directly says **S. Wolf of Portland** owned the building where the fire began. A June Oregon City merchant roster separately includes **S. Wolf**. The visually verified 13 July sale notice now reports that S. Wolf acquired the remainder of Harding's named Main Street store group after the restaurant location was allocated to C. Gates. This is a material property-chain advance, but it is still newspaper reporting rather than the recorded deed and does not locate the remainder within 501/503/505.

Possible Portland directory candidates include **Samuel Wolf** (clothing), **Simon Wolf** (Wonder Millinery), and other Wolf entries. **Victor Wolf** is independently associated with Oregon City but does not match the `S.` initial and should not be merged into the fire-property identity.

No evidence yet proves that S. Wolf was:

- the Wolf in Wolf & Miller;
- the Wolf associated with Wolf & Nat;
- Samuel or Simon Wolf of Portland;
- a relative of Gilbert Miller's wife Clara Rose Wolf;
- the same Wolf whose family was later reported as owning 503 Main from about 1922 to 1972.

These remain separate research questions pending a deed, tax roll, probate, directory, partnership record, or other direct identity bridge.

## 10. Highest-value next records

The research is now close enough that generic keyword searching is less likely to resolve the central fire question than several surviving record groups:

1. **Oregon City council / pool-hall records around 1 Feb 1922.** Contemporary reporting confirms city action on poolrooms, and earlier municipal records show that pool/billiard licensing could identify particular premises. An operator-and-address list could directly identify the hall adjoining the ignition room.
2. **1921–1922 R. L. Polk Oregon and Washington State Gazetteer and Business Directory — Oregon City section.** The volume survives, but the actual Oregon City pages remain a retrieval gap. It could resolve Schieremann, Timms, Leland & Little, Gilbert Miller, S. Wolf and other fire-cluster businesses in one contemporary source.
3. **1922–1925 assessment/tax and land/lot books.** These survive for Clackamas County and could identify the owner of the target frontage and test whether S. Wolf owned the parcel corresponding to 501, 503, 505 or another nearby premises.
4. **July 1922 Harding-to-Wolf/Gates deed(s).** Search grantor/grantee indexes under George A. Harding, S. Wolf, and C. / C. E. Gates; the legal description and dimensions could directly locate the dividing line.
5. **August 1922 Gates/Junken building permit or plans.** The stated two-store hollow-tile replacement needs frontage, entrance, orientation, and address evidence.
6. **Underlying Museum `MOT:P-391` photograph (M/R y # 011-0048).** Direct visual inspection may provide storefront and neighboring-building geometry beyond the catalog metadata.
7. **Exact pre-fire address for Alex Schieremann.** This remains the most direct single-source solution to the ignition-storefront question.

### Parcel-numbering caution

Research has encountered older block-number reconstructions and current legal descriptions that should not be silently equated. Current target-frontage work must preserve the distinction between historic mapping references and the modern/current **Block 4 / Lots 3–4** legal-description evidence until a documented crosswalk proves the relationship.

## Current conclusion

The post-v5 evidence materially strengthens the history of both storefronts without yet closing the July 1922 question:

- **505 Main** has a catalog-documented George H. Young second-hand-store use in 1910, a Chicago Store occupancy by March 1911, Wolf & Miller in 1918, and a visually verified billiards/cigars use on the 1925 Sanborn. The museum's `MOT:P-391` catalog also remembers the 1910 505 location as the same location later used as a pool hall.
- **503 Main** is directly proven as a pool-hall address in 1912 and is a restaurant on the 1925 Sanborn.
- The **1922 fire-origin building** was owned by S. Wolf of Portland and had previously contained Alex Schieremann's tailor shop; a pool hall adjoined it.
- Five days before the fire, Harding's named store group was reported divided between Gates's restaurant location and Wolf's remainder; Gates then rebuilt the corner restaurant site with two ground-floor stores.
- **503 ignition / 505 adjoining pool hall remains the strongest working hypothesis, but the Gates building can presently be modeled either principally as 501 or as 501+503. No source fixes that line, and 501 remains a viable alternative pending its exact July 1922 occupant.**

Future Historical Society editions should incorporate this supplement only at the evidence level stated here and should not promote the 1922 storefront hypothesis until an exact contemporary bridge is recovered.
