# Karl / Carl Schoenheinz (older Schoenheinz generation)

**Person ID:** P-404

## Project role

Older Schoenheinz-family man and leading candidate for the shoe-business father associated with the 1911–1922 Oregon City shoe-trade sequence. He is also a plausible identity candidate for the **C. Schoenheinz** tenant handwriting at **503 Main** in the 1916–1917 Water Rent Record Book 4.

## Name and identity controls

Preserve the source-specific forms **Karl**, **Carl**, and **Charles** rather than silently normalizing them.

- The official Oregon City Mountain View Cemetery burial list records **KARL SCHOENHEINZ**, died 2 January 1937, age 80, 1st Addition, Block 179, grave 4.
- The same cemetery list records **ALBERTINA SCHOENHEINZ**, died 15 July 1912, age 48, immediately adjacent in Block 179, grave 3.
- The 19 July 1912 *Oregon City Courier* obituary for **Mrs. Charles Schoenheinz** identifies surviving husband Charles and children including Edward, Charles and Elizabeth.
- The 10 December 1914 *Courier* calls Elizabeth's father **Carl Schoenheinz**, living on 7th Street.

Taken together, **Karl = Carl = the older newspaper-Charles husband/father** is a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE IDENTITY HYPOTHESIS**, not yet a source-explicit equivalence.

## Shoe-business context

- 1911 advertisements name **C. Schoenheinz, proprietor**, Old Reliable Oregon City Shoe Shop, **716 Main between Sixth and Seventh**.
- 26 January 1912 *Courier* reports Edward Schoenheinz had been **connected with his father in the shoe business**.
- 10 September 1920 *Morning Oregonian* visually verified classified reads **SHOEMAKER wanted. Oregon City. K. Schoenheinz.**
- By 14 April 1922 advertisements document **Schoenheinz Shoe Repair Shop** in the Pacific Highway Garage Building; recovered ads do not name the proprietor.

These records create a strong older-family shoe-trade context but do not by themselves prove continuous proprietorship or that every initial form is this person.

## 503 Main question

Book 4 directly numbers 503 Main and classifies the premises **Store**. The project steward's enlarged visual reading of the faint tenant entry is **C. Schoenheinz**, with high confidence/probable transcription.

Karl/Carl is an increasingly plausible identity for that tenant because of the older-generation shoe-business evidence. However:
- no independent recovered source yet prints **Schoenheinz — 503 Main**;
- the ledger does not call the 503 premises a shoe store;
- the younger Charles is a separate possible C. Schoenheinz and must not be collapsed into this profile.

Therefore **P-404 = Book 4 C. Schoenheinz** remains **PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS**, and **503 = shoe store** remains unproven.
