# Charles Schoenheinz (younger musician generation)

**Person ID:** P-405

## Identity control

This profile intentionally separates the younger **Charles Schoenheinz** from the older Karl/Carl/Charles Schoenheinz (`P-404`). The July 1912 obituary for Mrs. Charles Schoenheinz names both a surviving husband Charles and a son Charles, establishing at least two Charles-name generations.

## Documented chronology

- 3 May 1912 *Morning Enterprise*: Charles Schoenheinz is one of the young Oregon City musicians engaged by Arnold's Carnival Company; documented as a cornet/orchestra musician.
- 1913 newspaper coverage continues his orchestra activity.
- 16 March 1916 *Oregon City Courier*: Margaret Louisa Cushing became the bride of **Charles Schoenheinz**; he is described as well known locally as a musician.
- 9 November 1916 *Courier*: **Charles Schoenheinz** is treasurer and business manager of the reorganized Oregon City band.
- 3 May 1917 *Courier*: Mr. and Mrs. Charles Schoenheinz and their son Edward moved to Camas, Washington; Charles had been employed in the mills there and had **disposed of his interest in the Theroux music store in Oregon City**.
- 1916 advertisements independently place F. F. Theroux's piano/sewing-machine business at **519 Main**, so the Theroux interest does not establish 503 occupancy.
- 28 February and 2 May 1918 Oregon City reporting again identifies Charles/Chas. Schoenheinz in cornet performances, showing continued or resumed Oregon City musical activity after the Camas report.

## 503 Main question

The younger Charles is a plausible identity candidate for the Book 4 **C. Schoenheinz** tenant at 503, but no source recovered independently places him at 503. His documented music-store interest points to Theroux at 519 Main, not 503.

Current evidence increasingly makes the older Karl/Carl shoe-business father (`P-404`) an important competing candidate. Do not decide between them without an exact-address or other independent identity bridge.
