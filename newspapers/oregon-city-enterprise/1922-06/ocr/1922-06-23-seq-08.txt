Gasoline Reserve Is
Said To Be Grooving
WASHINGTON, D. C, June 17
The nation's gasoline reserve con
tinues to mount to new heights, the
bureau of mines said today in mak
ing public figures showing that on
May 1 the stocks on hand amounted
to 892,267,763 gallons, an increase "of
38,000.000 gallons over the previous
high record mark of April 1.
"The seasonable decline in the
gasoline stock curve, occurring gen
erally during April or May," the bu
reau said, "has not yet started, as is
evidenced by the sharp increase in
reserves."

GIRL HELD CAPTIVE ON
BOAT FOR SIX MONTHS
BREMERTON, Wash., June 16.
That she was held captive six months
on a fishing boat, is the story told
authorities here by Grace Rahman,
aged 15, who was taken today by her
father and police agents from , the
craft Of Elmer Weber, aged 55, a fish
erman. Weber was arrested on a statutory
charge. The girl disappeared from
her home December 26. Her story,
police authorities say, is that Weber
seized her on the beach near Bremer
ton, took her aboard his boat, forced
her to don boy's clothing and to cut
her hair.

MAN KILLED; TWO HURT
WHEN TRAIN HITS AUTO
EUGENE, June 20 W. A. Brast
field, 50 years old, was killed outright
and Harry Thompson and G. R. Smith
were injured when a freight car crash
ed into an automobile in which they
were riding shortly after 3 o'clock this
morning at Springfield. The three
men, all employes of the Booth-Kelly
mill, were just coming off the night
shift. Their machine was struck at
the Fifth street crossing by a string
of freight cars being switched.
The rear end of a freight car hit
the automobile squarely, knocking-it
fifty feet down the track. Brasfield
and Thompson, who were riding in
the seat of the car, were hurled under
the freight train, while Smith, who
was on the running hoard, was thrown
on top of the flat car.
The automobile was wrecked and
the freight car had to be lifted up
before the lifeless body of Brasfield
could be extricated.
TWO WOMEN KILLED IN
AUTO CRASH AT ASTORIA
ASTORIA, June 17. Two women
are dead, one man is believed fatally
injured and another is seriously hurt
as the result of an automobile acci
dent late last night on the Astoria
Warrenton road, a few hundred yards
west of the Lewis and Clark river
bridge.
The driver of the car was L. G.
Stinson of this city and he was bruis
ed, but not seriously hurt. He has
been placed under arrest pending an
investigation of the accident by the
coroner's jury.
The car went into the ditch at the
side of the road. The wind shield and
top were demolished and it is believed
passengers in the rear seat were
thrown through the top.
