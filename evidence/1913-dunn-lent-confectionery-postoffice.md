# 1913 M. E. Dunn → F. G. Lent confectionery transition

This note records the September 1913 sale of **M. E. Dunn's confectionery near the old Oregon City Post Office** to **F. G. Lent** and evaluates the storefront's probable numbered address.

## Bottom line

Primary newspaper evidence establishes that:

- **M. E. Dunn** operated a confectionery **near / next door to the old Post Office** through September 1913.
- **F. G. Lent** purchased Dunn's confectionery and took possession in September 1913.
- By 23 October 1913, the successor business was referred to as **"Lent's next door to Postoffice."**
- The old Post Office is now independently reconstructed with high confidence as **513 Main**.
- George A. Harding is directly documented at **511 Main** in January 1912.

Taken together, the Dunn/Lent confectionery is **strongly reconstructed as 515 Main**, on the other side of the old Post Office from Harding.

However, no recovered primary source yet literally prints **"M. E. Dunn, 515 Main"** or **"F. G. Lent, 515 Main."** Therefore **515 Main must remain a high-confidence reconstructed address rather than a direct-address transcription**.

## 1. Dunn's earlier Post Office adjacency

Contemporary 1912 advertising places **M. E. Dunn** in the Post Office business cluster, selling cigars, candies, Wilhoit water and soft drinks. Newspaper wording describes the business as **near** and, in repeated *Morning Enterprise* advertising, **next door to the P.O.**

See:
- `evidence/1912-main-street-postoffice-cluster.md`
- `evidence/1913-1914-postoffice-relocation.md`

This establishes adjacency but not a number.

## 2. F. G. Lent purchases Dunn's confectionery — 11 September 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 11 September 1913, page 5:

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-09-11/ed-1/seq-5/`

The paper reports that **Mr. and Mrs. F. G. Lent and son Harold returned to Oregon City to make their home after purchasing the confectionery store of M. E. Dunn near the Post Office and taking possession Wednesday morning**.

The same item says the Lents had left Oregon City in 1911 after selling their earlier confectionery business, lived in California, and returned in 1913 to re-enter the confectionery trade.

This is direct primary evidence for a **Dunn → Lent business succession at the same near-Post-Office storefront**.

## 3. Dunn family residence is a separate location

The same 11 September 1913 page reports that **Mr. and Mrs. M. E. Dunn and family had been making their home at Sixth and Main Street** and moved to the Caufield residence at Seventh and John Adams.

This is a **residential-location statement** and must not be treated as the confectionery's storefront address. The same page separately identifies the confectionery as **near the Post Office**.

## 4. Lent's is explicitly next door to Post Office — 23 October 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 23 October 1913, page 5:

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-5/`

A classified notice states that several counter show cases were available and directs inquiries to **"Lent's next door to Postoffice."**

Because the replacement Post Office opposite the courthouse did not open until **28-29 March 1914**, this October 1913 reference necessarily means the **old Fifth-to-Sixth-block Post Office**.

The October wording confirms that the former Dunn storefront continued under Lent with the same direct Post Office adjacency.

## 5. Why 515 Main is the strongest reconstruction

The old Post Office is independently reconstructed as **513 Main** from multiple converging evidence chains. See:

- `evidence/1913-1914-postoffice-relocation.md`
- `evidence/1911-1913-505-harding-building-postoffice-reconstruction.md`

A key neighboring fact is that **George A. Harding was directly at 511 Main** on 5 January 1912 and remained associated with the Willamette Building/drug business through the period.

If:

- Harding = 511
- old Post Office = 513
- Dunn/Lent = next door to Post Office

then the remaining adjacent odd-side storefront is **515 Main**.

This is also consistent with the physical storefront sequence reconstructed from the Hogg Bros./old Harding Building evidence.

### Confidence

**High-confidence reconstruction:** M. E. Dunn / F. G. Lent confectionery = **515 Main**.

**Not yet direct proof:** no recovered primary source literally prints the number 515 with Dunn or Lent.

## 6. Address-handling rule

Recommended archive wording:

> **515 Main — M. E. Dunn confectionery → F. G. Lent, 1913: high-confidence reconstructed address.** Primary sources directly establish the business as next door to the old Post Office and directly establish the September 1913 Dunn-to-Lent sale; 515 derives from the independently reconstructed 513 Post Office and Harding's documented 511 Main occupancy.

Do not mark 515 as a directly documented exact address until a directory, advertisement, lease, tax record, Sanborn annotation, or other source prints it.
