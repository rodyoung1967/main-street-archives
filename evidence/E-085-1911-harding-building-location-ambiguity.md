# E-085 — Harding Building Location Ambiguity and Willamette Adjacency, 1911

Type: Primary newspaper / building-location and business-move evidence  
Date: 24 February–10 March 1911  
Source: `S-098`  
Related evidence: `E-077`  
Related research leads: `RL-029`, `RL-035`

## Claims Supported

Two contemporary *Oregon City Courier* items describe the **Harding Building** in ways that cannot yet be reduced to one exact numbered address:

- **24 February 1911:** O. G. Elliott and Mort Parks opened a general repair shop in the **Harding building, on 6th street between Main street and Railroad avenue**. The shop handled general repairs and automobiles/bicycles and acted as an agent for Ford automobiles.
- **10 March 1911:** George Young moved his second-hand store **one door north into the Willamette building**. The article says he had formerly occupied the **Harding building on Main street between Fifth and Sixth streets**.

## Significance

These descriptions establish that a Harding-named building/premises was associated with both the Sixth Street and Main Street contexts in early 1911. Possibilities include a corner or through-building with frontage on more than one street, two Harding-named premises, or imprecise contemporary location wording.

The 10 March item additionally establishes a direct adjacency relationship: **George Young's Willamette Building premises were one door north of his former Harding Building premises**.

## Limitation

The sources do **not** print an exact street number for either building. The archive therefore must not assign the Harding Building to 505, 507, 509, 511, or another number solely from these descriptions.

Later evidence that George Young was at **507 Main in January 1912** creates a useful hypothesis that the Willamette Building may correspond to 507 and the adjacent Harding premises to a neighboring number, but that later address cannot be backdated without continuity evidence (`RL-035`).

## Confidence

**Very High** for the two 1911 published location descriptions and for George Young's one-door-north move into the Willamette Building.  
**Unresolved** for exact numbers, lot/footprint, whether both descriptions refer to one physical Harding Building, and the relationship to later 505/507 numbering.

## 1912 Willamette-over-Harding constraint
`E-138` / `S-154` adds a direct September 1912 relationship that makes the exact-building-number caution even more important. A visually verified Spirella advertisement prints **“Willamette Bldg., Room 4, Over Harding Drug Store.”** The advertisement gives no number. At the same time, `E-092` directly places George Young at 507 Main and George A. Harding's drug business at 511 Main in January 1912.

This means the archive must not simply equate **Willamette Building = 507 only** and **Harding Building/Drug Store = the adjacent single number**. Possible multi-bay building extent, a change in Young's premises, and broader contemporary building naming all remain open. Preserve the 1911 one-door-north statement, the 1912 exact address ladder, and the September 1912 “over Harding” wording as simultaneous constraints.
