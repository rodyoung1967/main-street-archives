## Confirmed findings

### L. Hylton — 503 Main

**Oregon City Courier, 12 February 1914, p. 15**

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-15/

A business profile explicitly states that **L. Hylton** was located at **503 Main Street** and sold cigars, tobacco, smokers' supplies, and soft drinks. This is direct primary evidence and establishes a clear change from the H. H./H. N. Smith pool-room evidence documented in 1912.

### George A. Harding — 511 Main

**Oregon City Courier, 12 February 1914, p. 12**

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-12/

The profile explicitly identifies the pharmacy owned and operated by **George A. Harding at 511 Main Street**. It describes Harding as a longstanding Oregon City businessman and the premises as a high-class pharmacy carrying drugs, toilet articles, and patent medicines.

This is direct exact-address evidence and strengthens the 1913 Harding / Willamette Building chronology. The relationship between the Willamette Building name and individual storefront numbers remains a mapping question rather than a reason to reject the direct 511 address.

### G. H. Young — 507 Main

**Oregon City Courier, 16 April 1914, p. 4**

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-16/ed-1/seq-4/

A city-business listing explicitly gives **G. H. Young — 507 Main**. This converts the 1913 continuity inference into a direct 1914 exact-address anchor and confirms Young remained at 507 at least into April 1914.

### H. P. Brightbill — Main Street grocery

The same 16 April 1914 business-listing page names **H. P. Brightbill** among Oregon City merchants. The OCR rendering drops the street number after his name, so this particular page should not be cited as direct 509 proof. The surrounding chronology places Brightbill at 509 before and after 1914, making 509 a high-confidence continuity assignment pending recovery of an exact-number 1914 printing.

## 505 Main after the December 1913 Chicago Store fire

The 1914 search did not recover a satisfactory exact-address source showing the immediate post-fire occupant of **505 Main**. The December 1913 fire evidence remains the last strong event tied to the Chicago Store premises: the Chicago clothing store immediately south of the Post Office was badly damaged, and George A. Harding was identified as owner of the building. Earlier direct evidence places the Chicago Store at 505 Main.

Do not convert the absence of a 1914 hit into a claim that 505 was vacant or that the Chicago Store ceased business. Its 1914 occupant/status remains unresolved and should be carried as a standing lead.
