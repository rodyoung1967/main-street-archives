## Purpose

This checkpoint corrects the next-step assumption in the 1921 annual-audit handoff at `evidence/source-captures/2026-09-20-1921-annual-audit-conversation-handoff.md`.

The handoff correctly recognized that the **Morning Enterprise** daily existed in 1921, but it incorrectly treated January 1921 Morning Enterprise as an online page-by-page review workstream that could begin immediately.

## What the sources establish

### Publication continuity

The University of Oregon / Historic Oregon Newspapers title record identifies **Morning Enterprise** (Oregon City), LCCN `sn00063701`, as published **1911–1933**, frequency **daily (except Monday)**. The Oregon Digital Newspaper Program history likewise states that E. E. Brodie established the daily in 1911 and that it continued until 1933.

The already visually reviewed 14 January 1921 weekly Oregon City Enterprise also contemporaneously names E. E. Brodie as publisher of the Morning Enterprise and Hal Hoss as its advertising manager. This independently confirms that the daily was active in January 1921.

### Online image coverage

The public Historic Oregon Newspapers title page currently exposes **913 issues**, with the **latest online issue dated 23 December 1913**.

More importantly, Oregon City's official digitization-project page explicitly states that the digitized Morning Enterprise run is **8 January 1911 through 23 December 1913**. The Oregon Digital Newspaper Program's project documentation likewise says the Morning Enterprise material re-filmed/digitized for this project was **January 1912–December 1913**.

Therefore **no 1921 Morning Enterprise issue images are currently exposed in the public Historic Oregon Newspapers corpus**. NewspaperArchive's Oregon City Morning Enterprise collection likewise reports only **1911–1913**.

### Evidence that 1921 issues survive outside the public online corpus

A National Register nomination bibliography cites Morning Enterprise issues dated **13, 15, and 16 December 1921**. This is evidence that at least some 1921 issues were consulted in historical research and strengthens the case for a surviving microfilm/physical run, but the nomination is not a substitute for inspecting those newspaper scans.

The Historic Oregon Newspapers title record says the title was also issued on **microfilm from the University of Oregon**.
