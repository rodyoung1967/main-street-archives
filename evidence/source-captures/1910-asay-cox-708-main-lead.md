# 1910 Asay & Cox at 708 Main — identity lead

Status: **exact business/address proven; partner identities not yet fully resolved**

## Direct business/address evidence

The *Oregon City Courier*, 23 September 1910, page 3, identifies:

**The Pool Hall, Asay & Cox, 708 Main St.**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3/

The same page's contest description says the amusement parlor had **five tables**, sold smokes/tobacco, fresh candies and soft drinks, and had been **doing business for only four months**. That places the apparent opening of the Asay & Cox hall at roughly **May 1910**.

This exact-address evidence is important because the later Frank D. Cox pool hall is documented on Main Street in 1915–1916, and F. D. Cox was granted an Oregon City pool/billiard license in October 1912. If the Cox in Asay & Cox is proven to be Frank D. Cox, his documented pool-hall premises history would begin at **708 Main**, not 503.

## Cox identity — strengthened but not proven

**F. D. Cox is independently present in Oregon City records in September 1910**, the same month the Asay & Cox hall is listed at 708 Main. This timing materially strengthens the possibility that Frank D. Cox was the Cox partner in the firm, but no source recovered so far explicitly expands `Asay & Cox` to `William Asay & Frank D. Cox` or otherwise proves the identity.

Do **not** merge Asay & Cox into Frank D. Cox's biography/business record until a direct partnership source is found.

## Asay identity — plausible William Asay, not proven

Contemporary Oregon City social items show **William Asay and family living in Oregon City in 1910**. He is therefore a plausible candidate for the Asay partner, but no business source recovered so far explicitly identifies him as the partner in Asay & Cox.

Again, do not merge without direct evidence.

## Why this matters for 503 Main

Known 503-related chronology:

- March 1912 — H. H. Smith states intended pool-hall license application at **503 Main**.
- October 1912 — F. D. Cox receives a city pool/billiard license; address omitted.
- Feb./Apr. 1914 — L. Hylton directly at **503 Main**.
- 1915–Mar. 1916 — Frank Cox operates a **Main Street** pool hall; exact number unresolved.

If Frank Cox is the 1910 Asay & Cox partner, the research question becomes whether he **remained/moved from 708 Main**, later took over Smith's 503 premises, or operated at another Main Street number. The 1910 evidence by itself does not support a 503 assignment.

## Research targets

1. Search spring 1910 council minutes and newspapers for the original Asay & Cox pool-hall license/opening, approximately May 1910.
2. Identify the first names of both partners from council, recorder, tax, directory, or legal records.
3. Search 1911–1912 for dissolution, sale, relocation, or renewal of Asay & Cox.
4. Search F. D. Cox's October 1912 license application/bond for a premises address.
5. Compare any later Cox address directly with 708 Main and 503 Main.

## Research-standard warning

The **Asay & Cox / 708 Main** business/address is direct newspaper evidence. The approximate May 1910 opening comes from the paper's statement that the business had operated for four months. Partner identities remain hypotheses pending a direct source.
