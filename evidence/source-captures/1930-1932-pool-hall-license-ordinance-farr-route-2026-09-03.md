# 1930–1932 Oregon City pool-hall licensing ordinance route — Farr / 505 Main

Date: 3 September 2026

Status: **PRIMARY MUNICIPAL OCR/TEXT ONLY — ORIGINAL SCAN PAGE NOT VISUALLY VERIFIED; LICENSEE/PREMISES IDENTITIES UNRESOLVED.**

Purpose: preserve a newly identified municipal licensing route that may help determine when **Farr's Pool Hall** began operating at **505 Main Street** and who held Oregon City pool-hall licenses in the 1930–1932 period. This is a research-route checkpoint, not evidence that Edwin F. Farr held a license at 505 during this period.

## Primary municipal source inspected

Oregon City City Commission minutes, **2 March 1932**:

https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4723030/File/document

The public PDF text layer identifies a regular City Commission meeting on **2 March 1932** and states that **Ordinance No. 1234** was read the first time and ordered published.

The OCR/text layer reads Ordinance 1234 as:

- an ordinance **limiting the number of pool halls in Oregon City**;
- providing that no greater number of pool-hall licenses be issued than **one for each 2,000 population or major fraction thereof**; and
- **repealing Ordinance No. 1219**, which the text states had been passed **3 September 1930**.

The text further schedules Ordinance 1234 for second reading/final passage at a special meeting on **16 March 1932**.

## Why this matters for 505 Main

The archive currently has:

- **May 1925 Sanborn:** 505 Main mapped as **billiards/cigars**, operator unnamed;
- official historic-resource synthesis: **Edwin F. Farr and Alice Farr** identified as proprietors of **Farr's Pool Hall at 505 Main**, exact operating span unresolved;
- **1945** official-secondary Harr's Recreation Center at 505, with underlying page still needed;
- **11 February 1948** municipal text naming Harr and McDougald as pool-hall operators without address;
- **late 1948** municipal license text directly naming **Carl R. Harr — Harr's Recreation Center — 505 Main Street**.

A 1930–1932 ordinance regime that explicitly limits the number of legal pool halls materially increases the value of the underlying City Recorder license applications, renewals, receipts, petitions and Commission actions. Those records may identify every legal pool-hall operator and premises during the period and could provide the missing contemporary bridge between the unnamed 1925 billiards/cigars use and later Farr's Pool Hall.

## What this does NOT establish

This checkpoint does **not** establish:

- that Edwin F. Farr held a pool-hall license in 1930 or 1932;
- that Farr's Pool Hall was at 505 Main by either date;
- that the 1925 billiards/cigars occupant was Farr;
- how many pool halls were actually licensed at the time;
- the wording or regulatory mechanism of Ordinance 1219 beyond the repeal/date statement in Ordinance 1234;
- any property ownership, building construction, remodel, or business-transfer event.
