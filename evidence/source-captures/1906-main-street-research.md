# 1906 Main Street research source capture

Research date: 20 August 2026  
Scope: year-by-year reconstruction of Main Street between Fifth and Sixth Streets, with emphasis on 501 / 503 / 505 / 507, address-numbering chronology, and evidence of construction, demolition, fire, rebuilding, or major public works.

## S-075 — Oregon City formal street-numbering campaign, 1906

Type: Primary newspaper / municipal-context evidence  
Related evidence: `E-061`

Verified pages:

- 25 May 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 7: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-05-25/ed-1/seq-7/
- 6 July 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 1: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-07-06/ed-1/seq-1/
- 13 July 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 1: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-07-13/ed-1/seq-1/
- 17 August 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 1: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-08-17/ed-1/seq-1/

Observed sequence:

1. Postmaster T. P. Randall told the Council that free mail delivery required regularly numbered buildings and posted street names; ordinances were to be prepared.
2. Mayor Caufield approved the numbering ordinance; a **plat designating the correct numbering** was filed with Recorder Dimick and property owners were told to obtain the **true number** for a home or place of business.
3. Council expenses document work on house numbering and numbering plats; Councilman Rands described drawing city maps for the numbering project.
4. By 17 August street signs had arrived and the completed numbering plat was available at the recorder’s office.
5. The 17 August issue advertises **Lerow’s Glass Blowers at 504 Main Street**, demonstrating actual printed use of a numbered Main Street address during the implementation period.

Research implication: recover the original 1906 numbering ordinance and especially the filed numbering plat. It may be the best source for translating pre-1906 descriptions into later Main Street numbers.

## S-076 — Sixth Street improvement across Main, 1906

Type: Primary municipal notice / newspaper reporting  
Related evidence: `E-062`

Verified pages:

- 25 May 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 7: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-05-25/ed-1/seq-7/
- 13 July 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 1: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-07-13/ed-1/seq-1/

Observed claims:

- Sixth Street was to be improved from Water Street to Main and from Main to the Oregon & California Railroad stone wall on Railroad Avenue.
- Work included grading, crushed rock or gravel, wooden sidewalks, wooden curbs, drains, gutters, and corner blocks.
- The Council later re-established the Sixth Street grade between Main and Railroad Avenue and passed the improvement ordinance.
- A petition sought removal/abandonment of the hill steps at Sixth and construction of steps at Fifth.

Limitation: no recovered source says this work demolished or replaced a Main Street commercial building.

## S-077 — Rosenstein’s Clothing House fire, 1906

Type: Primary newspaper advertising / fire aftermath evidence  
Related evidence: `E-063`

Verified pages:

- 19 October 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 6: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-10-19/ed-1/seq-6/
- 14 December 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 7: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-12-14/ed-1/seq-7/

Observed claims:

- Rosenstein’s Clothing House was **two doors south of Postoffice, Main Street, Oregon City**.
- The December advertisement states that the Rosenstein stock had been **slightly damaged by fire in November last** and that the store was being cleared before the New Year.

Limitations:

- No numbered address appears in the recovered advertisements.
- Stock damage is proven; destruction, demolition, or replacement of the building is not.
- Do not map Rosenstein to 501/503/505/507 from the later working Post Office hypothesis.

## S-078 — William Tell Hotel / Block 26 lot 8 transaction, October 1906

Type: Primary newspaper and recorded-transfer reporting  
Related evidence: `E-064`

Verified pages:

- 5 October 1906, *Morning Oregonian*, p. 7: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83025138/1906-10-05/ed-1/seq-7/
- 19 October 1906, *Oregon City Enterprise*, p. 6: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1906-10-19/ed-1/seq-6/

Observed claims:

- The historic **William Tell Hotel at Sixth and Main streets** was a three-story frame building then used as a lodging house and saloon.
- Lewis Feuer sold it to Gambrinus Brewing Company for **$13,000**; the site was reported as approximately **61 × 100 feet**.
- The Enterprise transfer column records Gambrinus transactions for **lot 8, block 26**, including a Dresden Investment Company transfer for **$13,000**.

Research implication: this is a powerful lot-location anchor for Block 26. Recovering the original city plat/lot geometry and 1906 address-numbering plat may allow the remaining lots to be mapped toward Fifth Street and later 501/503/505/507 addresses.

## Additional 1906 block/fire-safety context

The 17 August 1906 *Enterprise* reports that representatives of the Pacific Fire Underwriters examined electrical wiring in Oregon City business houses and found many conditions faulty and hazardous. The article says it was thought safety might require Main Street wiring to be placed underground. This is useful evidence of contemporary fire-safety concern but does not identify a specific 5th–6th storefront or a demolition/rebuild event.

## Exact-address search status

Targeted searches were run for contemporaneous 1906 references to:

- `501 Main`
- `503 Main`
- `505 Main`
- `507 Main`

No primary 1906 item was recovered that directly identifies an occupant at one of those later-numbered target addresses. This is **not evidence of vacancy**. The year itself is the transition point at which the city formalized the numbering scheme, so relative-location and unnumbered Main Street descriptions remain common.

## Construction / demolition watch — 1906 result

The 1906 pass specifically searched for new construction, demolition, building removal/moving, fire, rebuilding, major remodeling, street grading, and related changes between Fifth and Sixth on Main.

Recovered:

- major Sixth Street grading/improvement work at Main;
- the Rosenstein Main Street fire, exact number unresolved;
- the William Tell Hotel still standing at Sixth & Main and sold in October;
- citywide fire-safety concerns over business-house electrical wiring;
- formal address-numbering implementation and a missing numbering plat.

Not recovered:

- a direct 1906 report that the predecessor buildings corresponding to later **501, 503, or 505 Main** were demolished, moved, replaced, or newly constructed.

This negative search result should remain a research-status note only. Continue the construction/demolition watch in 1907 and later years (`RL-024`).

## 1906 year status

Documented:

- formal address-numbering ordinance/plat implementation;
- substantial Sixth & Main public works;
- November fire damage at Rosenstein’s Main Street store;
- William Tell Hotel / lot 8 Block 26 as a fixed north-end block anchor;
- printed use of **504 Main Street** by August 1906.

Still unresolved / follow-up:

- actual 1906 numbering plat and ordinance text;
- exact 1906 occupants of later 501/503/505/507;
- Block 26 lot-to-address mapping;
- whether any 1906 fire or street work materially changed the target building footprints.
