## Conversation knowledge confirmed as already preserved on main

### July 1922 fire / property split

- Fire began in a vacant Main Street storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop.
- S. Wolf of Portland owned the ignition building according to the local fire report.
- A pool hall adjoined the former tailor room.
- Same-day Eugene reporting says a cleaning/dyeing concern had occupied the room until only days before the fire and that fire spread directly to Oregon City Laundry.
- The 13 Jul 1922 Harding property division gives C. E. Gates the Fifth Street Restaurant location and S. Wolf the remainder.
- Post-fire Gates construction produced a two-story hollow-tile building with two ground-floor store rooms, but the exact modern 501/503/505 mapping remains unresolved.
- Current fire-origin ranking remains unresolved; 501, 503, and 505-related configurations must remain live until direct property/address evidence closes alternatives.

### 1916 laundry conflict resolved

- 3 Feb 1916 planned replacement site was superseded.
- 17 Feb 1916 property trade moved the laundry project to Fifth Street.
- 2 Apr 1916 reporting places the new laundry on Fifth just off Main.
- 1918 direct address: Oregon City Laundry at 109 Fifth.

### June 8, 1922 Banner-Courier full visual review

- All 14 scanned pages were visually reviewed.
- Merchant roster separately includes Walter Little, Pantorium, Timms Pool Hall, Fifth Street Restaurant, Gilbert Miller, O. H. Leland, C. G. Miller Company, and S. Wolf.
- No exact address for Timms, Leland/O. H. Leland, Pantorium, S. Wolf, Fifth Street Restaurant, May & Washburn, or Schieremann was recovered in that issue; this is only issue-specific coverage, not negative historical evidence.
- S. F. Scripture visually advertises at 108 Fifth, between Main and Water, on that date. Therefore May & Washburn cannot simply be assumed to have inherited Scripture & May's 108 Fifth premises by June 1922.
- A. D. Wheeler & Co. and Fifth Street Restaurant are separate comma-delimited roster entries and must not be merged.

### W. H. Nelson exclusion

A visually reviewed 6 Jul 1922 ad places W. H. Nelson's blacksmith shop on Fifth between Main and Railroad, east of Main. He should not be used as an occupant candidate for the Main–Water fire block.

### Pool-hall chain

- 13 Feb 1920 Clem Dollar: pool hall on Main between Fifth and Sixth.
- 4 Jun 1920 visually verified municipal-reporting lead: A. Leland permitted a pool hall at 505½ Main and an electric sign.
- 25 Feb 1922: Leland & Little appears among Oregon City cigar dealers.
- 8 Jun 1922: Timms Pool Hall, Walter Little, and O. H. Leland appear as separate merchant entries.
- 17 Aug 1922: Leland and Little identified as billiards.
- May 1925 Sanborn: 505 Main = billiards/cigars.

Do not merge A. Leland with O. H. Leland, or Timms with Leland & Little, without a direct bridge. The 1920 505½ antecedent is highly important but does not by itself identify the July 1922 fire-adjacent pool hall.

### Pantorium / cleaning-dyeing chronology

- 1914: Oregon City Pantorium at 416 Main, cleaning/dyeing/dry cleaning.
- Oct 1918: Pantorium Cleaners visually verified at 500 Main, phone 185-J.
- Jan 1922: Pantorium Cleaners active in dry-cleaning/dyeing trade, address omitted in recovered ad.
- Jun 1922: Pantorium active merchant, address omitted.
- 20 Jul 1922: Pantorium at 519 Main; Oregon City Laundry used it as temporary post-fire office.
- Separate 1919 cleaner/dyer ad used phone 260 and wording `500 Fifth and Main streets`, `two doors south of new post office`; do not equate this unnamed advertiser with Pantorium without a bridge.

The cleaner/dyer that reportedly vacated the ignition room days before the fire remains unidentified.

### Miller's Shoe Store / Gilbert Miller

Visually verified pre-fire ads place `Millers Shoe Store` on Main Street opposite the Post Office and advertise shoes/gloves plus expert shoe repairing. The local fire report names Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop and shoe stock among the losses. A signed 21 Sep 1922 Gilbert Miller ad at 209 Fifth says his shoe machinery was restored for the same repairing done before the fire; by 26 Oct `MILLER'S SHOE STORE` appears at 419 Main with the same general shoe/repair profile.

`Miller's Shoe Store = Gilbert Miller` is therefore a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**, but the direct proprietor/trade-name bridge is still missing and exact pre-fire number remains unresolved.

### Post Office chronology — keep dates distinct

1. **Circa 1912:** earlier Post Office near Fifth/Main likely on the odd/west side; 513 Main remains a working candidate only.
2. **1913/14–1918:** Weinhard Estate Post Office at Eighth & Main / opposite courthouse.
3. **15 Apr 1919 onward, including July 1922:** Post Office moved to Fifth/Main on the east/even side of Main. Contemporary reporting says two store buildings were combined into one room, one First National Bank-owned and one Barlow-David-heirs-owned. Cross-source lot evidence strongly aligns these with Block 26 Lots 5–6, but exact 504/506/508/510/512/514 bay assignment remains unresolved.

Thus 1922 `Opposite Post Office` advertising points toward the odd-side Main frontage. Dr. Freeze is directly at 505½ Main and `Opposite Postoffice`; Miller's Shoe Store uses the same relative wording. This materially constrains the fire frontage but does not by itself select 501, 503, or 505.

A pre-award 8 Aug 1918 proposal naming Klemsen grocery/furniture rooms was later visually verified in parallel work; preserve its proposal wording separately from the final 1919 occupied Post Office location.
