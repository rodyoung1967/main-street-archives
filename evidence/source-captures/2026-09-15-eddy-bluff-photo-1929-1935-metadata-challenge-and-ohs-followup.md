### Critical provenance distinction

The words `pre 1922` are currently preserved as **filename/user-file metadata**. This research pass has **not yet recovered the original CCHS/OHS catalog entry proving that the institution itself assigned that date**. Do not attribute the `pre-1922` statement to CCHS or OHS without the underlying catalog record. The external-facing report should explicitly ask the holding institution to verify the source of that date.

## Hard contradiction to `pre 1922`: completed arch bridge

The completed Oregon City–West Linn bridge is plainly visible in the full photograph.

The formal opening/dedication was **28 December 1922**. Oregon City's surviving souvenir program gives that exact date, and West Linn Historical Society independently records construction completion in December 1922 and the 28 December dedication.

Therefore the photograph **cannot be pre-1922**. At minimum it is late December 1922 or later.

Evidence state: **DOCUMENTED chronology + DIRECT VISUAL landmark**.

## Decisive Railroad Avenue comparison — photograph is AFTER the improvement

The 1934 Municipal Activities Report contains a matched pair captioned:
- `Railroad Avenue from Fifth to Sixth Street, before improvement.`
- `Railroad Avenue from Fifth to Sixth Street, after improvement.`

The project steward supplied faithful images of both pages, permitting direct visual comparison rather than OCR-only inference.

The 1929 Municipal Activities Report independently documents the Railroad Avenue improvement from Fifth to Seventh Streets: full 40-foot grading, an 8-foot concrete sidewalk and curb, 32 feet of six-inch concrete roadway, 300 feet of sewer, 1,853 square yards of concrete, total cost $7,593.73.

### Visual match

The bluff photograph matches the **after-improvement** condition, not the before condition.

Strong comparison points:
1. A stop sign appears in the same Railroad Avenue/Fifth Street spatial relationship as in the `after improvement` photograph.
2. The stop-sign post has a conspicuous alternating light/dark or banded treatment in both views.
3. The developed roadway/sidewalk/curb geometry agrees with the after view and not the rough/excavated before view.
4. The west-side house/building relationship is substantially closer to the later view.
5. The railroad/street edge reads as the developed post-project corridor rather than the raw pre-improvement embankment.

Evidence state: **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE / VISUAL-SPATIAL INFERENCE**.

### Dating consequence

The prior mid-1920s hypothesis is rejected. The strongest present lower-bound year is **1929**, pending recovery of the exact project completion month/day.

## Upper bound — old courthouse still standing

The old Clackamas County courthouse with clock tower is plainly visible. The Clackamas County Family History Society identifies the older courthouse as finished in 1885 and replaced in 1936–37. The City of Oregon City's historical summary likewise states that a new Clackamas County Courthouse was constructed in 1936–1937.

Until the exact demolition/start-of-construction milestone for the old courthouse is documented, use a conservative working upper end of **1935** rather than claiming an exact 1936 cutoff date.

Evidence state: **DIRECT VISUAL landmark + DOCUMENTED replacement chronology**.

## Current working date

**Circa 1929–1935.**

Confidence:
- strong that `pre 1922` is impossible;
- moderate-to-strong for 1929-or-later from the Railroad Avenue comparison;
- moderate for the 1935 upper endpoint pending exact courthouse demolition/construction chronology.
