# 1930–1932 Oregon City pool-hall license records — survival/retention caveat

Date reviewed: 4 September 2026

Status: **CURRENT OFFICIAL RECORDS-MANAGEMENT CONTEXT ONLY — NOT EVIDENCE OF WHAT WAS DESTROYED OR RETAINED IN 1930–1932.**

Purpose: refine the municipal-record retrieval route for the unresolved 1930/1932 pool-hall licensing search. This does not replace the direct search for Ordinance 1219, the 16 March 1932 special meeting, or the City Recorder license register/predecessor records.

## Oregon City records-management policy

Oregon City's current official records-management FAQ states that the City follows the **State Records Retention Schedules** to determine how long City records are retained.

Official City FAQ:

`https://orcity.org/m/faq`

The City also states that records are retained according to authorized schedules and destroyed after the applicable retention period when eligible.

## Current State schedule for ordinary city-issued licenses

The Oregon Secretary of State's current **City General Records Retention Schedule** includes a series titled **Permit and License Records, City Issued**. The indexed schedule description says the series covers applications, investigations, permits, licenses, and related documentation for City-issued licenses and permits and gives a current minimum retention of **3 years after expiration, revocation, or denial**.

Official Oregon State Archives schedule result:

`https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/Documents/recordsmgmt/sched/2025-City-General-Records-Retention-Schedule-Annotated.pdf`

The current schedule is being used here only as a records-survival caution. It is **not evidence** that this same schedule, retention period, or disposition rule governed Oregon City's records in 1930 or 1932, and it does not establish that any specific historic pool-hall file was destroyed.

A direct page-image retrieval of the current schedule PDF was not available in the present research environment; the schedule wording is therefore retained at **official search-index/text level** pending visual verification.

## Research consequence

The active City Recorder request/search should not assume that routine 1930–1932 license applications or short-term license copies necessarily survive. It should explicitly ask whether the City retains any of the following historical record forms outside ordinary short-retention permit/application files:

1. **license register / register of licenses issued**;
2. **bound license book or ledger**;
3. **City Recorder cash/receipt book with license payer and purpose**;
4. **license index or card index**;
5. **pool/billiard license application or renewal files retained as historical records**;
6. **license transfer/petition/appeal files**;
7. **security microfilm, legacy microfilm, or digitized legacy volume**;
8. **records transferred to another City office, historical repository, or archival custody**;
9. any surviving record keyed to **Ordinance 1219 (3 September 1930)** or **Ordinance 1234 (March 1932)**.

For any surviving record, request the 1930–1932 entries for all pool/billiard rooms, not only Farr, so the licensed universe can be reconstructed without assuming an operator identity.

## Evidence boundary

No conclusion is made here about whether the sought 1930–1932 licenses survive. The correct state remains **RETRIEVAL GAP / SURVIVAL UNKNOWN** until the City Recorder or an archival inventory identifies the actual historical series and holdings.

This survival caveat reinforces rather than weakens the highest-value target: a contemporary municipal register/ledger entry naming the licensee, term/purpose, and ideally premises for the 505 Main pool hall.
