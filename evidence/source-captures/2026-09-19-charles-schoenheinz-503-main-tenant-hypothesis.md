# 2026-09-19 Charles Schoenheinz as possible 503 Main water-ledger tenant

## Research question

Project steward Rodney Young reports that the faint tenant signature/name on the **503 Main** row of the 1916–1917 Oregon City Water Rent Record Book 4 most closely reads **Charles Schoenheinz**, and considers that handwriting reading highly probable.

This pass tests whether a contemporary Oregon City person named Charles Schoenheinz existed and whether his documented activities make him a plausible 503 tenant. It does **not** promote the exact-address identification until an independent 503 bridge is recovered.

## Water-ledger evidence

Book 4 directly numbers **503 Main** and classifies/records the premises separately from 503½. The tenant handwriting is faint. Previous candidate readings L. Hylton and Schiermann were withdrawn as visually insecure.

**New steward reading:** `Charles Schoenheinz` / `Chas. Schoenheinz` is now the leading handwriting hypothesis for the 503 tenant line.

Evidence state: **PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS based on steward visual reading**, pending independent exact-address corroboration.

## Contemporary newspaper controls

### 16 March 1916 — Charles Schoenheinz is a local musician and marries Margaret Louisa Cushing

*Oregon City Courier*, 16 March 1916, p.5:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-03-16/ed-1/seq-5/

The original page was visually inspected on 19 September 2026. It reports that **Margaret Louisa Cushing became the bride of Charles Schoenheinz** in Oregon City and describes Schoenheinz as well known locally as a musician.

This directly establishes a Charles Schoenheinz in Oregon City during the Book 4 period.

### 9 November 1916 — Oregon City band business manager

*Oregon City Courier*, 9 November 1916, p.1:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-11-09/ed-1/seq-1/

The report on reorganization of the Oregon City band names **Charles Schoenheinz** as **treasurer and business manager**.

This establishes continued Oregon City presence and a business/organizational role in late 1916, but no 503 address.

### 3 May 1917 — moves to Camas; disposes of Theroux music-store interest

*Oregon City Courier*, 3 May 1917, p.5:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1917-05-03/ed-1/seq-5/

The original page was visually inspected on 19 September 2026. It reports that **Mr. and Mrs. Charles Schoenheinz and their son Edward** moved to Camas, Washington; Charles had been employed in the mills there; and **he had disposed of his interest in the Theroux music store in Oregon City**.

This is especially important because it proves Charles had a commercial interest in an Oregon City store during or immediately after the Book 4 period.

### Theroux exact-address control — 519 Main

Repeated 1916 Courier business-directory advertisements place **F. F. Theroux, dealer in pianos and sewing machines, at 519 Main**, including:
- 6 Jan. 1916 p.6: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-01-06/ed-1/seq-6/
- 10 Feb. 1916 p.8: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-10/ed-1/seq-8/
- 30 Mar. 1916 p.2: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-03-30/ed-1/seq-2/
- 4 May 1916 p.2: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-05-04/ed-1/seq-2/
- 27 Jul. 1916 p.3: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-07-27/ed-1/seq-3/

Therefore Charles's later documented interest in the Theroux music store **cannot by itself explain a 503 Main water account**. The store's published address was 519 Main during 1916. If Charles is the 503 tenant, the 503 relationship must be established separately (or a presently unknown address/use relationship must explain it).

### Earlier Schoenheinz shoe-business control — identity caution

*Morning Enterprise*, 15 Jan. 1911 p.3 and 14 Feb. 1911 p.2 advertise **C. Schoenheinz, proprietor**, Old Reliable Oregon City Shoe Shop, **716 Main between Sixth and Seventh**:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1911-01-15/ed-1/seq-3/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1911-02-14/ed-1/seq-2/

Do **not** automatically identify this `C. Schoenheinz` with the 1916 Charles. The Schoenheinz family contained multiple men whose names/initials can be confused.

A 19 July 1912 Courier obituary for Mrs. Charles Schoenheinz names her surviving husband and sons including **Edward and Charles**, demonstrating at minimum an older and younger Charles-name generation in the family:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-07-19/ed-1/seq-5/

A 10 Dec. 1914 Courier item separately names **Carl Schoenheinz on 7th Street** as Elizabeth Schoenheinz's father. Karl/Carl/Charles name equivalence is a possibility to research, not an automatic merge:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-12-10/ed-1/seq-5/

## Assessment

The new name hypothesis is materially stronger than the earlier Hylton/Schiermann guesses because:

1. **Charles Schoenheinz is directly documented in Oregon City in 1916**, exactly within the Book 4 period.
2. He was a known **musician**, was **treasurer/business manager** of the Oregon City band in November 1916, and by May 1917 is directly reported to have disposed of **his interest in the Theroux music store**.
3. The project steward independently arrived at **Schoenheinz** by reading the faint 503 ledger handwriting before this commercial-history connection was developed.

But the decisive bridge is still missing:
- no newspaper source recovered in this pass directly prints **Charles Schoenheinz — 503 Main**;
- the known Theroux store is repeatedly **519 Main**, not 503;
- the 1911 `C. Schoenheinz` shoe shop is at **716 Main** and requires family-identity disambiguation.

Therefore the responsible current formulation is:

> **503 Main, Book 4 tenant: Charles Schoenheinz — PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS, steward-led handwriting reading with strong contemporary person/business plausibility; exact-address corroboration still required.**
