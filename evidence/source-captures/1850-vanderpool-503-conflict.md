# Contested 1850 Jacob Vanderpool / 503 Main claim

Status: **OPEN SOURCE CONFLICT — DO NOT TREAT AS ESTABLISHED 503 HISTORY**

A 2024 Oregon statewide multiple-property documentation form for **Black Historic Resources in Oregon, 1788–2002** discusses Jacob Vanderpool and states that an Oregon Saloon and Boarding House he owned/operated was at **503 Main Street, Oregon City**. The study cites Kenneth R. Coleman's *Dangerous Subjects* for that location.

Source:
https://www.oregon.gov/oprd/OH/Documents/OR_Statewide_BlackHistoricResourcesInOregonMPD_form.pdf

The same statewide study immediately notes a material conflict: Elizabeth McLagan's *A Peculiar Paradise* places Vanderpool's business in **Salem**, across from the *Oregon Statesman* office.

## Why this cannot yet be attached to the modern 503 parcel

1. The cited historians disagree about the city/location.
2. The claim is secondary-source based; the underlying contemporary record has not yet been recovered.
3. Even if an 1850 Oregon City business can be proved, the modern street number **503 Main** may be a later retrospective/geocoded address rather than an 1850 street-number designation.
4. The current 503 building dates only to about 1919, so any genuine 1850 activity would concern an earlier site/parcel, not the surviving structure.

## Research actions

- Check Coleman's *Dangerous Subjects* citation and source notes for the exact basis of the `503 Main Street, Oregon City` statement.
- Check McLagan's source for the Salem placement.
- Search Oregon territorial-era newspapers, court records, tax rolls, city plats and property records for Vanderpool / Oregon Saloon / boarding house.
- Establish when Oregon City's modern Main Street numbering system came into use before assigning an 1850 business to current address 503.
- Do not place Vanderpool in the definitive 503 occupant timeline unless the location and address identity can be reconciled.
