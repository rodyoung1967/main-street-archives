# 1922 Fifth/Main fire — S. Wolf Main Street property lead

Status: **high-value lead; identity/property link not yet proven**

Purpose: preserve the potentially important connection between the July 1922 Fifth/Main-area fire, a Main Street building owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**, the earlier **Wolf & Miller** business at 505 Main, Gilbert Miller's later Fifth Street shoe-repair shop, the June 1922 Oregon City merchant roster, and the later historic-inventory statement that the **Wolf family owned 503 Main from about 1922 to 1972**.

## Primary fire source

**Banner-Courier, 20 Jul 1922, p. 1** reports that the major July 1922 fire destroyed the Oregon City Laundry and several neighboring businesses.

The article states that:

- the flames were discovered in a **vacant store room formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop**;
- the building in which the fire started was **owned by S. Wolf of Portland**;
- the fire then destroyed the Oregon City Laundry, the May & Washburn frame garage, the Fifth Street restaurant, Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop, and the Ketchner home/confectionery business;
- a Water Street residence owned by W. S. Blakeslee was also damaged;
- billiard tables in a pool hall adjoining the former tailor establishment were ruined by water and the roof of that building was partly burned;
- the fire therefore physically connected a **Main Street store room / adjoining pool-hall area** with the Fifth Street laundry/garage/restaurant/shoe-repair cluster.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1/

## June 1922 — S. Wolf has an active Oregon City business connection

A **8 Jun 1922 Banner-Courier** article on the Oregon City Buyers' Carnival names approximately seventy firms/business people backing the Retail Extension Department of the Commercial Club. The list includes:

- **Gilbert Miller**;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant**;
- **Timms Pool Hall**;
- **S. Wolf**;
- numerous other identified Oregon City merchants and businesses.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-06-08/ed-1/seq-10/

This is important because it shows **S. Wolf was recognized in an Oregon City merchant/business roster only about six weeks before the fire**. The July fire article's description of him as "S. Wolf of Portland" should therefore not be interpreted to mean that he had no active Oregon City business/property role.

The list does **not** give S. Wolf's business type or address, so it still does not identify the fire-start building as 501, 503, or 505 Main.

The same June roster names **Timms Pool Hall**, creating a new research lead because the July fire report says a pool hall adjoined the former Schieremann tailor shop. Timms' exact 1922 address has not yet been recovered; do not assume Timms was the adjoining pool hall until an address or other spatial source proves it.

## Wolf & Miller / Gilbert Miller continuity lead

A visually reviewed **10 Oct 1918 Oregon City Courier** business page identifies:

- **Wolf & Miller — 505 Main**;
- the firm was dissolving partnership;
- one partner would leave for Idaho and the other would restock and continue the business;
- the business included shoes, men's clothing/furnishings, and an up-to-date shoe-repair department.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-10-10/ed-1/seq-4/

### Miller identity — contemporary source conflict preserved

The **22 Aug 1918 Springfield News** page explicitly states that **“Gilbert Miller left last Saturday for Oregon City”** to join his family. A separate paragraph explains that his family had moved to Oregon City earlier while he remained in Springfield to close out his business.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071003/1918-08-22/ed-1/seq-3/

However, the **20 Aug 1918 Eugene Daily Guard** genuinely prints **“C. E. Miller”** of the Wolf and Miller Department Store in its report of the same Oregon City move. This is a real contemporary-source discrepancy, not merely an OCR artifact.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088107/1918-08-20/ed-1/seq-5/

Current evidence favors **Gilbert Miller** as the working identity because:

1. Gilbert Miller is independently documented as a Springfield resident/civic figure during the Wolf & Miller years;
2. the local Springfield News directly names Gilbert in the Oregon City move notice;
3. a Springfield historic-district National Register inventory independently identifies **Gilbert Miller of Wolf and Miller (men's furnishings)** around 1918;
4. Gilbert Miller is later directly documented in the Oregon City/Fifth Street sequence.

Historic-district source:
https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/272e10e2-b0bf-4107-b28c-e6d0aa35dd4e

The archive must nevertheless preserve the Guard's `C. E. Miller` wording rather than rewriting it.

By the July 1922 fire, **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop** was one of the Fifth Street businesses destroyed.

A **21 Sep 1922 Banner-Courier** advertisement shows Gilbert Miller had reopened after the fire at **209 Fifth Street**, in the former Goldman second-hand store. This is a post-fire relocation marker and should not be confused with his destroyed pre-fire shop.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-09-21/ed-1/seq-5/

This creates a real business/person continuity from:

1. **Wolf & Miller at 505 Main in 1918** →
2. Gilbert Miller strongly identified as the Springfield partner moving to Oregon City in Aug 1918 →
3. Gilbert Miller operating a shoe-repair shop in the Fifth Street fire cluster by July 1922 →
4. Gilbert Miller reopening at 209 Fifth after the fire.

This continuity is important, but it does **not** by itself identify the `Wolf` partner or prove that the 1922 building owner **S. Wolf** was the same person.

### Probability shift on `S. Wolf = Wolf & Miller partner`

The chronology now weakens, rather than strengthens, a simple identity equation between the 1918 Wolf partner and the 1922 property owner.

- Gilbert Miller is documented moving to **Oregon City by August 1918** and is still there afterward.
- The **10 Oct 1918** Wolf & Miller dissolution notice says **one partner would go to Idaho** and the other would restock and continue the business.

The most natural reading is therefore that **Gilbert Miller was the partner who stayed in Oregon City and the Wolf partner was probably the one who left for Idaho**.

That is an inference, not explicit wording in the dissolution notice. It means the archive should now treat **`S. Wolf of Portland in 1922 = Wolf & Miller partner` as possible but weaker than before**, unless later evidence shows that the Wolf partner returned from Idaho or that the dissolution wording had another meaning.

## Gilbert Miller / Clara Rose Wolf family-network lead

A compiled genealogy source identifies Gilbert Miller's first wife as **Clara Rose Wolf (1879–1921)**, daughter of **Henry and Elizabeth (Conrad) Wolf**. Clara died in Oregon City in May 1921. The same compiled source states Gilbert Miller had **no middle name**.

Source:
https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G001607.pdf

This provides a concrete family connection between Gilbert Miller and a Wolf family and makes it plausible that some Wolf/Miller business or property ties could have been family-based.

However:

- this is a **compiled genealogical source**, not a deed or contemporary business record;
- it does not identify **S. Wolf**;
- it does not identify the Wolf partner in Wolf & Miller;
- it does not prove that the later Wolf-family owner of 503 was Clara's family.

Use this only as a research lead until corroborated with census, probate, marriage/death, deed, or directory evidence.

## Portland / Oregon City Wolf candidate context — do not select yet

The **1920 Polk's Portland City Directory** includes multiple Wolf entries relevant to candidate screening:

- **Samuel Wolf**, in clothing, 33 N. 3rd, residence 691 Elliott Ave.;
- **Simon Wolf**, associated with Wonder Millinery, residence 340 10th;
- **Victor Wolf**, a clerk with Goodyear Rubber Co., whose residence is listed as **Oregon City**.

Source:
https://ldsgenealogy.com/OR/books/Polks-Portland-City-Directory-1920-Vol-II-part-110.htm

Samuel's clothing trade is superficially compatible with the clothing/shoe character of Wolf & Miller, but **that is not identity proof**. Searches using Samuel and Simon's full names have not yet produced a defensible Oregon City / Wolf & Miller / target-property bridge. Victor provides evidence of another Wolf with an Oregon City connection but does not match the `S. Wolf` initial. These entries should remain separate candidates/context until a deed, business record, family record, or property source makes a direct bridge.

## Related 503 Main Wolf-family lead

The City of Oregon City's historic inventory, as preserved in `buildings/503-main.md`, says 1983 survey research reported that the **Wolf family owned 503 Main from about 1922 to 1972**.

This creates a multi-part chronological/geographic pattern worth testing:

- **1918:** Wolf & Miller operates at **505 Main**.
- **1921:** Gilbert Miller's wife Clara Rose Wolf dies in Oregon City.
- **8 Jun 1922:** **S. Wolf** is named among Oregon City merchants/business people backing the Buyers' Carnival; Gilbert Miller is named separately in the same list.
- **July 1922:** **S. Wolf of Portland** owns a Main Street building where the Fifth/Main fire begins; Gilbert Miller has a nearby Fifth Street shoe-repair shop destroyed in the same fire.
- **about 1922 onward:** later survey research places **Wolf-family ownership at 503 Main**.

## Best surviving primary-record path — 1922–1925 assessment/tax rolls

The Oregon Secretary of State's **Clackamas County Records Inventory** confirms that unusually valuable assessment/tax records survive for the exact period in question.

The surviving record series include:

- **Assessment & Tax Roll, 1922–1925** at the Clackamas County Assessment and Taxation Office (microfilm);
- corresponding **1922–1925 assessment/tax rolls** in county security microfilm storage;
- **Index [Assessment and Tax Roll], 1901–1949** at the Oregon State Archives;
- **Indexes Old Books / Land and Lot Books**, including 1920 and 1925, at Clackamas County;
- **Index to Land and Lot Books**, including 1920 and 1925, at the Museum of the Oregon Territory.

Official inventory:
https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/county-records-guide/Pages/clackamas-inventory.aspx

Oregon State Archives land-record guide also confirms Clackamas **Assessment and Tax Rolls, 1850–1949**, an **Assessment and Tax Rolls Index, 1901–1949**, and deed records through 1957.

Source:
https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/records/Pages/aids-land.aspx

### Why these records matter

These rolls record **property owner, property description, assessed value and taxes**. A 1922/1923 entry for the target Main Street parcel(s) could therefore directly answer:

1. whether the fire-start parcel was owned by S. Wolf;
2. S. Wolf's full name if the index expands the initial or can be cross-referenced;
3. whether S. Wolf owned 501, 503, 505, or another nearby parcel;
4. whether the later `Wolf family about 1922–1972` 503 ownership began with the same person;
5. whether assessed value/building description changes between 1922 and 1925 indicate fire loss or rebuilding.

No public digitized image of the specific 1922 target roll/index has yet been recovered in the current web search. This is now an **actionable archival retrieval target**, not a generic suggestion to check tax records.

## Current assessment

Do **not** merge these Wolf references yet.

The current evidence does **not** prove that:

- S. Wolf of Portland was the Wolf partner in Wolf & Miller;
- S. Wolf was Samuel Wolf or Simon Wolf of Portland;
- Victor Wolf was connected to the target property chain;
- S. Wolf of Portland was the Wolf-family purchaser/owner of 503 Main;
- the former Schieremann tailor-shop building was 501, 503, or 505 Main;
- Timms Pool Hall was the pool hall adjoining Schieremann's former tailor shop;
- the fire destroyed or materially damaged the surviving 503 or 505 building;
- the fire explains the city's c.1919 / c.1920 construction estimates for 503/505.

However, the combined Wolf/Miller/business/property pattern is now strong enough to make this one of the highest-priority property-chain leads in the archive.

## Fire geometry implication

The Banner-Courier says the fire began in a Main Street store room, with a pool hall adjoining that tailoring establishment, and spread through the Fifth Street laundry/garage/restaurant/shoe-repair cluster. This strongly supports reconstructing the burned area as a connected **Main-to-Fifth commercial zone** rather than treating the laundry and side-street businesses as isolated from the Main frontage.

This may help interpret:

- the pre-fire laundry photographs;
- the 1925 Sanborn rear-of-block geometry;
- the 501/503/505 predecessor-building sequence;
- Wolf-family acquisition/rebuilding activity around 1922;
- the relationship between the 1918 Wolf & Miller business at 505 and later Gilbert Miller/S. Wolf property activity.
