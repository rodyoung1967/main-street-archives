## Primary purpose of this research thread

Reconstruct the **18 July 1922 Fifth/Main fire** and determine exactly which modern Main Street storefront — **501, 503, or 505 Main** — corresponds to the Main Street storeroom where the fire began.

The decisive linked questions are:

1. What exact address did **Alex Schieremann's former tailor shop** occupy?
2. What exact premises was the **adjoining pool hall**?
3. Was the unnamed cleaning/dyeing concern that reportedly vacated the ignition room only days before the fire the **Pantorium** or another cleaner?
4. Which Main Street property did **S. Wolf of Portland** own, and was that ownership the beginning of the later Wolf-family ownership reported for 503 Main?
5. How do the destroyed Fifth Street businesses — Oregon City Laundry, May & Washburn garage, Fifth Street Restaurant, Gilbert Miller shoe repair, and H. Ketchner confectionery/home — line up spatially?
6. Is S. Wolf connected to the earlier **Wolf & Miller** business, Gilbert Miller's Wolf-family connections, or neither?

## Current strongest evidence state

### Fire origin and S. Wolf

The local 20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier, visually reviewed, says:

- fire began in a vacant Main Street storeroom;
- it had formerly been occupied by **Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop**;
- the building was owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**;
- a pool hall adjoined the former tailor premises;
- the pool-hall roof partly burned and billiard tables were water-damaged;
- Oregon City Laundry, May & Washburn garage, Fifth Street Restaurant, Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop, H. Ketchner's home/confectionery, and other structures were destroyed.

A same-day Eugene account, also visually reviewed, says the ignition room had been occupied until only a few days earlier by a **cleaning-and-dyeing establishment** and that fire spread from that shop directly to Oregon City Laundry. Preserve the difference rather than normalizing the two accounts.

A later 13 Jul 1922 property-division source already integrated on `main` gives **C. E. Gates the Fifth Street Restaurant location and S. Wolf the remainder** of the Harding property division. Post-fire Gates construction produced a two-story hollow-tile building with two ground-floor store rooms. Exact mapping to modern 501/503/505 remains unresolved.

### 1916 Oregon City Laundry site change — resolved

The former February/April 1916 location conflict is resolved:

- 3 Feb 1916 — replacement laundry initially planned behind the Courier office / Seventh–Eighth block;
- 17 Feb 1916 — E. M. Blakeslee explicitly traded that intended property for a **Fifth Street location**;
- 2 Apr 1916 — replacement laundry on Fifth just off Main was nearing completion;
- 1918 — Oregon City Laundry directly listed at **109 Fifth**.

### June 8, 1922 Buyers' Carnival — full visual audit

All 14 scanned pages were visually reviewed.

The merchant roster separately includes Walter Little, Pantorium, Timms Pool Hall, Fifth Street Restaurant, Gilbert Miller, O. H. Leland, C. G. Miller Company, and S. Wolf.

Important corrections/results:

- **A. D. Wheeler & Co. and Fifth Street Restaurant are separate comma-delimited entries.** Do not merge them.
- **S. F. Scripture advertises at 108 Fifth, between Main and Water, on 8 Jun 1922.** Therefore May & Washburn cannot simply be assumed to have inherited Scripture & May's 108 Fifth premises by June 1922.
- No exact address for Timms, Leland/O. H. Leland, Pantorium, S. Wolf, Fifth Street Restaurant, May & Washburn, or Schieremann was recovered in this particular issue. This is issue-specific coverage only, not evidence of absence.

### W. H. Nelson — excluded from Main–Water fire cluster

A visually reviewed 6 Jul 1922 advertisement places W. H. Nelson's blacksmith shop on **Fifth between Main and Railroad**, east of Main. He should not be treated as an occupant candidate for the Fifth Street Main–Water fire block.

Keep the distinct chains separate:

- Feb 1922 — **Leland & Little** appears among Oregon City cigar dealers;

### Pantorium / cleaning-dyeing candidate

Documented address/trade sequence:

- Oct 1918 — Pantorium Cleaners at **500 Main**, phone 185-J;
- Jan 1922 — Pantorium Cleaners active in dry-cleaning/dyeing trade; recovered ad gives no address;
- Jun 1922 — Pantorium active merchant; no address in Buyers' Carnival roster;
- 20 Jul 1922 — Pantorium at **519 Main**; Oregon City Laundry used it as a temporary receiving office after the fire.

519 Main occupancy ladder:

- Feb 1918 — Theroux Music Company announces move from 519 Main;
- Oct 1918 — Frank Neldon at 519 Main;
- Jan 1922 — Neldon at 425 Main at Fifth, so he had left 519;

Pantorium's arrival date at 519 remains unresolved. It cannot yet be either identified as or excluded as the cleaner/dyer that had just vacated the ignition room.

A separate 1919 cleaning/dyeing advertisement used phone 260 and the wording `500 Fifth and Main streets`, `two doors south of new post office`. Do not equate that unnamed advertiser with Pantorium without direct evidence.

### Gilbert Miller / Miller's Shoe Store

Visually verified pre-fire ads place **Miller's Shoe Store** on Main Street **opposite the Post Office**, selling shoes/gloves and advertising expert shoe repairing.

The July fire report names **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop and shoe stock** among the losses.

A signed 21 Sep 1922 Gilbert Miller ad at **209 Fifth** says his shoe machinery was again set up for the same repairing done before the fire. By 26 Oct 1922 **MILLER'S SHOE STORE** appears at **419 Main** with a compatible shoe/repair profile.

Therefore `Miller's Shoe Store = Gilbert Miller` is currently a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**, not yet a direct proprietor/trade-name equation. The exact pre-fire Main Street number remains unresolved.

### Post Office chronology — critical distinction

The project now has two different Post Office periods that must not be conflated.

**Earlier Post Office, circa 1911–1912:**

- 505 Main Chicago Store advertised `Main St. just below Postoffice`;
- George A. Harding at **511 Main** was advertised `Next Door to Postoffice`;
- a visually verified **26 Apr 1912 Oregon City Shoe Store ad at 508 Main says `Opposite Postoffice`**;
- other cross-source evidence places I. Tolpolar at 514 Main and opposite the Post Office.

The 508 Main control strongly supports the **earlier Post Office being on the odd/west side of Main Street**. **513 Main remains only a working candidate; exact Post Office number is not proven.** This directly supports the user's recollection that there was an earlier Post Office on the odd-number side near Fifth/Main.

**Later Post Office, 1919–1922:**

- a 12 Sep 1918 Courier report records a ten-year lease for an M. D. Latourette building on the **east side of Main between Fifth and Sixth**;
- later evidence places the new Post Office in service by spring 1919;
- City historic-resource work identifies the Latourette-David-Barlow building as **504–514 Main**;
- cross-source property evidence strongly aligns the Post Office with Block 26 Lots 5–6, but the exact even-numbered storefront bay remains unresolved.

Thus in **July 1922**, `Opposite Post Office` advertising points toward the **odd/west Main frontage**, where 501/503/505 lie. Dr. Freeze is directly documented at **505½ Main** and `Opposite Postoffice`; Miller's Shoe Store uses the same relative wording. This materially constrains geography but does not by itself choose 501, 503, or 505.
