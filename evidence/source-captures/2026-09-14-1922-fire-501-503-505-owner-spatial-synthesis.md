# 18 July 1922 fire — 501/503/505 frontage, ownership, and spatial synthesis

Date integrated: 14 September 2026

## Purpose

Integrate the project-steward's frontage reconstruction with the already visually verified 13, 20, and 21 July 1922 newspaper evidence. This record supersedes the earlier treatment of **503 Main as merely an unranked fire-origin candidate** to the extent stated below. It does **not** convert newspaper ownership reporting into deed proof, and it preserves the unresolved Red Front location and the S. Wolf / L. Wolf initial conflict.

## Evidence-state summary

The current reconstruction distinguishes direct statements from conclusions forced by the combined frontage/fire evidence.

### Direct / visually verified source facts

1. **13 July 1922 Banner-Courier:** George A. Harding's Main Street property/store group was reported sold to **S. Wolf and C. Gates**. The **Fifth Street Restaurant location went to Gates** and the remainder of the named property group went to Wolf (`E-096` / `S-111`). The article does not print modern street numbers or dimensions.
2. **20 July 1922 Banner-Courier:** the fire was discovered in a vacant room formerly occupied by **Alex Schiermann as a tailor**; **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair/shop business and shoe stock were destroyed**; a **pool hall adjoining the tailoring establishment** suffered water damage to billiard tables and part of its roof burned, rather than being reported razed; the fire-origin building was reported owned by **S. Wolf of Portland** (`E-103` / `S-117`).
3. **21 July 1922 Oregon City Enterprise, p.1:** the fire broke out in a **vacant half** formerly occupied by Schiermann as a tailor. The same report describes the origin structure as the **shoe-store building in which the fire started** and says **L. Wolf, 33 N. 3rd St., Portland**, had recently purchased it. The article says the **Fifth Street Restaurant was a total loss**, says the restaurant building was owned by **C. P. Gates of Portland**, and identifies Wolf as owner of the **Red Front clothing store**, which was damaged by the flames. It also says the **building to the north of the fire was saved by fireproof construction**. The article reports three structures at the corner as gutted. The S. Wolf / L. Wolf wording remains an identity/initial conflict until reconciled.
4. The project has an established numbered Main Street frontage sequence of **501 → 503 → 505 → 507** for the target odd-numbered side. The 1925 Sanborn directly maps 501, 503, and 505 as separate numbered commercial occupancies; it is later evidence and is not by itself proof of July 1922 use continuity.
5. Project-steward fire reconstruction treats **501 Main as one of the structures burned/destroyed in the July fire** and the **505 pool-hall structure as damaged but not destroyed**. The surviving 505 conclusion is consistent with the contemporary pool-hall damage wording and the Enterprise fireproof-construction statement; see inference below.

## 503 Main — Miller / Schiermann fire-origin building

### STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE

Given the established frontage sequence and the fire outcomes now assembled, the fire-origin shoe-store/tailor building is identified as **503 Main Street**:

- 501 was in the burned corner group;
- the adjoining pool hall is fixed in the project reconstruction at **505 Main** and was damaged rather than destroyed;
- the 20 July report says that pool hall **adjoined the former Schiermann tailoring establishment**;
- therefore the Main Street building immediately south of 505 in the numbered frontage is **503**;
- the 21 July report says the fire began in a **vacant half formerly occupied by Schiermann** and calls the same structure the **shoe-store building**;
- the 20 July report independently establishes that **Gilbert Miller's shoe shop was destroyed**.

The best reconstruction is therefore that **503 Main contained Gilbert Miller's shoe-store premises and a separate vacant half/portion formerly occupied by Alex Schiermann's tailoring establishment**. The word **half** is important because it resolves the earlier apparent contradiction between a vacant tailor room and an operating shoe business in the same building.

This is not a direct advertisement reading "Miller's Shoe Store, 503 Main." It is classified **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**, not DOCUMENTED / DIRECT, because the numbered address is derived from independent adjacency, fire-outcome, and frontage evidence.

### Ownership at 503

Contemporary fire reporting directly assigns the **fire-origin building** to Wolf: Banner-Courier prints **S. Wolf**, while Enterprise prints **L. Wolf** and gives the Portland address **33 N. 3rd St.** The Enterprise further says Wolf had **recently purchased the shoe-store building in which the fire started**. Because the fire-origin building is now identified by strong cross-source inference as 503, **Wolf ownership of the 503 fire-origin building on 18 July 1922 is likewise a STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**.

Do not yet state the Wolf owner's full identity as settled. Preserve **S. Wolf vs. L. Wolf** as an unresolved source conflict pending directory/deed/genealogical reconciliation. Also keep building ownership, land/title ownership, and business ownership separate until the Harding→Wolf/Gates deeds are obtained (`MF-006`).

## 505 Main — adjoining pool hall and fireproof survival

### STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE

The July fire account says the pool hall adjoining Schiermann's tailoring establishment had **water-damaged billiard tables and a partially burned roof**, rather than being destroyed. The Enterprise says the **building to the north of the fire was saved by fireproof construction**. In the accepted frontage reconstruction, the adjoining pool hall is **505 Main**, immediately north of the 503 fire-origin building.

Accordingly record:

- **505 Main was the adjoining pool/billiard-hall structure in the July 1922 fire reconstruction.**
- **505 did not burn down.** It sustained water/roof fire damage, but the structure survived.
- Its survival is attributed in the Enterprise fire account to **fireproof construction** of the building north of the fire. This should be phrased as **saved from destruction by fireproof construction**, not "did not burn"; the Banner-Courier specifically reports partial roof burning.

This is **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE** because the contemporary fire articles do not themselves print "505" beside the pool hall in the quoted fire description. Preserve the direct 1925 Sanborn billiards/cigars use as later corroborative spatial evidence, not as proof of uninterrupted operation or the July operator's identity.

### Ownership at 505

The 13 July sale report gives Wolf the **remainder** of Harding's named property group after the Fifth Street Restaurant location went to Gates. The current reconstruction places 503 in that Wolf remainder. It is plausible that 505 was also within Wolf's acquired remainder, but **the present newspaper evidence does not independently prove the legal/building owner of 505 on 18 July**. Do not silently extend Wolf's direct ownership statement for the ignition building to the adjoining pool-hall building. The Harding→Wolf/Gates deeds and title abstract remain the proper ownership discriminator (`MF-006`, `MF-007`).

## 501 Main — burned corner building and Gates ownership question

The project-steward reconstruction treats **501 Main as burned/destroyed in the July 1922 fire**. The Enterprise directly says the **Fifth Street Restaurant was a total loss** and that its **building was owned by C. P. Gates of Portland**. The 13 July sale report likewise assigns the Fifth Street Restaurant location to Gates.

The strongest present reconstruction maps the Gates/Fifth Street Restaurant corner building to **501 Main**. On that reconstruction:

- **501 Main burned/destroyed in the July fire**;
- **C. P. Gates was the building owner** associated with the Fifth Street Restaurant premises;
- the restaurant business/operator ownership remains separate from Gates's building ownership (the Enterprise names **Blakesley** in connection with ownership of the restaurant business).

However, no recovered July source prints **"501"** beside the Fifth Street Restaurant or Gates. Therefore **Gates = owner of the 501 building is retained as STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE, not deed-level/direct numbered-address proof**. `MF-006` and `MF-007` remain open for legal confirmation and exact parcel dimensions.

Post-fire construction reporting in August identifies Gates rebuilding the Fifth/Main restaurant site as a **two-story hollow-tile building with two ground-floor storerooms**. Do not automatically equate those two replacement storerooms with later 501+503 without a footprint/plan/deed bridge.

## Red Front Clothing Store — ownership known, exact location unresolved

The **21 July Enterprise** directly identifies **L. Wolf of 33 N. 3rd St., Portland** as owner of the **Red Front clothing store**, which was damaged by the fire. The 13 July property-sale reporting also places the Red Front within the Harding property/store group involved in the Wolf/Gates transaction context.

What is **not** yet established is the Red Front's exact numbered storefront in the July 1922 frontage. Do **not** assign Red Front to 501, 503, 505, 505½, 507, or another number merely from adjacency or ownership. Its precise location remains an open spatial question.

This is now a priority discriminator because locating Red Front could further constrain the Wolf/Gates boundary and the internal arrangement of the fire-affected frontage.

## Superseded / corrected project statements

- Supersede prior wording that treated **503 fire-origin / 505 pool hall as merely an unranked working hypothesis**. With the accepted 501-burned and 505-surviving pool-hall frontage constraints, the 503 identification is now **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**.

- Do not collapse **S. Wolf** and **L. Wolf** into one fully identified person until the conflict is resolved.
