# 1959 Oregon City mixed telephone-number format — 9179 conversion caution

Date reviewed: **8 September 2026**

Status: **DIGITIZED DIRECTORY-DERIVATIVE TEXT / ORIGINAL INTERNET ARCHIVE BOOK IDENTIFIED; TARGET PAGE IMAGE NOT RENDERED IN CURRENT RESEARCH ENVIRONMENT; NUMBER-CONVERSION CAUTION ONLY; NO 505 SUBSCRIBER IDENTIFIED.**

## Purpose

Test whether the visually verified four-digit telephone number **9179** printed in the 20 November 1955 *Eugene Register-Guard* advertisement for the beer/pool business at **505 Main Street, Oregon City** can safely be converted into a later seven-digit Oregon City number, especially a hypothesized `656-9179` / `OLive 6-9179` form.

This is a telephone-numbering methodology check, not an occupancy, proprietor, licensee, or business-transfer finding.

## 1959 Polk directory derivative — mixed Oregon City number formats

The second example is especially important because it explicitly distinguishes the printed four-digit **Oregon City** number `5228` from a separate **Portland** Olive-number contact in the same line.

## Additional contemporary seven-digit control

A **15 December 1960 Sandy Post** item prints the Clackamas County Extension Office in Oregon City as **`OLive 6-2641`**, providing an independent contemporary newspaper-text control that `OLive 6` was in active Oregon City use by the end of 1960.

Discovery route:

`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2024240281/1960-12-15/ed-1/seq-8.pdf`

The same general period also preserves seven-digit Oregon City numbers in later Sandy Post material. These controls establish use of the `OLive 6` / later `656` exchange form; they do **not** establish the conversion history of any particular four-digit subscriber number.

## Why this matters for 9179

The 1959 directory derivative shows that the available evidence does **not** support a simple project rule such as:

> old four-digit Oregon City number `NNNN` → later `656-NNNN`.

At least in the printed 1959 advertising evidence, Oregon City-associated contacts appear in both a seven-digit `OLive 6-xxxx` form and a separate four-digit local-number form. The bank advertisement also distinguishes its Oregon City four-digit number from a Portland Olive-number contact.

Therefore:

- **do not convert `9179` to `OLive 6-9179` or `656-9179` without a same-subscriber or telephone-company conversion source;**
- a later occurrence of `656-9179` cannot be treated as continuity from the 1955 505 advertisement solely because the final four digits match;
- later reassignment/reuse of the same final four digits remains possible; and
- the authoritative route remains a **1953–1956 Oregon City telephone directory** or another contemporary subscriber record that directly associates `9179` with a person or business.
