# 1913 Main Street research — expanded chronological pass

Date researched: 21 August 2026

This note records the 1913 chronological pass for the Main Street project. It distinguishes **direct numbered-address evidence**, **strong continuity evidence**, and **high-confidence reconstructed addresses**. Reconstructed addresses are not promoted to direct fact merely because multiple clues agree.

## 503 Main — still unresolved

### Documentary bracket

- **5 January 1912:** H. N. Smith directly advertised at **503 Main**, pool-room / cigars and tobacco.
- **March 1912:** pool-hall-license notice at 503 recorded as **H. H. Smith**; H.N./H.H. identity conflict remains unresolved.
- **28 February 1913:** *Oregon City Courier* reports **Ellery Baily**, owner of **a pool room on Main Street**, had contracted smallpox.
- The same newspaper period uses **Ellerd Bailey** for an apparently same local man; later records also use Ellerd Bailey.
- **12 February 1914:** L. Hylton is directly documented at **503 Main**.

The Bailey lead is attractive because the business type matches the Smith-era 503 pool-room context, but **business type plus Main Street is not an address**. No recovered 1913 source places Bailey at 503.

### Recommended handling

**503 Main — 1913 operator unresolved.** Search Ellerd Bailey / Ellery Baily / Bailey, Smith, Hylton, pool room, cigars, billiards, and license records.

## 505 Main — Chicago Store to Hogg Bros. reconstruction

### 26 January 1913 — Chicago Store directly at 505

*Morning Enterprise*, 26 January 1913, page 2:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-01-26/ed-1/seq-2/`

The Chicago Store advertisement explicitly gives **505 Main Street, Oregon City**.

### 10 March 1911 — the old Harding Building / George Young doorway chain

A 1911 *Courier* report says **George Young moved his second-hand store one door north into the Willamette Building** after occupying the **Harding Building on Main Street between Fifth and Sixth**.

George Young is then directly at **507 Main** on 5 January 1912. Absent evidence of another move, this strongly reconstructs the storefront one door south — the old Harding Building — as **505 Main**.

### 16 May 1913 — Hogg Bros. rents the old Harding Building

*Oregon City Courier*, 16 May 1913, reports that Hogg Bros. rented the **Harding Building on Main Street, four doors south of the Post Office**, and planned to open around 20 May.

### 13 June 1913 — Hogg Bros. open just below Post Office

*Oregon City Courier*, 13 June 1913, describes the new Hogg Bros. Main Street store as **just below the postoffice**.

### 505 conclusion

The evidence strongly supports this 1913 transition:

> **26 Jan 1913 Chicago Store directly at 505 → by 16 May Hogg Bros. in the old Harding Building, high-confidence reconstructed as 505.**

No exact move/sale notice has yet been found, and no recovered 1913 ad literally prints **Hogg Bros., 505 Main**.

Detailed note: `../1911-1913-505-harding-building-postoffice-reconstruction.md`.

## 507 Main — G. H. Young continuity

### Direct bracket

- **5 Jan 1912:** G. H. Young directly at **507 Main**, "The Curio Man."
- **2 May 1913:** Young advertises buying/selling curios, second-hand furniture and tools at **Main near Fifth Street**.
- **16 Apr 1914:** later business listing again gives G. H. Young at **507 Main**.

This is a strong continuity bracket. The 1913 source itself is relative rather than numbered, so the archive should say **G. H. Young very likely continued at 507 in 1913**, not that 507 is directly printed in the May ad.

## 509 Main — H. P. Brightbill continuity

### Evidence

- **5 Jan 1912:** H. P. Brightbill directly at **509 Main**, phone 74.
- **22 Jul 1913:** Brightbill advertising continues, retaining **phone 74**.
- Later exact-address evidence again places Brightbill at **509 Main** with the same phone.

This is strong continuity evidence for Brightbill at 509 through 1913, but the recovered July 1913 ad does not itself print 509.

## 511 Main — George A. Harding continuity

### Evidence

- **5 Jan 1912:** George A. Harding directly at **511 Main**.
- **22 Jul 1913:** Harding advertises his drug business in the **Willamette Building**.
- Later exact-address evidence again places Harding at **511 Main**.

This strongly supports 511 continuity in 1913. It also helps explain the early building geography: the **Willamette Building** appears associated with storefronts north of the separate old Harding Building occupied by George Young before his 1911 move.

Do not confuse the **old Harding Building** in the 1911-1913 storefront reconstruction with the later **1968 Harding Building at 507-511 Main**.

## 513 Main — old Post Office, high-confidence reconstruction

### Block-level proof

**28 Feb 1913:** the *Courier* describes an alley immediately **north of the Post Office building** running through to Water Street and ground behind the Post Office.

**20 Nov 1913:** Willamette Hall is described as **just south of the Post Office**.

**19 Nov 1914:** Willamette Hall is explicitly located on **Main Street between Fifth and Sixth Streets**.

Together these fix the old Post Office to the Fifth-Sixth Main Street block area.

### Independent door-count proof

**16 May 1913:** Hogg Bros. rents the old Harding Building **four doors south of the Post Office**.

The old Harding Building is independently reconstructed as **505 Main** from George Young's 1911 one-door-north move into his later 507 storefront.

Counting north from 505:

1. 507
2. 509
3. 511
4. **513**

This independently lands the old Post Office at **513 Main**.

### Other corroboration

- Chicago Store at **505**: "Main St. just below Postoffice."
- I. Tolpolar at **514**: opposite the Post Office.
- Harding directly at **511**.
- Dunn/Lent: next door to the Post Office.

### Recommended archive wording

> **Old Oregon City Post Office — 513 Main: probable / high-confidence reconstructed address.** Exact numbered-address primary source still sought.

Detailed note: `../1913-1914-postoffice-relocation.md`.

## 515 Main — Dunn to Lent confectionery reconstruction

### 11 September 1913 — direct business succession

*Oregon City Courier*, 11 September 1913, page 5:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-09-11/ed-1/seq-5/`

The paper reports that **F. G. Lent purchased the confectionery store of M. E. Dunn near the postoffice** and took possession Wednesday morning.

The same page separately says the Dunn family had been **living at Sixth and Main** before moving to Seventh and John Adams. That is a residential statement, not the confectionery address.

### 23 October 1913 — Lent still next door to P.O.

*Oregon City Courier*, 23 October 1913, page 5:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-5/`

A notice directs inquiries to **"Lent's next door to Postoffice."**

Because the Post Office did not move to its courthouse-area replacement until March 1914, this means the old reconstructed **513 Main** Post Office.

With Harding directly at 511, the remaining adjacent odd-side storefront is **515 Main**.

### Recommended archive wording

> **515 Main — M. E. Dunn confectionery → F. G. Lent, 1913: high-confidence reconstructed address; exact numbered-address source still sought.**

Detailed note: `../1913-dunn-lent-confectionery-postoffice.md`.

## 527 Main — do not place Price Bros. there in 1913

Current evidence:

- **5 Jan 1912:** Bailey & Price, Billiard and Pool Parlors, directly at **527 Main**.
- **2 May 1913:** Price Bros. is advertised at **Sixth and Main**.
- **22 Jul 1913:** Price Bros. remains at **Sixth and Main**.
- **1915:** Price Bros. is directly documented at **527 Main**.

Therefore **Price Bros. should not be back-projected into 527 Main for May-July 1913**. The 527 occupant and the timing/mechanics of the Bailey & Price → later Price Bros. relationship remain open.

## Old Post Office lease and replacement construction

### 13 June 1913

A government inspector criticized the old Post Office quarters and reported that the existing **ten-year lease would expire in December 1913**. Receipts had grown from roughly $7,000 to roughly $20,000 during the lease period.

### Replacement chronology

- **23 Oct 1913:** replacement Post Office announced **opposite the courthouse**.
- **6 Nov 1913:** private parties begin construction under a **five-year government rental contract**.
- **20 Nov 1913:** old house removed from the replacement site.
- **18 Dec 1913:** cement foundation walls are in.
- **26 Mar 1914:** newspaper announces the Post Office will move into the new building opposite the courthouse on Saturday/Sunday.
- **28-29 Mar 1914:** scheduled relocation weekend.

### Interpretive rule

> **Every ordinary 1913 business reference to "the Post Office" as a landmark refers to the old Fifth-Sixth-block Post Office, not the replacement under construction near the courthouse.**

Detailed note: `../1913-1914-postoffice-relocation.md`.

## Hogg Bros. later relocation

The first Hogg Bros. Oregon City store opened in the old Harding Building in May 1913, strongly reconstructed as 505 Main.

By early 1914 Hogg Bros. is described as next door to the *Courier* office in the Eighth/Main district, and by **16 April 1914** a business listing directly gives **806 Main**.

Thus Hogg Bros. **relocated between the mid-1913 505-area store and the 1914 806 Main store**. The exact move date remains open.

Do not back-project the later 806 (or still later 804) address into the May-June 1913 Hogg location.

## 1913-1914 Polk directory — highest-value remaining source

The **Oregon and Washington State Gazetteer and Business Directory, 1913-1914**, R. L. Polk & Co., is confirmed to exist. Available FamilySearch access required sign-in during this research session, so the Oregon City pages have not yet been inspected.

This remains probably the single best source for converting several reconstructions into direct-address evidence.

No address claim should be attributed to the directory until its Oregon City pages are actually read.

## 1913 closeout status

The 1913 pass has now established a coherent Fifth-Sixth-block reconstruction and several business transitions, but **1913 is not yet fully closed** because important exact-address questions remain.
