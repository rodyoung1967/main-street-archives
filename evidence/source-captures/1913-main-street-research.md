Date researched: 21 August 2026

This note begins the 1913 chronological pass after closing the 1912 archival pass. It records only claims supported by recovered primary sources and leaves storefront continuity unresolved where direct 1913 evidence has not yet been found.

## 505 Main — Chicago Store continuity

### Morning Enterprise — 26 January 1913 — page 2

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-01-26/ed-1/seq-2/`

A Chicago Store advertisement explicitly gives **505 Main Street, Oregon City**. This confirms that the Chicago Store remained at 505 Main into January 1913.

## Oregon City Post Office transition during 1913

The 1913 newspapers reveal that the Post Office location itself was in transition late in the year. This is important because references to businesses being "near," "below," or "opposite" the Post Office must be date-specific.

### Oregon City Courier — 13 June 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-06-13/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that a government post-office inspector criticized the existing quarters and that the existing **ten-year lease would expire in December 1913**. Better quarters were being sought.

The same issue describes **Hogg Bros.** as having opened a Main Street store **just below the postoffice**. This is a useful relative-location clue but does not by itself establish a numbered address for Hogg Bros. or the Post Office.

### Oregon City Courier — 23 October 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that a **new postoffice building** was to be erected **opposite the court house**.

Do not convert this phrase into a numbered Main Street address until the 1913 courthouse location/frontage is established from a contemporary source. Modern courthouse addresses are not sufficient evidence for the 1913 street geometry.

### Oregon City Courier — 6 November 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-06/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that **private parties** had begun erecting a post-office building under a **five-year rental contract with the government**. This establishes that the new quarters then under construction were privately built/leased rather than automatically proving construction of a federally owned post-office building.

### Oregon City Courier — 20 November 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-20/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that **David Williams** purchased the old house that stood on the property where the new postoffice building was to be erected and was moving the house to his Centre Street property between Eleventh and Twelfth streets. This gives a physical-site transition clue but still does not provide the new post-office site's numbered Main Street address.

### Oregon City Courier — 18 December 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-12-18/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that the **cement foundation walls for the new postoffice building were in** and that wall construction was proceeding.

## Interpretation of the 1913 Post Office evidence

The recovered evidence supports this sequence:

1. The old Post Office quarters were still in use in June 1913 and their ten-year lease was due to expire in December.
2. By October, a replacement post-office building was planned opposite the courthouse.
3. By November, private parties were building the replacement under a five-year government rental agreement.
4. An older house was removed from the construction site in November.
5. Foundation work was visibly underway by December.

This means **1913 contains a Post Office relocation/construction transition**. Any 1913 business advertisement using the Post Office as a landmark must therefore be tied to its publication date before using it in a storefront reconstruction.

The evidence does **not yet** establish the exact numbered address of either the old Post Office quarters or the replacement building.

## 503 Main — transition still unresolved in 1913

Current documentary boundary:

- January 1912: **H. N. Smith** is directly advertised at 503 Main.
- March 1912: pool-hall-license notice at 503 is recorded as **H. H. Smith**; identity relationship remains unresolved.
- 12 February 1914: **L. Hylton** is directly documented at **503 Main** in an Oregon City Courier business profile (`S-025`, `E-024`).

No clean primary source has yet been recovered in this first pass establishing exactly when the 503 business changed from the Smith-era pool-room operation to Hylton's business during 1913. Do not assign Hylton to 503 in 1913 without direct evidence.

## 507 / 509 / 511 Main — first-pass status

The 5 January 1912 page directly documents:

- 507 — G. H. Young
- 509 — H. P. Brightbill
- 511 — George A. Harding

This first 1913 pass did **not** recover a direct numbered-address advertisement for those three storefronts in 1913. Absence from this search is not evidence that they vacated. Continuity must be established from dated 1913 sources before being entered as fact.
