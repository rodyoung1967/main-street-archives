## First confirmed 1913 evidence — Block 26 ownership

### Morning Enterprise, 23 October 1913, p. 8

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-8/

A municipal assessment notice identifies owners of the four Main Street-side lots of **Block 26**:

- **Lot 5 — Mary E. Barlow and Juliette E. David — $10.71 assessment**
- **Lot 6 — First National Bank — $10.71 assessment**
- **Lot 7 — Frank Jaggar — $21.42 assessment**
- **Lot 8 — Gambrinus Brewing Company — $32.13 assessment**

This is direct primary evidence for the 1913 ownership pattern of Block 26. It is particularly valuable because Block 26 is the block under reconstruction for the later 501 / 503 / 505 / 507 Main addresses.

### Interpretation limits

This notice establishes the named owners for lots 5–8 for purposes of the published assessment. It **does not by itself establish which 1913 street number corresponded to each lot**, which business occupied each lot, or whether a particular building footprint was identical to the one shown in 1925. Those mappings require independent evidence.

### Why it matters

This gives the year-by-year reconstruction a strong property anchor immediately after the 1912 pass. Lot 8 also continues the documented Gambrinus connection at Sixth & Main after the 1910 construction evidence. Lots 5–7 give specific owners that can now be followed through deeds, assessments, directories, advertisements, and later Sanborn mapping.

## 1913 research targets

1. Find direct address/business evidence for **501, 503, 505, 507, 509, and 511 Main** during 1913.
2. Search 1913 newspapers for **Hylton, Smith, Young, Brightbill, Harding, Jaggar/Jagger, Barlow, David, First National Bank, and Gambrinus** in connection with the Fifth–Sixth block.
3. Search for construction, demolition, fire, remodeling, excavation, permits, or property transfers on Main between Fifth and Sixth, per `RL-024`.
4. Determine whether the Block 26 lot owners can be tied to numbered Main Street premises without assuming the later 1925 mapping.
5. Record negative searches as part of the 1913 status rather than treating absence of hits as proof of absence.
