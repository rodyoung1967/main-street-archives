## Consolidated 1913 findings

### 1. Block 26 ownership — Morning Enterprise, 23 October 1913, p. 8

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-8/

A municipal assessment notice identifies owners of the four Main Street-side lots of **Block 26**:

- **Lot 5 — Mary E. Barlow and Juliette E. David — $10.71 assessment**
- **Lot 6 — First National Bank — $10.71 assessment**
- **Lot 7 — Frank Jaggar — $21.42 assessment**
- **Lot 8 — Gambrinus Brewing Company — $32.13 assessment**

The same notice describes the relevant assessment boundary along the line between lots 4 and 5 and north through Block 26 toward Sixth Street. Combined with the independently documented 1906/1910 Gambrinus anchor, **Lot 8 is the Sixth & Main end of the block**.

Interpretation limit: this establishes assessed ownership by legal lot, but does **not** prove a one-to-one relationship between lots 5–8 and the later street numbers 501/503/505/507. The 1925 Sanborn storefront widths make clear that multiple street-address spaces may lie within one platted lot.

### 2. Chicago Store / probable 505 Main fire — Oregon City Courier, 25 December 1913, p. 1

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-12-25/ed-1/seq-1/

The Courier reports a serious December fire in the **Chicago clothing store immediately south of the Post Office**. The interior was badly burned and merchandise substantially ruined. The article states that **George A. Harding owned the building**.

This is highly important to the 505 chronology because earlier direct evidence places **The Chicago Store at 505 Main by 31 March 1911**, and the 5 January 1912 address/business material describes the Chicago Store as being on Main just below the Post Office. The 1913 fire description matches that geography closely.

Working conclusion: **Chicago Store at 505 Main in December 1913 — high confidence, not yet exact-address direct proof from the fire article itself.**

Property implication: **George A. Harding very likely owned the building corresponding to the Chicago Store/505 premises in December 1913.** This is building-specific ownership evidence and must be reconciled with the October Block 26 assessed-owner list before equating Harding with fee ownership of an entire legal lot.

### 3. G. H. Young / 507 Main continuity

Direct evidence already places **G. H. Young at 507 Main on 5 January 1912** (`E-092` / `S-102`). A 1914 Courier feature again places **G. H. Young at 507 Main**:

https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-16/ed-1/seq-4/

No intervening move has been recovered. Therefore **G. H. Young at 507 during 1913 is a high-confidence continuity inference**, but should remain distinguished from a direct 1913 exact-address citation until one is recovered.

### 4. H. P. Brightbill / 509 Main continuity

Brightbill is directly documented at **509 Main by September 1910** and remains active in 1913. A 24 June 1913 Morning Enterprise advertisement identifies Brightbill and phone 74:

https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-06-24/ed-1/seq-4/

Later directory/business material continues **H. P. Brightbill, groceries, 509 Main, phone 74**. The chronology strongly supports Brightbill at 509 through 1913. Treat this as **very high-confidence continuity**, while continuing to seek a 1913 item that prints the street number itself.

### 5. George A. Harding / 511 Main / Willamette Building

A 3 June 1913 Morning Enterprise advertisement identifies:

**Geo. A. Harding — Willamette Building — Drugs and Medicines**

Canonical source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-06-03/ed-1/seq-4/

A February 1914 Courier profile explicitly gives **George A. Harding, 511 Main Street**:

https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-12/

Working interpretation: absent evidence of an intervening move, Harding's 1913 pharmacy in the **Willamette Building** likely corresponds to his 511 Main premises. This materially weakens the earlier working hypothesis that the Willamette Building should simply be equated with 507 Main. A larger building spanning multiple storefront numbers remains possible and should be tested before assigning a single-number identity to the building name.

### 6. 503 Main remains unresolved in 1913

The 1912 evidence establishes H. N./H. H. Smith pool-room/pool-hall activity at **503 Main**. The 1913 pass did not recover sufficiently strong direct evidence establishing who occupied 503 throughout 1913 or when Smith left. This remains a targeted follow-up rather than a reason to keep the routine 1913 year pass open.

### 7. Street and infrastructure activity in 1913

The 1913 newspaper pass recovered downtown street/infrastructure activity but no separate direct demolition/reconstruction event at 503/505/507 beyond the Chicago Store fire:

- March 1913: Portland Railway Light & Power work involved pavement/track work on Main near Fourth Street and a spur toward Water Street.
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-03-02/ed-1/seq-3/
- April 1913: city discussion of a public hitching rail on Fifth Street just off Main gives immediate south-end block context.
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-04-11/ed-1/seq-1/
- January 1913: continued Main Street paving/public-work context appears in the Courier.
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-01-10/ed-1/seq-1/

None of these should be attributed to a specific target building without additional mapping evidence.

## Major implications

1. **The December 1913 Chicago Store fire is the most important new 1913 event for 505 Main.**
2. **George A. Harding is now a likely early building owner for the Chicago Store/505 premises**, subject to reconciliation with legal-lot ownership.
3. The **Willamette Building mapping must be revised**: 1913 Harding advertising ties his pharmacy to the Willamette Building, while 1914 gives his address as 511 Main.
4. The **Block 26 lot-owner list provides a fixed legal-property framework**, with Lot 8 anchored at Sixth & Main by Gambrinus, but legal lots must not be equated one-for-one with storefront addresses.
5. No independent 1913 demolition/rebuild of the 503/505/507 row was established beyond the Chicago Store fire damage.
