## 505 Main — Chicago Store continuity

### Morning Enterprise — 26 January 1913 — page 2

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-01-26/ed-1/seq-2/`

A Chicago Store advertisement explicitly gives **505 Main Street, Oregon City**. This confirms that the Chicago Store remained at 505 Main into January 1913.

This is consistent with existing `S-032` / `E-026`, which cover Chicago Store advertisements from 1911-1913.

## Old Post Office — new block-level reconstruction

The strongest new 1913 finding is that the **old Post Office can now be constrained geographically to the Main Street block between Fifth and Sixth Streets**, even though its exact numbered address remains unproven.

### Oregon City Courier — 28 February 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-02-28/ed-1/seq-1/`

In an article titled **"A Secret Burial"**, the paper states that immediately **north of the postoffice building** was an **alley running through to Water Street**, with a gateway from the alley into a small square of ground **behind the postoffice building**.

This is a valuable physical-site description:

- Post Office fronted the commercial district west/east of Water Street rather than being described only by nearby businesses.
- An alley immediately north of it ran through toward Water Street.
- There was rear ground directly behind the Post Office.

The same issue reports **Ellery Baily**, owner of **a pool room on Main Street**, had contracted smallpox. No street number is supplied; this does **not** establish Baily at 503 Main.

### Oregon City Courier — 20 November 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-20/ed-1/seq-1/`

A farmers' meeting notice places **Willamette Hall just south of the post office**.

### Willamette Hall location — later explicit geographic description

A 19 November 1914 *Morning Enterprise* notice explicitly places **Willamette Hall on Main Street, between Fifth and Sixth Streets**.

Taken together:

1. In November 1913, Willamette Hall was **just south of the Post Office**.
2. Willamette Hall is independently located on **Main Street between Fifth and Sixth**.
3. Therefore the old Post Office is strongly constrained to the **Fifth-to-Sixth Main Street block area**.

This is substantially stronger than the earlier reconstruction based only on businesses described as near/opposite/next to the Post Office.

### Relationship to 505 / 511 / Tolpolar clues

This block-level reconstruction is consistent with the 1912 evidence:

- Chicago Store at **505 Main** described itself as **"just below Postoffice."**
- I. Tolpolar, later documented at **514 Main**, advertised **opposite the Post Office**.
- Harding is directly at **511 Main** in January 1912.
- M. E. Dunn was repeatedly described as **next door to the P.O.**

These clues keep **513 Main** a strong working candidate for the old Post Office, but the archive must continue to label **513 as a hypothesis**, not a proved address.

## Old Post Office lease — 13 June 1913

### Oregon City Courier — 13 June 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-06-13/ed-1/seq-1/`

A government Post Office inspector criticized the existing Oregon City quarters. The article states:

- the **current lease was for ten years**;
- it would **expire in December 1913**;
- the old quarters had been adequate a decade earlier but were now considered inadequate;
- Post Office receipts had increased from about **$7,000 to about $20,000** over that period;
- the inspector encouraged the city or a private party to provide new quarters under a new government lease.

The same issue describes **Hogg Bros.** as having opened a Main Street store **just below the postoffice**. This is useful relative-location evidence but does not establish a numbered 1913 address for Hogg Bros.

## The replacement Post Office — separate 8th/Main-area relocation

The replacement Post Office built in late 1913 was **not the same Fifth-to-Sixth block location**. Contemporary evidence places the replacement **opposite the courthouse**, in the Eighth/Main courthouse district.

### 1913 courthouse / Eighth Street context

1913 advertisements place **Dillman & Howland** at or associated with **Eighth and Main**, while describing their location as **opposite the Court House**. This establishes a strong contemporary relationship between the courthouse and the Eighth/Main district.

This does not by itself establish the replacement Post Office's numbered street address, but it distinguishes the new site from the old Fifth-to-Sixth block Post Office.

### Oregon City Courier — 23 October 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that a **new postoffice building** was to be erected **opposite the court house**.

### Oregon City Courier — 6 November 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-06/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that **private parties** had begun erecting a Post Office building under a **five-year rental contract with the federal government**. This is important: the replacement was privately built/leased quarters, not a federal building merely because Congressman Hawley was separately seeking a federal appropriation.

### Oregon City Courier — 20 November 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-20/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that **David Williams** bought the old house standing on the property where the new Post Office building was to be erected and was moving it to his Centre Street property between Eleventh and Twelfth Streets.

### Oregon City Courier — 18 December 1913 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-12-18/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that the **cement foundation walls for the new postoffice building were in** and wall construction was proceeding.

## Crucial terminus: the Post Office did not move until March 1914

### Oregon City Courier — 26 March 1914 — page 1

Canonical page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-03-26/ed-1/seq-1/`

Under the headline **"Postoffice Will Move Saturday"**, the paper states that the Post Office would move into its **new building opposite the courthouse on Saturday and Sunday** and that the building was complete with new equipment.

Because 26 March 1914 was a Thursday, the move was scheduled for the weekend of **28-29 March 1914**.

### Interpretive consequence

This resolves an important chronological ambiguity:

> **All 1913 business descriptions using "the postoffice" as a landmark refer to the OLD Fifth-to-Sixth Main Street Post Office, because the replacement opposite the courthouse did not enter service until 28-29 March 1914.**

The late-1913 articles concern planning/construction of the replacement, not a completed 1913 relocation.

## 1913 Post Office chronology

1. **28 Feb 1913:** old Post Office still operating; alley immediately north runs through to Water Street; rear ground documented.
2. **13 Jun 1913:** old ten-year lease due to expire in December; inspector pushes for better quarters.
3. **20 Nov 1913:** Willamette Hall described as just south of the old Post Office; combined with Willamette Hall's documented Main-between-5th-and-6th location, this strongly fixes the old P.O. block.
4. **23 Oct 1913:** replacement announced opposite courthouse.
5. **6 Nov 1913:** private parties begin replacement under government rental arrangement.
6. **20 Nov 1913:** old house removed from replacement site.
7. **18 Dec 1913:** foundation walls of replacement are in.
8. **28-29 Mar 1914:** Post Office moves into replacement building opposite courthouse.

## 503 Main — transition still unresolved in 1913

Current documentary boundary:

- **5 Jan 1912:** H. N. Smith directly advertised at 503 Main.
- **Mar 1912:** pool-hall-license notice at 503 recorded as H. H. Smith; identity relationship remains unresolved.
- **28 Feb 1913:** Ellery Baily is called owner of **a pool room on Main Street**, but no number is given.
- **12 Feb 1914:** L. Hylton directly documented at **503 Main** (`S-025`, `E-024`).

The Baily article is a useful lead because 503 had a pool-room context in 1912, but **similar business type is not proof of address continuity**. Until a numbered source is found, do not place Baily at 503.

Search variants worth retaining: **Ellery Baily**, **Ellerd Bailey**, **Bailey**.

## 507 / 509 / 511 Main — continuity brackets

The 5 January 1912 page directly documents:

- **507 — G. H. Young**
- **509 — H. P. Brightbill**
- **511 — George A. Harding**

No clean exact-address 1913 advertisement was recovered in this pass. However later evidence again places these businesses/people in this sequence, making continuity plausible.

Archive rule: record this as a **continuity bracket**, not direct 1913 occupancy, until a dated 1913 source is found.

## 527 Main — possible transition year

Current bracket:

- **5 Jan 1912:** Bailey & Price, Billiard and Pool Parlors, directly at **527 Main**.
- **1915:** Price Bros. is directly documented at **527 Main**.

The archive currently lacks a clean 1913 numbered-address source explaining when or how 527 transitioned. This deserves targeted research and should not be filled by assuming that Bailey & Price and Price Bros. were the same enterprise.

## Hogg Bros. — useful relocation lead

In June 1913 Hogg Bros. is described as **just below the postoffice** — necessarily the **old** Post Office given the March 1914 move date.

By April 1914, later business-list evidence places Hogg Bros. at **806 Main**. This may reflect a relocation toward the new Post Office/courthouse district, but the exact move date has not been established. Do not back-project 806 Main into June 1913.

## 1913-1914 Polk directory — high-value source lead

The **Oregon and Washington State Gazetteer and Business Directory, 1913-1914**, R. L. Polk & Co., is confirmed to exist and is catalogued online. FamilySearch links divide it into multiple parts, but the viewer requires sign-in/access that was not available in this research session.

This directory is probably the single best remaining source for resolving direct 1913-1914 address assignments at 503, 507, 509, 511, 513, and 527.

No address claim should be attributed to the Polk directory until the actual Oregon City pages are inspected.
