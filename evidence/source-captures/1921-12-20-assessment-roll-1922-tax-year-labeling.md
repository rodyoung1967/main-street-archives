# 20 December 1921 — assessment-roll / 1922 tax-year labeling caution

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED AT PAGE LEVEL; underlying assessment roll not yet retrieved**

Purpose: preserve a contemporary explanation of how Oregon City / Clackamas County assessment valuation was described in relation to **1922 taxes**, so the S. Wolf property search does not assume that a record labeled only by one year necessarily corresponds to ownership as of a single obvious calendar date.

## Source

**The Morning Oregonian, 20 December 1921, p. 6**

The original scanned newspaper page was opened and visually inspected on 24 August 2026.

The Oregon City dispatch reports the city taxable valuation in connection with the assessment roll and states that the valuation was the basis **upon which the 1922 taxes would be computed**.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED newspaper report**.

## What this establishes

The source is useful for record-retrieval terminology, not for ownership of the Fifth/Main parcels themselves. It demonstrates that contemporary reporting could describe an assessment valuation prepared/reported in late **1921** in direct relation to **1922 taxes**.

Therefore, when requesting records to identify the owner of the July 1922 fire-origin property, the archive should not assume that asking only for a generically labeled “1922 assessment roll” will necessarily capture every relevant ownership/index entry.

A safer archival request is to ask for:

1. the assessment/index material underlying **1922 taxes**, including any roll or valuation created/closed in 1921;
2. the surviving **1922–1925 Assessment & Tax Roll** series documented by the Oregon Secretary of State inventory;
3. the **Assessment and Tax Roll Index, 1901–1949**;
4. the 1920/1925 land-and-lot-book indexes for the target lots, where useful for continuity.

## Relationship to S. Wolf / fire-origin property

The 20 July 1922 *Banner-Courier* directly states that **S. Wolf of Portland owned the Main Street building in which the fire began**. The exact parcel/address remains unresolved.

The assessment/tax records remain one of the strongest primary-record routes to determine whether S. Wolf owned the frontage corresponding to modern **501, 503, 505**, or another nearby premises. This newspaper item does **not** identify S. Wolf, a parcel, a lot, or a street number; it only sharpens the year-labeling/retrieval strategy.

## Existing official holdings evidence

The Oregon Secretary of State Clackamas County Records Inventory documents surviving:

- **Assessment & Tax Roll, 1922–1925**;
- **Index [Assessment and Tax Roll], 1901–1949**;
- land/lot-book index material including 1920 and 1925.

The Oregon State Archives land-record guide separately confirms Clackamas County assessment and tax rolls and the 1901–1949 index.

These remain **RETRIEVAL TARGETS**; the specific target parcel entries have not yet been visually inspected.

## What is NOT established

Do not infer from this newspaper report that:

- S. Wolf owned 501, 503, or 505 Main;
- the 1921 valuation article itself is the official assessment roll;
- property ownership could not change between valuation dates and the July 1922 fire;
- every archive or county record uses identical year-labeling conventions.

Search by both owner (`S. Wolf` / full Wolf candidates only after evidence identifies them) and legal description, preserving the unresolved Block 26 vs. Block 4 plat-numbering issue.
