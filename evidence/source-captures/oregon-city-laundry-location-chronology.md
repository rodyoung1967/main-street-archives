# Oregon City Laundry — location chronology and Fifth Street photo implications

Status: **active reconstruction; 1916 replacement-site sequence now resolved**

Purpose: separate the several documented Oregon City Laundry locations and use them to interpret the project photographs around Fifth/Main/Water. Do not assume all laundry photographs show the same building.

## 1910–1912 — Third/Main location

Primary newspaper business listings place Oregon City Laundry near Third and Main before the later Fifth Street plant:

- **23 Sep 1910, Oregon City Courier** — Oregon City Laundry, O. L. Blakeslee, **corner Third and Main Streets**.
- **5 Jan 1912, Oregon City Courier** — Oregon City Laundry, Blakeslee & Son, **Main Street near 3rd**.

Sources:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3/ocr/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-01-05/ed-1/seq-6/

## 1916 — replacement-plant site change is documented

What previously looked like a conflict between a February Courier report and an April Oregonian report is now resolved by a second February primary source. The sources describe a **change of building site**, not competing descriptions of one fixed plan.

### 3 Feb 1916 — initial rear-Courier plan

The Courier says a new two-story brick/mill-construction laundry building would be erected on the **block bounded by Seventh, Main, Eighth and Railroad**, fronting on the alley beside city hall, with the rear adjoining the Courier property. New quarters were needed because Hawley Pulp & Paper had acquired the former laundry property.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-03/ed-1/seq-6/

### 17 Feb 1916 — Blakeslee trades that property for a Fifth Street site

The actual scanned page of the **17 Feb 1916 Oregon City Courier, p. 7** has been visually inspected. A local item states that **E. M. Blakeslee, of the Oregon City Laundry, had traded the property in the rear of the Courier office, on which he had been going to build, for a location on Fifth Street, and would rebuild the laundry there.**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-17/ed-1/seq-7.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

### 2 Apr 1916 — Fifth just off Main plant nearing completion

The Sunday Oregonian says the new Oregon City Laundry building was **“on Fifth street just off Main”** and was nearing completion.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83045782/1916-04-02/ed-1/seq-14.pdf

### Current assessment

The chronology is now coherent:

1. **3 Feb** — rear-Courier / Seventh–Eighth block site announced;
2. **17 Feb** — Blakeslee explicitly trades that intended building site for a **Fifth Street location**;
3. **2 Apr** — the replacement laundry on **Fifth just off Main** is nearing completion.

Therefore the archive should no longer characterize the February and April reports as an unresolved location conflict. They document a plan change.

The later exact-address evidence strongly suggests that this Fifth Street replacement became the plant later numbered **109 Fifth**, but the exact equivalence of the April 1916 footprint and the 1918 numbered premises remains a strong inference pending deed/permit/assessment evidence.

## 1918 — exact Fifth Street number

The 24 Oct 1918 Oregon City Courier business guide lists:

- **Oregon City Laundry — M. L. Blakeslee, Prop., 109 Fifth St.**
- **Scripture & May — 108 Fifth St., carriage and auto works**
- **F. L. Davis — 5th & Water, blacksmithing / horseshoeing**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-10-24/ed-1/seq-6/ocr/

This is the best exact-address anchor currently recovered for the pre-1922 laundry plant and proves Oregon City Laundry was at **109 Fifth by 1918**.

## 1920–1922 — May & Washburn / garage continuity

The archive now documents May & Washburn by **December 1920**, earlier than the previous April 1921 lower bound. A predecessor business, **Scripture & May**, is directly documented at **108 Fifth** in 1918; a 1917 legal notice names the partners as **S. F. Scripture and L. E. May**.

This creates a potentially important predecessor chain for the garage beside the laundry. It is still unresolved whether **L. E. May** is the same May in May & Washburn and whether May & Washburn inherited the 108 Fifth premises.

The partnership appears in county road-payment records by 29 Apr 1921, again in June 1922, and after the July fire in October 1922.

## 1921 — direct pre-fire Fifth-near-Main bridge

A **20 Aug 1921 Oregonian** classified advertisement from Oregon City Laundry itself gives the location as **“5th st., near Main.”**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83025138/1921-08-20/ed-1/seq-14/

This creates a strong continuity chain:

- Feb 1916 — Fifth Street replacement site acquired;
- Apr 1916 — Fifth just off Main plant nearing completion;
- 1918 — **109 Fifth St.**;
- Aug 1921 — **5th St., near Main**;

## July 1922 fire — pre-fire Fifth Street cluster destroyed

The **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** reports a major fire in the older Oregon City business district. The fire destroyed:

- the **Oregon City Laundry**, the largest loss;
- the **frame garage of May & Washburn** with two automobiles;
- the Fifth Street restaurant;
- Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop;
- the home/confectionery store of H. Ketchner;
- other adjoining structures.

The article states that the laundry had recently installed modern machinery and was operated by **Knoepel & Schofield**. Immediately after the fire, the laundry arranged to continue business through a **temporary office with the Pantorium at 519 Main Street**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1/

A 19 Jul 1922 Morning Register wire report similarly says the Oregon City Laundry plant, May & Washburn garage, Fifth Street restaurant, Miller shoe shop and other buildings were destroyed.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088115/1922-07-19/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

A separately visually reviewed **18 Jul 1922 Eugene Daily Guard** account adds that fire spread **from the Main Street ignition shop directly to Oregon City Laundry**, and says a Blakeslee couple had living quarters **over the laundry**. It also describes the ignition shop as having been used until only a few days earlier by a cleaning-and-dyeing establishment, creating an occupancy-description question alongside the local Banner-Courier's Alex Schieremann tailor-shop wording.

Detailed source capture:
`evidence/source-captures/1922-07-18-eugene-daily-guard-fire-account.md`

### Photo implication

May & Washburn's business name itself is not a July 1922 upper-bound clue because the partnership continued after the fire. The useful clue is the **specific pre-fire frame garage** that burned. If the project laundry photograph can be visually matched to that exact garage facade/building, it must predate the July 1922 destruction.

Because May & Washburn is now documented by Dec 1920, the strongest current business-name window for such a photograph is **Dec 1920 → July 1922**, pending an exact facade/address match.

## Late 1922 — business resumes quickly; production location unresolved

Although the July fire destroyed the plant, Oregon City Laundry was again appearing in county expense records by **September 1922** and **November 1922**, showing that the business resumed service quickly.

Sources:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-09-28/ed-1/seq-5/ocr/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-11-23/ed-1/seq-7/ocr/

Portland help-wanted advertising provides an additional operational clue: by **16 Sep 1922** Oregon City Laundry was seeking an experienced marker/sorter, and by **3 Nov 1922** it was seeking a washman. Those jobs strongly suggest that production activity—not merely a receiving office—had been restored somewhere by late 1922. The ads do not identify the plant address.

Therefore the temporary **519 Main** receiving-office arrangement should not be mistaken for proof that production remained there.

A separate **21 Sep 1922 Banner-Courier** advertisement shows **Gilbert Miller** had re-established his shoe machinery at **209 Fifth Street**, in the former Goldman second-hand store. This is a post-fire relocation and not the destroyed pre-fire shop.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-09-21/ed-1/seq-5/

## 1925 Sanborn — direct visual review and high-resolution frontage alignment

The City of Oregon City hosts the complete May 1925 Sanborn set. The 1925 master map and **Sheet 6** have been visually inspected directly, including a high-resolution crop/read of the target block.

Sources:
- Sanborn collection: https://www.orcity.org/1301/Sanborn-Maps---McLoughlin-Downtown
- 1925 master map: https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4861/1925--Master-Map-PDF
- **1925 Sheet 6:** https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4866/1925-6-PDF
- detailed project review: `evidence/source-captures/1925-sanborn-sheet-6-fifth-main-water-visual-review.md`

### What Sheet 6 shows

The block bounded by **Water Street, Main Street, Fifth Street and Sixth Street** contains a clearly labeled **OREGON CITY LAUNDRY** building/complex on the Fifth Street/interior side of the block.

The Main Street commercial storefronts occupy the eastern edge of the same block. The high-resolution read makes the relevant address sequence legible, including:

- **501 Main** — separate store occupancy;
- **503 Main** — `Rest.` / restaurant;
- **505 Main** — billiards/cigars.

The laundry is **not** the Fifth/Main corner storefront.

### 501 / 503 geometry resolved at the spatial level

Projecting the Main frontage westward across the Sanborn plan shows that the mapped Oregon City Laundry complex lies spatially **behind both 501 and 503 Main**, and continues behind additional Main frontage as well.

Therefore the map-geometry answer is:

> **By May 1925, the Oregon City Laundry complex occupied rear/interior space behind both the 501 and 503 Main frontage positions, extending farther north beyond them.**

This is a plan-view spatial conclusion. It does **not** prove legal parcel ownership, that storefront walls followed lot lines, or that the 1925 laundry had the identical footprint as the pre-fire 1916–1922 plant.

### Post-fire implication

The July 1922 fire destroyed the Oregon City Laundry plant, but the **May 1925 Sanborn physically maps Oregon City Laundry back in the Fifth/Main/Water block**.

Therefore it is established that by May 1925 Oregon City Laundry again had a physical production complex in the same broader Fifth Street block. What remains unresolved is whether the 1925 facility was:

1. rebuilt on the exact pre-fire 109 Fifth footprint;
2. a replacement on a shifted footprint within the same block;
3. an enlarged/reconfigured complex spanning different rear areas.

A later City/ODOT memorandum independently summarizes the 1925 map as showing Oregon City Laundry between Water and Main near Fifth:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/12817/Tech-Memo-6-Most-Promising-Alternatives

## Two-laundry-photo issue

The Time Capsule Maps source package contains two laundry photographs:

- `04-oregon-city-laundry-co-front.jpg`
- `05-oregon-city-laundry-main-street.jpg`

The source manifest grouped them together but did not prove they show the same building.

User visual identification provides a camera-direction anchor for the **first photo** (`04`): it faces **west toward West Linn**, based on recognition of a building across the river.

Current rule:

- treat the two photos as **separate identification problems**;
- do not assume either image is automatically the pre-fire 109 Fifth plant;
- use camera direction, curb/street geometry, river background, garage signage, and Sanborn footprints to identify street side and generation;
- preserve the possibility that the photos show different generations/locations of Oregon City Laundry;
- preserve the observation that another photograph appears to show a laundry on the opposite side of Fifth until the images are matched to mapped footprints.

3. Was the 1925 laundry rebuilt on the exact pre-fire 109 Fifth footprint or reconfigured elsewhere within the same block?
4. What exact Fifth Street number did May & Washburn occupy before the fire, and did it inherit the 108 Fifth Scripture & May premises?
5. What building/location does each project laundry photo show?
6. Which project laundry image corresponds to the 1916–1922 plant, and which (if either) corresponds to the 1925 rebuilt facility?
