## Starting evidence

Family history says Dick and Sheila first operated **Brass Rail** at 505 while someone else still owned the building, and later purchased the real estate from a woman remembered approximately as **Mrs. Nesmeth / Nemeth**. The exact spelling, full legal name, purchase date, and immediately prior ownership remain unproved.

The **6 April 1988** Oregon City Commission record is a firm business-role bookend: an OLCC change-of-ownership application transferred **The Dug Inn (formerly The Brass Rail), 505 Main Street** from Dick or Sheila Wiitanen to Stillman H. and M. Diane Cotterell. It does **not** say when the Wiitanens acquired the real estate and does not identify their real-estate grantor.

## 1. County parcel-first ownership route is stronger than surname guessing

The Oregon State Archives **Clackamas County Inventory** confirms that Clackamas County preserves multiple record series spanning the exact likely acquisition window:

- **Recording Instruments, 1967–2000**;
- **Recording Index, 1973–1999**;
- **Deed Index, 1924–1985**;
- **Land Ownership Books, ca. 1966–2000**, explicitly described as including **deed and chronological ownership information**;
- **Tax Lot Cards, 1930–2000**, described as tracking land ownership, lot size, property changes and land owner and as potentially serving as a deed reference.

The Land Ownership Books are held by **Clackamas County Assessment & Taxation, Suite 135**. The recording/deed indexes are held by the **Clackamas County Clerk/Recording Office**.

Current official County guidance also states that owner names are not published online and directs parcel-specific ownership questions to **Assessment & Taxation**, `propertytaxinfo@clackamas.us`, 503-655-8671.

### Electronic-index limit confirmed

A current generic GovOS county-record search help page describes a system capable of searching grantor, grantee, legal description and recorded-date fields. However, the **Clackamas County archival inventory controls the availability question for this project**: Clackamas Recording Instruments are **electronically indexed from 1989-present**, while the target-era indexes are retained as physical/microform records — including the **1973–1999 Recording Index** and **1924–1985 Deed Index** in the Recording Office public research area.

The current Clackamas Recording webpage says the office maintains a public viewing area and preserves records on microfilm/computer image, but it does not expose a Clackamas public online index covering the 1970s–1980s target interval.

Therefore the generic GovOS search-help discovery does **not** convert the 1973–1988 Wiitanen acquisition search into an online-complete task. The target remains a manual/restricted retrieval.

## 2. Missing 1983-era 505 historic inventory form could supply a dated owner snapshot

The current repository's cumulative-inventory review establishes that the downtown was originally surveyed in the **1982–1983 / 1983-era Oregon City cultural-resource work**, and that later inventory forms are cumulative rather than replacements for older forms. The 2000 Downtown Re-Survey generated individual property forms; copies were held by the City and originals by Oregon SHPO.

A separate original Oregon City **Oregon Inventory of Historic Properties** form from the same general survey tradition demonstrates that the form itself contains an explicit **`OWNER:`** field. The parsed 1985 form for the Jacob & Henrietta Jones Residence at 1101 14th Street prints an owner name and cites TICOR Title Company records and tax rolls among its sources. A screenshot request for that PDF failed with a cache miss in the current web environment, so the OWNER-field observation is classified **OCR/TEXT ONLY** until the scan is visually certified.

The Oregon Historic Sites Database record for the Petzold Building at 714 Main independently cites **“Oregon City Planning Dept. 1982-1983”** as a historical source, reinforcing that the early survey/research files contained property-history information.

## 3. Post-March-1983 Commission minutes are not an exhaustive renewal trail

The 6 April 1988 Commission record states that at a special meeting on **28 March 1983**, the City adopted Commission Policy 1-7, **“Approval of Liquor License Renewals.”** This means routine renewals after that policy change need not appear as individual Commission approval actions in the same way earlier renewals did.

Therefore, absence of 505 Tavern / Brass Rail from post-March-1983 Commission minutes is not evidence of closure, operator change, or ownership change. Post-1983 routine renewal evidence should be sought in the underlying Mayor/Police/Fire/OLCC administrative files, while Commission minutes remain useful for **new outlets and change-of-ownership actions** such as the 1988 transfer.

The 1988 page was text-extracted in the current web environment; do not upgrade this policy detail beyond the underlying source's existing repository visual-verification status without inspecting the scan.

## 6. Open-web surname searches did not identify the seller

Targeted searches for **Nesmeth / Nemeth / Nesmith** variants with 505 Main, Oregon City, Brass Rail, Wiitanen and tavern terms produced no reliable property-owner match. Random surname hits elsewhere in Oregon were rejected rather than merged by name alone.

Possible Oregon-area Nemeth individuals surfaced in cemetery, obituary and modern public-record indexes, but **none has a documented bridge to 505 Main**. They are not promoted as seller candidates merely because the surname resembles the family recollection.

Public modern property-history aggregators expose 505 transfer rows only much later and/or provide inconsistent derivative sale metadata, so they do not establish the target-era chain.

This no-hit is a **coverage statement only**, not evidence that the remembered surname is wrong.
