# 1914 Post-Fire Follow-up Pass

Status: **active retrospective review**

Purpose: re-review Oregon City newspapers after the December 1913 Chicago Store fire, specifically looking for repair, rebuilding, demolition, vacancy, leasing, insurance settlement, business relocation, or evidence that the 503/505 structures ceased to exist.

## Direct evidence recovered

### 503 Main was occupied and operating in April 1914

The *Oregon City Courier*, 23 April 1914, page 6, contains a Home Industry advertisement for **L. Hylton at 503 Main Street**, selling cigars, tobacco, and soft drinks.

Source page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-23/ed-1/seq-6/

Direct PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-23/ed-1/seq-6.pdf

This is important physical-existence evidence: whatever happened in the December 1913 fire, **503 Main was an operating commercial premises four months later**. It argues strongly against a fire having destroyed the entire 503/505 frontage.

### Earlier February 1914 503 anchor

The *Courier* industrial edition of 12 February 1914 directly profiles **L. Hylton, 503 Main Street**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-15/

This is only about seven weeks after the December 1913 Chicago Store fire and provides an especially strong test of the neighboring building/frontage condition.

## 505 / Chicago Store follow-up

Targeted searches for 1914 combinations of **Chicago Store**, **505 Main**, **George A. Harding**, fire, rebuilding, repair, demolition, razing, vacancy, insurance, lease, and relocation have not yet produced a direct post-fire 505 article in indexed results.

That negative result is **not** evidence that no article exists. Full issue/page review remains required.

## Building-existence assessment

Current evidence does **not** support the proposition that the December 1913 Chicago Store fire eliminated the whole 503/505 frontage.

Reasons:

1. The fire account described severe interior/stock damage at the Chicago clothing store south of the post office, not yet a proven total structural loss.
2. 503 Main is directly occupied by L. Hylton in February 1914 and again in April 1914.
3. The current 503 building is independently dated by later historic-resource surveys to approximately 1919, while 505 is approximately 1920. Those dates remain a separate later-rebuilding question and should not be retroactively assigned to the 1913 fire without evidence.

The correct working interpretation is therefore:

- **503 definitely existed as a commercial address/premises immediately after the 1913 fire.**
- **505's precise structural condition and occupant after the fire remain unresolved.**
- **No recovered 1914 evidence yet says that 505 was razed or that the entire 503/505 frontage disappeared.**
- The c.1919/c.1920 replacement-building dates remain a standing question requiring later-year newspaper/building evidence.

## Review requirement

This retrospective is not complete until every available page of each Oregon City newspaper title for 1914 has been reviewed, not merely OCR/index hits. Record inaccessible/missing pages separately.
