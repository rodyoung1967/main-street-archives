# 1925 Sanborn — Sheet 6 — Fifth / Main / Water visual review

Status: **visually reviewed from City of Oregon City scan; high-resolution frontage alignment completed**

Purpose: establish the physical relationship among the Oregon City Laundry, Fifth Street, Water Street, Main Street, and the Main Street storefront block that includes the later 501/503/505 sequence.

The master map was visually inspected to identify the correct downtown riverfront sheet. Sheet 6 was then visually inspected directly rather than relying only on later summaries. A subsequent high-resolution render/crop of the original Sheet 6 scan was used to read the Main Street address sequence and compare the storefront positions directly with the mapped laundry footprint.

## What Sheet 6 shows

Sheet 6 covers the riverfront/downtown blocks around **Water Street, Main Street, Fourth Street, Fifth Street, and Sixth Street**.

The block bounded by:

- **Water Street** on the west,
- **Main Street** on the east,
- **Fifth Street** on the south,
- **Sixth Street** on the north

contains a clearly labeled **OREGON CITY LAUNDRY** building/complex on the Fifth Street side of the block.

The Main Street commercial buildings occupy the eastern edge of this same block. This is the block containing the Main Street frontage later associated with the **501 / 503 / 505** address sequence.

## Direct corner/storefront observation

The visual map makes one relationship especially clear: **the Oregon City Laundry is not the corner building at Fifth & Main.** A separate strip of commercial storefronts/buildings lies between the laundry complex and Main Street, including the Fifth/Main corner frontage and the adjoining Main Street storefront sequence.

This strengthens the rear-of-block interpretation. The laundry occupies the Fifth Street/interior side of the block while separate commercial premises front Main Street.

## High-resolution Main-frontage alignment

A closer visual read of the original Sheet 6 scan resolves the earlier 501-versus-503 footprint question at the **spatial/building-footprint level**.

The Main Street address sequence is legible along the east edge of the block. Among the clearest labels are:

- **501 Main** — occupancy abbreviation `S.`;
- **503 Main** — `Rest.` (restaurant);
- **505 Main** — billiards/cigars occupancy;
- **509 Main** — `Gro.` (grocery);
- **511 Main** — `Drugs`.

When those frontage positions are projected westward across the same Sanborn block, the mapped **OREGON CITY LAUNDRY** complex occupies rear/interior space behind the frontage positions of **both 501 Main and 503 Main**. The laundry complex also continues farther north behind additional Main Street frontage; it is not confined to the rear of a single 501 or 503 storefront.

Therefore the correct answer to the earlier map-geometry question is:

> **On the May 1925 Sanborn, the Oregon City Laundry complex lies spatially behind both 501 and 503 Main, and extends behind additional Main Street frontage as well.**

This is a plan-view spatial conclusion from the mapped footprints. It does **not** prove that the laundry legally occupied the parcels later associated with both 501 and 503, that parcel boundaries followed the storefront walls, or that the May 1925 laundry had the identical footprint as the pre-fire July 1922 plant.

## Geometry implication for 501 / 503 / 505 Main

The direct map read supports the interpretation that the laundry was a **side/rear-of-block use behind multiple Main Street storefront positions**, rather than a Main Street storefront itself.

The user's earlier observation that a Fifth Street laundry could appear in a photograph taken at Fifth & Main while sitting behind the 501/503 frontage is therefore geographically consistent with the 1925 Sanborn, and the higher-resolution review now shows that **both 501 and 503 are in front of portions of the mapped laundry complex**.

This does not by itself determine which legal lot or ownership parcel contained each laundry wall. Modern parcel descriptions also show that the later 503/505/507 parcels do not map one-address-to-one-original-lot, so storefront position, Sanborn footprint, and legal parcel ownership must remain separate concepts.

## Important 1922-fire implication

The July 1922 Banner-Courier states that the Oregon City Laundry plant and neighboring May & Washburn frame garage were destroyed by fire.

Yet the **May 1925 Sanborn Sheet 6 visibly shows Oregon City Laundry again in the Fifth/Water/Main block**.

That establishes an important post-fire fact:

> **By May 1925, Oregon City Laundry had a physical laundry building/complex again in the Fifth Street block between Water and Main.**

What remains unresolved is whether this was:

1. a rebuild on or near the pre-fire 109 Fifth site;
2. a different structure elsewhere in the same block;
3. a new building that later evolved into the McLoughlin-era property.

The Sanborn visual proves the 1925 physical presence and its relationship behind both 501 and 503, but not continuity of the exact pre-1922 footprint.

## Relationship to later corridor memo

A later City/ODOT technical memorandum says the 1925 Sanborn lists Oregon City Laundry on the east side of Fifth between Water and Main. The direct Sheet 6 visual is now the preferred evidence for block geometry because it shows the actual building footprint and surrounding streets.
