## Visually verified 19 March 1912 findings

### Page 2 — exact 501 Main address

A liquor-license notice signed **Joe Wilson** identifies his place of business as **“The Palace Saloon,” 501 Main Street**, and requests a three-month license. This is direct, visually verified evidence that the Palace Saloon was at 501 Main in March 1912. It does **not** establish the occupant in July 1922 or identify the 1922 fire-origin room.

The same page carries separate license notices for:

- **Davis & Cameron**, 722 Main Street; and
- **Wm. Trudell**, corner of Sixth and Main.

The Trudell notice independently supports the already documented March 1912 Gambrinus/Trudell Sixth-and-Main evidence, but does not print a numbered storefront.

### Other Main Street context

- Page 1 reports a house formerly on Fifth Street being moved through Main Street to Eleventh and Main, temporarily obstructing traffic.
- Page 1 reports a recommendation for a Main Street library site occupied by John W. Loder; no target-block number is printed.
- Page 2 includes a classified location for George Young at “Main street, near Fifth.” This relative wording is not independently treated here as proof that the advertiser was the G. H. Young documented at 507 Main.
- Page 3 includes a notice for Nobel at 714 Main Street and a residential reference to 513 Fifth Street; neither is a 501/503/505 occupancy finding.

No visually verified reference to **503 Main** or **505 Main** was found during the four-page 1912 review. That is a review-coverage statement only, not proof of vacancy, non-use, or absence.

## Visually verified 3 October 1913 findings

### Page 2 — Hunsaker & Taylor at 501 Main

A liquor-license notice states that **Hunsaker & Taylor** would apply at the next regular City Council meeting for a license to sell liquor at **their place of business, 501 Main Street**, for three months.

This is direct, visually verified evidence for Hunsaker & Taylor's place of business at 501 Main in October 1913. Read with the March 1912 Joe Wilson notice, it establishes two separately named liquor-license applicants at the same exact address about eighteen months apart:

- 19 March 1912 — Joe Wilson, Palace Saloon, 501 Main;
- 3 October 1913 — Hunsaker & Taylor, place of business at 501 Main.

The notices do **not** prove that Hunsaker & Taylor bought, renamed, or continued the Palace Saloon business; do not merge the applicants, business names, licenses, or ownership histories without additional evidence. Neither notice identifies the July 1922 occupant or fire-origin room.

### Page 2 — surviving official street-numbering plat/plan lead

A City Recorder notice says that a **plat and plan showing the proper street number for all property in Oregon City** was on file in the recorder's office and that a proper number for all houses or buildings could be obtained by applying there. The notice then quotes Section 5 of **Ordinance No. 357**, requiring the owner or custodian of a building to place the assigned number on it and specifying a penalty for refusal or neglect.

This is visually verified evidence that the official numbering plat/plan documented under `E-061` was still an operational city record in October 1913. It materially strengthens the retrieval route under `RL-025`: request Ordinance No. 357 and the recorder's accompanying plat/plan. The notice does **not** itself display the plat, lot boundaries, or the assignment of 501/503/505 to original Block 26.

### Other visually verified Main Street evidence

- Page 2: **Claus Krohn** gives his liquor-license place of business as **421 Main Street** for three months.
- Page 2: **L. G. Ice, dentist**, advertises in the **Beaver Building**. The advertisement itself gives no street address.
- Page 4: the **Portland Railway, Light & Power Company / The Electric Store** tells patrons to pay electric bills at its office at **617 Main Street** and identifies that office as the **Beaver Building, Main Street**. This directly links the Electric Store and 617 Main; read with the separate page-2 advertisement, it strongly places L. G. Ice in the same Main Street building but does not prove that his individual office used the exact number 617.
- Page 4: a bankruptcy-sale advertisement says the Barde & Levitt chain failure included its Oregon City store, described as Oregon City's biggest clothing house. The advertisement gives no address and is not direct evidence that the store was at 501, 503, 505, or elsewhere on Main Street.

Pages 1 and 3 produced no visually verified target-address finding. Across the complete four-page issue, no visually verified reference to **503 Main** or **505 Main** was found. These are review-coverage statements only, not evidence of vacancy, non-use, or absence.

## High-reasoning conclusions

1. **Proven / visually verified:** Hunsaker & Taylor had a liquor-license place of business at 501 Main on 3 October 1913.
2. **Proven / visually verified:** Joe Wilson's Palace Saloon was at 501 Main on 19 March 1912.
3. **Strong but limited continuity observation:** 501 had separately documented liquor-license premises in both 1912 and 1913. This does not establish a business transfer, identical trade name, building ownership, or continuous operation between the notices.
4. **Visually verified archival lead:** Oregon City's numbering plat/plan was represented as on file with the city recorder in October 1913, and Ordinance No. 357 governed placement of assigned numbers.
5. **Unresolved:** neither issue identifies 503 or 505; neither issue settles the modern storefront containing the July 1922 fire-origin room.
