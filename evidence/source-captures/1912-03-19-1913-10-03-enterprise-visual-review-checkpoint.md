## Visually verified 19 March 1912 findings

### Page 2 — exact 501 Main address

A liquor-license notice signed **Joe Wilson** identifies his place of business as **“The Palace Saloon,” 501 Main Street**, and requests a three-month license. This is direct, visually verified evidence that the Palace Saloon was at 501 Main in March 1912. It does **not** establish the occupant in July 1922 or identify the 1922 fire-origin room.

The same page carries separate license notices for:

- **Davis & Cameron**, 722 Main Street; and
- **Wm. Trudell**, corner of Sixth and Main.

The Trudell notice independently supports the already documented March 1912 Gambrinus/Trudell Sixth-and-Main evidence, but does not print a numbered storefront.

### Other Main Street context

- Page 1 reports a house formerly on Fifth Street being moved through Main Street to Eleventh and Main, temporarily obstructing traffic.
- Page 1 reports a recommendation for a Main Street library site occupied by John W. Loder; no target-block number is printed.
- Page 2 includes a classified location for George Young at “Main street, near Fifth.” This relative wording is not independently treated here as proof that the advertiser was the G. H. Young documented at 507 Main.
- Page 3 includes a notice for Nobel at 714 Main Street and a residential reference to 513 Fifth Street; neither is a 501/503/505 occupancy finding.

No visually verified reference to **503 Main** or **505 Main** was found during the four-page 1912 review. That is a review-coverage statement only, not proof of vacancy, non-use, or absence.
