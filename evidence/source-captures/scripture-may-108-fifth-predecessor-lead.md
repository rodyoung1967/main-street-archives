# Scripture & May at 108 Fifth — predecessor lead for May & Washburn

Status: **verified address anchor; partner-transition hypothesis unresolved**

Purpose: preserve the exact-address and partner-name evidence for the earlier Scripture & May blacksmith/carriage business and test whether its `May` partner or premises continued into the later May & Washburn garage destroyed in the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire.

## 5 October 1916 — 108 Fifth visually verified

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Courier, 5 Oct 1916, p. 6** was visually inspected.

A Scripture & May advertisement at the bottom of the page gives the business address as:

- **SCRIPTURE & MAY**
- **108 Fifth St.**

The advertisement promotes sharpening/repair work and is consistent with the firm's blacksmithing and carriage-related trade.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-10-05/ed-1/seq-6.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## 1917 — partners named S. F. Scripture and L. E. May

The **19 Apr 1917 Oregon City Courier** reports a lawsuit by:

- **S. F. Scripture**
- **L. E. May**

`doing business as Scripture & May`, seeking payment for labor and materials.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1917-04-19/ed-1/seq-5/

This reading is presently based on the archive's text/OCR rendering and is therefore **OCR/TEXT ONLY pending direct visual confirmation of that specific item**. The exact partner initials are nevertheless a strong discovery lead.

Oregon City's historic-resource page independently identifies **S. F. Scripture as Silas F. Scripture** and describes him as a principal in Scripture and May during the teens:
https://www.orcity.org/2014/1112-Washington-Street

The full identity of **L. E. May** remains to be established from a directory, vital record, obituary, or another primary business source.

## 1918 — 108 Fifth and 109 Fifth side-by-side address anchors

The archive's visually reviewed 24 Oct 1918 Courier business guide lists:

- **Scripture & May — 108 Fifth St.**
- **Oregon City Laundry — 109 Fifth St.**

See:
`evidence/source-captures/oregon-city-laundry-location-chronology.md`

This creates a very strong spatial anchor for the pre-fire Fifth Street cluster. The two businesses had consecutive street numbers by 1918.

Do not infer from consecutive numbering alone whether they shared a wall, faced one another, or occupied the same side of Fifth until the historical numbering convention/footprints are independently established.

## December 1920 — May & Washburn already exists

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Enterprise, 17 Dec 1920, p. 7** was visually inspected. In the county road-payment listings, **May & Washburn** appears as a payee.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1920-12-17/ed-1/seq-7.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

This moves the proven lower bound for the May & Washburn partnership back from **29 Apr 1921 to 17 Dec 1920**.

The same 1920 county page also lists **S. F. Scripture** separately in county expenditures. That is consistent with Scripture and the newer May & Washburn concern existing as separate payees by late 1920, but it does not prove a formal dissolution sequence or identify the `May` partner.

## Transition hypothesis

A plausible business transition is now visible:

1. **1916–1918:** Scripture & May operates at **108 Fifth St.**;
2. the partners are reported as **S. F. Scripture and L. E. May**;
3. **by Dec 1920:** May & Washburn exists, while S. F. Scripture appears separately in county accounts;
4. **Jul 1922:** the frame garage of May & Washburn is destroyed in the Fifth/Main fire alongside Oregon City Laundry.

The most obvious hypothesis is that **L. E. May may be the `May` in May & Washburn**, possibly with the later partnership inheriting or continuing some of Scripture & May's Fifth Street trade or premises.

That is **not yet proven**. No recovered source currently states:

- that L. E. May formed May & Washburn;
- the full name of the May & Washburn partners;
- that May & Washburn occupied 108 Fifth;
- that Scripture & May transferred the 108 Fifth shop to May & Washburn.

## Why this matters for the 1922 fire cluster

If the identity and premises continuity can be proven, **108 Fifth** could become the first exact address for another business in the destroyed July 1922 cluster besides Oregon City Laundry at 109 Fifth.

That would substantially improve reconstruction of the relationship among:

- Oregon City Laundry;
- May & Washburn garage;
- Fifth Street Restaurant;
- Gilbert Miller shoe repair;
- H. Ketchner confectionery/home;
- the Main Street Schieremann/S. Wolf ignition premises.
