# Scripture & May at 108 Fifth — predecessor lead for May & Washburn

Status: **108 Fifth chronology now extends through June 1922; May & Washburn premises-continuity hypothesis substantially weakened**

Purpose: preserve the exact-address and partner-name evidence for the earlier Scripture & May blacksmith/carriage business and test whether its `May` partner or premises continued into the later May & Washburn garage destroyed in the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire.

## 1914–1915 Book 3 — probable 110 Fifth predecessor clue

The official Oregon City **1914–1915 Water Rent Record Book 3** target/Fifth-Main audit (`S-131` / `E-117`) directly records **110 Fifth, Block 3, classification Stable**. The tenant field is layered/crossed; visually it appears consistent with **Scripture & May**, but not clearly enough for a final transcription.

This is therefore a **probable predecessor-location lead only**. It does not establish a precise date within the 1914–15 book span and does not prove a move from 110 to the independently verified **108 Fifth** address in October 1916. It also does not prove that 110 Fifth was the newspaper's unnumbered **Fashion Stable**. Those bridges require independent evidence.

The same municipal audit separately records **107 Fifth, Block 4, Blacksmith**, with tenant handwriting unresolved. Trade similarity alone is not an identity bridge.

## 5 October 1916 — 108 Fifth visually verified

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Courier, 5 Oct 1916, p. 6** was visually inspected.

A Scripture & May advertisement gives:

- **SCRIPTURE & MAY**
- **108 Fifth St.**

## 1917 — partners named S. F. Scripture and L. E. May

The **19 Apr 1917 Oregon City Courier** reports a lawsuit by **S. F. Scripture** and **L. E. May**, `doing business as Scripture & May`, seeking payment for labor and materials.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1917-04-19/ed-1/seq-5/

This reading remains **OCR/TEXT ONLY pending direct visual confirmation** of that specific item.

Oregon City's historic-resource page independently identifies **S. F. Scripture as Silas F. Scripture** and describes him as a principal in Scripture and May during the teens:
https://www.orcity.org/2014/1112-Washington-Street

The full identity of **L. E. May** remains unresolved.

## 1918 — 108 Fifth and 109 Fifth are consecutive address anchors

The archive's visually reviewed 24 Oct 1918 Courier business guide lists:

- **Scripture & May — 108 Fifth St.**
- **Oregon City Laundry — 109 Fifth St.**

See:
`evidence/source-captures/oregon-city-laundry-location-chronology.md`

These are consecutive street numbers, but **consecutive numbering is not proof of physical adjacency**. Do not say the two businesses were “side by side” or shared a wall without mapping the historic odd/even numbering and footprints.

## 8 June 1922 — decisive new Scripture address anchor

The actual scanned page of the **Banner-Courier, 8 Jun 1922, p. 7** was visually inspected during a page-by-page review of the Buyers' Carnival issue.

A business card clearly advertises:

- **S. F. Scripture**
- **GENERAL BLACKSMITHING AND HORSESHOEING**
- automobile and truck springs repaired
- **108 - 5th St. between Main and Water St.**

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-06-08/ed-1/seq-7.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

This is only about six weeks before the 18 July 1922 fire.

## Consequence for the May & Washburn hypothesis

The old working possibility that **May & Washburn had simply inherited Scripture & May's 108 Fifth premises** is now **substantially weakened**.

S. F. Scripture himself was still operating/advertising a blacksmithing shop at **108 Fifth** on 8 June 1922, while May & Washburn was already an independently documented partnership and its separate frame garage was destroyed in the July fire.

Unless evidence shows an unusual shared-premises, sublease, or other arrangement, May & Washburn should **not** be assigned to 108 Fifth.

This finding does **not** answer whether **L. E. May** later became the May in May & Washburn; a personnel/business relationship could still exist even if the premises did not pass to May & Washburn.

## Address-geometry caution

The June 1922 Scripture ad adds useful wording: **108 Fifth was between Main and Water**. Oregon City Laundry is independently documented at **109 Fifth** in 1918.

However, do not infer from 108/109 alone that Scripture and the laundry were neighboring buildings. Historic street-side odd/even assignment and Sanborn placement must be checked explicitly.

## Why this matters for the fire reconstruction

The correction removes a potentially misleading assumption from the Fifth Street sequence:

- **108 Fifth:** S. F. Scripture still directly documented there on 8 Jun 1922;
- **109 Fifth:** Oregon City Laundry documented there in 1918 and at Fifth near Main in 1921;
- **May & Washburn:** separate frame garage destroyed July 1922, exact number still unknown.

The search for May & Washburn's exact premises therefore remains open and should not use 108 Fifth as a default.

## Confidence

- Scripture & May at 108 Fifth in Oct 1916: **Very High / visually verified**
- Scripture & May at 108 Fifth in 1918: **Very High**
- S. F. Scripture individually at 108 Fifth on 8 Jun 1922: **Very High / visually verified**
- May & Washburn operating by Dec 1920: **Very High / visually verified**
- L. E. May = May partner in May & Washburn: **Plausible / unproven**
- May & Washburn inherited 108 Fifth: **Now unlikely absent evidence of sharing/transfer after 8 Jun 1922**
- 108 and 109 Fifth physically adjacent: **Unproven; do not infer from numbering alone**
