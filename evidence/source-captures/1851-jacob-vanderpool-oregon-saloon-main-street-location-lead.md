# 1851 Jacob Vanderpool — Oregon Saloon and Boarding House — Main Street location lead

Status: **primary business/location wording established; exact later 503 Main mapping unproven**

Purpose: preserve the newly surfaced claim connecting Jacob Vanderpool's 1851 Oregon City business to later 503 Main while distinguishing primary evidence from later site interpretation.

## Primary / near-primary location wording

Jacob Vanderpool operated the **Oregon Saloon and Boarding House** in Oregon City in 1851.

A reproduced 1851 advertisement gives the business location as:

> **Main Street, opposite the Statesman Office.**

The ad offers meals at regular hours and invites persons from the country to call; it names **Jacob Vanderpool** as proprietor.

A 2024 Clackamas Workforce Partnership justice-history report reproduces the advertisement image and identifies it as an Oregon Statesman advertisement.

Source:
https://www.clackamasworkforce.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2024-Justice-Report-WEB.pdf

Additional historical sources independently confirm Vanderpool operated a saloon/boarding house in Oregon City and was expelled from Oregon under the exclusion law in 1851:

- Clackamas County Historical Society:
  https://clackamashistory.org/from-the-pages-of-history-cchs-blog/2021/3/27/black-americans-in-early-oregon-city-1840-1900
- Oregon Humanities:
  https://oregonhumanities.org/rll/magazine/skin-summer-2013/dangerous-subjects/
- Oregon Remembrance Project:
  https://oregonremembrance.org/vanderpool-project/jacobs-story/

## Modern 503 Main claim

A recent Oregon State Historic Preservation / NPS-related multiple-property historical document indexed by web search states that Vanderpool's Oregon Saloon and Boarding House was at **503 Main Street, Oregon City**.

This is potentially significant, but the recovered 1851 advertisement itself **does not use the number 503**.

Because Oregon City's standardized modern street-numbering system was adopted much later, the `503 Main` wording should presently be understood as a **later retrospective site identification**, not a contemporary 1851 street address.

## Current archive rule

Do **not** state yet:

- “503 Main was occupied by Jacob Vanderpool in 1851” as established fact;
- that the surviving 503 building dates to 1851;
- that the modern 503 parcel is proven to be the exact footprint of Vanderpool's business.

Current defensible statement:

> **Jacob Vanderpool operated the Oregon Saloon and Boarding House on Main Street in Oregon City in 1851, opposite the Oregon Statesman office. A recent historic-resource document retrospectively identifies the location as 503 Main, but that exact modern-site mapping still requires independent verification.**

## Why the claim needs testing

To map the 1851 business onto the later numbered frontage, we need to establish:

1. the exact **1851 Oregon Statesman office location**;
2. which side of Main Street the office occupied;
3. the opposite historic lot/building footprint;
4. correspondence between the 1851 lot and later original Block 26 / numbered 501–505 frontage.

## Related 1851 geography lead

A later historical account says the **1850–51 territorial capitol** was on the **southeast corner of Sixth and Main Streets** in Oregon City.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn85042470/1936-04-26/ed-1/seq-7.pdf

This is useful period context but does **not** locate the Statesman office by itself.

## Significance if the 503 mapping is verified

If the Statesman-office/lot reconstruction confirms the later 503 Main site, this would establish an extremely early **site-use history** for the 503 frontage, about seven decades before the surviving building's city-survey construction estimate of c.1919.

It would still remain essential to distinguish:

- **site/address history** from
- **surviving building history**.

The archive already follows this principle for 1908/1912/1914 numbered occupants who predate the city's c.1919 estimate for the current 503 building.
