## Portland House and the public market

[August 13 p1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-08-13/ed-1/seq-1.pdf) describes **Mrs. M. L. Foster** as owner of property on the **southeast corner of Fifth and Main** and names her property as the **Portland House**. She complained that the underused public-market sheds detracted from it. The Board of Trade's J. J. Tobin described the agreement with her when the market was built as verbal. The report discusses removal or relocation. [Page 5](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-08-13/ed-1/seq-5.pdf) independently gives Fifth and Main for the market in a fruit-sale notice. Neither item states a shed footprint or records a conveyance.

The [November 1911 Sanborn, sheet 16](../../maps/sanborn-map-register.md) labels the same corner **Portland Ho.**, shows boarding use, prints **426** on Main and shows **204** on the Fifth Street side of the mapped premises. The 1915 southeast-corner wording matches that mapped site at **original Block 27** across the intersection from the odd-numbered Fifth–Sixth frontage. Classification is **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT** for the 1911 map and the 1915 reported property location. The link from the 1915 business to the 1911 mapped number is a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**. The newspaper prints no number, deed or proof of physical continuity. The [July 1916 report](1916-enterprise-july21-visual-review-2026-09-12.md) names Minnie L. Foster and expressly says the Portland House had recently been rebuilt. The [1919 report](1919-december-enterprise-visual-review-2026-09-08.md) calls Mr. and Mrs. Beck owners at the intersection without stating when or how an interest changed. The 1896 photographic sign has not been tied conclusively to this mapped building.

## Price Bros. at Sixth and Main

[August 6 p3](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-08-06/ed-1/seq-3.pdf) independently reports Friday night's electric-iron incident at the **Price Brothers store, Sixth and Main**, identifies employee **Arthur Soesbe** as tailor and presser and says the iron was burning into a tabletop. It reports no damage to the building or stock. The [August 5 Courier evidence](1915-courier-sheets-073-100.md) separately describes rear-store boards burning, names proprietor Al Price and advertises Price Bros. at 527 Main. Both Friday-night accounts support **July 30** as the calendar-derived incident date. Keep the different burned-surface descriptions. The Enterprise prints the intersection only. The number comes from the Courier directory and the combined identification is a strong inference for the incident. The reports do not identify the current 1935 building as the pre-1935 premises or connect Price Bros. to Bailey & Price.

## Main Street paving

- [July 16 p5](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-07-16/ed-1/seq-5.pdf) reports that the council approved Worswick's bonds, with a ten-year personal bond, a five-year surety bond and a 15 percent withholding for ten years. Railway track work was already in progress.
- [July 23 p3](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-07-23/ed-1/seq-3.pdf) projects a July 24 paving start and describes selection of a plant site by the river near Eighth. [July 30 p5](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-07-30/ed-1/seq-5.pdf) reports a shipment delay and city consent for two shifts.
- [August 6 p5](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-08-06/ed-1/seq-5.pdf) reports arrival of the equipment, a plan to assemble the mixing plant behind the Frank Busch building, and a hoped-for finish around September 1. [August 13 p3](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-08-13/ed-1/seq-3.pdf) places the plant at the foot of Twelfth and reports actual lifting and hauling of old Main Street bricks for its setup. It says the streetcar track work had finished and the paving company's installation was still being prepared. These are distinct stages, not contradictory completion dates.
- The August 27 p4 editorial compares the service and costs of the old brick surface with the new material. The public-works items do not establish a storefront construction or demolition event.
