## Task

Task 3 asked for a visual inspection of **Oregon City Ordinance No. 357**, with particular attention to any provisions that could explain the official street-numbering system and map the Main Street frontage between Fifth and Sixth Streets.

## Result

Repeated targeted online searches of Oregon City's indexed municipal-record environment, general web indexes, historic newspaper indexes, and later-code routes did not expose an image or official reproduction of Ordinance No. 357 itself. ## What has been visually inspected

### 3 October 1913 City Recorder notice — Section 5 excerpt

The complete four-page 3 October 1913 *Morning Enterprise* issue was previously preserved and visually inspected at original rendered resolution, with enlarged review of the small City Recorder notice (`S-119`; `E-105`; `evidence/source-captures/1912-03-19-1913-10-03-enterprise-visual-review-checkpoint.md`).

That notice directly establishes that:

1. a **plat and plan showing the proper street number for all property in Oregon City** was on file in the City Recorder's office;
2. a person could apply there to obtain the proper number for a house or building;
3. the notice explicitly identifies **Section 5 of Ordinance No. 357**;
4. Section 5 required the **owner or custodian** of a house/building to place the assigned number on it;
5. refusal or neglect was subject to a penalty.

This is direct, visually verified evidence for the function of Section 5 and for the continued municipal use of the numbering plat/plan in 1913. The newspaper notice is an excerpt/republication, not the original ordinance document.

## What the surviving excerpt does NOT establish

The visually verified Section 5 notice does **not** provide or prove:

- the ordinance's full title;
- the complete enactment, passage, approval, or effective dates;
- the full text of Sections 1–4 or any section after Section 5;
- the mathematical or geometric rule used to assign street numbers;
- whether Oregon City used 100 numbers per block;
- which side of Main Street was odd or even as a matter of Ordinance 357 text;
- where a block's numbering sequence began;
- frontage increments or interpolation rules;
- corner-building treatment;
- treatment of multi-storefront buildings;
- whether half-addresses such as 505½ were specified by the ordinance;
- any original-lot or block number;
- any direct mapping of **501, 503, 505, or 507 Main** to original Block 26 lots/buildings;
- any connection between original Block 26 and later/current `(2) OREGON CITY, Block 4`.

None of those rules may be inferred from numbering practices in another Oregon city.

## 1908 false lead resolved

A 13 February 1908 *Morning Oregonian* house-numbering article discovered during this task described **100 numbers to each block, even numbers on one side and odd numbers on the other**. Direct page inspection shows the headline **"McMinnville to Number Houses"** and dateline **"M'MINNVILLE, Or., Feb. 12."**

The article therefore concerns **McMinnville**, not Oregon City. It is excluded from Oregon City's numbering evidence and cannot be used to fill missing provisions of Ordinance No. 357. The adjacent following newspaper item concerns a Clackamas teachers' examination and has an Oregon City dateline, which explains how snippet-level discovery could misleadingly associate the page with Oregon City.

## Relationship to the 1906 numbering program

Primary 1906 newspaper evidence (`E-061` / `S-075`) independently establishes:

- the City's formal numbering effort for free mail delivery;
- mayoral approval of a street-numbering ordinance by 6 July 1906;
- filing/completion of a plat designating correct numbering with Recorder W. A. Dimick;
- public instructions to consult the plat for the true number of a home or place of business;
- subsequent implementation work and street signs.

A 24 August 1906 *Courier* report separately says Council passed measures providing for street naming and house numbering. Because the original ordinance register/minutes have not been inspected, the precise procedural relationship among the July and August reports and **Ordinance No. 357** remains unresolved.

The strongest safe classification is therefore:

- **Proven:** a formal Oregon City numbering ordinance/plat program operated in 1906.
- **Proven:** by 3 October 1913, Section 5 of **Ordinance No. 357** governed placement of assigned numbers and the recorder-held plat/plan was the reference for proper street numbers.
- **Strong working hypothesis, not direct proof:** Ordinance No. 357 is the same specific numbering ordinance described in the July/August 1906 implementation reports.
- **Retrieval gap:** original Ordinance No. 357 title/header, complete text, enactment chronology, signatures, and attachments.

## 1963 code lead

An official Oregon City 1963 Municipal Code index includes a chapter titled **"Numbering of Buildings."** The chapter may preserve later codified descendants of the earlier numbering regime, but the full chapter could not be visually inspected through the current web interface and no legislative-history note directly tying it to Ordinance No. 357 was verified.

Do not use the later-code chapter as a substitute for the original ordinance unless the actual pages and source/legislative notes are inspected.
