Date: 14 September 2026.

## Purpose

Record the integrated conclusions reached from the current conversation and identify the newer records that supersede older 1922 model-test wording where the evidence state has materially changed.

## Newly integrated controlling facts

1. **Banner-Courier, 13 July 1922, p.1 — Property Changes Hands — VISUALLY VERIFIED.** George A. Harding's Main Street property group is reported as including the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller's shoe store, the adjoining barber shop and the Red Front Clothing Store. The property was sold to **S. Wolf and C. Gates, both of Portland**; Gates took the Fifth Street Restaurant location and Wolf took **the remainder**. Complete page already preserved as `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07/1922-07-13-seq-01.pdf` (`E-096` / `S-111`).
2. **Banner-Courier, 20 July 1922, p.1 — VISUALLY VERIFIED.** Fire began in a vacant room formerly occupied by Alex Schiermann as a tailor; Gilbert Miller's shoe business was destroyed; the adjoining pool hall suffered water damage and partial roof burning rather than being razed (`E-103` / `S-117`).
3. **Oregon City Enterprise, 21 July 1922, p.1 — VISUALLY VERIFIED.** Fire origin described as a **vacant half** formerly occupied by Schiermann; same structure described as **the shoe-store building in which the fire started**. The building immediately north of the fire was saved by **fireproof construction**. **L. Wolf** is identified as owner of the Red Front clothing store and as recent purchaser of the shoe-store building.
4. **Aurora Observer, 22 September 1921, p.3 — STEWARD VISUALLY VERIFIED.** Advertisement directly prints **Red Front Clothing Store, 507 Main Street, Oregon City**. Archive-side independent page preservation/review remains desirable, but the steward's reading is treated as direct address evidence.
5. **Wolf & Miller, 1918.** Direct 505 Main advertising shows men's clothing, shoes and shoe repairing. October 1918 sale wording says one member would go to Idaho and the other would restock and continue. This materially supports, but does not prove, a later Wolf/Miller business split.
6. **Gilbert Miller post-fire.** Steward-supplied 24 August 1922 Banner-Courier p.2 ad directly prints **419 Main Street** as the new location of Miller's Shoe Store. Prior unsupported 209 Fifth placement is superseded unless separately proved by a visually verified primary source.

## Current address conclusions

### 503 Main

**STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE:** the July 1922 fire-origin structure at 503 contained Gilbert Miller's shoe-store premises and a vacant former Alex Schiermann tailor portion/half. The Enterprise wording explains how the building could simultaneously contain an active shoe business and a vacant former-tailor half. A pre-fire source literally printing Miller or Schiermann at 503 has not yet been recovered.

### 505 Main

**STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE for numbered match; direct for survival/damage description:** the adjoining pool/billiard premises was at 505 Main. It was **not razed**; billiard tables were water damaged, part of the roof burned, and the Enterprise credits fireproof construction with saving the building immediately north of the fire.

Do not describe the building as wholly unburned; some roof burning is directly reported.

### 507 Main / Red Front

**DOCUMENTED / DIRECT for 22 September 1921:** Red Front Clothing Store at **507 Main**.

**STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE for July 1922 continuity:** Red Front likely remained at 507 at the time of the fire because the 13 July sale article still names the business in Harding's property group and the fire reports continue to name Red Front days later, with no recovered move notice. A July 1922 exact-address occurrence remains desirable corroboration.

### 501 Main / Fifth Street Restaurant

The Fifth Street Restaurant location is directly allocated to C. Gates in the 13 July sale report and the restaurant is directly reported as destroyed in the fire. Exact pre-fire footprint-to-modern-501 mapping remains source-sensitive; business ownership and building/property ownership must remain separated.

## Wolf identity state

- 13 July property sale: **S. Wolf**.
- 20 July fire report: **S. Wolf of Portland** as origin-building owner.
- 21 July Enterprise: **L. Wolf** as Red Front owner and recent purchaser of the shoe-store building.
- 1918 Wolf & Miller: Wolf partner not yet identified by full name.

**PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** L. Wolf may be the Wolf of Wolf & Miller, and the later Miller shoe / Wolf clothing businesses may reflect the post-partnership division of the earlier firm's business lines. The 1918 split notice materially supports the hypothesis. Do not upgrade until the Wolf partner is independently identified or an explicit dissolution/business-division source is found. Preserve the S./L. initial conflict.
