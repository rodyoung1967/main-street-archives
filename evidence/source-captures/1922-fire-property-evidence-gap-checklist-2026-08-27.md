## Established starting point

- The target odd-numbered Main Street frontage is original **Block 4**, not Block 26 (`E-094`, `E-108`).
- The 13 July 1922 *Banner-Courier* sale report is visually verified: C. Gates took the Fifth Street Restaurant location and S. Wolf took the remainder of George A. Harding's named property group (`E-096` / `S-111`).
- The 20 July local fire report is visually verified: the fire began in a vacant Main Street room formerly used by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop; S. Wolf of Portland owned its building; and a pool hall adjoined it (`E-103` / `S-117`).
- The 17–18 August rebuild reports are visually verified: C. E. Gates's Fifth/Main replacement was two-story hollow tile with two ground-floor storerooms (`E-097` / `S-112`–`S-113`).

Two models remain live: **A**, Gates principally at 501 with Wolf beginning at 503; and **B**, Gates's two Main-facing rooms at 501+503 with Wolf beginning near 505. Neither is established; 501 remains a live alternative. The 503-ignition / 505-adjoining-pool-hall model is a hypothesis only.

## 1. Recorded Harding → Wolf / Gates deeds — highest property priority

- **Question:** What did Harding transfer, to whom, on which effective/recording dates, and with what legal description, dimensions, and ownership interest?
- **Prior work:** The newspaper report was visually inspected, but prints no deed reference, consideration, legal description, dimensions, or street number. No deed image is in the archive.
- **Next source:** Clackamas County grantor/grantee indexes and recording books. Search George A. Harding, S. Wolf, C. Gates/C. E. Gates, and spelling variants for June–August 1922; retrieve all candidate instruments, including simultaneous or sequential deeds.
- **Completion test:** Inspect the recorded image or faithful official reproduction, transcribe legal descriptions, and compare with `E-094`/`E-108`. A no-hit result must specify indexes and dates inspected.

## 2. OHS MSS 1503, Series B, Box 19 abstract — highest title-chain priority

- **Question:** Does the Main/Fifth abstract trace Block 4 Lots 3–4 or 3–5, Harding, Wolf, Gates, later parcel splits, or relevant maps/sketches?

- **Next source:** Oregon Historical Society Research Library: request the complete item, folder label, cover, maps, sketches, continuations, and attachments; ask OHS to explain the internal lot-field conflict.
- **Status:** **MANUAL CALLOUT.** The index proves no abstract content.
- **Completion test:** Visually inspect every supplied page and record ownership names, legal descriptions, recording references, maps, and negative results with page references.

## 5. Poolroom licenses and typewritten council minutes

Timms, Leland & Little, and Wheeler remain distinct possibilities; the November McAnulty & Barry license does not establish the July hall.

- **Status:** **RETRIEVAL GAP**, potentially a manual callout if images remain unavailable.
- **Completion test:** Inspect actual municipal records before associating any business with the fire-adjacent hall.

## 6. Pre- and post-fire exact-address notices

- **Question:** Where were the former Schieremann tailor room, restaurant, Miller's shoe store, Red Front, barber shop, and first Gates stores?
- **Prior work:** Discovery OCR/JSON across 928 1922 Banner-Courier/Enterprise pages produced no defensible exact-address announcement. Verified notices place Miller post-fire at 209 Fifth and later 419 Main, not at his pre-fire location. Leland & Little and Timms remain unaddressed.
- **Next source:** Bounded visual review of likely notices/advertisements; September–December 1922 and 1923–1924 directories; licenses, assessor books, trade ads, construction notices, and the Polk directory. Search name and address variants.
- **Completion test:** Change an address classification only when a scan/record ties the party to a numbered or otherwise fixed premise.
