# 505 Main — 1939–1941 historic permit-index follow-up — 27 August 2026

Status: **ONLINE FOLLOW-UP COMPLETE; HISTORIC MAIN STREET PERMIT INDEX IDENTIFIED; 505-SPECIFIC 1939–1941 ENTRY NOT YET RETRIEVED**

Purpose: follow up `E-101` / `E-102` and `RL-005` on whether the official **1940** construction-date line for 505 Main could reflect a major remodel, addition, rear expansion, rebuild, or assessor/improvement event rather than first construction of the premises.

## Existing repository baseline reviewed before this pass

The project already established:

- `E-100` / `S-116`: the 2009 Oregon Historic Site Form states a **primary construction date of 1940** for 505 Main; the 2021 ODOT cultural-resources table independently carries the same date.
- The 2000 Oregon City Downtown inventory assigns **c.1920** to 505 Main.
- `E-020` / `E-102`: the 1925 Sanborn visually shows a separate 505 Main billiards/cigars storefront, so **1940 cannot safely be interpreted as the first-ever existence of a building/premises at the address**.
- `E-021` / `E-102`: a 1936 aerial shows Main-frontage building fabric before 1940; the 1936/1940/1944 aerial pass identified block-level change but did not isolate a 505-specific rear-wall change.
- `E-101`: working hypothesis only — the 1940 inventory date might represent a rear addition, major remodel, rebuild, or related improvement event; whole-building reconstruction, survey/assessor dating convention, or an erroneous inherited date remain alternatives.
- The 24 August permit pass searched the modern Oregon City permit system and Clackamas County Document Retriever. No 1939–1941 construction/addition permit for 505 was recovered; only a later electrical permit appeared. That result was recorded as a **retrieval gap**, not evidence that no work occurred.

## New high-value finding — City of Oregon City Main Street permit index begins in 1939

The Oregon SHPO **Oregon Historic Sites Database** record for the Oregon City Masonic Lodge at 707–709 Main Street includes, in its research bibliography, the City record series:

> **Building Permit Index for Main Street, 1939 – 2001**

The database says this index is **held by the City of Oregon City**.

Institutional source:
https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=v.dsp_siteSummary&resultDisplay=30553

This is materially better than the prior generic permit-search route. It proves that researchers compiling Oregon City historic-resource records used a dedicated **Main Street permit index whose coverage starts exactly in 1939**. It does **not** establish that 505 has a 1940 entry or that the underlying permit survives.

### Online retrieval result

Targeted web and City/SHPO searches did **not** expose a digitized copy of the 1939–2001 Main Street permit index and did not recover a 505 Main entry for 1939, 1940, or 1941.

Classification: **verified archival locator; 505-specific contents remain a retrieval gap**.

## Why the earlier online permit search could not close the question

An official Oregon City *Trail News* article explains that the City's public online permit-history search reaches back to approximately **2002** and directs researchers seeking older records or document copies to the **City Recorder / public-records request** process.

Official City source:
https://orcity.org/Archive/ViewFile/Item/131

Current City public-record request page:
https://orcity.org/594/Public-Records-Requests

Accordingly, the absence of a 1940 record in EnerGov/self-service is not meaningful evidence against a 1940 permit. The historical Main Street index is a separate, older record series.

## New assessor/tax retrieval route — 1940 records survive

The Oregon Secretary of State's **Clackamas County Records Inventory** documents surviving records capable of testing for a 1940 improvement/value change at 505 Main:

- Oregon State Archives: **Index [Assessment and Tax Roll], 1901–1949**;
- Oregon State Archives: **Index to Assessment Rolls, 1938–1940**;
- Clackamas County Assessment & Taxation archive: old land/lot ownership indexes covering **1931–1949**;
- Clackamas County Assessment & Taxation archive: **Tax Roll microfilm, 1940–1977**.

Official inventory:
https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/county-records-guide/Pages/clackamas-inventory.aspx

State Archives land-record guide also lists Clackamas **Assessment and Tax Rolls, 1850–1949** and the related **1901–1949 index**:
https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/records/pages/aids-land.aspx

These records may show owner, legal description, land/improvement valuation, or a year-to-year assessment change. They are **not building permits** and cannot by themselves identify construction scope unless the record supplies an improvement notation or is paired with permit/assessor-card evidence.

## New 1930s occupant/search key

Oregon City's official historic-resource page for **401 3rd Street** states that **Edwin F. and Alice Farr were proprietors of Farr's Pool Hall at 505 Main Street** and that they acquired their residence at 401 3rd in **1934**.

Official City source:
https://www.orcity.org/2014/401-3rd-Street

This gives useful 1930s–1940s name search keys for the permit/index inquiry:

- Edwin F. Farr
- Alice Farr
- Farr's Pool Hall
- 505 Main Street

The source establishes their business connection to 505, **not ownership of the 505 real estate**, and therefore their names should be searched without assuming that a permit would necessarily have been filed under either Farr.

## Current conclusion

The 1940-remodel/rear-addition hypothesis is **not proved**, but the permit question is now much more narrowly defined:

1. A City-held **Building Permit Index for Main Street, 1939–2001** is independently documented by Oregon SHPO research.
2. The actual **505 Main index entry/entries for 1939–1941 have not been retrieved**.
3. Clackamas County **1940 tax-roll and 1938–1940 assessment-index records survive** and can independently test for an improvement/value change.
4. Farr's Pool Hall provides a new period business-name/person search key, but does not prove property ownership or construction activity.
