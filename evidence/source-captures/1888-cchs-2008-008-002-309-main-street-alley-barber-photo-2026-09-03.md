## What the photograph directly establishes

The right storefront is a barber shop in the functional sense: **HAIR CUTTING** and **SHAVING** are plainly painted on its windows. The photograph also directly shows a narrow open passage immediately to the barber shop's left.

The handwriting at the lower margin appears to include a Trembath name, and the catalog's other-number field includes `Trembath, Beauliau #3`, but neither fact is sufficient to identify the photographed barber or proprietor. No person/business identity is assigned from that handwriting.

## Interpreting the confusing alley description

The catalog wording is grammatically compressed. A defensible working reading is: **buildings on Main Street at the opening of the alley located between 4th and 5th Streets**. The visible narrow passage is compatible with that reading, but the archive does not yet declare that passage to be the alley as a documented fact.

The bluff immediately behind the buildings is compatible with an **east-side Main Street frontage photographed from the west side of Main looking east toward the bluff**. That orientation is a **PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS**, not a cataloged statement. Under that hypothesis, the barber storefront would lie on the south side of the alley and the central two-story building on its north side.

No modern numbered Main Street address is assigned. If the CCHS location is correct, this is the Main Street block between Fourth and Fifth, one block south of the project's principal Fifth-to-Sixth 501/503/505 frontage. It is therefore valuable adjacent-block streetscape evidence but not evidence that any pictured building is 501, 503, or 505 Main.
