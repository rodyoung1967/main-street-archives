## New constraint: the 1932 ordinance formula likely allowed three pool-hall licenses

The previously recovered 2 March 1932 City Commission text for **Ordinance No. 1234** states that no greater number of pool-hall licenses could be issued than **one for each 2,000 population or major fraction thereof**.

Official 1930 U.S. Census Table 5 lists **Oregon City, Clackamas County: 5,761 population in 1930** and **5,686 in 1920**:

- U.S. Census Bureau, *Fifteenth Census of the United States: 1930*, Population, Table 5 — Population of Incorporated Places: 1930 and 1920.
- Search-indexed Census PDF: `https://www2.census.gov/prod2/decennial/documents/03815512v1ch08.pdf`

### Derived interpretation — not an observed license count

Using 5,761 as the relevant population base, Ordinance 1234's formula points to a **likely ceiling of three pool-hall licenses**: two complete 2,000-person units plus a remaining 1,761 people, which is more than half of another 2,000-person unit and therefore appears to be a “major fraction.”

This is a **derived regulatory interpretation**, not evidence that three licenses were actually outstanding. The archive still needs a contemporary record showing how Oregon City administratively applied “major fraction” and how many licenses were actually issued.

This narrows the operator search materially: if the interpretation is correct, the relevant City Recorder records should identify a very small licensed universe rather than an open-ended number of pool halls.

## Later code identifies the highest-value municipal record class

The official **1963 Oregon City Municipal Code** contains two especially useful record-locator provisions:

1. **Recorder license register — Chapter 1, §1-10-2.** The Recorder was to issue authorized licenses and keep a register showing **to whom granted, for what time, and for what purpose**.
2. **Pool-room licenses — Chapter 5, §5-2-3.** Licenses for billiard and pool rooms were issued **quarterly**. The code states that quarterly licenses expired March 31, June 30, September 30, and December 31.
3. **Transfers — Chapter 5, §5-2-5.** A person seeking to transfer a business license from one location to another or to another person was to present proof to the Recorder; denial could be appealed to the Commission.

Official 1963 code:

`https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/11782/1963-Oregon-City-Municipal-Code`

### Temporal boundary

The 1963 code is **not evidence that these exact procedures or quarterly dates governed in 1930 or 1932**. No retroactive assumption is made here.

Its value is as a **records-route locator**: by 1963 Oregon City explicitly maintained an operator/time/purpose license register and treated pool-room licenses and business-location/ownership transfers as Recorder-controlled municipal records. The next 1930/1932 search should therefore prioritize any surviving predecessor **license register, pool/billiard license applications, receipts, renewal records, transfer records, and Commission appeals/actions**, rather than relying only on Commission-minute keyword searches.
