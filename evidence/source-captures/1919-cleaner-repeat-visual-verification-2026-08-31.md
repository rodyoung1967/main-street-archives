# 1919 cleaner/dyer repeat advertisements — visual verification

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED PRIMARY NEWSPAPER REPEAT SET; advertiser identity and address normalization unresolved**

Purpose: test the OCR-discovered repeats of the unnamed cleaner/dyer advertisement already established by `S-151` / `E-136`, especially the OCR conflict between `500` and `600`.

## Source set

The following *Oregon City Enterprise* page-7 scans were preserved under `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1919-cleaner-repeat-pages/` and directly inspected from high-resolution renders on 31 August 2026:

- 18 July 1919
- 25 July 1919
- 8 August 1919
- 15 August 1919
- 22 August 1919
- 29 August 1919
- 5 September 1919

The already-preserved and visually verified 1 August 1919 page (`S-151` / `E-136`) falls inside the same run.

## Visual result

Across the eight consecutive weekly appearances from **18 July through 5 September 1919**, the classified is materially the same and prints:

- `Clothes cleaned, pressed and dyed.`
- **`500 Fifth and Main streets`**
- **`two doors south of new post office`**
- **Phone 260**

The 25 July and 5 September OCR text had rendered the first number as **600**. Direct scan inspection shows that both pages actually print **500**. The `600` readings are OCR errors.

No inspected advertisement in this run prints a business or proprietor name.

## Historical disposition

This repeat set materially strengthens `E-136` in three ways:

1. `500` was intentional printed newspaper text repeated over multiple weeks, not a one-issue typesetting or OCR anomaly.
2. The cleaner/dyer was advertising continuously for at least the eight-week run from 18 July through 5 September 1919.
3. `two doors south of new post office` was likewise stable advertising language, strengthening its value as a relative-location clue.

However, the unusual phrase **`500 Fifth and Main streets`** is still not silently normalized to **500 Main** or **500 Fifth**. The advertiser remains unidentified, and the project still does not merge it with Pantorium solely because Pantorium was separately documented at 500 Main in October 1918.
