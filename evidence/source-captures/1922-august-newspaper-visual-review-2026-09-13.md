## Direct historical evidence visually confirmed

### 17 August 1922 Banner-Courier, page 1 / sequence 1 — Gilbert Miller re-enters business

The rendered front page was visually inspected. A local item states that **Gilbert Miller**, former owner/proprietor of Miller's Shoe Store, whose store had been completely destroyed by the recent fire at Fifth and Main, had purchased the store of **H. S. Goldman and son, 519 Main Street**, and taken possession.

This is direct post-fire business-relocation evidence. It confirms that by 17 August Miller was operating at 519 Main rather than his destroyed Fifth/Main location. It does not establish ownership or reconstruction status of 501/503/505.

Source path: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-08/1922-08-17-seq-01.pdf`

### 17 August 1922 Banner-Courier, page 2 / sequence 2 — Leland and Little, Billiards

The rendered page was visually inspected. In the list of local business enterprises the page separately lists **“LELAND AND LITTLE, Billiards.”** The same list also separately includes **R. A. Junken, Contractor, Builder**, Dr. Freeze, and numerous other firms.

This is direct dated evidence that Leland and Little were publicly listed in the billiards business on 17 August 1922, one month after the Fifth/Main fire. The listing does **not** give an exact street address for Leland and Little and therefore must not be used by itself to identify the July fire-adjacent pool hall as 505/505½ or any other exact premises.

Source path: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-08/1922-08-17-seq-02.pdf`

## Interpretation controls

- Miller's 519 Main relocation is a business-location transition and does not identify what happened structurally to his former premises after the fire.
- Leland and Little's continued public billiards listing establishes business activity, not the exact building occupied.
- R. A. Junken's appearance as a contractor in the same dated business list is useful context but does not by itself prove which specific building project he was performing.
- Existing separately preserved reporting about post-fire Gates construction must continue to be evaluated on its own exact wording and spatial evidence.
- Do not collapse business operation, tenancy, building identity, and property ownership.
