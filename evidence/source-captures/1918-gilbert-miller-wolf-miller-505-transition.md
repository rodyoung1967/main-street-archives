# 1918 Gilbert Miller / Wolf & Miller transition into Oregon City

Status: **STRONG IDENTITY/CONTINUITY INFERENCE; direct source naming Gilbert Miller at 505 Main still sought**

Purpose: test whether the `Miller` in **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main** in October 1918 was the same Gilbert Miller whose shoe-repair shop was destroyed in the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire.

## 1. Springfield identity — official historic inventory

The National Register nomination for Springfield's Washburne Historic District states, in its discussion of a residence on E Street:

- **`Around 1918 Gilbert Miller of Wolf and Miller (men's furnishings) lived there.`**

Source: National Park Service, National Register of Historic Places Inventory—Nomination Form, Washburne Historic District, continuation page containing the 606/618 E Street entries.

This is an official secondary-source identification of **Gilbert Miller with Wolf & Miller in Springfield around 1918**.

## 2. 19 July 1918 — Wolf & Miller's Miller has family in Oregon City

The *Eugene Daily Guard*, 19 July 1918, p. 3, reports in the item **`Returns From Oregon City`** that a Miller **`of the Wolf & Miller store`** had returned from Oregon City after being called there by an accident involving his second-oldest daughter. It says his family was in Oregon City and that he would join them after disposing of his Springfield stock.

The archive page text renders his initial imperfectly (`K. Miller`) and his daughter's name imperfectly. Because the page image was not successfully visually certified in this session, this item remains a **supporting text/OCR lead**, not the decisive identity bridge.

Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088107/1918-07-19/ed-1/seq-3.pdf

## 3. 22 August 1918 — Gilbert Miller leaves Springfield for Oregon City

The actual scan of the *Springfield News*, 22 August 1918, p. 3, was directly inspected.

The town-news item visibly states that **Gilbert Miller left the previous Saturday for Oregon City**, where he would join his family. It explains that his family had moved there earlier and that Miller had remained in Springfield to close out his business.

Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071003/1918-08-22/ed-1/seq-3.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED PRIMARY SCAN**.

## 4. 10 October 1918 — Wolf & Miller at 505 Main, Oregon City

The actual scan of the *Oregon City Courier*, 10 October 1918, p. 4, was directly inspected. Its business feature identifies:

- **Wolf & Miller — 505 Main**;
- shoes, men's clothing/furnishings, and an up-to-date shoe-repair department with Landis electric machinery;
- after the advertised sale, **one member of the firm was going to Idaho and the other would restock the store and continue the business**.

This source is already `S-034` / `E-027`.

Verification state for this re-check: **VISUALLY VERIFIED PRIMARY SCAN**.

## 5. 1922 Gilbert Miller continuity

The project's already verified July 1922 fire evidence identifies **Gilbert Miller** as the proprietor whose shoe-repair shop and shoe stock were destroyed. Post-fire advertising places Gilbert Miller's replacement shoe machinery at **209 Fifth Street**.

Thus Gilbert Miller was unquestionably operating the same specialized trade in Oregon City by 1922.

## Safe inference

The combined chronology is unusually tight:

1. official Springfield inventory identifies **Gilbert Miller of Wolf & Miller** around 1918;
2. primary August 1918 Springfield reporting shows **Gilbert Miller leaving Springfield for Oregon City to join his family after remaining behind to close his business**;
3. less than two months later, **Wolf & Miller is operating at 505 Main in Oregon City**, selling shoes and running a shoe-repair department;
4. by July 1922 **Gilbert Miller** operates a shoe-repair shop in the Fifth/Main fire cluster.

Taken together, this supports a **strong inference** that Gilbert Miller was the `Miller` in the Oregon City Wolf & Miller partnership at 505 Main in late 1918.

It is **not yet direct proof**. The archive still needs a source that explicitly prints a formulation such as `Gilbert Miller, Wolf & Miller, 505 Main`, identifies the two Wolf & Miller partners by full name, or otherwise directly bridges Gilbert to the 505 partnership.

## S. Wolf / family hypothesis

A separate compiled genealogy reports that Gilbert Miller's first wife was **Clara Rose Wolf**, daughter of Henry and Elizabeth (Conrad) Wolf. This could eventually matter to the unresolved identity of the Wolf partner and later **S. Wolf of Portland**, but it is presently only a secondary genealogy lead. No relationship between Clara's Wolf family, the Wolf & Miller partner, and S. Wolf is asserted here.
