# 31 August 1916 — Fifth Street Restaurant in state food inspection report

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED; exact address and continuity to 1922 unresolved**

Purpose: establish an earlier primary-source anchor for an Oregon City business called **Fifth Street Restaurant**, while avoiding an unsupported assumption that the 1916 establishment was necessarily the same operator, room, or exact premises destroyed in the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire.

## Source

**Oregon City Courier, 31 Aug 1916, p. 3**

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-08-31/ed-1/seq-3/

PDF:

The actual scanned newspaper page was visually inspected. The item appears under the heading **`HOTELS ARE FAIR — Dairy and Food Commissioner Report on Cleanliness`**.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Directly supported fact

The article reports results from the quarterly report of the Oregon state dairy and food commissioner, covering inspections made in Clackamas County during the preceding months.

Under **Oregon City — Hotels and Restaurants**, the printed list includes:

- Electric — 82.2
- Seventh Street Hotel — 81.1
- Home — 78.8
- E. T. Mass — 78.2
- J. E. Bann — 77.6
- Mrs. M. J. Moreland — 75.6
- **Fifth Street Restaurant — 75.3**
- Red Front Boarding House — 73.8
- additional establishments

The article does **not** state a proprietor or numbered street address for Fifth Street Restaurant.

## Why this matters

The local **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** fire report says the 18 July Fifth/Main fire destroyed the **Fifth street restaurant**, which that report describes as owned by Ed. Blake.

The 1916 inspection therefore proves that an Oregon City establishment using the name **Fifth Street Restaurant** existed by August 1916, almost six years before the 1922 fire.

This substantially extends the known lower bound for the business/place name. It may indicate a long-lived Fifth Street restaurant location or trade name, but continuity of operator and premises is **not yet proven**.

## Important caution

Do not automatically treat the 1916 and 1922 references as one uninterrupted business. Possible explanations include:

1. the same restaurant location/name continued across the period with changing proprietors;
2. the business changed rooms on Fifth Street but retained the generic descriptive name;
3. separate establishments used the same descriptive name at different times;
4. the 1922 fire report used a descriptive place-name rather than a formal trade name.

Additional directory, licensing, inspection, tax, lease, or advertisement evidence is required.

The same 1916 inspection list also contains a **Red Front Boarding House**. That name must remain separate from the **Red Front clothing store** mentioned in the July 1922 Eugene fire report unless a direct source connects them.

## Relationship to current Fifth Street reconstruction

Current exact-address anchors include:

- **108 Fifth** — Scripture & May by 1916–1918;
- **109 Fifth** — Oregon City Laundry by 1918;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — named in 1916 inspection but exact number unknown;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — active in the 8 Jun 1922 Buyers' Carnival roster;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — destroyed in the 18 Jul 1922 fire; local report attributes ownership to Ed. Blake.

The 1916 source adds chronology but does not establish where the restaurant sat relative to 108/109 Fifth, Gilbert Miller, H. Ketchner, or the Main Street ignition room.
