# 18 Jul 1922 — Eugene Daily Guard same-day Oregon City fire account

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED from scanned newspaper page; preserves material source differences from the local Banner-Courier account**

Purpose: preserve a same-day wire/account of the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire because it adds several spatial and occupancy details not present in the 20 Jul local Banner-Courier report. Where the accounts differ, preserve both rather than normalizing them.

**Eugene Daily Guard, 18 Jul 1922, p. 1**

## Businesses / losses named

The article's loss list includes:

- **Oregon City Laundry** — destroyed;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — destroyed;
- **Gilbert Miller shoe shop** — destroyed;
- **Washburn and May garage** — destroyed;
- **Kitchener/Ketchner confectionery** — destroyed;
- **Red Front clothing store** — **damaged**, with reported stock damage rather than listed as destroyed;
- a **W. F. Blakeslee residence** — damaged/loss reported.

The exact spellings are difficult in the scan/OCR, but the business identities align substantially with the local fire coverage except for the newly named **Red Front clothing store**.

## Critical origin/occupancy detail

The article says the fire destroyed only frame structures and states that the **shop in which the fire started had been used until only a few days earlier by a cleaning and dyeing establishment**.

It then states that the flames spread **from this shop to the Oregon City Laundry**, which caught and burned rapidly.

This is materially important because the local **20 Jul Banner-Courier** instead describes the same origin as a **vacant store room formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop**, owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**.

These descriptions are not necessarily mutually exclusive. Possible interpretations include:

1. Schieremann's tailoring business itself also performed cleaning/dyeing;
2. a cleaning/dyeing concern briefly occupied the room after Schieremann;
3. the same establishment was described differently by the two newspapers;
4. the wire account compressed or confused the recent occupancy history.

No interpretation is yet proven. **Do not replace the Schieremann evidence with the cleaning/dyeing wording, and do not assume two separate tenants without a direct source.**

## Direct spread-to-laundry implication

The same-day account's statement that fire spread **from the ignition shop directly to Oregon City Laundry** is a strong spatial constraint. It suggests the origin premises physically adjoined or immediately connected to the laundry complex closely enough for direct spread.

This strengthens the value of comparing the pre-fire laundry footprint and Main-frontage rear geometry, but it still does not identify the origin as 501, 503, or 505 Main.

## Red Front boundary clue

The **Red Front clothing store** is reported as damaged, while the core Fifth Street businesses were destroyed.

If Red Front's 1922 address can be recovered, it could provide a useful surviving boundary marker for the burned area. Do not confuse this with earlier historical Oregon City businesses called `Red Front` or with the **Red Front Barn/Livery** at Sixth & Water unless a contemporary 1922 source proves identity/location continuity.

## Upstairs-resident details

The article specifically names:

- **C. A. Rosecrans, wife, and son Everett**, who escaped down burning stairs;
- a **Blakeslee couple** in quarters **over the laundry**, who escaped;
- another **Blakely/Blake-like couple** described as living **in the rear of the restaurant**, who were awakened as fire ate through the wall.

The scan is sufficiently clear to establish the broad residential relationships, but the last surname is difficult and should not be normalized without corroboration.

The statement that a Blakeslee couple had quarters **over the laundry** is especially useful because it proves mixed residential/commercial use within the laundry structure or complex.

## Comparison with local Banner-Courier

The **20 Jul Banner-Courier**, the local Oregon City paper, remains the preferred source for the precise S. Wolf / Schieremann / adjoining-pool-hall wording. The Daily Guard adds earlier same-day observations and should be used as corroborating and supplemental evidence.
