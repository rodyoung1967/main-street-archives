# 1922 Harding property sale to S. Wolf / C. E. Gates and post-fire corner rebuild

Status: **THREE PRIMARY NEWSPAPER SCANS VISUALLY VERIFIED; EXACT LEGAL DESCRIPTION AND MODERN-STOREFRONT MAPPING UNRESOLVED**

Purpose: record a newly recovered ownership-and-rebuilding sequence immediately around the 18 July 1922 Fifth/Main fire. These sources directly connect **George A. Harding**, **S. Wolf**, **C. / C. E. Gates**, the **Fifth Street Restaurant**, **Miller's shoe store**, an adjoining barber shop, and the Red Front Clothing Store. They materially narrow the property search but do not assign the former Alex Schieremann tailor room to modern 501, 503, or 505 Main.

Related evidence: `E-096`, `E-097`; cross-reference `E-103`
Related sources: `S-111`, `S-112`, `S-113`; cross-reference `S-021`, `S-117`

## 13 July 1922 — Harding property sale, five days before the fire

The actual page-one scan of the **13 July 1922 Banner-Courier** was visually inspected at high resolution on 24 August 2026. The short item headed **“Property Changes Hands”** is legible on the page itself.

Canonical page PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-13/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

Repository PDF:
`newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07-13-page-1-harding-wolf-gates-property-sale.pdf`

The article reports that:

- George A. Harding had owned a Main Street property group containing stores occupied by the **Fifth Street Restaurant**, **Miller's shoe store**, an **adjoining barber shop**, and the **Red Front Clothing Store**;
- the property had been sold to **S. Wolf** and **C. Gates**, both described as being of Portland;
- the article's `latter` / `former` construction assigns the **Fifth Street Restaurant location to C. Gates** and the **remainder to S. Wolf**.

The article gives no consideration, deed date, recording number, legal description, block/lot, dimensions, or numbered street address. It is therefore strong contemporary property-transfer reporting, not a substitute for the recorded deed or assessment entry.

## 18–20 July 1922 — relationship to the fire report

The established, visually verified **20 July Banner-Courier** fire article says:

- the fire began early on 18 July in a vacant Main Street storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop;
- **S. Wolf of Portland owned the building in which the fire started**;
- the Fifth Street Restaurant and Miller shoe-repair shop were among the separately named losses;
- a pool hall adjoined the former tailor premises.

Registered source/evidence: `S-117` / `E-103`
Repository PDF: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07-20-page-1-fifth-main-fire.pdf`

Read together, the 13 and 20 July articles provide a direct contemporary sequence:

1. Harding's named Main Street store group was reported sold to Wolf and Gates five days before the fire.
2. Gates was assigned the Fifth Street Restaurant location; Wolf was assigned the remainder.
3. The fire-origin building was then reported as owned by S. Wolf.

This is a **strong cross-source inference** that the S. Wolf fire-origin building belonged to the freshly reported Harding-to-Wolf remainder, while the restaurant occupied the separately allocated Gates location. It is not yet a deed-level conclusion because the instrument, effective date, exact property lines, and legal description have not been recovered.

The finding directly validates the project's decision to search newspaper realty/building-sale notices as well as land assessments. It does **not** prove that Wolf owned only improvements rather than land; the legal mechanism remains unresolved.

## 18 August 1922 — replacement building and former restaurant site

The actual page-five scan of the **18 August 1922 Oregon City Enterprise** was visually inspected at high resolution on 24 August 2026. The article headed **“New Building Being Erected at Corner Of Fifth and Main”** adds that:

- the new **two-story hollow-tile** building stood on property owned by **C. E. Gates of Portland**;
- it replaced the building recently destroyed by fire that had been occupied by the **Fifth Street Restaurant**;
- the first floor was planned as **two storerooms**, one already leased for a barber shop and the other expected to be a store;
- the upper floor was planned for apartment/sleeping-room use;
- R. A. Junken was contractor and occupancy was expected about 1 October.

## Consequence for the 501 / 503 / 505 reconstruction

The visually verified 1925 Sanborn (`S-021`) shows, from the Fifth Street corner northward:

- **501 — store**, with `TILE` notation;
- **503 — restaurant**;
- **505 — billiards/cigars**.

The 1922 Gates building was two-story hollow tile with two ground-floor storerooms, but neither August article prints its width, orientation, addresses, or property boundaries. Two materially different models therefore remain possible:

### Model A — Gates corner principally maps to 501

If the two Gates storerooms were one Main-facing bay plus a Fifth-facing or internal/rear room, the Wolf remainder could begin at the modern 503 footprint. This remains compatible with the existing **503 predecessor = ignition room / 505 = adjoining pool hall** hypothesis.

### Model B — Gates corner maps to 501 + 503

If the two Gates storerooms became the two Main-facing 501 and 503 bays shown in 1925, the Wolf remainder could begin around modern 505. That would make **505 or another room farther north** a stronger ignition candidate and would require a different identification for the adjoining July pool hall.

The 1925 plan is compatible with Model B, but compatibility is not proof. Later subdivision, changed entrances, changed use, a Fifth-facing storeroom, or a post-fire footprint adjustment could produce Model A or another arrangement. No source presently fixes the Gates/Wolf line against the 1925 façade.

### Conditional implication for 501

The sale and fire reports separate the **Gates restaurant location** from the **Wolf-owned ignition building**. If future evidence proves that the July 1922 Fifth Street Restaurant occupied the modern 501 footprint, 501 would be strongly disfavored as the ignition room. That address equation remains unproven, so **501 stays live**.

## Identity cautions

- The July sale notice prints **C. Gates**; both August construction notices print **C. E. Gates**. Their shared transaction/property/building context strongly supports treating those forms as the same buyer for this sequence.
- Do not merge this C. E. Gates with another person of the same initials— including the prominent Medford automobile dealer/politician—without a direct identity source. The local articles describe the property buyer as being of Portland.
- Do not merge S. Wolf with Wolf & Nat, the Wolf & Miller partner, Samuel Wolf, Simon Wolf, Victor Wolf, Gilbert Miller's Wolf relatives, or the later Wolf-family 503 ownership without direct evidence.

## What is now established

- A visually verified contemporary paper reported the Harding property-group sale and the Wolf/Gates allocation on 13 July: **Very High for published wording**.
- C. / C. E. Gates held the Fifth Street Restaurant location and owned the Fifth/Main corner property after the fire: **Very High across three visually verified articles**.
- S. Wolf received the reported remainder and was named one week later as owner of the ignition building: **Very High for each published statement; High / strong cross-source inference for the same property chain**.
- A two-story hollow-tile replacement for the restaurant building was under construction by 17–18 August: **Very High**.
- The replacement had two ground-floor storerooms and upper residential rooms: **Very High**.

## What remains unresolved

- the recorded deed, legal description, purchase price, recording/effective dates, and whether the newspaper's ownership language precisely matches fee title;
- the exact line between the Gates restaurant location and the Wolf remainder;
- whether the Gates replacement maps to 501 only, 501+503, or another post-fire configuration;
- which modern storefront contained the ignition room;
- the exact adjoining pool-hall address and operator;
- the full identities of S. Wolf and C. E. Gates.
