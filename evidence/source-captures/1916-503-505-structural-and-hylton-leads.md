# 1916 — 503/505 structural context and Hylton identity leads

Status: **active / lead file; no 1916 exact-address occupant for 503 or 505 yet**

This file records context that may help resolve the unresolved 1916 occupants and structures at 503 and 505 Main. Newspaper-derived items remain **OCR/TEXT ONLY** until visually verified from the scanned page under `RESEARCH-STANDARD.md`.

## Structural context from Oregon City's downtown historic-resource survey

The City of Oregon City's downtown historic-resource report groups the east-side building range as follows:

- **503 Main Street** — Latourette-David-Barlow Building / Kwality Cafe resource, c.1919.
- **504-514 Main Street** — one historic commercial building range, c.1892.
- **505 Main Street** — separate resource, c.1920.

Source:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4025/Downtown-Final-Report---2000-PDF

### Why this matters

The c.1892 **504-514 Main** range is a valuable physical clue for interpreting the 1916 numbered-address grid. It supports treating 504, 506, 508, 510, 512 and 514 as addresses within an older continuous commercial building/range on the even-numbered side rather than assuming each number represented a freestanding building.

This does **not** establish that the current 504-514 fabric is unchanged from 1916, nor does it identify individual 1916 tenants for every storefront.

The same report dates the current 503 and 505 resources later, around 1919 and 1920 respectively. Those dates are useful as later-building anchors but must not be converted into claims that either address was vacant, destroyed, or rebuilt in 1916 without direct evidence.

## 503 Main — earlier exact-address business sequence

### March 1912 — H. H. Smith pool-hall license application

The *Morning Enterprise*, 24 Mar 1912, p2, prints a notice in which **H. H. Smith** says he will apply to the city council for a license to run and regulate a **pool hall at his place of business, 503 Main Street**, for three months.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-24/ed-1/seq-2/ocr/

Relevant OCR text is at the notice beginning around lines 820–827 of the page representation.

This is a direct exact-address 503 anchor and establishes a documented 503 business use before Hylton:

- **Mar 1912 — H. H. Smith — pool hall — 503 Main**
- **Feb–Apr 1914 — L. Hylton — cigars/tobacco/smokers' supplies/soft drinks — 503 Main**

The transition between Smith and Hylton is not yet documented. Because pool halls commonly sold tobacco/soft drinks, the similarity of business type is suggestive but **does not prove** Hylton bought Smith's business or continued the same establishment.

A useful related 1911 city-license report lists both **L. Hylton, shooting gallery** and **H. H. Smith, pool room** among municipal license receipts. That shows both men were already independently operating licensed amusement/recreation-type businesses in Oregon City before the 1912 503 notice, and cautions against assuming a simple identity or continuity between them.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1911-02-03/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

## Hylton identity narrowing for 503 Main

The 1914 exact-address business profile identifies **L. Hylton** at **503 Main**, selling cigars, tobacco, smokers' supplies and soft drinks:

- 12 Feb 1914, Oregon City Courier, p15:
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-15/
- 23 Apr 1914, Oregon City Courier, p6:
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-23/ed-1/seq-6/

A 1917 obituary for **Louis Jackson Hylton**, age 81, says he had lived in Mulino for 13 years and lists surviving sons including **Louis Hylton of Oregon City** and **James Hylton of Twilight**:

- 7 Jun 1917, Oregon City Courier, p4:
  https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1917-06-07/ed-1/seq-4/

A 6 Apr 1916 Courier local item says **L. J. Hylton** lived in the Twilight district and left on an eastern trip:

- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-04-06/ed-1/seq-5/ocr/

Taken together, this makes the 1916 Twilight `L. J. Hylton` a poor match for the 1914 503 proprietor if the latter was the **Louis Hylton of Oregon City** named in the 1917 obituary. The evidence is still not sufficient to prove that identification, but it sharpens the distinction:

- **James Hylton** is explicitly associated with Twilight in the 1917 obituary.
- **Louis Hylton** is explicitly associated with Oregon City in the same obituary.
- Earlier county records also contain the form **Lou Hylton**, showing that `Lou/Louis Hylton` was present in Oregon City-area records well before 1914.

### Current working hypothesis

The best working hypothesis is that the `L. Hylton` at 503 Main in 1914 may have been **Louis ("Lou") Hylton**, son of Louis Jackson Hylton, rather than the Twilight-district Hylton mentioned in April 1916.

This remains **unproven** and should not be promoted to a final identity until a directory, census, license, business-sale notice, obituary, or other source explicitly links Louis/Lou Hylton to 503 Main or to the cigar/tobacco business.

## Indexed-search gap for 1915–1917

Targeted exact-address searches for `503 Main`, `503 Main Street`, Hylton, pool hall, cigar, tobacco and related terms have not yet surfaced a direct 1915–1917 exact-address continuation after the Apr. 1914 Hylton evidence. This is a **search gap only**, not evidence of vacancy or closure. OCR and web indexing can miss display advertising, and full visual review remains outstanding.

The strongest exact-address sequence currently visible is therefore:

- **24 Mar 1912 — H. H. Smith, pool hall, 503 Main**
- **12 Feb / 23 Apr 1914 — L. Hylton, tobacco/cigars/soft drinks, 503 Main**
- **1915–1917 — unresolved**

## New direct 1916 address control outside the 500 block

The 6 Apr 1916 Courier also gives **Wm. A. Schilling, Optometrist & Optician — 617 Main Street**. This is another useful exact-number control point for the 1916 address system:

## Research implications

1. Search specifically for **H. H. Smith → L. Hylton** transition evidence between Mar. 1912 and Feb. 1914: business sale, pool-hall license transfer, cigar/tobacco license, lease, closure, or relocation.
2. Search `Louis Hylton`, `Lou Hylton`, `L. Hylton`, cigar, tobacco, soft drinks, and `503 Main` across 1914-1917.
3. Search city-license and council notices for transfer or cessation of the 503 cigar/tobacco business.
4. Use the c.1892 **504-514 Main** building-range clue when interpreting even-numbered storefronts, but keep it separate from legal-lot mapping.
5. Do not use the c.1919/c.1920 resource dates for 503/505 as proof of 1916 vacancy or demolition.
6. Continue seeking the 1916-17 Sohns & Woodbeck directory, which remains the strongest single source lead for a direct 503/505 occupant answer.
