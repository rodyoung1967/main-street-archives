## E-139 — corrected fire rows

Printed p. **13**, right half of **PDF page 7**:

| Date in 1925 | Time | Location as printed | Cause as printed | Approx. damage | Hose laid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| March 27 | 10:00 a.m. | 811 Jefferson | Parafin explosion | $2,500.00 | 1,300 ft. |
| April 18 | 10:15 a.m. | 10th and Washington | Explosion of wax | $75.00 | 550 ft. |
| **April 30** | **6:15 a.m.** | **Main bet. 5th and 6th** | **Spark from chimney** | **$15.00** | **300 ft.** |

The reported $2,500 belonged to March 27, the April 18 date/time belonged to Tenth/Washington, and the Main/Fifth–Sixth location and chimney-spark cause belonged to April 30. The prior combined claim is withdrawn, not carried forward as a competing historical account.

Printed p. **14**, left half of **PDF page 8**, additionally lists **18 August 1925, 10:00 a.m., Fifth St., Flue**. Its damage and hose fields are blank: retain **not stated**, not zero. The street is unnumbered and no cross street, occupant or business appears. No new numbered Fifth Street building is assigned.

Other Main Street intersection calls in the reviewed list remain distinct from the target event. In particular, the February 22 entry is Sixth/Main, and the May 23 electrical-wire/box-car call is Fourth/Main; neither supplies the April 30 storefront.

### Historical limits

- A $15 approximate loss is the report's estimate, not an independently audited loss or a description of which building fabric was affected.
- The table supplies **no side of Main**, lot, proprietor, trade name or building number. It cannot be restricted to the odd-numbered 501–511 frontage.
- The April 30 incident precedes the May 1925 Sanborn, but sequence alone proves no rebuilding connection.
- This removes the supposed substantial April 18 block fire as support for a 503/505 replacement hypothesis; it does not prove that no construction or other fire occurred.
- The unrelated March 27 and April 18 rows are retained to explain the transcription error, not expanded into new target-building histories.

## E-140 — Fifth Street paving, not Main-corner narrowing

Printed pp. **17–18** (PDF pages **9–10**) describe the 1925 asphaltic-concrete program. The introductory description gives **40 feet curb to curb**. The Fifth Street item is explicitly **High Street to Jackson Street**, **9,990 square yards**, total cost **$18,159.85**. It says the earlier improvement, laid in **1903**, was only **18 feet wide in the center**, requiring **4,000 square yards of 3-inch black base** for the new work.

This is public-street work uphill from Main, not a numbered business or a 501/503/505 construction event. The retrospective 1903 paving description is not a 1903 primary record. Neither the earlier 18-foot pavement nor the 1925 40-foot curb-to-curb width is the legal right-of-way width, and neither establishes a 60-to-50-foot narrowing beside the target buildings. Keep it separate from the corrected Sanborn reading that Fifth is labeled 60 feet on both the 1911 and 1925 target sheets.

## Newly precise record-retrieval leads

These statements describe records/work reported in 1925; **their present survival and accessibility have not been established**.

1. **Recorder's duplicate transaction receipts and lien/assessment card index — printed p. 8.** The Recorder says duplicate receipts were being filed for transactions through the departments and lots/parcels with assessments were card-indexed and cross-referenced. Seek the original license receipts/registers for pool halls, cigars and restaurants, plus relevant improvement assessments for original Block 4. Municipal street assessments are not automatically assessor building-improvement dates.
2. **Engineering maps/plans/specifications — printed p. 17.** The department reports establishing permanent filing for maps, plans and other engineering records. Seek Fifth Street High–Jackson project drawings separately from any Main-corner or target-building plans.
3. **Restaurant inspections — printed p. 20.** The report says all restaurants in the City were inspected, but prints no list of restaurant names/addresses. Any surviving 1925 inspection returns could identify the 503 restaurant. This is not confirmation of a surviving inspection ledger or a Kwality attribution.
4. **Commission coverage reconciliation — printed p. 7.** The report gives **12 regular + 72 special meetings = 84 meetings**, while the saved Laserfiche name query returned **81 entries**. These are different counting units: one entry may include multiple meetings or overlapping pages. Do not announce three missing meetings without a date-level reconciliation. The [1925 catalog inventory](1925-city-commission-minute-inventory-2026-09-01.json) remains 81 observed entries, not a claim of annual completeness.
5. **Farr disambiguation — printed p. 4.** The officer roster gives Commissioner **C. P. Farr**, appointed December 14, 1925. This does not identify Edwin F. Farr's pool hall or its dates. Keep official-role references separate from the business-proprietor question.
