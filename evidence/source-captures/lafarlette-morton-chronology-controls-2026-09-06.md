## 1. Independent family recollection — Mitch Young

On **6 September 2026**, project steward Rodney Young asked his brother **Mitch Young** when their grandparents Raye and Floyd LaFarlette were in Morton and whether it was before or after Coquille.

Mitch replied:

> “Before. Mom wasn’t of school age”

and then:

> “As far as I know, they came to Morton from Chicago”

Mitch's “Mom” is **Sheila Wiitanen**, daughter of Raye and Floyd LaFarlette.

Rodney states that he independently remembers the same basic sequence: **Chicago → Morton → Coquille**, with Raye working at the Morton Wheel and the Morton Wheel owners possibly becoming connected to the later Oregon City Wheel before Raye bought it.

Rodney supplied a screenshot of the exchange as **`IMG_5678.jpeg`** in the ChatGPT research session. The prior Morton property-control capture preserves the screenshot provenance and notes the current binary-ingest limitation.

Classification: **FAMILY / ORAL-HISTORY LEAD — TWO FAMILY MEMBERS AGREE; NOT DOCUMENTARY PROOF.** Agreement between Rodney and Mitch increases the importance of testing the lead but does not establish residence, employment, ownership, or a business transfer.

## 2. Sheila's birth date makes “not of school age” a useful chronology control

The published *Oregonian* obituary for **Sheila (LaFarlette) Wiitanen** states that she was born **13 June 1944**.

Source:
https://obits.oregonlive.com/us/obituaries/oregon/name/sheila-wiitanen-obituary?id=23217490

Classification: **SECONDARY BIOGRAPHICAL CONTROL** for Sheila's reported birth date.

This makes Mitch's phrase “Mom wasn’t of school age” more useful than a generic before/after recollection. If the remembered **Chicago → Morton → Coquille** sequence is substantially correct, the Morton interval would have occurred during Sheila's very early childhood.

## 3. Original 1954 Raye profile visually rechecked — direct chronology remains Chicago → Coquille

The original scanned page of the **11 March 1954 Coquille Valley Sentinel**, p. 1, was visually rechecked during this pass.

The profile says, in substance, that:

- Raye first came to Coos County with Floyd and the children on a vacation trip in **1944**;
- they returned to their **home in Chicago**;
- they sold their **rooming-house business**;
- they moved to **Coquille**;
- Raye had worked at the **Coquille Hotel coffee shop** for several years;
- daughters **Carolyn was 15 and Sheila was 9** at the March 1954 publication date.

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088222/1954-03-11/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED** for Raye's contemporary published account and the daughters' stated ages.

The article does **not** mention Morton. That omission is not proof that Morton never occurred, but the detailed contemporary Chicago → Coquille account remains a real conflict with the family-memory sequence.

Classification of the combined chronology: **UNRESOLVED CONFLICT.** Plausible explanations still include a short Morton stop omitted from the profile, temporary residence/work rather than a durable move, reporter compression, or family chronology that shifted in retelling.

### Working chronology consequence

Combining only the chronology controls — **not** treating the oral history as proof — gives the highest-value working Morton search window:

- **not earlier than Sheila's reported birth on 13 June 1944**, if Mitch's “Mom wasn't of school age” memory refers to a period after her birth; and

That would place Sheila at roughly **age 0–3** during the remembered Morton interval.

Classification: **PROBABLE SEARCH BRACKET / WORKING HYPOTHESIS ONLY.** The 1954 contemporary profile still points to a direct Chicago → Coquille move in 1944 and prevents promotion of this bracket to a documented residence chronology.

## 6. Present 145 Main building cannot be projected backward into a 1944–1947 employment story

The separate Morton property-control capture records a public-record-derived lead that the surviving commercial building at **145 Main Ave, Morton** may date to **1950**. That year remains unverified against the original Lewis County historical appraisal card.

If the 1950 date proves correct, it would mean only that Raye could not have worked in the **surviving 145 Main building** during a 1944–1947 Morton stop. It would **not** by itself disprove a Morton Wheel memory.

Possible alternatives remain:

1. an earlier Wheel Cafe operated elsewhere in Morton;
2. an earlier building occupied the same parcel and was replaced about 1950;
3. the assessor-derived year is approximate or wrong;
4. the Wheel business began later and the remembered employment chronology shifted;
5. the family Morton chronology itself is inaccurate.

Classification: **UNVERIFIED BUILD-DATE LEAD / BUILDING-CONTINUITY CAUTION.** Do not state that the Morton Wheel was at 145 Main during the proposed 1944–1947 interval without direct period evidence.
