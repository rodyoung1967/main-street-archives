## Purpose

Test the project-steward recollection that Raye and/or Floyd LaFarlette may have acquired **The Wheel at 503 Main Street** around **1967**, and recheck the archive's existing `S-027` / `E-025` municipal liquor-license anchor before using it as an acquisition date.

The controlling separation rule remains: a liquor application can establish a municipal licensing action but does **not** by itself prove business ownership, real-estate ownership, tenancy, or a sale. The applicant/licensee must be identified before the action can be tied to Raye or Floyd.

### Searchable-text sequence on bound page 453

The page first records a motion by **Conrad**, seconded by **Danielson**, authorizing the mayor to sign a liquor application for:

The vote is recorded as **McElroy, Conrad, Danielson — Ayes; None — Nays**.

Only **after** the preceding meeting is adjourned does the bound page print the heading:

> Oregon City, Oregon June 7, 1967 — REGULAR MEETING

The June 7 roll call lists **Frank McElroy** and **Richard Conrad** as the commissioners present; **G. T. Danielson is not listed**. In the June 7 portion of the minutes, a separate liquor-license application for **Midway Tavern, 1003 7th Street** is approved with **McElroy and Conrad** voting.

## Date-boundary conclusion

Classification: **PRIMARY MUNICIPAL SEARCHABLE TEXT / DATE-BOUNDARY CORRECTION; VISUAL RE-CERTIFICATION RETRIEVAL GAP**.

The Wheel liquor action did **not** occur in the June 7 meeting section represented by the heading that follows it. It belongs to the **preceding Commission meeting** in the bound minutes. The exact date of that preceding meeting has not yet been recovered from the accessible text segment.

## Acquisition significance

The 1967 liquor application is now a **high-value acquisition / license-transfer lead** because it falls in the family-remembered general period and is not merely a business-name advertisement. However, the surviving Commission text does **not** state:

- the applicant's name;
- whether the action was a renewal, new license, change of ownership, change of licensee, or another application type;
- Raye LaFarlette's name;

Accordingly, **1967 is a leading acquisition hypothesis, not a proved purchase date**. Do not write that the LaFarlettes bought The Wheel in 1967 unless the underlying application/license file or another contemporary source supplies the party names and transaction relationship.

## LaFarlette chronology control — late 1969 / early 1970

A separate contemporary legal record confirms that Floyd and Raye were still acting jointly in Oregon business/investment matters after 1967:

- *Bruckman v. Breitenbush Hot Springs, Inc.*, 272 Or. 1, 534 P.2d 971 (Oregon Supreme Court, decided 1 May 1975): https://law.justia.com/cases/oregon/supreme-court/1975/534-p-2d-971-5.html
- the opinion states that in **November 1969** Breitenbush received **$15,000 from Floyd and Raye LaFarlette**;
- December 1969 / January 1970 corporate transactions issued shares and a promissory note to the LaFarlettes;
- the opinion says **Floyd LaFarlette was a member of the Breitenbush board** by mid-January 1970.

Classification: **PRIMARY APPELLATE-COURT RECITATION OF THE LITIGATED RECORD** for the named joint financial activity. This source does **not** connect either LaFarlette to The Wheel and is retained only as an independent chronology/control point consistent with the project-steward clarification that they were still together during the Oregon City period.
