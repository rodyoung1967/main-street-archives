# Wolf & Miller at 503 Main — 31 October 1918

## Status

**PROJECT-STEWARD VISUALLY VERIFIED / DIRECT EXACT-ADDRESS EVIDENCE.**

Rodney Young supplied and visually verified a photograph of the relevant *Oregon City Courier* item from **31 October 1918**. The printed text plainly reads:

> `22.—Wolf & Miller at 503 Main St., not only sell men's clothing and furnishing goods, but also do shoe repairing.`

The number **503** is clearly legible in the supplied image. This must be preserved as direct contemporary evidence and must not be normalized to 505 merely because a separate 10 October 1918 source prints Wolf & Miller at 505 Main.

## Source

- Publication: *Oregon City Courier*
- Issue date: **Thursday, 31 October 1918**
- Item number in printed feature/list: **22**
- Exact printed address: **503 Main St.**
- Business: **Wolf & Miller**
- Activities stated: men's clothing, furnishing goods, and shoe repairing
- Visual verification: **Rodney Young / project steward, 14 September 2026**
- Supplied image filename in conversation: `IMG_7FDE225A-9405-4F23-884F-9A55E311D671.jpeg`

## Conflict with 10 October 1918 evidence

Existing archive evidence from the **10 October 1918 Oregon City Courier, p.4** directly prints **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main**. Therefore the archive now has a genuine contemporary address conflict or short-interval address change:

- **10 Oct 1918 — Wolf & Miller — 505 Main** (direct printed evidence)
- **31 Oct 1918 — Wolf & Miller — 503 Main** (direct printed evidence; steward visually verified)

Do **not** silently correct either source. Possible explanations include a move between adjacent premises, use of both adjacent addresses, a newspaper/address error in one item, or a complex/shared premises arrangement. No explanation is presently proved.

## Significance

This materially affects the 1918–1922 frontage reconstruction. It shows that Wolf & Miller cannot presently be treated as exclusively documented at 505 during late 1918. The exact 503/505 relationship should remain an explicit research question, especially given later S. Wolf ownership evidence in the July 1922 fire cluster.
