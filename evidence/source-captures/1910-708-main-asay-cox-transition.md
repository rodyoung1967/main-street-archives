# 1910 — 708 Main transition from Asay & Cox to G. P. Miller

Status: **direct exact-address evidence; partner identities unresolved**

## 23 Sep 1910 — Asay & Cox at 708 Main

The Oregon City Courier business contest identifies:

**The Pool Hall, Asay & Cox, 708 Main St.**

The accompanying business description says the amusement parlor had five billiard/pool tables and also sold tobacco, candies and soft drinks. The contest text indicates the business had been operating for about four months, suggesting an opening around **May 1910**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3/

## 23 Dec 1910 — G. P. Miller applying for liquor license at 708 Main

Only three months later, the Oregon City Courier published a legal notice from **G. P. Miller** stating that he would apply to the City Council for a license to sell liquor at **his place of business, 708 Main Street**, for three months.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-12-23/ed-1/seq-10/

## Interpretation

The same exact street number was associated with:

- **Sep 1910:** Asay & Cox pool hall;
- **Dec 1910:** G. P. Miller liquor-license application.

This strongly suggests that the Asay & Cox 708 Main phase was short-lived, or that the premises changed operator/use by late 1910. It does **not** yet prove whether Miller purchased the pool hall, whether Asay & Cox dissolved, or whether multiple uses shared the premises.

This finding is relevant to the Frank D. Cox investigation because it weakens any assumption that a Cox partner necessarily remained at 708 Main through 1911–1912. If the Cox in Asay & Cox was Frank D. Cox, the December 1910 Miller notice suggests he may have left 708 before his independently documented Oregon City pool/billiard license of October 1912.

## Building context

The City of Oregon City's later historic inventory treats **706–708 Main** as a historic commercial building/resource, commonly identified in later records as the Delta/Stratton Building. Building-name variations and later remodeling should not be used to infer 1910 business continuity.

## Research-standard warning

The exact-address newspaper evidence is strong. The transaction/succession interpretation is provisional until a sale, lease, license, directory or council record explicitly links the parties.
