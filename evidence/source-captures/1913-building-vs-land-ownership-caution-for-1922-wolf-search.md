# 1913 target-block building-owner vs. assessed-lot-owner caution — implication for the 1922 S. Wolf search

Status: **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE / VISUAL CERTIFICATION INCOMPLETE**

Purpose: preserve an important property-research caution that emerged while tracing the July 1922 fire-origin building owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**. The available 1913 evidence shows that a person described by a contemporary newspaper as owner of a target-frontage **building** need not appear as the assessed owner of the underlying original Block 26 lot. Therefore the 1921/1922 land assessment roll is a high-value source, but it must not be treated as the only possible place where S. Wolf's ownership can appear.

## 1. Chicago Store exact-address continuity at 505 Main

Primary advertisements independently place the **Chicago Store at 505 Main Street** before the December 1913 fire.

Examples already documented in the archive include:

- **31 Mar 1911 Oregon City Courier** — Chicago Store, **505 Main St.**, visually verified;
- **26 Jan 1913 Morning Enterprise** — Chicago Store, **505 Main Street**, recovered in the primary newspaper source.

Related capture:
`evidence/source-captures/1911-chicago-store-wolf-nat-405-to-505.md`

The December 1913 fire article itself says only that the Chicago clothing store was on Main Street south of the Post Office. The `505` identification at the fire date therefore remains a **strong continuity/cross-source inference**, not wording printed in the fire article itself.

## 2. December 1913 fire report calls George Harding the building owner

The **25 Dec 1913 Oregon City Courier** reports that the Chicago clothing store had been gutted by fire and states that **the building was owned by George Harding**.

## 3. October 1913 Block 26 assessment lists different lot owners

A **23 Oct 1913 Morning Enterprise** municipal assessment notice identifies the assessed owners of the Main-side original Block 26 lots as:

- Lot 5 — **Mary E. Barlow and Juliette E. David**;
- Lot 6 — **First National Bank**;
- Lot 7 — **Frank Jaggar**;
- Lot 8 — **Gambrinus Brewing Company**.

The same notice describes the line separating Lots 1–4 from Lots 5–8 running north from Fifth Street through Block 26 toward Sixth Street. Independent 1906 evidence fixes **Lot 8 / Gambrinus / former William Tell Hotel at Sixth & Main**, so Lots 5–8 are the Main-side sequence relevant to the target frontage.

## 4. The mismatch and its meaning

George Harding is **not** one of the four assessed owners listed for original Block 26 Lots 5–8 in the October notice, yet the December newspaper calls him owner of the building containing the Chicago clothing store.

Given the independent exact-address evidence placing the Chicago Store at 505 Main before the fire, the combined evidence strongly warns that at least one of the following could apply:

1. a building or improvement could be owned separately from the underlying platted lot;
2. a newspaper's term `building owner` could reflect an ownership/leasehold/improvement interest not represented by the assessment notice's land-owner name;
3. title or assessment ownership could have changed between the October assessment notice and the December fire;
4. the Chicago Store could have moved within the interval, although no such move is presently documented.

The current evidence does **not** establish which mechanism explains the 1913 mismatch. It is therefore a research-method warning, not proof of a particular legal arrangement.

## 5. Consequence for the July 1922 S. Wolf search

The **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** directly says the fire began in a Main Street building owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**.

The project has correctly prioritized the surviving 1921/1922-era assessment, tax, land, lot, deed and title records. The 1913 comparison now shows that the search should be broader:

### Search the land/property chain

- original Block 26 Lots 5–8 and any later cadastral equivalents;
- 1921 assessment-year records used for 1922 taxation;
- 1922–1925 assessment/tax rolls and indexes;
- 1920/1925 land and lot books;
- deed grantor/grantee indexes;
- OHS MSS 1503 title abstract for the later Block 4 / Lots 3–4 Main/Fifth parcel area.

### Also search building/improvement ownership

- building or improvement assessment schedules if separately maintained;
- fire-insurance records;
- mortgages/chattel mortgages involving the building or business premises;
- leases and lease assignments;
- building permits, repair/reconstruction files and contractor notices;
- newspaper property-sale or building-sale notices that may not describe a full land conveyance;
- city license records that identify the premises owner as well as the operator.

### Interpretation rule

**If a 1921/1922 land assessment for the likely fire parcel does not name S. Wolf, that absence must not be treated as proof that the fire report was wrong or that the parcel has been excluded.** The 1913 evidence provides a concrete target-block reason to test building/improvement ownership separately.

Conversely, an S. Wolf entry in a land/deed record would be highly valuable positive evidence, but it would still need to be mapped to the exact storefront footprint before assigning the ignition room to 501, 503 or 505.

## 6. Relationship to original Block 26 geometry

Official City historic-district guidance says original Oregon City blocks are approximately **200 x 200 feet**, generally divided into **50 x 100-foot lots**. The 1913 boundary description separates Lots 1–4 from Lots 5–8 through Block 26; the independently documented Lot 8 / Sixth & Main anchor strongly supports the working Main-side sequence from Fifth northward:

**Lot 5 → Lot 6 → Lot 7 → Lot 8 (Sixth/Main end).**

This is a **strong geometric reconstruction**, but modern storefront numbers should not be assigned one-for-one to these 50-foot lots without measuring the Sanborn/frontage geometry. Multiple storefronts can occupy one platted lot and later parcels can cross original lot boundaries.

## What is NOT established

Do not claim from this note that:

- George Harding owned the underlying fee title to original Block 26 land in December 1913;
- Harding's Chicago Store building definitely stood at 505 on the exact fire night without the cross-source continuity qualification;
- S. Wolf necessarily held a separate building-only ownership interest in 1922;
- the 1922 land assessment must omit S. Wolf;
- original Lot 5, 6, 7 or 8 has yet been conclusively assigned to modern 501, 503 or 505;
- the 1913 and 1922 ownership arrangements were legally identical.

## Confidence

- Chicago Store documented at 505 Main before Dec 1913: **Very High**
- Dec 1913 primary text says George Harding owned the Chicago Store building: **High / primary OCR-text, visual certification pending**
- Oct 1913 primary text lists Block 26 Lots 5–8 under Barlow/David, First National Bank, Jaggar and Gambrinus rather than Harding: **High / primary OCR-text, existing archive synthesis**
- Building ownership and assessed land ownership therefore cannot safely be assumed identical for the target frontage: **Strong methodological conclusion**
- Same mechanism explains S. Wolf's 1922 ownership: **Unproven; must be tested**
