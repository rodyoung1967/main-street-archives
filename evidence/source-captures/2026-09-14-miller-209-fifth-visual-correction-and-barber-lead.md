# 1922 Miller `209 Fifth` visual correction + barber lead

Date: 2026-09-14 (Pacific)

## Purpose

Resolve the handoff's warning that the previously reported **209 Fifth Street** post-fire address for Gilbert Miller was suspect, and record a new lead on the barber shop named in the 13 July 1922 Harding property-sale article.

## 21 September 1922 Banner-Courier p.5 — visual correction

Source: *The Banner-Courier*, Oregon City, Oregon, Thursday, 21 September 1922, p.5 / image 5.

Public source page: `https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-09-21/ed-1/seq-5/`

The actual scanned page was visually inspected on 14 September 2026. The page layout shows two separate boxed advertisements in the right column:

1. An upper **Gilbert Miller** advertisement headed **“I Have Sold Out all Hardware and have a New Stock of Workingmen's Clothing, Shoes Gloves, Pants, Shirts, Underwear, just in”**. It says his shoe machinery is set up for the same shoe repairing as before the fire and identifies **GILBERT MILLER**, **“Formerly Goldman's Second-Hand Store.”**
2. Immediately below is a distinct **PORTLAND OVERTIRE CO.** advertisement. The line **“209 Fifth Street — Oregon City”** belongs to this lower Portland Overtire advertisement, not to Miller's advertisement.

### Conclusion

**DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED:** the 21 September 1922 page does **not** establish Gilbert Miller at 209 Fifth Street. The earlier 209-Fifth attribution resulted from OCR/text-order concatenation across adjacent advertisements and must not be used as a Miller-location fact.

The separately visually verified 24 August 1922 Banner-Courier advertisement placing **Miller's Shoe Store at 419 Main Street** remains the current verified post-fire Miller address unless another original scan independently establishes an intermediate location.

This is a useful example of why OCR/search text is discovery only: the OCR correctly exposed both Miller and `209 Fifth Street`, but page layout changes which advertiser the address belongs to.

## Barber-shop lead

The 13 July 1922 Harding sale article names, in the property group, **Fifth Street Restaurant; Miller's shoe store; the adjoining barber shop; Red Front Clothing Store**. The current 503 unified timeline already preserves a separate exact-address control from the **1914–1915 Oregon City water ledger: `503½ / Barber Shop`**, alongside `503 / Store`.

A 8 June 1922 Banner-Courier page also includes **O. K. Barber Shop** in a list of Oregon City advertisers/businesses, but the recovered text does not supply its street number. This is therefore an **UNVERIFIED LEAD**, not an identification of Harding's adjoining barber.

### Working implication

The older exact `503½ / Barber Shop` control makes a half-address/internal-bay solution materially plausible: the barber named as adjoining Miller in the July 1922 Harding property description need not occupy 505. That matters because the fire reconstruction independently places the adjoining surviving pool/billiard premises at 505. Do **not** identify the July 1922 barber as 503½ or as O. K. Barber Shop without a 1922 bridge.
