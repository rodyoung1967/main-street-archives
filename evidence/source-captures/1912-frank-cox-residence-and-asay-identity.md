# 1912 Frank D. Cox residence and Asay identity follow-up

Status: **supporting identity/timing evidence; 503 Main not established**

## Frank Cox living in Oregon City immediately before Oct. 1912 pool/billiard license

The **22 August 1912 Independence Monitor** reports that **Frank Cox**, after visiting his parents opposite Independence, left for **Oregon City, where he makes his home**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2012260081/1912-08-22/ed-1/seq-6/

This is useful identity/timing evidence because Oregon City Council minutes later granted **F. D. Cox** a pool/billiard license on **2 October 1912**. The August residence item therefore independently places a Frank Cox in Oregon City less than two months before the municipal license.

It does **not**, by itself, prove that the Independence item and license holder are the same Frank Cox, but the fit is strong when combined with later records identifying Frank D. Cox as an Oregon City billiards/cigar operator and husband of Ollie Jane Hugger Cox.

## Asay & Cox at 708 Main remains an unresolved partnership identity

The **23 September 1910 Oregon City Courier** directly identifies:

**The Pool Hall, Asay & Cox, 708 Main St.**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3/

The same contest description characterizes the business as a large amusement parlor with five billiard/pool tables plus tobacco, candies and soft drinks and indicates it had been operating for only about four months, suggesting an opening around **May 1910**.

The identity of the partners remains unresolved.

### Possible Asay identity — William Asay

Oregon City social news repeatedly places **William Asay and his family in Oregon City in 1910**, including July and September references. This makes William Asay a plausible candidate for the `Asay` partner in Asay & Cox.

Sources:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-07-29/ed-1/seq-7/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-16/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

However, no recovered source yet identifies William Asay as a pool-hall proprietor or explicitly connects him to **Asay & Cox**. Do **not** merge William Asay with the business without a direct license, directory, partnership, advertisement or similar record.

### Cox identity still unresolved

Likewise, no recovered source yet explicitly names **Frank D. Cox** as the Cox partner in Asay & Cox. If future evidence proves that identity, it would establish a Frank Cox pool-hall premises at **708 Main in 1910**, followed by an Oregon City F. D. Cox pool/billiard license in Oct. 1912 and a Frank Cox Main Street pool hall by 1915–1916.

That would be strong evidence of a Cox business history elsewhere on Main and would make any later move to **503 Main** something requiring direct proof rather than assumption.

## Current implication for 503 Main

Nothing in these findings puts Cox at 503 Main.

The 503 sequence remains:
- March 1912 — H. H. Smith announces intended pool-hall license application at 503 Main;
- February/April 1914 — L. Hylton directly at 503 Main;
- 1915–March 1916 — Frank D. Cox has a pool hall on Main Street, exact number unresolved.

The key unresolved question remains whether Cox ever took over Smith's 503 premises or operated at another Main Street address.

## Research-standard warning

These newspaper-derived identity and timing leads do not substitute for direct premises evidence. Do not assign Frank D. Cox or William Asay to a specific business/address unless the source explicitly supports the link.
