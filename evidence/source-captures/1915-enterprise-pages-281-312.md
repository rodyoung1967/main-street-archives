## Fifth Street public market and Portland House control

[September 3 p1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-09-03/ed-1/seq-1.pdf) reports that the market was established the previous year **on Fifth Street between Main and Railroad Avenue**, reopened early in spring 1915 and was later removed after complaints. A location on the hill proved unsatisfactory and the Board of Trade was reported to have effectively abandoned the effort. This follows up the August 13 coverage and distinguishes the market sheds from Foster's southeast-corner Portland House premises. The article supplies no exact removal date or numbered site of the sheds.

## Main Street paving and neighboring controls

[September 10 p1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-09-10/ed-1/seq-1.pdf) reports Worswick superintendent W. V. Wellman's arrest and bindover over the municipal eight-hour law. It says the arrest followed completion of more than half of Worswick's local work. The complaint concerned mixing-plant hours, which the company distinguished from street crews' hours. This independently corroborates the September 9 Courier report and does not establish conviction. The same page reports a separate liquor raid at Fourth and Main, outside the target block.

[September 24 p5](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1915-09-24/ed-1/seq-5.pdf) reports first reading of the Main Street assessment ordinance, a stated total of **$14,861.01** and an **October 6** opportunity for owners to protest. This independently corroborates the September 23 Courier. It is an assessment milestone, separate from formal pavement acceptance or any work to the storefronts.

The September 17 and 24 Sunday-closing and poolroom items describe regulatory context and name no target operator or premises. Holman & Randall and Scripture & May mentions corroborate activity without fixing a new address.
