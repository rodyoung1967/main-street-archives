## Why 1922 is now the strongest documented transition anchor

No inspected 1919–1920 source has yet dated demolition/rebuilding/enlargement of surviving 503 or 505. By contrast, the July–August 1922 record contains a direct, visually verified property/fire/rebuild sequence:

1. **13 July 1922 — Harding property division.** `S-111` / `E-096` reports George A. Harding's Main Street property group sold to S. Wolf and C. Gates, with the Fifth Street Restaurant location allocated to Gates and the remainder to Wolf.
2. **18 July fire / 20 July report.** `S-117` / `E-103` says the fire began in a vacant Main Street storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann; S. Wolf owned the ignition building. The fire destroyed the laundry, garage, Fifth Street Restaurant and Gilbert Miller shoe-repair shop and damaged neighboring premises.
3. **Pool-hall survival clue.** The same visually verified fire report says billiard tables in the pool hall adjoining the former Schieremann room were water-damaged and the roof of that building was only partly burned. This is direct evidence that the adjoining pool-hall premises were damaged but not described as wholly destroyed. Its exact address/operator remains unproved.
4. **17–18 August 1922 — Gates replacement.** `S-112` / `S-113` / `E-097` directly document a new two-story hollow-tile building rising at Fifth/Main on the C. E. Gates property, replacing the fire-destroyed Fifth Street Restaurant building. The ground floor was planned as two storerooms, one already leased as a barber shop, with apartments/sleeping rooms above.

This makes the July–August 1922 event the strongest currently documented candidate for at least part of the physical change visible by 1925. It still does **not** prove that the Gates building equals modern 501 only, 501+503, or any specific modern storefront combination.

## Billiards / pool-hall caution

The session rechecked the 1922 billiards chronology already preserved in `evidence/source-captures/1922-timms-to-leland-little-billiards-lead.md`:

- **Leland & Little existed by 25 February 1922** as an Oregon City cigar-dealer listing;
- **Timms Pool Hall** is separately named in the 8 June merchant roster;
- **O. H. Leland** and **Walter Little** are separately named in that same June roster;
- **Leland and Little — Billiards** is visually verified on 17 August 1922;
- none of those sources supplies an exact 1922 address for Timms or Leland & Little.

Therefore the partly burned pool hall adjoining the Schieremann fire-origin room must remain unnamed/unmapped. Do not convert the later 1925 **505 = billiards/cigars** use into proof that the July 1922 pool hall was 505 until an exact-address source is recovered.

## Gilbert Miller post-fire movement

The existing visually verified fire/property capture also preserves a useful negative continuity warning: Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop was destroyed in the July fire, and a **21 September 1922 Banner-Courier** advertisement places his reopened operation at **209 Fifth Street**. This demonstrates a post-fire relocation for Miller and cautions against assuming that each burned business immediately returned to the same rebuilt footprint.
