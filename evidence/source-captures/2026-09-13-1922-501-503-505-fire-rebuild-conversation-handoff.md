# 1922 Fifth/Main fire and rebuilding — conversation handoff — 13 September 2026

## Purpose

Preserve the conclusions and hypotheses developed in the 13 September 2026 conversation about the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire and the subsequent physical history of 501, 503, and 505 Main Street, Oregon City.

This note is intentionally conservative. It distinguishes contemporary documentary evidence from steward photo interpretation and from working hypotheses. It does **not** convert uncertain reconstruction sequences into proven facts.

## Core question

The project steward observed an apparent conflict:

- contemporary reporting described only relatively limited damage to the adjoining 503/505-side premises after the July 1922 fire;
- a November 1922 Fifth/Main photograph appears, in the steward's reading, to show 501 present, an open/empty space at 503, and a substantially different/rebuilt-looking 505;
- this raised the question of whether 503 was demolished after initially being described as only damaged, and whether 505 had already been rebuilt or substantially reconstructed before the November photograph.

## Contemporary fire evidence carried forward

The controlling 1922 fire account says the fire began in a vacant Wolf-owned storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor/cleaning-and-dyeing business.

The report distinguishes several outcomes rather than saying the whole frontage was destroyed.

Most importantly for the 505-side pool-hall structure, the adjoining pool hall was described as surviving the fire but suffering substantial damage:

- billiard tables were damaged by water;
- the roof was described as partly burned off.

Therefore the July 1922 article does **not** establish total destruction of the adjoining pool-hall building. Any later claim that 505 was completely rebuilt because of the fire must remain a hypothesis unless supported by additional evidence.

## Post-fire business continuity clue

Existing 1922 newspaper work also carried forward the August 1922 Leland & Little billiards advertisement/business listing.

This is significant because it suggests that the billiards operation either resumed quickly after the July fire or continued from surviving/repaired premises. It is compatible with substantial repair of a damaged pool-hall building; it does not by itself prove a completely new building.

## Gates replacement-building evidence

Existing project research records a clearly documented post-fire construction project by C. E. Gates after the July fire.

The reported project was a two-story hollow-tile building at Fifth and Main replacing the premises formerly occupied by the Fifth Street Restaurant. The design included two ground-floor storerooms, and occupancy was expected around October 1, 1922.

This construction program is chronologically compatible with a visibly changed Fifth/Main frontage by the November 1922 bridge photograph.

However, the exact modern-address mapping of the Gates replacement project must not be assumed without parcel/address evidence. In particular, the November photograph interpretation raises doubt that 501 and 503 were simply the two storefronts of one Gates building.

## November 1922 photograph — steward interpretation

For the November 1922 Fifth/Main bridge photograph context, the current steward interpretation is:

- the white building at left is 501 Main;
- immediately to its right, the 503 position appears open/empty rather than occupied by a normal storefront building;
- farther right, the largely shadowed building at 505 appears present and may already represent the later/rebuilt-generation structure.

This is a **photo interpretation**, not a source-proven demolition or construction date. It should be tested against original full-resolution imagery, parcel geometry, deeds/tax records, and later Sanborn mapping.

## Working model for 505 Main

Two competing models remain viable.

### Model A — substantial post-fire reconstruction

The pre-fire pool-hall building survived the July fire structurally but was sufficiently damaged — including the partly burned roof and water damage — that it underwent major repair or substantial reconstruction between July 18 and November 1922.

This model is compatible with the newspaper description because 'roof partly burned off' does not imply total structural loss.

### Model B — newer-generation 505 already existed before the fire

The building visible at 505 in November may already have existed before July 1922 and may simply have sustained roof/fire/water damage that was repaired quickly.

This model is also compatible with the fire report and with later historic-resource dating that places 505 broadly around c.1920, although that later date remains an approximate inventory estimate rather than proof of an exact construction year.

### Current conclusion for 505

Do **not** say that the July 1922 fire proved construction of the present 505 building.

Use conservative language instead:

> 505 may already have been the newer-generation structure by July 1922, or it may have undergone substantial reconstruction immediately after the July fire. The contemporary fire report establishes only partial roof/fire/water damage to the adjoining pool hall, not total destruction.

## Working model for 503 Main

The more significant new hypothesis concerns 503.

If the November 1922 photograph truly shows an open/empty space at 503, then the surviving/present 503 building cannot simply be assumed to be the same structure described by later inventory material as approximately c.1919.

A plausible sequence becomes:

1. an earlier 503-side structure existed before the July 1922 fire;
2. the fire and/or subsequent demolition/redevelopment removed that structure despite the initial contemporary damage description being less severe;
3. the 503 position appears open in the November 1922 photograph;
4. a later replacement 503 building was erected after November 1922;
5. by the 1925 Sanborn, 503 is again shown as a separately numbered occupied structure, then used as a restaurant.

### Current conclusion for 503

Treat this as a **high-value working hypothesis**, not a proven construction date:

> If the November 1922 photo interpretation is correct, the surviving/replacement 503 building may date from after November 1922 and before the 1925 Sanborn, rather than literally from c.1919.

This hypothesis could materially revise the building chronology and should be tested aggressively.

## Structural controls

Continue to use the following only as controls, not as proof of exact dates:

- the November 1922 Fifth/Main bridge photograph;
- the 1925 Sanborn showing 503 as a separate occupied address;
- later historic-resource inventory construction estimates;
- post-1922 photos showing later storefront geometry.

## Evidence discipline

Keep the following separate in all future conclusions:

- business operation;
- business ownership;
- tenancy;
- property ownership;
- parcel identity;
- street address;
- physical-building continuity;
- repair versus reconstruction versus total replacement.

Do not infer that same address means same building.

Do not infer demolition solely because a business disappeared.

Do not infer total rebuilding from fire damage unless the source explicitly supports it.

## Recommended next action

Resume the 1922 annual workstream with a targeted post-fire rebuild search, beginning with every visually available newspaper issue from the July 1922 fire through the end of 1922, then extending into 1923–1924 if the reconstruction event is not found.

The immediate objective is to determine whether a source can establish:

1. demolition of the 503 structure after the July fire;
2. construction date of the replacement 503 building;
3. whether 505 was already the newer-generation building before July 1922 or was substantially rebuilt afterward;
4. the precise parcel/address relationship of the Gates hollow-tile replacement building.

Until one of those is sourced, preserve the November-photo reading and the 503/505 rebuild models as unresolved but high-priority hypotheses.
