# 1919-1920 construction search checkpoint: 501, 503, and 505 Main

Status: **paused checkpoint; research not complete**  
Checkpoint date: 2026-08-31

## Question

Were the narrow storefronts shown at 501, 503, and 505 Main on the 1911 Sanborn map demolished, enlarged, or reconstructed during 1919-1920? The city inventory's approximate construction dates make this period a priority, but those dates are not themselves proof of a construction event.

## Map correction and visually established facts

- The downtown 501-505 Main block is on **1911 Sanborn Sheet 16**, preserved as `maps/sanborn/originals/1911-16 (5th-Main).pdf`.
- The previously preserved 1911 Sheet 22 is a Canemah sheet and is not the target-block map. It remains useful as an authentic geographic-control sheet and should not be deleted.
- Visual comparison of 1911 Sheet 16 with 1925 Sheet 6 shows a changed storefront/footprint arrangement at 501-505 Main. The maps support physical change by 1925, but do not date each demolition or replacement.
- Fifth Street is labeled **60 feet wide on both the 1911 and 1925 target sheets**. The maps do not support a 60-to-50-foot narrowing of Fifth Street. What changed is the storefront/lot configuration.

## Leads preserved for visual verification

The following newspaper page PDFs are preserved unchanged in `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/`. Their OCR supplied the leads below. The relevant printed passages still require direct scan verification before they are entered as proven evidence elsewhere in the archive.

1. **February 13, 1920, page 5** (`1920-02-13-page-5.pdf`)
   - OCR reports that Clem Dollar was proprietor of a pool hall on Main between Fifth and Sixth streets.
   - If visually confirmed, this is block-level occupancy evidence, not an exact-number or construction record.

2. **June 4, 1920, page 3** (`1920-06-04-page-3.pdf`)
   - OCR reports that the city council granted A. Leland's application to operate a pool hall at what appears to be **505 1/2 Main Street** and to erect an electric street sign.
   - This is the strongest current lead. If visually confirmed, it establishes active premises at 505/505 1/2 by June 1920, but does not by itself prove that the building was newly constructed in 1920.
   - Do not merge A. Leland with O. H. Leland without independent identity evidence.

3. **December 3, 1920, page 1** (`1920-12-03-page-1.pdf`)
   - OCR refers to a pool hall between Fifth and Sixth streets on Main.
   - If visually confirmed, this is block-level continuity context only.

## Current interpretation

- The search has not yet found a 1919-1920 newspaper notice explicitly saying that 503 or 505 was demolished, rebuilt, enlarged, or replaced.
- The June 1920 lead may close part of the 505 occupancy gap and establishes a terminus by which a usable 505/505 1/2 premises existed, if the scan confirms the OCR.
- No inference should yet be made that Clem Dollar and A. Leland operated the same pool hall or that one succeeded the other.
- No direct 1919-1920 occupant for 503 has yet been established in this pass.
