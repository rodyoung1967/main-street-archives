## Proven historical custody and creation trail

Existing primary evidence already establishes the following:

- `E-061` / `S-075`: on 6 July 1906 contemporary reporting said Mayor E. G. Caufield had approved a street-numbering ordinance and that a **plat designating the correct numbering** had been filed with City Recorder W. A. Dimick. Property owners could consult the plat to obtain the **true number** of a home or place of business.
- The 13 July 1906 council report says Councilman Rands was **drawing maps of the city for numbering houses** and that the numbering work exposed duplicate street names. Existing project evidence also records a municipal payment to **R. B. McAlpin for “numbering plats.”** Preserve the exact Rands/McAlpin identity wording until the relevant council page or original payment record is visually certified.
- `E-061` / `S-075`: on 17 August 1906 the newspaper said **the plat for numbering houses was completed and at City Recorder Dimick’s office**, and residents were urged to obtain their assigned numbers there.
- `E-105` / `S-119`: the visually verified 3 October 1913 City Recorder notice says a **plat and plan showing the proper street number for all property in Oregon City** remained on file with the Recorder and quotes Section 5 of Ordinance No. 357.

These sources prove that an official numbering plat/plan existed, was completed, and remained in City Recorder custody from at least August 1906 through October 1913. They do not prove where the record is physically or electronically stored today.

### 2. Oregon City Public Library council/commission proceedings index — concrete 1906 locator

Official library page:

- https://www.orcity.org/490/Microfilm-Scanning-System

The Oregon City Public Library lists an **“Oregon City Council and Commission Proceeding — Index: August 6, 1850 to June 26, 1978.”** This is a concrete manual route to the 1906 council proceedings.

The index should be checked for entries around:

- 25 May 1906 — free-delivery / house-numbering request
- late June–early July 1906 — numbering ordinance passage/approval and plat filing
- 6 July 1906 — ordinance/plat implementation period

- July–August 1906 — street-name revisions / duplicate-name work
- 17 August 1906 — completed numbering plat in Recorder office

The library listing proves that the proceedings **index** exists. It does **not** establish that the numbering plat itself was microfilmed.

### 3. University of Oregon — Harold A. Rands papers, Ax 089 — high-value parallel lead

Official institutional finding aid:

- https://archiveswest.orbiscascade.org/ark:/80444/xv69735

The **Harold A. Rands papers, 1906–1941**, collection **Ax 089**, is held by University of Oregon Libraries Special Collections and University Archives. The finding aid describes 8.5 linear feet in 17 containers and says the collection includes field diaries, correspondence, reports, project files and photographs. Rands’ field diaries begin in **1906** and span his career with few lapses.

This is potentially relevant because the July 1906 Oregon City reporting says **Councilman Rands** was drawing city maps for the house-numbering work. The finding aid does **not** specifically list an Oregon City numbering plat, so the collection is a strong archival lead rather than proof that the final map survives there.

Important identity caution: Oregon City sources also identify **Ernest P. Rands** as a councilman, county surveyor and former city engineer, while Harold A. Rands was a separate engineer/surveyor and his archival papers begin in 1906. Do not silently merge Harold and Ernest or attribute the 1906 numbering maps to Harold solely because his collection survives. The original council proceedings/payment records need to resolve which Rands performed each part of the work.
