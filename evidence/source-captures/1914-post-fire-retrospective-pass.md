# 1914 Post-Fire Retrospective Newspaper Pass

Status: **active retrospective exhaustive pass**

Purpose: re-review the full calendar year immediately following the December 1913 Chicago Store fire, with special attention to evidence bearing on **505 Main**, the continued existence or non-existence of the building, rebuilding/remodeling, vacancy, tenancy, ownership, and neighboring 503/507 conditions.

## Newspapers that must be reviewed page-by-page

Historic Oregon Newspapers confirms two Oregon City weeklies available for 1914:

- **Oregon City Courier** (`sn00063698`)
- **Oregon City Enterprise** (`sn00063700`)

This retrospective pass corrects an earlier scope gap: the first 1914 routine pass did not explicitly require every available page of both titles. The year therefore remains historically usable for its direct findings, but the post-fire question is being re-opened for exhaustive page coverage.

The daily **Morning Enterprise** ended in December 1913 and is not a 1914 title.

## Search focus

Review every page for:

- Chicago Store / Chicago clothing store;
- **505 Main** and adjacent **503 / 507 / 509 / 511 Main**;
- George A. Harding as building owner / landlord as distinct from Harding's pharmacy;
- wording such as burned, destroyed, gutted, repaired, rebuilt, remodeled, removed, razed, vacant, empty, ruins, site, lot, new building, temporary quarters, moved, reopened, lease, rent, sale, insurance adjustment, permit;
- photographs or advertisements whose absence/presence or address wording helps establish whether the 505 structure continued to exist;
- Fifth/Main and Post Office relative-location wording;
- any replacement tenant or business after the fire.

This means any prior 1914 conclusion based only on Courier/indexed searches is incomplete for the project's exhaustive-standard requirement.

### Separate November 1914 fire elsewhere on Main

The *Oregon City Courier*, 26 November 1914, p. 5, reports a gasoline-stove fire in the Belts & Colebank cleaning/pressing establishment on **upper Main Street**. It says the wooden building, owned by **E. D. Kelley**, was gutted. This is **not presently tied to 505 Main** and must not be conflated with the December 1913 Chicago Store fire.

Source:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-11-26/ed-1/seq-5/

### 1914 direct address anchors remain valid

The retrospective does not withdraw the direct 1914 findings already recorded:

- **503 Main — L. Hylton**, 12 February 1914 (*Courier*)
- **507 Main — G. H. Young**, 16 April 1914 (*Courier*)
- **511 Main — George A. Harding pharmacy**, direct 1914 evidence

The retrospective question is narrower: what happened physically and commercially at **505 Main after the December 1913 fire**, and is there any evidence that the building ceased to exist, was substantially rebuilt, or stood vacant?

## Interpretation rule

Do not infer demolition merely because a business disappears from advertisements. Positive evidence is required for building destruction/removal/rebuilding. Conversely, evidence that adjacent businesses continue at stable addresses does not by itself prove the same 505 structure survived.

## Completion condition

This retrospective pass is not complete until every available page of both 1914 Oregon City newspaper titles has been reviewed or logged as inaccessible, with relevant links captured and original PDF/JP2 links preserved where available.
