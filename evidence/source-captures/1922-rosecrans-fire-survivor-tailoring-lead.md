# 1922 Fifth/Main fire — Rosecrans fire-survivor / tailoring lead

Status: **high-value identity/occupation lead; same-person link not yet proven**

Purpose: preserve a potentially important connection between the Rosecrans family that escaped from living quarters in the July 1922 Fifth/Main fire and a September 1922 Portland tailoring partnership involving **C. A. Rosecrans**. This may eventually help identify the residential/business premises around Alex Schieremann's former tailor shop, but the identity and address bridge remain unproven.

## 20 July 1922 — Rosecrans family escapes Fifth/Main fire

The **Banner-Courier, 20 Jul 1922, p. 1** reports that the July 18 fire began in a vacant Main Street store room formerly occupied by **Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop** and owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**.

The same report states that **Mr. and Mrs. Rosecrans and son Everett** escaped down a stairway with the flames close behind them and left nearly all their possessions. The article then says that other people were driven from living quarters over the burned business places.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1/

The fire article does not give Mr. Rosecrans's first name, occupation, or exact address, and it does not state which ground-floor business was directly beneath the Rosecrans living quarters.

## Oregon City cemetery evidence — Charles A., Susan, and Everett Rosecrans

Oregon City Mount View Cemetery records list, together in **Section/Unit A, Block 162**:

- **Susan Rosecrans** — died 6 Feb 1924, age 54, grave/crypt 1;
- **Charles A. Rosecrans** — died 9 Jul 1952, age 80, grave/crypt 2;
- **Everett Rosecrans** — died 10 Aug 1969, age 68, grave/crypt 3.

City-derived cemetery listing:
https://www.interment.net/data/us/or/clackamas/mt-view-cemetery/records-p-r.htm

The shared block, adjacent grave numbering, names, and ages make **Charles A. + Susan + Everett** a strong candidate identification for the `Mr. and Mrs. Rosecrans and son Everett` family in the 1922 fire report. This is strong circumstantial family evidence, not yet a contemporary household record.

## 22 September 1922 — C. A. Rosecrans in Portland tailoring partnership

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon Daily Journal, 22 Sep 1922, p. 18** was visually inspected.

A legal notice states that:

- **C. A. Rosecrans** and **Edward Ulk** had been doing business in Portland under the style **Rose City Tailoring Co.**;
- they dissolved the partnership by mutual consent on **19 Sep 1922**;
- Ulk and **D. Mashin** would assume the liabilities and continue the business.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn85042444/1922-09-22/ed-1/seq-18.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

The notice proves that a **C. A. Rosecrans was a Portland tailoring-business partner only about two months after the Oregon City fire**.

## Later Portland address lead

A **1931 Directory of Former Ottawans** lists:

- **Rosecrans, Charles A. — 730 Everett Street, Portland, Oregon**.

Source:
https://libsysdigi.library.illinois.edu/oca/Books2008-06/lasallecountycen00otta/lasallecountycen00otta.pdf

This establishes a later Charles A. Rosecrans at a Portland address. It is compatible with the September 1922 Portland business notice, but does not by itself prove that the 1931 man is the same C. A. Rosecrans or the Oregon City fire survivor.

## Why this matters for Schieremann / fire geometry

The coincidence is potentially significant:

1. the fire began in a **former tailor shop**;
2. a **Rosecrans family lived in the fire-affected business/residential cluster** and escaped down a stairway;
3. a **C. A. Rosecrans** was engaged in the tailoring business in Portland only two months later;
4. Oregon City cemetery records strongly suggest the fire-survivor household may have been **Charles A., Susan, and Everett Rosecrans**.

If the fire-survivor Charles A. Rosecrans can be proven to be the Rose City Tailoring Co. partner, that would create an important tailoring-network connection within the fire cluster. It could mean Rosecrans was personally involved in tailoring, knew Schieremann through the trade, occupied rooms associated with a tailoring premises, or simply worked in Portland while residing in Oregon City.

**None of those spatial/business interpretations is currently proven.**

In particular, do not state that:

- Charles A. Rosecrans was Alex Schieremann's successor or partner;
- Rosecrans operated the former Schieremann shop;
- the Rosecrans family lived directly above Schieremann's store room;
- the Rose City Tailoring Co. had an Oregon City branch;
- the Portland C. A. Rosecrans is definitely the Oregon City fire survivor.
