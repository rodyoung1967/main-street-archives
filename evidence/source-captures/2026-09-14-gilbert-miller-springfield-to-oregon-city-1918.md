# Gilbert Miller — Springfield to Oregon City bridge, 1918

Date: 2026-09-14 (Pacific)

## Purpose

Test the identity and continuity behind the October 1918 `Wolf & Miller, 505 Main` Oregon City evidence, especially whether Gilbert Miller can be connected to the earlier Springfield Wolf & Miller business and whether the unnamed Wolf partner can yet be identified.

## Springfield Wolf & Miller — direct context

Historic Oregon Newspapers contains repeated Springfield, Lane County evidence for a `Wolf & Miller` business before the Oregon City appearance. The Springfield business sold/repaired shoes and harness and also carried clothing/furnishings. Examples recovered in this pass include:

- 1915–1917 Springfield/Lane County newspaper references to Wolf & Miller shoe/harness work and merchandise.
- 16 June 1917 Eugene Daily Guard Springfield report: the `Wolf and Miller repair shop` was remodeling show windows and **Mr. Miller** was preparing a dry-goods display.
- 28 March 1918 Springfield News: `Miss Lila Miller` would clerk in the Wolf & Miller store during its coming sale; the same page advertises shoe repairing by Wolf & Miller.
- 27 June and 4 July 1918 Springfield News: Wolf & Miller advertised that it was **closing out its entire stock**.

These are direct newspaper references to the Springfield firm. They do not name the Wolf partner.

## 22 August 1918 Springfield News — high-value migration bridge

A Springfield News report dated 22 August 1918 states that **Gilbert Miller left the preceding Saturday for Oregon City**, where he would join his family, which had moved there some time earlier. The report explicitly says **Miller had remained in Springfield to close out his business**.

This is a high-value direct chronological bridge:

1. Gilbert Miller was associated with the Springfield Wolf & Miller business.
2. Wolf & Miller was closing out its Springfield stock in June–July 1918.
3. Gilbert Miller stayed behind in Springfield to close his business.
4. By mid-August 1918 he left Springfield for **Oregon City** to rejoin his family.

## 10 October 1918 Oregon City Courier — 505 Main

The Oregon City Courier's `In Oregon City` knowledge-competition page directly identifies:

- **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main**;
- shoes for the whole family;
- men's clothing and furnishing goods;
- a modern shoe-repair department;
- a partnership-dissolution sale;
- after the sale, **one partner was going to Idaho and the other would restock and continue the business**.

This page is explicitly an Oregon City business/address feature and therefore establishes Wolf & Miller at **505 Main, Oregon City** by 10 October 1918.

## Integrated conclusion

The August migration report materially strengthens the identification of **Gilbert Miller as the Miller in the October 1918 Oregon City Wolf & Miller firm at 505 Main**. The timing and business profile align closely: Springfield Wolf & Miller closes out; Gilbert Miller leaves Springfield for Oregon City; less than two months later Wolf & Miller is directly listed at 505 Main in Oregon City selling shoes/clothing and doing shoe repair.

Evidence state for the same Gilbert Miller participating in both firms: **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**. A source explicitly saying `Gilbert Miller of Wolf & Miller, 505 Main, Oregon City` would still be preferable for DOCUMENTED/DIRECT status.

Do **not** yet state that the entire Springfield partnership simply relocated intact. The October Oregon City article itself says the partnership is dissolving and one partner will go to Idaho. The identity and movements of the Wolf partner remain unresolved.

## Wolf identity remains unresolved

This pass did **not** recover a reliable source naming the Wolf partner in the Springfield firm or the October 1918 Oregon City partnership. Therefore:

- do not identify the partner as S. Wolf;
- do not identify the partner as L. Wolf;
- do not infer that the partner was a relative of Gilbert Miller's wife solely from surname;
- preserve the later July 1922 **S. Wolf / L. Wolf** conflict separately.

A secondary genealogy identifies Gilbert Miller's first wife as **Clara Rose Wolf**, daughter of Henry and Elizabeth (Conrad) Wolf, but that is only a family-network lead and does not identify the business partner.

## O. K. Barber Shop caution

Searches also recovered an **O. K. Barber Shop in Springfield** in 1916–1918, associated there with **M. V. / Martin V. Endicott**. This is a useful negative-control warning because an O. K. Barber Shop is also named in the 8 June 1922 Oregon City Banner-Courier business list. The shared generic trade name does **not** establish continuity or identity between the Springfield and Oregon City shops.

Therefore the July 1922 Harding-property barber remains unidentified. The older Oregon City water-ledger `503½ / Barber Shop` control remains relevant spatial evidence but is not yet a 1922 identity bridge.
