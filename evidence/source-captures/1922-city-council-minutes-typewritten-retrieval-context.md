# 1922 Oregon City council minutes — typewritten-record retrieval context

Status: **VERIFIED RETRIEVAL CONTEXT / TARGET MINUTES NOT YET RETRIEVED**

Purpose: narrow the search for the **1 February 1922 Oregon City council meeting** that acted on Sunday opening of poolrooms. The target minutes themselves have not yet been recovered or visually inspected.

## Verified record-format transition

In a 2018 Oregon Digital Newspaper Program interview, Oregon City historian **Karin D. Morey** stated that she had transcribed the city's handwritten City Council minutes from **1850 through 1911**, **when the city converted to typewritten minutes**. Morey also stated that those transcriptions had been shared with the Oregon City Recorder and that she was researching the city's municipal history through **1923**.

Source:
https://odnp.uoregon.edu/2018/03/26/oregon-city-author-shares-research-about-the-history-of-the-city/

Verification state: **WEB-PAGE TEXT VERIFIED**. This is retrieval metadata/context, not visual verification of the 1922 minutes.

### Implication

The **1 February 1922** council minutes should be sought as part of the city's **post-1911 typewritten minute record**, not as a continuation of the older handwritten volumes.

This is useful because the publicly indexed HPRM result already recovered for **City Council Minutes Volume 7** covers **11 August 1909 through 28 June 1911** and identifies itself as a handwritten-minute volume. That volume ends essentially at the transition Morey describes.

HPRM source for Volume 7:
https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/6375928/File/document

## Official Oregon City online archive status

Oregon City's official **Historic Documents and Research** page says the City possesses historic documents dating to the 1840s and provides selected digitized records online. The currently indexed public page includes selected City Commission/Council minutes and other historic series, but it does **not** expose a direct link to the **1 February 1922** minutes.

Source:
https://www.orcity.org/1710/Historic-Documents-and-Research

Verification state: **OFFICIAL WEB-PAGE TEXT VERIFIED**.

## Relationship to newspaper evidence

The existing project evidence establishes from the **2 February 1922 Banner-Courier** that the council had voted the previous night to permit poolrooms to open Sundays after Mayor Shannon had ordered them closed during the preceding month.

That newspaper item proves the council action, but it does not identify the licensed poolrooms or their premises. The underlying typewritten minutes/license applications remain a high-priority address source because a premises-specific entry could identify the 1922 operator at or near **505 Main**.

## Current conclusion

- The target **1 February 1922** minutes remain a **RETRIEVAL GAP**.
- The search can now be narrowed to the **post-June-1911 typewritten council-minute sequence**.
- Do **not** infer any Timms, Leland & Little, Wheeler, or 505 Main address from the newspaper council-action report alone.
