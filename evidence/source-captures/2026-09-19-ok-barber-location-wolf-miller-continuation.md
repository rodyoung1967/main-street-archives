## New O.K. Barber Shop location lead

A secondary historical-marker record published by the Historical Marker Database records an **“OK Barber Shop”** marker in Oregon City. The marker text says the shop was **built by Ed Johnson as a full-service barber shop with four chairs and bathing facilities**, retained a basement boiler for hot water, and was converted to a restaurant in 1986. The marker location is described as **Main Street near 7th Street**.

Public record:
- Historical Marker Database, “OK Barber Shop,” marker 8746: https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=8746
- Oregon City marker-results page likewise lists “OK Barber Shop” and repeats the Ed Johnson/full-service-barber wording.

### Evidence state

**SECONDARY LOCATION/IDENTITY LEAD — NOT YET PRIMARY-SOURCE VERIFIED.**

This is potentially important because the June 8, 1922 *Banner-Courier* business list independently names an **O. K. Barber Shop** in Oregon City. If the marker refers to the same 1922 business and if its Main-near-7th location can be fixed from contemporary evidence, that could weigh against identifying O.K. Barber Shop with the separate Harding-property barber adjoining Gilbert Miller near Fifth/Main.

Do **not** make that exclusion yet. The marker does not provide a construction date or a 1922 street number, and continuity of the trade name has not been established. A contemporary directory, advertisement, city license, Sanborn, water record, or property record is required.

## Wolf & Miller continuation

Fresh public searching again recovered the 10 October 1918 *Oregon City Courier* notice at 505 Main: one partner would go to Idaho and the other would restock and continue. Searches using Gilbert Miller, S./Samuel/Sam Wolf, L./Louis Wolf, Oregon City, Springfield, Portland, Boise, Lewiston, Idaho, clothing, shoes, and shoe repair did **not** recover a source naming the departing partner.

A 1920 Portland Polk directory remains consistent with the already-known candidate controls: **Saml Wolf (Lena), clothing, 33 3d N**; **Simon Wolf (Carolyn), Wonder Millinery**; and **Victor Wolf (Alice), clerk, residence Oregon City**. This is not new identity proof and does not justify merging any candidate with the Wolf & Miller partner.

A January 16, 1919 *Oregon Daily Journal* want ad says Oregon City shoemaker **Wm. Feitelson** sought an all-around shoemaker for steady work. This does not identify Wolf & Miller or Gilbert Miller and is retained only as a control against assuming every Oregon City shoe-repair reference belongs to Miller.

## Current conclusion

The strongest genuinely new lead from this pass is the historical-marker route that may permit the O.K. Barber Shop to be geographically separated from the Fifth/Main Harding-property barber once a contemporary address is recovered.
