## Page 1 / page 10 merchant roster

The Buyers' Carnival article says approximately seventy firms backed the Commercial Club retail-extension event. The roster includes, among others:

- Walter Little;
- Pantorium;
- Timms Pool Hall;
- Fifth Street Restaurant;
- Gilbert Miller;
- O. H. Leland;
- C. G. Miller Company;
- S. Wolf.

The roster is a comma-separated list of separate participants. In particular, **A. D. Wheeler and Co.** and **Fifth Street Restaurant** are separate entries; they must not be combined into one business.

The roster does not give exact street addresses for Timms, Leland, Little, Pantorium, S. Wolf, Fifth Street Restaurant, or Gilbert Miller.

## Page 7 — S. F. Scripture at 108 Fifth

The most important new exact-address result from the issue is a business card for:

- **S. F. Scripture**;
- general blacksmithing and horseshoeing;
- automobile and truck springs repaired;
- **108 - 5th St. between Main and Water St.**

This places Scripture at 108 Fifth on 8 June 1922, only about six weeks before the July fire, and substantially weakens the old hypothesis that May & Washburn had simply inherited the former Scripture & May premises by 1922.

## Page 7 — Miller's Shoe Store

A separate display advertisement for **Miller's Shoe Store** offers men's and boys' shoes, gloves, laces, shoe polish, oils and greases and advertises **expert shoe repairing**. Its location wording is:

- **Main Street**;
- **Opposite Post Office**.

The advertisement does **not** print a proprietor's first name.

The same complete issue separately contains the Buyers' Carnival participant **Gilbert Miller** and the distinct **C. G. Miller Company**. Therefore the archive must not automatically equate `Miller's Shoe Store` with either Miller entity merely from surname and trade type.

Gilbert Miller's known shoe-repair trade makes the ad highly relevant, but identity requires an explicit bridge.

## Page 3 — C. G. Miller Company is an automobile business

Page 3 contains a large advertisement headed **C. G. MILLER CO. — EVERY NEED OF AN AUTOMOBILE**.

This visually confirms that C. G. Miller Company in the merchant roster is an automobile-related concern, not a shoe-repair business. It should remain distinct from Gilbert Miller and from the unidentified proprietor of the `Miller's Shoe Store` ad unless direct evidence links them.

## Page 10 — Dr. Freeze

The visually inspected Dr. Freeze display ad reads **505½ Main St.** The typographic half character is easily flattened by OCR into `5052`.

This remains evidence for an upstairs/half-number suite at the 505 address structure and does not identify the ground-floor occupant.

## Issue-wide negative result, narrowly stated

Across the actual 14 scanned pages, this visual review did **not locate an exact-address display ad or local item in this issue** for:

- Timms Pool Hall;
- Leland & Little / O. H. Leland;
- Walter Little in a billiards context;
- Pantorium;
- S. Wolf;
- Fifth Street Restaurant;
- May & Washburn;
- Alex Schieremann.

This statement applies **only to the 8 June 1922 issue**. It is not evidence that those businesses lacked addresses, were absent, vacant, closed, or located elsewhere. Other issues, directories, licenses, tax records, and municipal records remain open research paths.

## Other useful address context from the issue

The visual pass also shows numerous businesses advertising exact or intersection addresses, confirming that the special issue is a useful address-reconstruction source. Examples include businesses on Main, Fifth, and other downtown streets. This is why the absence of a target's address in its merchant-roster entry cannot be expanded into a broader historical conclusion.

## Research consequence

The issue has now been exhausted visually for the immediate fire-address targets. Further progress on Timms/Leland/S. Wolf should shift toward:

1. other 1921–1923 newspaper issues;
2. city council pool-hall ordinance/license records;
3. the 1921–1922 business directory;
4. tax/deed/land-lot records;
5. OHS MSS 1503 Block 4 Lots 3 & 4 title abstract.

The new Scripture result also makes historical Fifth Street number geometry a priority: **108 Fifth and Oregon City Laundry's known 109 Fifth address must not be described as physically adjacent until the odd/even address sides are mapped from primary evidence.**
