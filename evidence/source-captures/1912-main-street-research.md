# 1912 Main Street Research — Consolidated Source Capture

Year status: **IN PROGRESS**
Research scope: Oregon City Main Street, especially the Fifth–Sixth block and later-numbered 501 / 503 / 505 / 507 Main.  
Method: targeted address/business/construction searches plus issue/page-level review of accessible/indexed *Oregon City Courier* material, with cross-checks in the *Morning Enterprise* and relevant public records.  
Standing construction watch: `RL-024`.

## High-confidence 1912 findings

### 5 January — visually verified Main Street address ladder

The *Oregon City Courier* business page provides an unusually dense address sequence. The original page image was visually inspected because OCR misreads several numbers.

Canonical page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-01-05/ed-1/seq-6.pdf

Visually verified readings:

- **H. N. Smith — Pool Room, Cigars and Tobacco — 503 Main Street**. OCR incorrectly reads `603`.
- **G. H. Young — The Curio Man — 507 Main Street**.
- **H. P. Brightbill — 509 Main St.** OCR incorrectly reads `609`.
- **Geo. A. Harding — 511 Main St.** OCR garbles the address, but the page image clearly reads `511`.

The same page gives useful relative-location anchors but no exact numbers for them:

- The Chicago Store — **Main St. just below Postoffice**;
- M. E. Dunn — **Near Postoffice**;
- J. Tolpolar — **Opposite Postoffice**;
- Schrader's Bakery — **Near Postoffice**;
- Corbett & Company — **Post Office Building**;
- Clems Chop House — **5th and Main Street**;
- J. Y. Humphry's Fish Market — **Main Street near 5th**;
- Price Bros. — **Sixth and Main Streets**.

Research conclusion: the page directly supports `E-092` and the January portion of `E-089`. It materially tightens the 503/507/509/511 frontage chronology, while leaving the exact 505 occupant unresolved. Relative Post Office descriptions are not converted to numbers.

### Smith pool-room / pool-hall sequence at 503

Primary sources:

- 5 January *Courier*, p. 6 — **H. N. Smith**, pool room, **503 Main**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-01-05/ed-1/seq-6.pdf
- 24 March *Morning Enterprise*, p. 2 — legal notice signed **H. H. Smith** applying for a three-month pool-hall license at **his place of business, 503 Main Street**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-24/ed-1/seq-2/
- 12 July *Courier*, p. 5 — **H. H. Smith**, who had conducted a pool hall at **Fifth and Main streets**, left Wednesday for Aurora to open a pool hall there.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-07-12/ed-1/seq-5/

Research conclusion: 503 Main had a pool-room/pool-hall use in early 1912, and the H. H. Smith operation ended or changed by about 10 July when he left Oregon City. The January `H. N.` versus March/July `H. H.` initials discrepancy is preserved as unresolved rather than silently merged (`E-089`, `RL-037`).

The March exact address and July Fifth/Main wording strongly support a close geographic relationship between the 503 premises and Fifth/Main, but are not treated as conclusive lot/corner mapping without an independent address plat or similar source.

### 24 March — Gambrinus Saloon at Sixth & Main

Primary page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-24/ed-1/seq-2/

A report about a short-change attempt identifies the **Gambrinus Saloon at Sixth and Main streets** and names **William Trudell** as proprietor of “The Gambrinus.”

Research conclusion: the Gambrinus property chronology now extends from the 1906 William Tell/Gambrinus sale, through new-block construction in 1910 and second-story Electric Hotel annex occupancy in 1911, to a named Gambrinus Saloon at the intersection in March 1912 (`E-091`). Exact numbered storefront remains unresolved.

### 10 May — active Main Street demolition/replacement

Primary page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-05-10/ed-1/seq-1/

In an editorial titled around the cost of progress / build-up-and-tear-down cycle, the *Courier* states in the present tense that Oregon City was **tearing down buildings on Main Street** and **building new buildings in their places**.

Research conclusion: 1912 is a confirmed Main Street structural-change year (`E-090`). This is highly relevant to `RL-024`, but the article gives no block, owner, business, or address. Targeted follow-up searches did not identify the structures well enough to tie the statement to 501/503/505/507 or the Fifth–Sixth block. The archive therefore records confirmed Main Street demolition/rebuilding without assigning it to the target buildings (`RL-038`).

### 20 December — Schrader Main Street bakery sold

Primary page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-12-20/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

The *Courier* reports that **Herman Schrader**, after seven years in the Oregon City baking business, sold two bakeries—one at Seventh and John Adams and the other **on Main Street**—to **Wemmo & Haas**. January advertising had placed Schrader's Bakery **near the Post Office**.

Research conclusion: this is a genuine Main Street business-transfer event but not an exact-address or building-transfer record (`E-093`).

## Harding / Willamette implications

The January page directly places **G. H. Young at 507 Main**. In March 1911 Young had been reported moving **one door north into the Willamette Building** from the Harding Building on Main between Fifth and Sixth (`E-085`). The 1912 exact number strengthens the hypothesis that the Willamette premises may have been 507, but continuity from March 1911 to January 1912 is not directly proved (`RL-035`).

The same January page puts **Geo. A. Harding's own business at 511 Main**. This reinforces the archive rule that George Harding's business address and the separately named Harding Building must be reconstructed independently.

## Construction / demolition / fire watch

Targeted 1912 searches included combinations of:

- 501 / 503 / 505 / 507 / 509 / 511 Main;
- Fifth & Main / Sixth & Main / between Fifth and Sixth;
- Smith / George Young / Harding / Chicago Store / Brightbill / Gambrinus / Schrader;
- new building / construction / excavation / remodel / demolition / tear down / moved building / fire / rebuilding;
- Post Office / opposite / near / below Post Office;
- Block 26 / lot references where available.

Other 1912 construction and fire items encountered during the page review were checked but not mapped to the target block—for example construction near Eleventh/Main and Fourteenth/Main, the Carnegie library, Water Street industrial work, and a Molalla hotel fire.

No direct 1912 source recovered in the current pass explicitly reports a fire at a premises identified as 501, 503, 505, or 507 Main. The May Main Street demolition statement remains geographically unresolved.

## Target-address status for 1912

- **501 Main:** no exact 1912 occupant recovered in the current pass.
- **503 Main:** positive exact occupant/use — H. N. Smith pool room on 5 January; H. H. Smith pool-hall license application at 503 on 24 March.
- **505 Main:** no exact-number 1912 occupant recovered in the current pass. The Chicago Store appears on 5 January only as `Main St. just below Postoffice`; its 1911 505 address is not mechanically carried forward.
- **507 Main:** positive exact occupant — G. H. Young on 5 January.
- **509 Main:** H. P. Brightbill on 5 January.
- **511 Main:** Geo. A. Harding on 5 January.

These are research-status findings. Missing exact listings are not evidence of vacancy.

## Building-date limitation

Exact 1912 occupancy proves numbered-address use, not survival of the present buildings. The archive continues to treat later c.1919/c.1920 construction dates as working estimates for surviving fabric rather than first use of the 503/505 addresses.

The May 1912 citywide Main Street teardown/rebuilding statement makes predecessor-building replacement in this era especially plausible, but it cannot yet be assigned to the target block.

## Courier page-review coverage limitation

The annual *Courier* issue/page-review protocol was applied alongside exact-address and topical searches. Accessible/indexed pages were reviewed for target addresses, ads, business moves, construction, demolition, fire, street work, and relative-location clues. The page-level approach materially helped by exposing the January multi-business address page and the May present-tense demolition statement.

Some 1912 issue/page images are not reliably exposed through the current research interface. Those pages are **not** counted as visually reviewed. Completing a physical image-by-image checklist through Historic Oregon Newspapers/Oregon Digital, library microfilm, or another full-page viewer is retained as `RL-036`.
