# 1945 C. P. Farr recreation-building rent lead — 3 September 2026

Status: **PRIMARY MUNICIPAL OCR/TEXT ONLY FOR RENT ROWS — PAGE IMAGE NOT VISUALLY VERIFIED; C. P. FARR NOW STRONGLY IDENTIFIED AS CLARENCE P. FARR; FEBRUARY 1946 CLAIM EXPLICITLY IDENTIFIES THE RECREATION PREMISES AS 708 MONROE.**

Purpose: preserve repeated 1944–1946 Oregon City municipal-finance entries involving **C. P. Farr** and rent for the City's recreation program, because they may become useful in distinguishing the Farr-era property/business history from the later Harr's Recreation Center chronology. Follow-up work on 5 September 2026 strongly identifies the municipal **C. P. Farr** as **Clarence P. Farr**. A continuation pass then recovered a February 1946 municipal claim that explicitly describes the rent as **“Rent at 708 Monroe - Rec. Dept.”** This resolves the premises address in the municipal text for at least that month. It does **not** place the rented building at 505 Main and does not merge Clarence P. Farr with Edwin F. Farr or Farr's Pool Hall.

## Identity update — C. P. Farr now strongly identified as Clarence P. Farr

A targeted identity follow-up on **5 September 2026** recovered a direct middle-initial bridge that was missing from the earlier pass.

Historic Oregon Newspapers, *Oregon City Courier*, **23 June 1911, page 7**, in an estate legal notice, explicitly names:

> **CLARENCE P. FARR, Executor of the Estate of Isaac Farr, Deceased.**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1911-06-23/ed-1/seq-7/

A 7 July 1911 issue repeats the same executor wording. This establishes that the Oregon City **Clarence Farr** family line carried middle initial **P**.

Current official Oregon City historic-resource pages independently identify **Clarence Farr** as a son of pioneer **Isaac Farr** and as the grocer/butcher who operated **Farr Brothers** with his brother Arthur Louis Farr at the northwest corner of Monroe and Seventh. Relevant current City controls:

- `https://www.orcity.org/2386/300-302-Center-Street`
- `https://orcity.org/2142/919-7th-Street`

Separate 1940–1943 Oregon City Commission records use the initials **C. P. Farr** for an Oregon City commissioner. Taken together, the direct 1911 **Clarence P. Farr** name, the official City Isaac/Clarence family identification, and the later municipal **C. P. Farr** role now support a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE IDENTITY MATCH**: the municipal C. P. Farr is best identified as **Clarence P. Farr**.

A 1940s record spelling out the commissioner's full name as **Clarence P. Farr** has not yet been recovered, so retain the cross-source qualification rather than silently treating initials alone as direct same-record proof.

## Spatial / property follow-up created by 708 Monroe

The new **708 Monroe** address creates a more precise property-history question.

Oregon City's current historic-resource page for the **Farr Store, 919 7th Street**, describes Clarence Farr's store at the **northwest corner of Seventh and Monroe** and says it remained there until the **mid-1940s**, when **Dewey Krueger bought the property from Clarence Farr**.

That chronology and physical proximity make the Farr Store property/deed chain a high-value comparison route for the 708 Monroe recreation lease. But **708 Monroe and 919 7th must not be treated as the same building or parcel without a direct map, deed, assessment, address, or building-geometry bridge**. Corner properties can carry multiple or changing addresses, and the current evidence has not yet established that relationship.

## Later exact-address recreation-use lead

A later independent newspaper lead shows that **708 Monroe** remained associated with organized recreation/community programming decades later. The *Sandy Post*, **4 July 1963**, directs readers seeking information about a Clackamas County YMCA camping trip to write **“the Clackamas Y, 708 Monroe St., Oregon City.”**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2024240281/1963-07-04/ed-1/seq-4/

Other 1963 *Sandy Post* references likewise use 708 Monroe for the Clackamas County YMCA / “Y” building. This is a **later-use corroboration / continuity lead only**. It does not yet prove uninterrupted recreation use from 1946 to 1963, City-to-YMCA institutional succession, or ownership continuity.

### Critical boundary

Resolving **C. P. Farr → Clarence P. Farr** and identifying **708 Monroe** does **not** establish any of the following:

- that the City's rented recreation building was **505 Main** — the February 1946 municipal text instead identifies **708 Monroe**;
- that the rent payments concerned **Farr's Pool Hall**;
- that municipal recreation-program wording was related to **Harr's Recreation Center**;
- that Clarence P. Farr was the same person as **Edwin F. Farr**, the proprietor associated with Farr's Pool Hall at 505;
- that **708 Monroe** was the same building/parcel as Clarence Farr's historic Farr Store at **919 7th / Seventh and Monroe**;
- that the 1963 YMCA use represents direct institutional or ownership continuity from the 1946 City recreation lease.

Those remain separate premises, business, property, and identity questions unless a direct record bridges them.

## Research consequence for the Farr → Harr / 505 question

This finding is useful partly because it **removes a misleading possible connection**. The repeated C. P. Farr “recreation building” rent series is now tied in February 1946 to **708 Monroe**, not 505 Main. It therefore should not be used to explain the City's separate historic-resource statement placing **Harr's Recreation Center at 505 Main in 1945**.

The existing 505 chain remains:

- May 1925: 505 mapped as billiards/cigars, operator unnamed;
- Farr's Pool Hall at 505: official historic association, exact operating span unresolved;
- 1945: City historic-resource synthesis reports Harr's Recreation Center at 505, underlying page still needed;
- 11 February 1948: municipal text names Harr and McDougald as pool-hall operators without address;
- late 1948: municipal license text directly prints Carl R. Harr / Harr's Recreation Center / 505 Main.

The exact Farr → Harr changeover remains unresolved.
