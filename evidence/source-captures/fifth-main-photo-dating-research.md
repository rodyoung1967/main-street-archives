# Fifth & Main photographic dating research

Status: **active / hypothesis building**

Purpose: date undated or loosely dated Fifth/Main street-level photographs using businesses, street improvements, parade events, and independently dated newspaper references. These are dating aids, not substitutes for visual source verification.

## Critical camera-geometry correction

The undated parade photograph is annotated **“Taken at 5th & Main St.”**, but the large **GARAGE** sign is on **Fifth Street**, not on the Main Street frontage. It therefore must **not** be used as evidence that Elliott Garage occupied the Main/Fifth corner or the premises later mapped as 501 Main.

Working geometry:
- camera at/near Fifth & Main;
- view extends along Fifth Street toward Water Street;
- GARAGE / auto-work building is on Fifth Street;
- Oregon City Laundry could therefore be behind the Main Street frontage, roughly behind 501/503 depending on lot depths and footprints.

## Laundry-photo orientation correction

The project has two different laundry photographs in the Time Capsule Maps source package (`04-oregon-city-laundry-co-front.jpg` and `05-oregon-city-laundry-main-street.jpg`). They should **not** be assumed to show the same laundry building.

User visual identification adds an important orientation anchor for the **first laundry photo** (`04-oregon-city-laundry-co-front.jpg`):

- the photograph is **facing West Linn**;
- the user recognizes a building visible across the Willamette River;
- therefore the camera is looking **west toward the river / West Linn**.

### Implication

This gives the first laundry photograph a known camera direction independent of uncertain captions. It should be used to determine which side of Fifth Street the photographed laundry occupies by comparing:

1. the position of the laundry relative to the curb/street;
2. the visible side of Fifth Street in the frame;
3. the river/West Linn background alignment;
4. the 1925 Sanborn footprint and the known 108/109 Fifth numbering.

Because there may have been laundry premises on **both sides of Fifth Street**, do not automatically identify every laundry photograph as the later 109 Fifth / east-side building. The first photo's west-facing orientation is now a key discriminator.

## Oregon City Laundry chronology — major reconstruction

The laundry history is more complicated than a single static Fifth Street location.

### 1916 move / new Fifth Street plant

A **3 Feb 1916 Oregon City Courier** report said Oregon City Laundry planned a new two-story brick/mill-construction building because its former property had been sold to Hawley Pulp & Paper for mill expansion. The article's block description is confusing, but a much clearer **2 Apr 1916 Sunday Oregonian** report states that the laundry's new building was **“on Fifth street just off Main”** and was then nearing completion.

Sources:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-03/ed-1/seq-6/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83045782/1916-04-02/ed-1/seq-14/

This is strong evidence that a purpose-built Oregon City Laundry plant was established on Fifth Street near Main in 1916.

### 1918 exact address

The **24 Oct 1918 Oregon City Courier** business guide lists:

- **Oregon City Laundry — M. L. Blakeslee, Prop., 109 Fifth St.**
- **Scripture & May — 108 Fifth St., carriage and auto works**
- **F. L. Davis — 5th & Water, blacksmithing / horseshoeing**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-10-24/ed-1/seq-6/ocr/

This gives a firm numbered address for the laundry by 1918.

### July 1922 fire — critical photo-dating anchor

The **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** reports a major fire that destroyed the Oregon City Laundry and several neighboring Fifth Street businesses. The laundry was the largest loss. The fire also destroyed:

- the **frame garage of May & Washburn**;
- a Fifth Street restaurant;
- the Miller shoe-repair shop;
- a confectionery/home and other adjoining premises.

The newspaper says the laundry had just installed new and modern machinery and was operated by **Knoepel & Schofield**. A temporary laundry office was opened at 519 Main immediately afterward.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1/

### Strong implication for the first laundry photo

One of the project laundry photographs visibly includes **Oregon City Laundry** together with a **May / Garage / blacksmith-auto-work** context. Because the July 1922 fire explicitly destroyed both the laundry and **May & Washburn's frame garage** in the same Fifth Street fire, that image is a strong candidate for the **pre-July-1922 Fifth Street cluster**.

This is not yet a final identification, but it is much stronger than the earlier generic 1930s dating guess for that image.

If the visible garage can be matched to May & Washburn, the photograph must predate the July 1922 fire.

### Post-fire / later McLoughlin location

Later municipal evidence establishes Oregon City Laundry at **510 McLoughlin Boulevard**:

- a 1953 letter from the laundry asks for a loading zone at **510 McLoughlin Blvd**;
- a 1967 city resolution again identifies **Oregon City Laundry, 510 McLoughlin Boulevard**;
- the city's historic-resource survey lists an Oregon City Laundry building on McLoughlin dating to about **1939** (table parsing in web extracts is inconsistent, so use the direct 1953/1967 address records as the firmer location proof).

Sources:
- 1953 city record: https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4722849/File/document
- 1967 city record: https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4753768/File/document
- city historic survey: https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4025/Downtown-Final-Report---2000-PDF

### Working two-generation hypothesis

The user's recollection of a laundry on **both sides of Fifth Street** may reflect **two generations/locations of Oregon City Laundry**, rather than two simultaneously operating plants:

1. **pre-July 1922 Fifth Street plant near Main / 109 Fifth**, destroyed in the 1922 fire;
2. **later McLoughlin Boulevard plant**, definitely at 510 McLoughlin by 1953 and 1967, likely associated with the post-realignment era.

This is now a high-priority hypothesis to test against the two project photographs. Do **not** yet state that the two photos definitely show these two generations until the second image is visually matched.

## Fifth Street curb lower bound

`E-059` documents the 1905 Fifth Street improvement across Main, including grading, crushed rock, sidewalks, **concrete curbs**, corner blocks and drains.

If the photograph shows the completed curb work, **circa 1906 or later** is a reasonable lower bound.

## Price Bros. upper bound

Price Bros. is documented at **Sixth & Main** from 1910 through 1917 and announced a move to Seventh & Main after April 1, 1918.

If the visible Price Bros. sign is correctly identified, the parade photograph should be **pre-April 1918**.

## Major new anchor — Fifth St. Garage, 1915

The Oregon City Courier repeatedly advertised a business literally named **FIFTH ST. GARAGE** during 1915.

Verified examples:

- **20 May 1915** — advertisement: auto supplies, expert repair work on any make of car, Firestone tires, **FIFTH ST. GARAGE**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-05-20/ed-1/seq-5.pdf
- **27 May 1915** — Fifth St. Garage advertisement for auto supplies and repair work.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-05-27/ed-1/seq-2/ocr/
- **17 Jun 1915** — advertisement: **AUTO REPAIRS**, Red Crown gasoline, **FIFTH ST. GARAGE**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-17/ed-1/seq-2/ocr/
- **1 Jul 1915** — advertisement: auto repairs, Red Crown gasoline, **FIFTH ST. GARAGE**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-07-01/ed-1/seq-7.pdf
- **8 Jul, 22 Jul, 5 Aug, 9 Sep 1915** — continued Fifth St. Garage ads with auto repairs / Red Crown gasoline.

The OCR/page layout also shows the name **F. B. Shoenborn** near these ads, but the exact relationship and exact street number of the garage are not yet visually proven from the page layout. Do not assign a specific 5th Street number to the garage until a clean page image or directory listing establishes it.

### Dating implication

The literal GARAGE sign in the parade photo has a plausible candidate in **Fifth St. Garage**, active by May 1915.

If the photographed sign can be matched to Fifth St. Garage, the parade image is **May 1915 or later**.

## Scripture & May — Fifth Street near Main / 108 Fifth

A separate business occupied the same short side-street zone:

- **22 Jul and 29 Jul 1915** — Scripture & May, **5th St., near Main**, scientific horseshoeing / general blacksmithing.
- late 1916 — **Scripture & May, 108 Fifth St.**
- 1917 — continuing at 108 Fifth.
- 24 Oct 1918 business guide — **Scripture & May, 108 5th; carriage and auto works**.

This means Fifth St. Garage and Scripture & May may have been **distinct neighboring Fifth Street businesses** in 1915. Their exact building relationship remains to be reconstructed.

## Possible 501 Main visibility

The 1925 Sanborn labels **501 Main** at/near the Fifth/Main corner, with 503 and 505 immediately north along Main. The parade photo can therefore plausibly include some or all of the corner premises later mapped as 501 Main.

Do not yet equate the visible pre-1925 corner building with numbered 501 Main without a map/address bridge.

## Parade-event hypotheses

Previously identified candidates:
- 8 Jun 1912 Rose Show parade;
- 7 Jun 1913 Rose Show parade.

Those remain possible only as event matches. The former garage-based argument favoring 1913 is withdrawn.

If the visible GARAGE sign proves to be **Fifth St. Garage**, then 1912 and 1913 are too early.

## Current best parade-photo dating bracket

- lower broad bound: **circa 1906 or later** from Fifth Street curb work;
- stronger sign-based lower bound: **May 1915 or later** if the GARAGE sign is Fifth St. Garage;
- upper bound: **before April 1918** if Price Bros. is still at Sixth/Main in the photograph.

### Current strongest working zone

**1915–early 1918**, with **1915–1916** now especially important.

Separately, the laundry/May-garage photograph appears to have its own strong **pre-July-1922** bound if the May & Washburn identification is correct.
