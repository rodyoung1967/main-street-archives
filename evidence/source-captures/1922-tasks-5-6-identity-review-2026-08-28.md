## New direct Miller association — S-125 / E-113

The *Lane County News*, Springfield, Thursday 3 February 1916, front page, fourth column, prints the heading **To Manufacture Leather Dressing**. Its opening names **“Gilbert Miller of the Wolf & Miller Harness shop”**. It reports that he had put his own leather dressing and shoe-waterproofing product on the market, in paste form, and expected cans for its sale.

The original page was downloaded and visually inspected, including its masthead/date and the complete short item. This directly establishes a named Gilbert Miller's association with Wolf & Miller in Springfield in 1916; it is stronger than inferring that association merely from contemporaneous church notices or a later historic inventory. The item does not specify a legal partnership interest, name Wolf, identify either man's relatives, or give an Oregon City address.

The previously verified 1918 move reports and later Oregon City trade chronology still support continuity with the Oregon City Gilbert Miller. However, the 20 August 1918 *Daily Guard* really prints **C. E. Miller**, while the 22 August *Springfield News* prints **Gilbert Miller**. The new notice strengthens the Gilbert identification without erasing that contemporary conflict or identifying the Idaho-bound partner.

## Medford Gates candidate — S-127; secondary historical source

The 1990 National Register nomination for the C. E. “Pop” Gates House identifies **Charles Edwin “Pop” Gates**, with wife **Leah A. Farmsley**, and describes his 1912 move to Medford and automobile business with brother W. A. “Bill” Gates. This wording was visually verified on **PDF page 15, section 8, printed page 1B**.

This identifies the familiar Medford candidate, **not** the 1922 Oregon City purchaser. The nomination is a retrospective historical source; viewing its scan does not make its biography contemporaneous evidence. The Portland description in the Oregon City reports is a discrepancy to investigate, not sufficient proof that the men are different.
