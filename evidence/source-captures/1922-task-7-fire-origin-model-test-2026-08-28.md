## Model tests and failure conditions

### A — Gates principally at 501, Wolf beginning at 503

- **Needed:** a July restaurant/Gates boundary tied to the later 501 footprint and evidence placing the Wolf remainder north of that line.
- **Not yet supplied:** Gates's two-store orientation, Schieremann's room number, and the July adjoining hall's position. Even a proven Wolf parcel at 503 would not by itself prove that the fire began in that particular room.
- **Would challenge the simple model:** a contemporaneous dimensioned record showing Gates also held the later 503 footprint before the fire, provided land and building interests and any subsequent transfer are reconciled.
- **Disposition:** compatible but unproved. The familiar 503-origin/505-hall reconstruction remains a working hypothesis; prior wording calling it “strongest” is not a demonstrated comparative ranking.

### B — Gates at 501+503, Wolf beginning near 505

- **Needed:** evidence that both Gates rooms correspond to the later Main-facing 501/503 bays, plus a separate bridge from the **post-fire** replacement footprint to the **pre-fire** ownership division.
- **Not yet supplied:** no dimensioned permit/plan/deed, exact first-tenant addresses, or boundary-change history. Tile material, two rooms and the 1925 use sequence are compatible but insufficient.
- **Would challenge the simple model:** a contemporaneous instrument/plan assigning the later 503 footprint to Wolf and confining Gates's replacement to a different footprint, after separating dates and ownership interests.
- **Disposition:** compatible but unproved. A proved northern Wolf boundary would still require locating the actual ignition room and adjoining hall.

### Retained alternatives and sensitivity checks

1. **501:** both A and B presume a Gates-to-501 correspondence. That assumption cannot be used to eliminate 501 before it is independently established. A precise pre-fire restaurant footprint, read with the separate Wolf ignition-building report and ownership-interest evidence, could disfavor or exclude that same room; a broad corner address alone may not.
2. **Changed post-fire arrangement:** transfers, expanded footprints, subdivided rooms or changed entrances between July 1922 and May 1925 could make either simple back-projection wrong. These are possibilities, not recorded events.
3. **Ownership categories:** Ed. Blake's restaurant-owner wording does not conflict automatically with Gates's property-owner wording. A business owner, landowner, building/improvement owner and license holder need not be the same person. Wolf's newspaper building-owner label does not settle the land interest.
4. **Remove weak premises:** without the secondary Wolf-family/date estimate, without the OCR-only 1913 owner claim, and without assuming 1925 uses persisted from July 1922, the strong published Harding/Wolf connection survives but the address still does not follow. Repeating that chain in several derivative records adds no independent spatial evidence.
