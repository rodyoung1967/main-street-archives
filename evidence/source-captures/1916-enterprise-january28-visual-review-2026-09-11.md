## Useful controls and findings

### Page 4 — local Prohibition transition context

The Enterprise editorial page states that the paper had advocated prohibition in Oregon City and Oregon and discusses the practical transition after the law took effect. It says former saloon proprietors had turned their places of business into resorts for men featuring pool tables, a soft-drink bar, reading tables, etc., with an expectation that these businesses could become useful, orderly community resorts.

Evidence state: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT for the general Oregon City transition described by the newspaper; NOT an address-specific claim.**

Research significance: this is directly relevant to the broader 503/505 hypothesis space because it documents a contemporaneous local pattern in which former saloon premises could become pool/recreation/soft-drink businesses after prohibition. It does **not** identify 503 Main, 505 Main, 505½ Main, or any particular saloon/pool operator, so it must not be used as an address bridge by itself.

The same page carries an advertisement for the Bank of Oregon City, described as the oldest bank in Clackamas County, but no street number is printed.

### Page 5 — Main Street control

Larsen & Co. advertises groceries, crockery, granite-ware, household supplies, heating stoves, and kitchen ranges at **Corner Tenth and Main**. This is a numbered/intersection control outside the target block.

### Page 6 — prohibition enforcement/control

A local item describes cider shipped into Oregon that had become sufficiently alcoholic to raise a state-prohibition-law question; the merchant sought guidance rather than risk violating the law. This is a useful contemporaneous control on how the new prohibition regime was being interpreted and enforced, but it is unrelated to 503/505 premises.

### Page 7 — First National Bank control

The professional-directory area contains an advertisement for **The First National Bank of Oregon City**, listing D. C. Latourette as president and F. J. Meyer as cashier. It states capital of $50,000 and general banking business, but prints **no street address**. It therefore does not independently convert I. Tolpolar's later 1916 wording “next door 1st Nat'l Bank” into an exact street number.

### Page 8 — prohibition enforcement outside target

A Portland report describes seizure of sixteen barrels of liquor from an empty house and an arrest under the prohibition law. This is statewide/prohibition context only and has no 503/505 connection.
