Review date: 13 September 2026

## Method and source boundary

The University of Oregon Oregon Digital Newspaper Program title calendars were used to inventory every online issue of the two relevant Oregon City local titles in January and February 1922. Every exposed page PDF was downloaded, SHA-256 hashed in the monthly manifest, rendered, and visually inspected from the original page scan. OCR was used only to identify candidate passages for enlarged visual rereview; no OCR-only reading is promoted here.

The reviewed corpus is preserved under:

- `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1922-01/` and `1922-02/`;
- `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-01/` and `1922-02/`.

## Verified findings

### Dr. Harvey Freeze at 505½ Main

The 13 January *Enterprise*, page 3, visibly prints a Dr. Freeze eye-specialist advertisement at **505½ Main St., Oregon City**, opposite the post office. The same half-number is visible again on 3 February *Enterprise*, page 3, and 9 February *Banner-Courier*, page 7. These pages add dated 1922 anchors within the already established April 1921–June 1922 Freeze interval at 505/505½. They document a professional/business address only: they prove no real-property ownership and do not establish whether the half-numbered space was upstairs, beside, or behind the ground-floor 505 premises.

### January–February pool-room regulation sequence

- 6 January *Enterprise*, page 3, reports that local pool halls would be required to post a **$500 bond** and have applications passed by the City Council; it also reports the mayor's Sunday-closing order.
- 19 January *Banner-Courier*, page 1, visually prints the proposed ordinance after first reading on 13 January. The text required each petition and published notice to state the particular place, made licenses non-transferable without Council consent, required a ground-floor room with an unobstructed glazed view, fixed a $10 quarterly fee plus $2.50 per table, imposed the $500 bond, and barred minors, gambling, intoxicated persons and lunch counters. It scheduled second reading for 1 February. The headline's “passed” wording is not converted into final adoption on that date.
- 20 January *Enterprise*, page 1, likewise describes the measure as having passed first reading and reports that two city pool halls then had lunch counters.
- 27 January *Enterprise*, page 1, reports a Sunday-closing petition and the pending final consideration.
- 3 February *Enterprise*, page 3, reports the Council's 1 February final passage, six to one: Sunday operation remained permitted, while the $500 bond, revocation power and lunch-counter ban were adopted.

This is valuable premises-record context because the published ordinance required location-specific petitions and notices. None of these reviewed pages names the operators or street addresses of the regulated Oregon City halls. They therefore do not identify the later fire-adjacent pool hall, do not assign Timms or Leland & Little to 505, and do not prove physical continuity from A. Leland's 1920 505½ permit anchor.

### May & Washburn remains a separate partnership

The 16 February *Banner-Courier*, page 5, visibly prints a joint horseshoeing price-reduction advertisement headed **“May & Washburn and S. F. Scripture.”** It establishes coordinated advertising, not a merger, ownership transfer, or shared premises. A separate S. F. Scripture advertisement on the same page directly gives **108 Fifth St., between Main and Water Sts.**; the May & Washburn notice gives no address. Do not transfer Scripture's 108 Fifth address to May & Washburn, and do not equate the May partner with L. E. May from this page alone.

## Bounded no-hit result

Beyond the findings above, the 142 reviewed pages yielded no source-explicit January–February event assigning **501 Main, 503 Main, 505 Main, Alex Schieremann, Timms Pool Hall, Leland & Little, the Fifth Street Restaurant, or the later July fire premises** to an exact target bay; and no source-explicit target demolition, construction, rebuilding or deed transfer was found in this bounded corpus. This is page-set coverage only. It is not evidence of vacancy, non-use, non-ownership, absence of construction, or business closure.
