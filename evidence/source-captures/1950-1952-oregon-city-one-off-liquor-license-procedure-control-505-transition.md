## Why these controls matter for 505

These two records, together with the already preserved licensing evidence, show that Oregon City's procedure in this period was fragmented across:

- late-1948 larger license list naming **Carl R. Harr / Harr's Recreation Center / 505 Main**;
- 9 Nov. 1949 five-item beer-renewal batch;
- 6 Apr. 1950 **single Pastime Poolhall application** naming Walter F. Nebert;
- 5 Dec. 1951 nineteen-applicant liquor-license batch;
- 10 Oct. 1952 **single Seid's Restaurant application**;
- 22 Dec. 1952 three-applicant supplemental action; and
- 7 Jan. 1953 separate small renewal approvals.

The methodological conclusion is therefore strong: **the Harr→Well's search must inspect individual in-year license approvals, substitutions, transfers and change actions; omission from any one annual or December list is not negative evidence.**

## Evidence boundary

This capture establishes licensing **procedure and coverage**, not a 505 business succession. Do not infer from these records:

- that Harr left 505 before April 1950 or October 1952;
- that Well's Recreation had opened by either date;
- that Walter F. Nebert or Seid's had any connection to 505;
- that Harr transferred directly to a person surnamed Well/Wells; or
- any building-ownership or business-sale relationship.

## Research consequence

Continue month-by-month Commission searching for 1949–1953 one-off actions using:

- `505 Main` / `505 Main Street`;
- `Carl R. Harr` / `Harr's Recreation Center`;
- `Well`, `Well's`, `Wells`, `Welle`, `Welsh`;
- `pool`, `poolhall`, `billiards`, `recreation`;
- liquor-license application, transfer, substitution, and ownership-change wording.

The original 1953 T. W. Salisbury directory and OLCC license-history/tradename cards remain the highest-value independent sources for directly naming the successor proprietor.
