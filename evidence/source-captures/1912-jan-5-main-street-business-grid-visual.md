## Visually verified business/address anchors

### 503 Main — H. N. Smith
The original page clearly shows an advertisement reading approximately:

**H. N. SMITH**  
**Pool Room. Cigars and Tobacco.**  
**Oyster Cocktails made here.**  
**503 Main Street**

This is stronger than the previously registered March 1912 license-application evidence because it proves an **operating pool-room/cigar business at 503 Main on 5 January 1912**.

Important identity issue: a 24 March 1912 *Morning Enterprise* legal notice recovered in OCR/text form appears to identify **H. H. Smith** as the applicant for a pool-hall license at 503 Main. The January visual ad reads **H. N. Smith**. Do not silently normalize these initials. The March notice needs direct visual inspection or independent identity evidence before deciding whether H. N. and H. H. Smith are the same person or one source contains a typographical/reading error.

### 507 Main — G. H. Young
The same visual page gives **G. H. Young, The Curio Man, 507 Main Street**.

This corrects a misleading OCR association that appeared to attach 507 Main to M. E. Dunn. The page layout shows that **M. E. Dunn is described only as near the Post Office**, while 507 belongs to G. H. Young.

### 509 Main — H. P. Brightbill
The visual page clearly gives **H. P. Brightbill, 509 Main St.**

This confirms the project correction that OCR readings of `609 Main` for this advertisement are wrong.

### 511 Main — George A. Harding
The visual page gives **Geo. A. Harding, 511 Main St.** The address is readable in the page layout even though OCR can lose a digit.

### 527 Main — Bailey & Price
The page also advertises **Bailey & Price, Billiard and Pool Parlors, 527 Main Street**, opposite the Grand Theater. This is already represented elsewhere in the archive but serves as a useful address-control anchor on the same page.

## Separate pool/billiards business — Cox & Ward

The page contains a distinct advertisement for:

**COX & WARD**  
Choice Cigars and Candies  
Soft Drinks  
Billiard and Pool Parlors  
Main Street

The recovered visual ad does **not** print a street number.

This is potentially important to the Frank D. Cox research because it proves a **Cox & Ward** billiards/pool/cigar business was operating on Main Street on 5 January 1912, months before the October 1912 council minutes granted a pool/billiard license to **F. D. Cox**.

However, there is currently **no direct evidence that the Cox in Cox & Ward was Frank D. Cox**. Do not merge the identities without a direct source. Likewise, do not assign Cox & Ward to 503: H. N. Smith is independently and visually documented at 503 on the same page.

## Post Office-relative businesses

The page places **M. E. Dunn near the Post Office** rather than at 507 Main. The Chicago Store advertisement says **Main St. just below Postoffice**. These relative-location descriptions remain useful for reconstructing the block but should not be converted into exact addresses without independent evidence.

## OCR corrections demonstrated by the visual page

The original-page review demonstrates several OCR/layout hazards:

- `603 Main` should read **503 Main** for H. N. Smith.
- `609 Main` should read **509 Main** for H. P. Brightbill.
- Harding's address may lose a digit in OCR; the visual page gives **511 Main**.
- `507 Main` belongs to **G. H. Young**, not M. E. Dunn.

This is a strong example of why the archive's visual-review standard must take precedence over OCR-only extraction.
