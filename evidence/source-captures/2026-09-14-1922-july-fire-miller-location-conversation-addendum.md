## Oregon City Enterprise — 21 July 1922, p.1 — VISUALLY VERIFIED

The project steward supplied two photographs of the original page-one scan and identified the source as *Oregon City Enterprise*, Friday, 21 July 1922, p.1. The photographs were visually inspected in this conversation.

Headline visible in the scan:

> MAIN STREET FIRE DAMAGE $56,000; CAUSE IS ENIGMA

Subheads state that the early-morning blaze swept over a quarter block, that a laundry and restaurant were razed, and that eight people were endangered.

### Direct spatial / fire evidence visible in the article

- The article says the blaze broke out in a **vacant half of a block building formerly occupied by Alex Schiermann as a tailor**. The wording is important: the source describes a vacant *half* rather than necessarily an entire vacant building.
- It reports that the three structures at the corner were entirely gutted by flames.
- The article identifies the **Oregon City Laundry** as a major loss.
- It identifies the **Fifth Street restaurant** as a total loss and says **Blakesley owned the Fifth Street restaurant**.
- It says the restaurant building was owned by **C. P. Gates of Portland**.
- It identifies **L. Wolf, 33 N. 3rd St., Portland**, as owner of the Red Front clothing store damaged by the flames.
- Critically, it says Wolf had **recently purchased the shoe-store building in which the fire started**.
- It says the building to the north of the fire was saved by fireproof construction.
- The article also discusses Washburn and May's garage at the rear of the laundry building as destroyed and a car lost.

### Significance for Gilbert Miller's pre-fire address

This Enterprise account materially sharpens the location problem. The same account (1) places the fire origin in a vacant half formerly occupied by Schiermann's tailoring business and (2) describes Wolf as having recently purchased **the shoe-store building in which the fire started**. Because the 20 July Banner-Courier account independently identifies Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair/shop loss in the fire, the evidence strongly links Miller's pre-fire shoe operation to the building where the fire originated, while also indicating that a vacant former-tailor portion existed in that building.

This does **not yet directly state a numbered address** for Miller. The current working hypothesis is that the destroyed shoe-store/fire-origin building may be **503 Main**, but that remains a PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS until an address-bearing source or sufficiently constrained parcel/building geometry resolves it.

The hypothesis is strengthened if the independently documented adjoining pool/billiard premises is correctly mapped to surviving **505 Main** and if **507 Main** is independently shown to have survived. Those propositions must remain separately sourced rather than folded into the Miller conclusion.

## Banner-Courier — 20 July 1922, p.1 — fire comparison

The conversation reread the 20 July Banner-Courier fire report. Important points carried forward:

- Fire event date: **Tuesday, 18 July 1922**, approximately 2:00 a.m.; source date 20 July.
- Fire was discovered in a vacant storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor.
- The report names **Gilbert Miller / Miller's shoe-repair shop and shoe stock** among the fire losses.
- The adjoining pool/billiard hall was **damaged rather than described as destroyed**: billiard tables were damaged by water and part of the roof burned.
- Therefore do not describe the pool hall as razed on the evidence presently in hand.

The Enterprise account above is especially valuable because its wording about the **shoe-store building in which the fire started** helps reconcile the vacant former-tailor space with Miller's destroyed shoe business: they may have occupied separate halves/portions of the same building.

## Miller's Shoe Store — pre-fire location evidence

### Direct / documented

- Proprietor: **Gilbert Miller**.
- June 1922 Buyers' Carnival material names Gilbert Miller separately from other Miller entities; do not merge him with similarly named Miller businesses without identity evidence.
- Miller advertisements immediately before the fire describe the business as **Main Street — Opposite Post Office** but, in the advertisements discussed in this conversation, do not print a numbered address.
- Project steward reports that the 22 June, 29 June, and 13 July 1922 Banner-Courier issues show Miller's Shoe Store using the Main Street / opposite-Post-Office description while Dr. Freeze is also associated with explicit **505½ Main** wording. These issue-specific ad details should be retained as steward-observed leads until each exact page is separately captured/transcribed in the source register.
- The 20 July Banner-Courier fire report establishes that Miller's shoe-repair/shop operation was lost in the fire.
- The 21 July Enterprise account says the fire began in the **shoe-store building** recently purchased by L. Wolf, while also describing the vacant former-Schiermann half.

### Working spatial conclusion

**503 Main is currently the leading candidate for Gilbert Miller's pre-fire shoe-store building, but is not yet directly proven.**

Reasoning: the shoe-store building burned / was at the fire origin; the pool hall appears to have survived with damage; project geometry presently favors the pool hall at 505; the project steward states 507 survived. If 505 and 507 are independently secured as surviving structures, 503 becomes the strongest remaining numbered-address candidate. This is an elimination inference and must not be upgraded to DOCUMENTED / DIRECT without a numbered-address source or equivalent parcel/building proof.

## Gilbert Miller — post-fire relocation — VISUALLY VERIFIED steward-supplied ad

The project steward supplied and visually inspected an advertisement from *Banner-Courier*, 24 August 1922, p.2. The ad explicitly prints:

> 419 MAIN STREET

and

> The New Location Of MILLER'S SHOE STORE

The ad states that Gilbert Miller bought the stock and fixtures of the **H. S. Goldman Second Hand store**, intended to carry men's and boys' everyday clothing and shoes, was closing out the existing second-hand stock, and was installing his shoe-repairing machinery. It closes with wording referring to a better and bigger business than **before the fire** and is signed **GILBERT MILLER**.

Evidence state: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT; VISUALLY VERIFIED** for Miller's post-fire location at **419 Main Street by 24 August 1922**.

This corrects any prior unsupported placement of Miller at 209 Fifth Street. Do not use 209 Fifth as Miller's post-fire location unless a separate primary source establishes a temporary/intermediate stop.

The project steward also found a 21 September 1922 Banner-Courier p.5 Miller advertisement stating that the hardware had been sold out, new working men's clothing/shoes/gloves/pants/shirts/underwear had arrived, and the shoe machinery was set up for the same high-class shoe repairing as before the fire. That later ad is consistent with the 24 August transition into the former Goldman premises.

## Dr. Harvey Freeze / 505½ control

Conversation evidence carried forward:

- Freeze had earlier been at 507½ Main in spring 1921, then moved to 505½ Main by May 1921.
- In late June 1922 Freeze advertised 505½ Main and announced a move to the Masonic Building after 1 July.
- By 13 July a 'New Location' ad places him at 207–8 Masonic Building.

Therefore Freeze's explicit 505½ address is a useful control demonstrating that 'Opposite Post Office' was not a unique street-number identifier. Do not use that landmark wording alone to place Miller at 505.

## First National Bank address control

Project steward observation: *Banner-Courier*, 27 July 1922, p.7, advertises the First National Bank of Oregon City at **512 Main Street**. Preserve as a dated address-control lead; exact page should be separately captured/transcribed if used for a formal spatial claim.
