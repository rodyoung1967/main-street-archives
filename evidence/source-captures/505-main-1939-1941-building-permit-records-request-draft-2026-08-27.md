## Research context to retain with the draft

### Why 1940 is being tested

The repository currently preserves a real date conflict rather than selecting one date as fact:

- The **2009 Oregon Historic Site Form** for 505 Main gives **1940** as the primary construction date (`E-100` / `S-116`).
- A later ODOT cultural-resources inventory also carries **1940**.
- The **2000 Oregon City Downtown inventory** assigns approximately **c.1920** to 505.
- The **1925 Sanborn** visually shows **505 Main** as a separate billiards/cigars commercial storefront.
- The project therefore does not interpret 1940 as first-ever use of the address or first-ever building there.

### Current working hypothesis — not yet proven

`E-101` treats the following as a testable but unresolved possibility:

> The later inventory's 1940 date may represent a major remodel, rearward expansion, partial/whole rebuild, concrete-floor or structural work, or another substantial improvement event affecting the surviving resource.

Competing explanations remain live, including a survey/assessor dating convention, an erroneous inherited date, or a more extensive reconstruction than currently understood.

### What has already been searched

The project has already checked:

- Oregon City's modern online permit system, which does not reach the 1940 period;
- Clackamas County Document Retriever, which returned only a later electrical permit for 505 rather than a 1939–1941 building permit;
- web searches for 505 Main with 1939/1940/1941 construction/remodel/addition terms;
- 1925 vs. 1950 Sanborn maps;
- preliminary 1936, 1940, and 1944 aerial comparisons.

No primary 1939–1941 505 construction permit has yet been recovered. That is a **retrieval gap only**, not evidence that no work occurred.

### Adjacent/parallel record route

If the City permit-index search produces a useful permit date or owner name, the next independent check should be the surviving Clackamas County/Oregon State Archives assessment and tax records:

- assessment/tax-roll index, 1901–1949;
- index to assessment rolls, 1938–1940;
- tax-roll microfilm beginning in 1940.

Those records may show a contemporaneous improvement-value change or ownership/legal-description context, but they are not substitutes for the actual City permit.
