Recovered text from the **2 March 1932** City Commission record states that **Ordinance No. 1234** was read the first time and ordered published. Its title concerns limiting the number of pool halls in Oregon City and repealing Ordinance No. 1219.

Recovered substance:

- Section 1 says no greater number of pool-hall licenses shall be issued than **one for each 2,000 population or major fraction thereof**;
- Section 2 says **Ordinance No. 1219**, passed **3 September 1930**, is repealed;
- Ordinance 1234 was scheduled for second reading/final passage at a special meeting on **16 March 1932 at 1:00 p.m.**

Evidence state is **PRIMARY MUNICIPAL OCR/TEXT ONLY**. Page-image screenshot attempts failed with a cache miss, so this is **not visually certified**.

No recovered text from the 2 March record names Farr, 505 Main, or a specific license holder. Do not treat the ordinance itself as Farr occupancy evidence.

Why it matters: it proves a formal municipal pool-hall licensing framework existed during the exact 1925–1934 gap, making applications, renewals, Recorder ledgers, petitions, transfers and denials a high-value route to identifying active operators and premises.
