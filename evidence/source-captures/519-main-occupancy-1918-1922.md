# 519 Main Street occupancy ladder — 1918 to July 1922

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED address anchors; January–July 1922 tenant transition still unresolved**

Purpose: reconstruct the occupancy of 519 Main Street to determine when the Oregon City Pantorium arrived there and test whether Pantorium could have been the cleaning/dyeing establishment reported to have recently vacated the July 1922 fire-origin room.

## 7 February 1918 — Theroux Music Company preparing to leave 519 Main

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Courier, 7 Feb 1918, p. 8** was visually inspected.

A large Theroux Music Company advertisement says:

- `WILL MOVE`
- `FROM 519 MAIN STREET`
- to Seventh Street on the hill;
- the move was expected about **15 February 1918**.

Canonical PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-02-07/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

This provides a direct move-out anchor for 519 Main.

## 10 October 1918 — Neldon at 519 Main

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Courier, 10 Oct 1918, p. 4** was visually inspected.

In the `Who's Who in Oregon City` feature, the watchmaker/jeweler clue identifies:

- **Neldon — 519 Main**.

The text says he had been in Oregon City for about eighteen months and describes his watchmaking, jewelry, engraving and diamond-setting work.

Canonical PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-10-10/ed-1/seq-4.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

Thus by October 1918 the 519 Main storefront had passed from Theroux's music business to Neldon's watch/jewelry business.

## 5 January 1922 — Frank Neldon now at 425 Main at Fifth Street

The actual scanned page of the **Banner-Courier, 5 Jan 1922, p. 2** was visually inspected.

A boxed advertisement reads:

- **Neldon's Watch Shop**
- **FRANK NELDON**
- Expert Watchmaker and Jeweler
- **425 Main at 5th Street**.

Canonical PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-01-05/ed-1/seq-2.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

This proves Neldon had left 519 Main by 5 January 1922.

## 20 July 1922 — Pantorium at 519 Main

The visually reviewed **Banner-Courier, 20 Jul 1922, p. 1** says Oregon City Laundry, after losing its plant in the July 18 fire, took a temporary office **with the Pantorium at 519 Main Street**.

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1/

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Current occupancy sequence

The currently supported ladder is:

1. **7 Feb 1918** — Theroux Music Company still at 519 Main but publicly announces an expected move about 15 Feb;
2. **10 Oct 1918** — Neldon at 519 Main;
3. **5 Jan 1922** — Frank Neldon at 425 Main at Fifth, proving he had left 519 by then;
4. **20 Jul 1922** — Pantorium at 519 Main.

The crucial unresolved period is therefore **between Neldon's departure from 519 (no later than 5 Jan 1922) and Pantorium's documented 519 occupancy on 20 Jul 1922**.

The exact date Pantorium arrived at 519 remains unknown.

## Fire-origin significance

A same-day **18 Jul 1922 Eugene Daily Guard** account says the Main Street room where the fire began had been occupied until only a few days earlier by a **cleaning-and-dyeing establishment**.

Pantorium was independently an active Oregon City dry-cleaning/dyeing business in January and June 1922. Therefore its move date to 519 matters directly:

- if Pantorium is proved at **519 Main well before 18 July**, that would strongly weigh against Pantorium being the cleaner/dyer that had just vacated the separate fire-origin room;
- if Pantorium is shown to have **moved to 519 immediately before or after the fire**, that would materially strengthen the hypothesis that Pantorium had previously occupied another Main Street room;
- at present neither scenario is proven.

This occupancy ladder narrows the search but does not identify the ignition-room tenant.

## What is NOT established

Do not infer that:

- Pantorium immediately succeeded Neldon at 519;
- no intervening tenant occupied 519;
- Pantorium moved because of the July fire;
- Pantorium occupied the ignition room;
- Neldon had anything to do with the July fire.

Absence of recovered advertisements between the anchors is not vacancy evidence.
