# 1 February 1922 — Oregon City council reopens poolrooms on Sundays

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED newspaper report; underlying council minutes not yet retrieved**

Purpose: pin the 1922 pool-hall ordinance chronology to a specific council meeting and identify a high-value municipal-record retrieval target for the Timms / Leland & Little / Fifth-Main fire reconstruction.

## Source

**Banner-Courier, 2 Feb 1922, p. 10**

Canonical page:

PDF:

The actual scanned page was visually inspected.

## Directly supported fact

A short item headed **“Poolrooms Open Sunday.”** states that the **Oregon City council the previous night voted for open poolrooms on Sunday**. It adds that, by order of Mayor Shannon, the poolrooms had been closed during the preceding month.

Because the newspaper is dated Thursday, 2 Feb 1922, “last night” identifies the council action as **Wednesday, 1 Feb 1922**.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Relationship to the January ordinance debate

A visually reviewed / recovered 20 Jan 1922 Oregon City Enterprise editorial says petitions were expected at the council's next regular session asking that pool halls be closed Sundays, while the proposed new pool-hall ordinance as then drafted contained no Sunday-closing clause after councilmen had removed it.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1922-01-20/ed-1/seq-3/

Taken together, the two sources establish a specific regulatory sequence:

1. January 1922 — poolrooms were being kept closed Sundays under Mayor Shannon's order;
2. 20 Jan — Sunday closing was an active issue in the pending pool-hall ordinance debate;
3. 1 Feb — council voted to allow poolrooms to open Sundays.

## Why this matters for the fire research

The project needs the exact 1922 premises of:

- Timms Pool Hall;
- Leland & Little;
- the unnamed pool hall adjoining Alex Schieremann's former tailor shop in the July fire.

Earlier Oregon City council records prove that municipal minutes could describe pool/billiard premises at detailed location level. For example, a 4 May 1910 council record refers to permission involving pool/billiard tables **back of a saloon at Fifth & Main**.

Therefore the **1 Feb 1922 council minutes, associated ordinance file, petitions, licenses, and permit applications** are high-value primary records. They may name the operators or the “particular place” of one or more poolrooms affected by the ordinance.

This is a **RETRIEVAL GAP**, not evidence that the minutes or applications do not survive.
