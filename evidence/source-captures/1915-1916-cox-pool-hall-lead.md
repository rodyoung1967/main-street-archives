# 1915–1916 Cox pool hall — possible 503 succession lead

Status: **active lead; address unresolved**

This note records a potentially important business-continuity lead for the unresolved 503 Main gap after L. Hylton's documented 1914 cigar/tobacco/soft-drink shop.

## What is directly documented

### 3 Jun 1915 — Cox's pool hall on Main Street
The *Oregon City Courier* reports that officers, after leaving the Moose Lodge, marched down **Main Street** and next raided **Cox's pool hall**. This independently places the hall on Main Street.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-03/ed-1/seq-1/

### 10 Jun 1915 — Frank D. Cox is the principal/employer; William Myers Jr. is a partner
The *Oregon City Courier* reports that **William Myers Jr. was a partner in the Cox pool hall** and was tried over allegations of liquor sales there. Later testimony identifies **F. D. Cox as Myers's present employer**, while **Sol Cox worked with Myers in the pool hall**. This makes Frank D. Cox the clearest principal operator recovered so far rather than a casual employee or witness.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-1/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-8/

### 17 Jun 1915 — explicitly a Main Street pool hall
A follow-up Courier editorial describes the same matter as involving William Myers Jr. in a **Main Street pool hall** and the related case against **Frank Cox**. This establishes the Cox pool hall on Main Street, but the recovered text does **not** give its numbered address.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-17/ed-1/seq-4/

### 30 Mar 1916 — Cox pool hall still operating
The Courier reports a man cashing a check at the **Cox poolhall**; Constable Jack Frost then called **Frank Cox from the pool hall** to identify the man. This establishes continuity of the Cox pool hall into late March 1916.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-03-30/ed-1/seq-1/

### Aug 1916 — possible but unproven household-relocation clue
An Aug. 24, 1916 social item says Mrs. J. H. Graham went to **Lyle, Washington**, to visit her sister, **Mrs. Frank Cox**. This may or may not be the wife of the pool-hall Frank Cox. No identity link has yet been established, so it must **not** be used as evidence that the Oregon City operator had moved away.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-08-24/ed-1/seq-5/

## Relationship to Raasch & Lamb

Raasch & Lamb are independently documented as pool/billiards operators in 1916. On 1 Jun 1916 the Courier reported that they would vacate their pool hall next to the Andresen Building entrance and move into the building's basement as part of a seven-store reshuffle. The June 22 report says the council had granted them a temporary permit for that basement move.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-01/ed-1/seq-5/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-22/ed-1/seq-1/

This proves Raasch & Lamb were already an established separate pool-hall operation by June 1916. It does **not** prove whether Cox sold to them, whether they were competitors, or whether Cox's hall had closed by then. No direct Cox→Raasch & Lamb succession evidence has yet been recovered.

## Why this may matter for 503 Main

The last direct exact-address occupant currently documented at **503 Main** is **L. Hylton on 12 Feb 1914**, selling cigars, tobacco, smokers' supplies, and soft drinks. A pool hall is a plausible subsequent use for a premises already associated with cigars/soft drinks, and the Cox pool hall operated on Main Street in 1915–Mar 1916.

However, **there is currently no direct evidence that Cox's pool hall was at 503 Main**. Business-type similarity and chronology are not enough to assign the address.

The Cox lead must therefore remain separate from the later **Raasch & Lamb** pool/billiards business and from the **Wade/Oldstein 504 Main** pool/billiards parlor documented in Dec. 1916.

## Research targets

1. Recover the **1916–1917 Sohns & Woodbeck directory** (FamilySearch catalog `1844954` / Oregon Historical Society) and search Frank D. Cox, Cox pool hall, William Myers Jr., Sol Cox, and 503 Main.
2. Search Oregon City council/recorder minutes for 1914–1916 pool-hall license applications or renewals naming Frank Cox and giving a numbered address.
3. Search 1915–1916 newspaper legal notices, advertisements, and business directories for `Cox`, `Frank Cox`, `F. D. Cox`, `William Myers`, `Sol Cox`, `pool`, `billiards`, and address variants around 501–505 Main.
4. Determine whether Cox sold or transferred the business, closed it, or remained a competitor after Raasch & Lamb's June 1916 move.

## Research-standard warning

The newspaper items above are strong text evidence for the existence and Main Street location of the Cox pool hall, but they do **not** resolve its exact numbered address. Do not promote Cox to 503 without a direct address source or sufficiently strong primary cross-reference.
