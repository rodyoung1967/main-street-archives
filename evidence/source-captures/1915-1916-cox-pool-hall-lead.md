# 1910–1916 Oregon City pool halls — Cox / Gross partnerships and possible 503 lead

Status: **active lead; exact Cox address after 1910 remains unresolved**

This note tracks pool-hall operators relevant to the unresolved 503 Main gap after L. Hylton's documented 1914 cigar/tobacco/soft-drink shop. The evidence now establishes **F. D. Cox as a licensed Oregon City pool/billiard operator by October 1912**, substantially earlier than the 1915 court coverage. A separate 1910 source directly places **Asay & Cox, The Pool Hall, at 708 Main**, but the identity of that Cox with Frank D. Cox is not yet proven.

## Earlier exact-address lead — Asay & Cox at 708 Main, 1910

The **23 September 1910 Oregon City Courier, page 3** business-answer list explicitly gives:

**The Pool Hall, Asay & Cox, 708 Main St.**

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3/
PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1910-09-23/ed-1/seq-3.pdf

This is strong exact-address evidence for an Asay & Cox pool hall at 708 Main in September 1910. **It is not yet proven that the Cox partner was Frank D. Cox.** Do not merge the 1910 Asay & Cox business into Frank D. Cox's later record without a direct identity source.

If the Cox partner is ultimately proven to be Frank D. Cox, this would establish that his earlier hall was on the **even-numbered side of Main**, and would substantially weaken any assumption that his later Main Street hall was automatically at 503. A later move would still be possible and would need direct documentation.

## Major new municipal anchor — F. D. Cox licensed by 2 Oct 1912

Actual **Oregon City Council minutes for 2 October 1912** state that applications for Pool and Billiard Licenses were granted to:

- **F. F. Curren**
- **F. D. Cox**
- **Bailey and Price**
- **Bert Crowley**

Primary municipal source:
https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4826171/File/document

This proves that **F. D. Cox was a licensed Oregon City pool/billiard operator by October 1912**, nearly three years before the 1915 Cox-pool-hall court reporting.

The council entry does **not** give Cox's premises address. Bailey & Price are independently documented at **527 Main** in January 1912, proving that the council list comprises multiple separate halls and cannot itself identify Cox's location.

### Why the October 1912 license matters for 503

H. H. Smith published a March 1912 notice stating that he intended to apply for a pool-hall license for his place of business at **503 Main Street**. The newly recovered October F. D. Cox license raises a specific testable question:

**Did Cox later acquire or take over Smith's 503 Main pool hall during 1912, or was Cox operating another hall elsewhere?**

There is currently **no evidence** proving Smith→Cox or placing Cox at 503. The correct next targets are Smith's council license decision/renewal, Cox's October 1912 application or bond, and any legal notice that prints Cox's premises.

## Major chronology correction — Gross pool hall already licensed in March 1914

### 5 Mar 1914 Courier — Gross & Ruconich application visually reviewed

The **5 March 1914 Oregon City Courier, page 1** reports the previous evening's council meeting. The original page scan has been visually inspected in this research pass. The article says an application for a pool-hall license was presented by **Gross & Ruconich**; Councilmen Andrews and Van Auken opposed it, but the council ultimately granted the license.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-03-05/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

OCR renders the partner name imperfectly, but the scan and local-name context support **Ruconich**. Exact first names should still be confirmed from the underlying council/license record.

### Why this changes the 503 theory

L. Hylton is directly documented at **503 Main on 12 Feb 1914 and again 23 Apr 1914**. The Gross & Ruconich pool-hall license was granted between those two exact-address Hylton anchors. Therefore the Gross-led hall was apparently **concurrent with Hylton's 503 business**, not a simple successor to it.

Treat the Gross hall as a separate unidentified Oregon City location unless direct address evidence proves otherwise.

## Gross partnership change / later license — Sept. 1914

The actual **Oregon City Council minutes for 2 September 1914** state that the **application of Gross and Hungate for a six-month Pool Hall license** was read and permission granted.

Primary municipal source:
https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4826060/File/document

The March Gross & Ruconich license followed by the September Gross & Hungate license suggests a continuing Gross-led pool-hall enterprise with a partner change or a new Gross-related operation. That is a hypothesis; the September minutes do not explicitly call it a renewal or transfer and do not give the address.

## What is directly documented for Cox in 1915–1916

### 3 Jun 1915 — Cox's pool hall on Main Street
The *Oregon City Courier* reports that officers marched down **Main Street** and next raided **Cox's pool hall**. This independently places Cox's hall on Main Street by June 1915.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-03/ed-1/seq-1/

### 10 Jun 1915 — Frank D. Cox principal/employer; William Myers Jr. partner
The Courier reports **William Myers Jr. was a partner in the Cox pool hall**. Later testimony identifies **F. D. Cox as Myers's present employer**, while **Sol Cox worked with Myers in the pool hall**.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-1/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-8/

### 17 Jun 1915 — explicitly a Main Street pool hall
A follow-up editorial describes the same matter as involving William Myers Jr. in a **Main Street pool hall** and the related case against **Frank Cox**. The recovered text does not print the street number.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-17/ed-1/seq-4/

### 30 Mar 1916 — Cox hall still operating
The Courier reports a check being cashed at the **Cox poolhall**; Constable Jack Frost called **Frank Cox from the pool hall** to identify the man. This establishes continuity into late March 1916.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-03-30/ed-1/seq-1/

### Aug 1916 social item — not evidence of relocation
An Aug. 24, 1916 social item says Mrs. J. H. Graham went to Lyle, Washington to visit her sister, Mrs. Frank Cox. A later obituary for **Ollie Jane Hugger Cox** identifies her as Frank D. Cox's wife, says they married in Oregon City in 1907, lived in Oregon City, and Frank died in 1957. The Lyle item should therefore be treated as travel/visiting, not Cox relocation.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-08-24/ed-1/seq-5/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2024240281/1983-11-10/ed-1/seq-3/

## Additional Feb. 1916 proposed entrant — P. B. Dimm

On **10 Feb 1916**, the Courier reported that **P. B. Dimm of Vancouver, Washington** was in Oregon City **looking over locations for a pool hall which he planned to open**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-10/ed-1/seq-7/

No later source recovered so far proves Dimm actually opened one.

## Relationship to Raasch & Lamb

Raasch & Lamb are independently documented pool/billiards operators in 1916. On 1 Jun 1916 the Courier reported that they would vacate their hall next to the Andresen Building entrance and move into the building's basement. The June 22 report says the council had granted a temporary permit for that basement move.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-01/ed-1/seq-5/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-22/ed-1/seq-1/

This proves Raasch & Lamb were already an established separate operation by June 1916. No direct Cox→Raasch & Lamb succession evidence has yet been recovered.

## Current implications for 503 Main

The strongest chronology is now:

- **23 Sep 1910:** Asay & Cox, The Pool Hall, **708 Main**; Cox identity with F. D. Cox unproven;
- **Mar 1912:** H. H. Smith announces intended pool-hall license application at **503 Main**;
- **2 Oct 1912:** **F. D. Cox** granted an Oregon City pool/billiard license, address omitted;
- **12 Feb & 23 Apr 1914:** L. Hylton directly at **503 Main**;
- **5 Mar 1914:** Gross & Ruconich pool-hall license granted elsewhere/unidentified;
- **2 Sep 1914:** Gross & Hungate six-month pool-hall license, address omitted;
- **3 Jun 1915:** Cox pool hall directly described as on **Main Street**, exact number omitted;
- **30 Mar 1916:** Cox hall still operating;
- **Jun 1916:** Raasch & Lamb independently operating/moving hall near Andresen Building;
- **Dec 1916:** Wade/Oldstein pool/billiard parlor directly at **504 Main**.

There is **still no direct evidence placing Frank D. Cox at 503 Main**. The October 1912 license creates a Smith→Cox takeover question but does not answer it. The 1910 Asay & Cox address, if identity is eventually proven, may point toward a different Cox premises history.
