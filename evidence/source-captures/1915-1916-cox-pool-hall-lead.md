# 1914–1916 Oregon City pool halls — Cox / Gross partnerships and possible 503 lead

Status: **active lead; exact addresses unresolved**

This note tracks pool-hall operators relevant to the unresolved 503 Main gap after L. Hylton's documented 1914 cigar/tobacco/soft-drink shop. The evidence now shows that an earlier Gross-led pool hall was already licensed while Hylton was still at 503, so the former working idea of a simple **Hylton → Gross & Hungate → Cox** succession at 503 is **downgraded**.

## Major chronology correction — Gross pool hall already licensed in March 1914

### 5 Mar 1914 Courier — Gross & Ruconich application visually reviewed

The **5 March 1914 Oregon City Courier, page 1** reports the previous evening's council meeting. The original page scan has been visually inspected in this research pass, not merely searched by OCR. The article says an application for a pool-hall license was presented by **Gross & Ruconich**; Councilmen Andrews and Van Auken opposed it, but the council ultimately granted the license.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-03-05/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

OCR renders the partner name imperfectly (`Ruconiciv`), but the scan and local-name context support **Ruconich**. Lawrence Ruconich is independently documented as a longstanding Oregon City business figure and former saloon-license holder. Exact first names of the Gross and Ruconich partners in this 1914 pool-hall partnership should still be confirmed from the underlying council minutes/license file before assigning person IDs.

### Why this changes the 503 theory

L. Hylton is directly documented at **503 Main on 12 Feb 1914 and again 23 Apr 1914**, selling cigars, tobacco, smokers' supplies and soft drinks. The Gross & Ruconich pool-hall license was granted between those two exact-address Hylton anchors.

Therefore the Gross-led pool hall was apparently **concurrent with Hylton's 503 business**, not a later business that simply replaced Hylton. This materially weakens the previous hypothesis that the September Gross & Hungate license represented the first post-Hylton pool-hall use of 503.

It remains theoretically possible that multiple operations shared premises or that address/business arrangements changed rapidly, but there is currently **no evidence** for such a shared 503 arrangement. Treat the Gross hall as a separate unidentified Oregon City location unless a direct address source proves otherwise.

## Gross partnership change / later license — Sept. 1914

The actual **Oregon City Council minutes for 2 September 1914** state that the **application of Gross and Hungate for a six-month Pool Hall license** was read and permission granted.

Primary municipal source:
https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4826060/File/document

The March Gross & Ruconich license followed by the September Gross & Hungate license suggests a continuing Gross-led pool-hall enterprise with a partner change or a new Gross-related operation. That is a research hypothesis; the September minutes do not call it a renewal or transfer and do not print a numbered address.

A six-month September license would nominally run to about **March 1915**, immediately before Frank D. Cox is directly documented with a Main Street pool hall by June 1915. A Gross→Cox business transition remains worth testing, but it can no longer be used as indirect evidence for 503 because Gross's hall already existed concurrently with Hylton at 503 in spring 1914.

### Municipal-record significance

The archived council minutes and contemporary council reporting show that individual pool-hall license actions can survive even when ordinary advertisements do not. Separate 1908 ordinance evidence shows Oregon City pool-room operators were subject to license fees and bond/surety requirements before the Recorder issued a license. Recorder/license/bond files may therefore preserve proprietor and premises details that the council-minute summaries omit.

## What is directly documented for Cox

### 3 Jun 1915 — Cox's pool hall on Main Street
The *Oregon City Courier* reports that officers, after leaving the Moose Lodge, marched down **Main Street** and next raided **Cox's pool hall**. This independently places the hall on Main Street.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-03/ed-1/seq-1/

### 10 Jun 1915 — Frank D. Cox is the principal/employer; William Myers Jr. is a partner
The *Oregon City Courier* reports that **William Myers Jr. was a partner in the Cox pool hall**. Later testimony identifies **F. D. Cox as Myers's present employer**, while **Sol Cox worked with Myers in the pool hall**. This makes Frank D. Cox the clearest principal operator recovered so far.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-1/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-10/ed-1/seq-8/

### 17 Jun 1915 — explicitly a Main Street pool hall
A follow-up Courier editorial describes the same matter as involving William Myers Jr. in a **Main Street pool hall** and the related case against **Frank Cox**. The recovered text does **not** give its numbered address.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1915-06-17/ed-1/seq-4/

### 30 Mar 1916 — Cox pool hall still operating
The Courier reports a man cashing a check at the **Cox poolhall**; Constable Jack Frost then called **Frank Cox from the pool hall** to identify the man. This establishes continuity into late March 1916.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-03-30/ed-1/seq-1/

### Aug 1916 social item — not evidence of relocation
An Aug. 24, 1916 social item says Mrs. J. H. Graham went to **Lyle, Washington**, to visit her sister, **Mrs. Frank Cox**. A later obituary for **Ollie Jane Hugger Cox** identifies her as the wife of Frank D. Cox, says they married in Oregon City in 1907, lived in Oregon City, and that Frank died in 1957. The Lyle item should therefore be treated as travel/visiting, not evidence that Cox moved away.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-08-24/ed-1/seq-5/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2024240281/1983-11-10/ed-1/seq-3/

Implication: Frank Cox's disappearance from recovered pool-hall references after March 1916 cannot presently be explained by relocation. The business may have closed, sold, changed ownership/name, or simply stopped appearing in the recovered newspaper record.

## Additional Feb. 1916 proposed pool-hall entrant — P. B. Dimm

On **10 Feb 1916**, the Courier reported that **P. B. Dimm of Vancouver, Washington**, was visiting Oregon City and **looking over locations for a pool hall which he planned to open**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-10/ed-1/seq-7/

No later source recovered so far proves Dimm actually opened a hall. This shows the local pool-hall landscape may have been expanding and cautions against assuming every later operator was Cox's successor.

## Relationship to Raasch & Lamb

Raasch & Lamb are independently documented as pool/billiards operators in 1916. On 1 Jun 1916 the Courier reported that they would vacate their pool hall next to the Andresen Building entrance and move into the building's basement. The June 22 report says the council had granted them a temporary permit for that basement move.

Sources:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-01/ed-1/seq-5/
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-06-22/ed-1/seq-1/

This proves Raasch & Lamb were already an established separate pool-hall operation by June 1916. It does **not** prove whether Cox sold to them, whether they were competitors, or whether Cox's hall had closed by then.

The 1 Jun 1916 business grid separately advertises **Raasch & Lamb — Pool and Billiards / Everything for Smokers**, reinforcing their identity as a distinct named business.

## Current implications for 503 Main

The strongest exact-address evidence remains:

- **12 Feb 1914:** L. Hylton at **503 Main**;
- **5 Mar 1914:** Gross & Ruconich pool-hall license granted somewhere in Oregon City, address not stated;
- **23 Apr 1914:** L. Hylton still at **503 Main**;
- **2 Sep 1914:** Gross & Hungate six-month pool-hall license granted, address not stated;
- **by 3 Jun 1915:** Cox pool hall on **Main Street**, exact number not stated;
- **30 Mar 1916:** Cox hall still operating;
- **Jun 1916:** Raasch & Lamb independently operating/moving their hall near the Andresen Building;
- **Dec 1916:** Wade/Oldstein pool/billiard parlor directly at **504 Main**.

The March 1914 overlap means **Gross-led pool-hall evidence should no longer be presented as a likely direct successor to Hylton at 503**. Cox at 503 remains possible only as an unresolved exact-address hypothesis; there is still no direct evidence placing him there.
