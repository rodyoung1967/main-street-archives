## 17 August 1922 Morning Oregonian — George Newton / Quality restaurant

Historic Oregon Newspapers recovered the exact page locator:

- *Morning Oregonian*, **17 August 1922**, p. **4**, image 4;
- persistent page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83025138/1922-08-17/ed-1/seq-4/
- item heading in the archive text: **“Firemen Are Dinner Guests.”**

The archive page text reports from Oregon City on 16 August that **George Newton**, proprietor of the **Quality restaurant**, entertained the Oregon City fire department at dinner because of the department's work **saving his restaurant from destruction during the recent fire in Fifth street**.

## Same-day Banner-Courier — Quality Cafe roster entry

A separate same-day Oregon City newspaper page is also recoverable through Historic Oregon Newspapers:

- *Banner-Courier*, **17 August 1922**, p. **2**, image 2;
- OCR/text page: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-08-17/ed-1/seq-2/ocr/

The “Buy at Home / Friends of Labor” business roster includes **QUALITY CAFE**. The same roster also includes **LELAND AND LITTLE, Billiards**, among many other Oregon City firms.

This page gives **no street address** for Quality Cafe. The OCR/text was recovered in this continuation, but the page image itself was not re-inspected here, so do not upgrade the Quality Cafe row beyond its present archive-text locator without visual confirmation.

## Relationship to the July 1922 fire

Existing visually verified project evidence already establishes that the **Fifth Street Restaurant** was among the businesses destroyed in the 18 July fire and that C. E. Gates then built a two-story hollow-tile replacement on the restaurant site (`S-111`–`S-113`, `S-117`; `E-096`, `E-097`, `E-103`).

If the Morning Oregonian text is confirmed directly on the page image, it would establish a useful distinction:

- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — destroyed in the fire;
- **George Newton's Quality restaurant** — reported as **saved from destruction** in that fire.

That would make the Quality business a potentially important surviving-premises anchor. It still would **not** by itself identify a numbered Main Street address.

## Quality Restaurant / Quality Cafe / Kwality caution

The same-day wording creates a strong research question but not an identity merger:

- *Morning Oregonian*: **Quality restaurant**, proprietor **George Newton**;
- *Banner-Courier*: **QUALITY CAFE**, no proprietor/address in the recovered roster;
- later project evidence: **New Kwality Cafe, 503 Main**, J. Jager proprietor, from a later matchbook and historic-resource context.

Do **not** assume:

- Quality restaurant = Quality Cafe;
- either 1922 business = the later New Kwality/Kwality Cafe;
- George Newton operated at 503 Main;
- a shared “Quality/Kwality” name proves business continuity or premises continuity.

A same-context proprietor/address source is required.

## Why this lead matters to the 1911–1925 physical-change question

The July–August 1922 fire/rebuild sequence remains the strongest documented candidate for at least part of the footprint change visible between the corrected 1911 Sanborn Sheet 16 and the 1925 Sanborn Sheet 6.

A direct address for Newton's restaurant could materially constrain the competing storefront models:

- if Newton's saved restaurant were proven at **503 Main**, that would be strong evidence that a 503 restaurant premises survived the July fire rather than being the Gates fire-destroyed restaurant site;
- if it were at another address, that hypothesis would weaken or disappear.

Until such an address is recovered, **no change to Model A / Model B is warranted**.
