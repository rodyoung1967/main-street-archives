# Red Front clothing store / Wolf & Miller identity and location research — 14 September 2026

## Purpose

Preserve the current Red Front clothing-store location problem and the project steward's proposed identity connection between **L. Wolf**, named in the 21 July 1922 Enterprise fire account, and the `Wolf` of **Wolf & Miller**. This note deliberately separates direct evidence, OCR/text discovery, and working inference.

## Direct evidence already visually verified in the archive

### Wolf & Miller at Oregon City, 1918

The archive has visually verified contemporary Oregon City Courier evidence for Wolf & Miller at the target frontage. On 10 October 1918 the business is printed as **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main**. The same item says the firm was selling shoes, men's clothing and furnishings, had an up-to-date shoe-repair department, and that after the sale **one member of the firm would go to Idaho while the other would restock the store and continue the business**.

This is important evidence of an impending division/dissolution of the partnership and of continuation by one former partner, but the advertisement as presently transcribed does **not identify which partner went to Idaho and which partner remained**. Do not silently assign those roles until another source names them.

Earlier Springfield evidence establishes Wolf & Miller as a clothing/shoe/shoe-repair concern before the Oregon City exact-address record. The Springfield News in June–July 1918 advertised that Wolf & Miller were closing out their entire stock. An August 1918 Eugene Daily Guard Springfield item reports that **C. E. Miller, of the Wolf and Miller Department store**, after closing out stock, left for Oregon City to join Mrs. Miller and family. The archive's separate Gilbert/C. E. Miller identity problem remains relevant; do not merge names solely from this item.

### July 1922 fire / Red Front

The project steward visually inspected the original Oregon City Enterprise, 21 July 1922, p.1. It identifies **L. Wolf, 33 N. 3rd St., Portland**, as owner of the **Red Front clothing store**, which was damaged by the fire. The same account says Wolf had recently purchased **the shoe-store building in which the fire started**.

The 18 July 1922 Eugene Daily Guard fire report independently lists **Red Front clothing store — damage to stock, $1,000** among the fire losses, alongside Gilbert Miller's shoe shop. This outside-city report corroborates the Red Front trade name and that it was a distinct fire-loss line, but its OCR/text was used for discovery in this pass and should not receive VISUALLY VERIFIED credit until the original page image is inspected in the annual workflow.

## Current Red Front location result

**The exact numbered address of the Red Front clothing store remains unresolved.**

The strongest present constraints are:

- Red Front was part of the July 1922 fire-loss group.
- L. Wolf owned the Red Front business according to the visually verified Enterprise account.
- Wolf also had recently purchased the shoe-store/fire-origin building.
- The fire-origin building is now reconstructed by the project as most probably 503 Main through strong cross-source inference, while the adjoining pool/billiard premises is most probably 505 Main and survived with damage.
- None of those facts proves that Red Front occupied the same room/building as Miller, nor that it occupied 501, 503, 505, 505½ or 507.
- The 13 July Harding property-sale report groups the Red Front Clothing Store with the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller's shoe store, and an adjoining barber shop, but the internal storefront order and street numbers remain to be fixed.

Evidence state for Red Front numbered location: **UNRESOLVED / WORKING SPATIAL QUESTION**.

## L. Wolf = Wolf of Wolf & Miller?

Project-steward hypothesis: it is reasonable to suspect that **L. Wolf, owner of Red Front in July 1922, is the same Wolf who had been Gilbert Miller's partner in Wolf & Miller**, especially given the business and property continuity around the same small Main Street frontage.

### Evidence favoring the connection

1. Wolf & Miller is directly documented at **505 Main in 1918** with shoes, clothing/furnishings and shoe repairing — the same general business categories later represented separately by Miller's Shoe Store and Wolf's Red Front clothing store.
2. The October 1918 Wolf & Miller notice explicitly anticipates a split: one partner would leave for Idaho and the other would restock and continue the business.
3. By July 1922, **Gilbert Miller** has a separately named shoe business in the fire group, while **L. Wolf** owns a separately named clothing business, Red Front, in that same fire/property group.
4. The Enterprise further connects L. Wolf to the shoe-store/fire-origin building through his recent purchase of that building, showing a continuing property/business relationship around Miller's premises rather than a random same-surname appearance.

### Evidence still missing

- A source giving the Wolf & Miller partner's full name or initials and matching that person to **L. Wolf**.
- A dissolution, sale, assumed-business-name, directory, tax, deed, or newspaper notice explicitly saying that Wolf & Miller divided into Miller's shoe business and Wolf's Red Front business.
- Identification of which Wolf & Miller partner went to Idaho and which remained/restocked after the October 1918 sale.
- Direct proof that the C. E. Miller wording in the 1918 Springfield report refers to Gilbert Miller rather than another Miller.

### Evidence state

**PROBABLE / WORKING IDENTITY HYPOTHESIS:** L. Wolf of the 1922 Red Front is probably the `Wolf` of Wolf & Miller, and the later Miller shoe / Wolf clothing businesses may represent the post-partnership division of the earlier firm's shoe-repair/shoe and clothing lines. The fit is meaningful enough to guide research, but it is **not yet a documented identity or documented division of assets**.

Do not upgrade this to DOCUMENTED / DIRECT until a source independently identifies the Wolf & Miller partner or explicitly describes the business division.

## New research targets

1. Search Oregon City newspapers from October 1918 through 1922 for `Red Front`, `Wolf`, `Wolf & Miller`, `Miller`, `clothing`, `shoe`, `Idaho`, `dissolution`, `partnership`, `sold`, `retire`, `restock`, and `continue business`.
2. Search for exact-address Red Front advertisements and notices, especially 1918–July 1922.
3. Identify the full name of **L. Wolf, 33 N. 3rd St., Portland** through Portland directories, deeds, tax/property records and business notices.
4. Identify the Wolf partner in Wolf & Miller from Springfield records, assumed-business-name records, directories, deeds, probate/genealogy and later notices.
5. Determine which partner left for Idaho after the October 1918 Oregon City sale and which continued the store.
6. Reconstruct the July 1922 storefront order among Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller shoe store/former Schiermann half, barber shop, Red Front and adjoining pool hall.
7. Prioritize the Harding → Wolf/Gates deeds (`MF-006`) and OHS title abstract (`MF-007`), because buyer names, dimensions, improvements or tenant descriptions may fix both Wolf identity and Red Front geometry.

## Research discipline

Do not confuse the unrelated **Red Front Barn / Red Front Livery** at Sixth & Water with the July 1922 **Red Front clothing store**. Searches produce many Red Front Barn references; those are separate businesses and are not evidence for the clothing-store address.
