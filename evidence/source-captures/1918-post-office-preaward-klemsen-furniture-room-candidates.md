## 8 August 1918 — pre-award Post Office bid names the existing tenants

The actual scanned page of the **Oregon City Courier, 8 Aug 1918, p. 1** was visually inspected on 9 Sep 2026. The item is headed **`UNOFFICIAL REPORT SAYS POST-OFFICE MAY MOVE`**.

The scan directly states that:

- the local Post Office might move from its then-current location opposite the courthouse to a new site **at Fourth and Main streets**;
- the current Weinhard-estate Post Office lease would expire **1 March 1919**;
- the government had called for bids for the following five-year period;
- **First National Bank and Barlow & David** had offered a location at **$150 monthly**;
- the proposed location was then occupied by the **Klemsen grocery store and a furniture store**;
- the proposed rooms were on **Main Street between Fourth and Fifth**.

### Important location conflict

The August report is explicitly **pre-award / unofficial** and conflicts geographically with stronger later evidence:

- **12 Sep 1918 Courier** reports the selected Post Office lease on the **east side of Main between Fifth and Sixth**;
- **18 Apr 1919 Enterprise**, visually verified, reports that the Post Office actually moved on 15 April to **Fifth and Main**, into **two store buildings combined into one room**, one owned by First National Bank and one by the Barlow-David heirs.

The newly recovered August scan confirms that the `Fourth and Main` / `between Fourth and Fifth` wording is genuinely present in the primary page and is not an OCR artifact.

Therefore do **not** silently normalize the August wording into `between Fifth and Sixth`. Preserve the possibilities that the proposed site changed before award, the August article described a different bid than the one ultimately selected, or another documentary explanation applies. The September lease decision and April 1919 actual move control the later Post Office location.

## D. M. Klemsen — exact 508 Main anchor

Independent primary newspaper evidence places **D. M. Klemsen's grocery at 508 Main Street** in 1914.

Examples:

- **5 Feb 1914 Courier** — reports Klemsen leasing the former Oregon City Shoe Store building and opening a grocery;
- **12 Feb 1914 Courier** — directly states the grocery was at **508 Main**;
- **16 Apr 1914 Courier** — business-location list again gives **D. M. Klemsen — 508 Main**.

Canonical examples:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-02-12/ed-1/seq-18/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-04-16/ed-1/seq-4/

These exact-address anchors predate August 1918 and do **not** by themselves prove that Klemsen remained at 508 in August 1918.

## Terk Bros. — exact 506 Main anchor by October 1918

The actual **10 Oct 1918 Courier** business-knowledge page has been available as a scanned/PDF source and identifies:

- **Terk Bros. — 506 Main**;
- a new- and second-hand furniture store;
- the description says that a furniture store of that character had long occupied the stand and that the current two-person firm had owned the store for years.

The **24 Oct 1918 Courier classified guide** again lists:

- **Terk Bros. Furniture Exchange — 506 Main — phone 464**.

### Candidate identity of the August `furniture store`

Because Terk Bros. was a long-established furniture concern at **506 Main** only two months after the August Post Office bid article, **Terk Bros. is a strong candidate for the unnamed furniture store** in that article.

This is **not proven**. The August article does not name the furniture business, and no same-date source recovered in this pass explicitly says `Terk Bros., 506 Main`.

## Strong but unproven 506 + 508 candidate model

Combining the source streams creates a potentially important bay model:

- Klemsen grocery — independently documented at **508 Main** in 1914;
- Terk Bros furniture — independently documented at **506 Main** in Oct 1918, with wording indicating long occupancy of the stand;
- Aug 1918 Post Office proposal — rooms occupied by **Klemsen grocery + a furniture store**;
- Apr 1919 actual Post Office — **two store rooms combined**, one First National Bank-owned and one Barlow-David-owned.

Thus **506 + 508 Main** remains a **STRONG RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS** for the two pre-Post-Office store rooms.

The visual verification of the August article does **not** upgrade that 506+508 mapping, because the article itself gives no street numbers and expressly places the proposed site between Fourth and Fifth rather than the later selected Fifth–Sixth block.

It is **not an established Post Office address** because:

1. Klemsen's exact August 1918 address has not been directly recovered;
2. the August furniture store is unnamed;
3. Terk remains documented at 506 in October 1918, after the August proposal;
4. the visually verified August article genuinely says `at Fourth and Main` / `between Fourth and Fifth`, conflicting with the later Fifth–Sixth lease/move evidence;
5. no move/vacate source has yet been recovered proving that 506 and/or 508 became the postal rooms by April 1919.

Do not state that the 1919–1922 Post Office was at 506–508 until a direct continuity/lease/address bridge is found.

## First National Bank ownership does not equal postal occupancy at 512

A separate **24 Oct 1918 Courier classified guide** lists:

- **First National Bank — 512 Main — phone 7**.

Later 1922 Banner-Courier text also repeatedly lists First National Bank at **512 Main**.

This is an important methodological constraint on the April 1919 Post Office report. The fact that First National Bank **owned one of the two rooms** leased/sublet for postal use does **not** mean the Post Office occupied the bank's own operating storefront at 512 Main. The bank was already operating at 512 before the April 1919 move and continued at 512 later.

Property ownership, bank operation, and Post Office tenancy must therefore remain separate relationships.

## Why this matters for the July 1922 fire

The fire-period Post Office is already established on the east/even side of Main in the Fifth–Sixth block. On **8 Jun 1922**, Dr. Freeze advertised **505½ Main — Opposite Postoffice**, and Miller's Shoe Store also advertised on Main **Opposite Post Office**.

If the exact Post Office bays can be proved, those `opposite` relationships may materially narrow the odd-side storefront corresponding to Gilbert Miller's pre-fire shoe shop and may help distinguish 501 / 503 / 505 in the July fire reconstruction.

At present, however, the exact postal bay remains unresolved.
