# George A. Harding family papers (OHS Mss2000) — 503/505/Harding Building archival lead

Status: **high-priority archival source lead; collection contents not yet examined item-by-item**

The Oregon Historical Society Research Library catalog identifies the **George A. Harding family papers**, collection **Mss2000**, as an open research collection spanning approximately **1895–1945**.

Catalog record:
https://ohs.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/resources/1140

## Cataloged contents relevant to the Main Street project

The collection includes:

- correspondence and documents;
- **business records**;
- **account books**;
- ephemera, diaries and a scrapbook;
- an **architectural drawing of the Harding Building on Main Street in Oregon City**;
- additional Harding-family materials and George Lee Harding blueprints/notebooks.

The finding aid states that the collection is **open for research**.

## Why this matters for 503/505

Primary newspaper evidence now indicates that the building occupied by the **Chicago Store** during the December 1913 fire was **owned by George Harding**. Independent advertisements place the Chicago Store at **505 Main** before the fire, creating a strong cross-source inference that Harding owned the fire-damaged 505 premises or its immediate predecessor structure.

The Harding family papers therefore offer several unusually high-value possibilities:

1. **Business/account records** may identify tenants, rents, repairs, insurance, construction expenses or property transactions associated with the 505 premises after the 1913 fire.
2. The **architectural drawing of the Harding Building on Main Street** may clarify which Main Street structure the Harding family called the Harding Building, its footprint, storefront divisions and relationship to the later 503/505/507 addresses.
3. Records from **1913–1920** may help determine whether the 505 predecessor building was repaired after the Chicago Store fire, replaced later, or incorporated into the c.1920 surviving building.
4. The collection may help resolve the persistent distinction between the **Harding Building**, George Harding-owned storefronts, and the later 503/505/507 numbering/footprints.

## Current caution

The catalog does **not** state that the architectural drawing is specifically 505 Main, nor does it date the drawing in the public description. Do not equate the cataloged `Harding Building` drawing with 505 or 507 without inspecting the item itself.

Likewise, the existence of Harding business records does not prove they contain tenant/rent records for the target properties. This is a source-recovery lead until the relevant folders/items are inspected.

## Target extraction

When the collection can be examined, prioritize:

- architectural drawing of the Harding Building on Main Street;
- property/business ledgers and account books covering **1911–1920**;
- entries for **Chicago Store**, **505 Main**, **503 Main**, **Harding Building**, **Main Street**, fire damage, insurance, repairs, rent, lease, construction and demolition;
- names/businesses appearing in the 1914–1918 occupancy gap;
- any property descriptions, lot/block references or storefront dimensions that can be compared with Sanborn maps.
