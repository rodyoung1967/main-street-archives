## Task 2 findings

### Ordinance identity and chronology

The 1906 newspaper sequence is consistent with Ordinance No. 357 being the formal house/building-numbering ordinance associated with the official numbering plat:

- 25 May 1906: Postmaster T. P. Randall told Council that free mail delivery required regularly numbered houses and posted street names; Council referred the matter for ordinance preparation (`E-061`).
- 6 July 1906: contemporary reporting says Mayor E. G. Caufield had approved the street-numbering ordinance and that a **plat designating the correct numbering** had been filed with Recorder Dimick. Owners could consult the plat for the **true number** of a home or place of business. The implementation report also described a 15-day posting period and minimum two-inch numerals.
- 13 July 1906: council expenses included work "in re house numbering" and "numbering plats" (`E-061`).
- 17 August 1906: reporting says the house-numbering plat was completed and remained in Recorder Dimick's office; residents were directed there to obtain assigned numbers (`E-061`).
- 3 October 1913: the recorder notice explicitly names **Ordinance No. 357** and confirms that the plat/plan remained operational municipal reference material (`E-105`).

The original ordinance scan was **not** recovered in this pass, so the complete section-by-section text, formal passage date, signature block, and any attachments have not yet been visually certified.

### Council-volume clue

The surviving Oregon City council-minutes sequence provides a useful chronological locator: the next identified volume begins 11 August 1909 and is already at **Ordinance No. 450**. This is consistent with Ordinance No. 357 belonging to the preceding 1906-era minute/ordinance volume rather than representing a later reused number. Retrieve the predecessor volume when requesting the 1906 proceedings.

### 1910 official ordinance-compilation route

A separate online route was identified through contemporary newspapers:

- In September 1910 Oregon City solicited printing for roughly 500 copies of a municipal compilation of the City Charter and ordinances, described as approximately 300 pages.
- In February 1911 the City adopted a compiled volume covering the Charter and ordinances through **8 September 1910** and placed copies in the custody of the City Recorder.
- A 1914 newspaper reference independently describes the city charter/ordinance book as having been printed by the *Oregon City Enterprise* in 1910.

If Ordinance No. 357 remained in force at compilation, this official volume is a high-value secondary route to its operative text. No digitized copy of the 1910 compilation was recovered during this pass from the major indexed book/archive searches attempted. Treat this as a retrieval route, not as a substitute for visual inspection of the original ordinance.

### Later municipal-code route

Oregon City's 1963 Municipal Code index still includes a chapter titled **"Numbering of Buildings."** This may preserve or restate provisions descended from Ordinance No. 357. The large code PDF could not be visually opened through the current research interface, and no legislative-history note tying the chapter directly to Ordinance 357 was certified. Do not treat the 1963 chapter as Ordinance 357 until inspected.

### Current City archive route

Current City of Oregon City records pages state that the City's electronic archive includes **ordinances from 1852 forward** and provide ordinance, meeting-minute, and public-records search/request routes. The old ORMS/WebDrawer / current records-portal endpoints needed for the 1906 material were not usable from this environment (access/cache/403-style failures during the pass).

This materially changes the retrieval assessment: the present problem is **access to an identified municipal holding**, not evidence that the ordinance was destroyed or lost.
