## Task 2 findings

### 1906 numbering chronology

The 1906 newspaper sequence establishes a formal street-numbering program but, until the original ordinance register/header is recovered, should not by itself be used to prove that every 1906 reference is specifically to Ordinance No. 357:

- 25 May 1906: Postmaster T. P. Randall told Council that free mail delivery required regularly numbered houses and posted street names; Council referred the matter for ordinance preparation (`E-061`).
- 6 July 1906: contemporary reporting says Mayor E. G. Caufield had approved a street-numbering ordinance and that a **plat designating the correct numbering** had been filed with Recorder Dimick. Owners could consult the plat for the **true number** of a home or place of business. The implementation report also described a 15-day posting period and minimum two-inch numerals.
- 13 July 1906: council expenses included work "in re house numbering" and "numbering plats" (`E-061`).
- 17 August 1906: reporting says the house-numbering plat was completed and remained in Recorder Dimick's office; residents were directed there to obtain assigned numbers (`E-061`).
- 24 August 1906: a *Courier* council report says five ordinances were passed, including measures "providing for the naming of streets" and "providing for numbering of houses." This later report creates a chronology question rather than proof of a second system: it may reflect a companion/revised measure, a procedural stage, or reporting differences. Preserve the conflict pending council minutes and ordinance records.
- 3 October 1913: the recorder notice explicitly names **Ordinance No. 357** and confirms that a plat/plan remained operational municipal reference material (`E-105`).

The original Ordinance No. 357 scan was **not** recovered in this pass, so its complete section-by-section text, formal passage/approval date, title, signature block, and attachments have not been visually certified. The direct link between the July/August 1906 ordinance reports and the number **357** therefore remains a high-value proposition to test, not a fact to assume.

### 1908 search-result false lead resolved

A discovery search surfaced a 13 February 1908 *Morning Oregonian* item about a city preparing a complete house-numbering system using **100 numbers to each block, even numbers on one side and odd numbers on the other**. The search snippet placed this near Oregon City material and initially created a possible chronology conflict.

The original page was then opened and visually checked. Its headline is **"McMinnville to Number Houses"** and its dateline is **"M'MINNVILLE, Or., Feb. 12."** The following separate item on the page concerns a Clackamas teachers' examination and carries an Oregon City dateline. The house-numbering item therefore concerns **McMinnville, not Oregon City**.

Accordingly:

- the 1908 item is **excluded** from the Oregon City numbering chronology;
- the **100-numbers-per-block** rule and **odd/even-side** rule in that article must **not** be attributed to Oregon City or Ordinance No. 357;
- the apparent 1908 conflict is closed as a search-context false positive.

### Council-volume clue

The surviving Oregon City council-minutes sequence provides a useful chronological locator: a later identified volume begins 11 August 1909 and is already at **Ordinance No. 450**. This is compatible with Ordinance No. 357 predating that volume, but does not by itself establish a 1906 date. Retrieve the immediately preceding minute/ordinance volume and the ordinance register when requesting the original record.

### 1910 official ordinance-compilation route

A separate online route was identified through contemporary newspapers:

- In September 1910 Oregon City solicited printing for roughly 500 copies of a municipal compilation of the City Charter and ordinances, described as **about 800 pages**.
- In February 1911 the City adopted a compiled volume covering the Charter and ordinances through **8 September 1910** and placed copies in the custody of the City Recorder.
- A 1914 newspaper reference independently describes the city charter/ordinance book as having been printed by the *Oregon City Enterprise* in 1910.

If Ordinance No. 357 remained in force at compilation, this official volume is a high-value secondary route to its operative text. No digitized copy of the 1910 compilation was recovered during this pass from the major indexed book/archive searches attempted. Treat this as a retrieval route, not as a substitute for visual inspection of the original ordinance.

### Later municipal-code route

Oregon City's 1963 Municipal Code index still includes a chapter titled **"Numbering of Buildings."** This may preserve or restate provisions descended from an earlier numbering ordinance. The large code PDF could not be visually opened through the current research interface, and no legislative-history note tying the chapter directly to Ordinance No. 357 was certified. Do not treat the 1963 chapter as Ordinance No. 357 until inspected.

### Current City archive route

Current City of Oregon City records pages state that the City's electronic archive includes **ordinances from 1852 forward** and provide ordinance, meeting-minute, and public-records search/request routes. The old ORMS/WebDrawer / current records-portal endpoints needed for the early material were not usable from this environment (access/cache/403-style failures during the pass).

This materially changes the retrieval assessment: the present problem is **access to an identified municipal holding**, not evidence that the ordinance was destroyed or lost.
