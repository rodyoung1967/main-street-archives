### Visual observation and limits

The supplied 400 × 400 crop visibly shows the upper portion of a storefront fascia reading **GEO. A. HARDING** or closely equivalent lettering; the crop excludes the street number and most of the storefront. The institution—not the visible pixels—identifies the business as George Harding Drug Store and the location as 505 Main.

Because Oregon City's formal citywide street-numbering work is documented in 1906, the catalog's **505 Main** label may be a retrospective location identification rather than proof that the number 505 was physically displayed or officially used in 1880. The structured `1880` field and the image's `1880s` caption have different precision; both are preserved, and the archive does not silently choose an exact exposure year. This record does not prove the present 505 building existed, that the later Harding Building was at 505, or uninterrupted Harding occupancy.

### Visual observation and limits

The photograph visibly shows **505** above the entrance and **Young's Secondhand Store** on both display windows. Four men stand at the storefront. The institutional description identifies George Young as the man in the white shirt without a coat; the archive does not independently identify the other three men.

This is direct visual evidence for the Young trade name and 505 doorway, combined with institutional evidence for the 1910 date and George Young identification. It materially predates the existing 1912/1914 507 Main records and strengthens the March 1911 reported move-one-door-north sequence. It does not, by itself, prove the exact move day, the name of the building at 505, property ownership, uninterrupted operation, or that the photographed structure is the surviving 505 building.

## Integrated chronology

- The Harding catalog record places an earlier Harding drug-store image at the location later cataloged as 505, but the address and date are catalog metadata and the pre-1906 number may be retrospective.
- The Young photograph visibly establishes the **505** doorway and store name in the institutionally dated 1910 image.
- A March 1911 newspaper report then says Young moved his second-hand store one door north from a Harding Building premises into the Willamette Building (`E-085`).
- Exact records place Young at **507 Main** in 1912 and 1914 (`E-092`).

Together, the 1910 photograph plus the 1911 move report and later 507 records strongly support a 505→507 Young sequence and a 507/Willamette correspondence. They do not independently prove that the 1911 origin building was the same 1880 Harding storefront, that it was formally called the Harding Building in 1880, or that any pictured structure survives.
