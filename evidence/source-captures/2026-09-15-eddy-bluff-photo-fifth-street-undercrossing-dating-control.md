# Ralph Eddy bluff photograph — Fifth Street undercrossing dating control

Date researched: 2026-09-15

## Purpose
Test the project-steward observation that Fifth Street does not appear to continue beneath the Southern Pacific tracks in the user-supplied Ralph Eddy/CCHS bluff photograph captioned `BUSINESS SECTION OF OREGON CITY, OREGON FROM THE BLUFFS`, and determine whether that feature can constrain the photograph date.

## Documentary chronology

### 1921–1922 planning
The Oregon State Highway Commission 1921–1922 biennial report describes the finally selected Canemah–Oregon City alignment as running from the east end of the new bridge at Seventh Street, via Seventh to Railroad Avenue, south on the west side of the Southern Pacific tracks to Fifth Street, then **under grade by a proposed new structure at Fifth Street** to the east side of the tracks, continuing south toward Canemah. Thus the Fifth Street railroad undercrossing was not an ancient/permanent feature; it was a new highway project.

Contemporary November–December 1922 Oregon City reporting documents continuing dispute over the south-end highway routing and the cost of improving Railroad Avenue. This confirms that the intended Seventh/Railroad/Fifth route and Fifth Street undercrossing were still an active project rather than an old inherited street configuration.

### March 1923 contract
Capital Journal, 21 Mar 1923, reports that the approximately 0.9-mile Pacific Highway grading contract between Fifth Street in Oregon City and Canemah had been awarded to Rejotte & Winters, with completion specified for 31 Dec 1923. It explicitly states that the new alignment crosses **under the railroad at Fifth Street**, and that Southern Pacific's contribution was construction of the undercrossing.

Capital Journal, 16 Mar 1923, likewise describes traffic from the new bridge down Seventh to Railroad Avenue, then to Fifth and under the Southern Pacific tracks toward Canemah.

### By October 1925 the grade was carrying traffic
Capital Journal, 7 Oct 1925, reports preliminary work for concrete paving of the Pacific Highway grade between Oregon City and Canemah. The article says the highway would remain open to traffic while one half was paved at a time, and describes motorists using/detouring from the grade. A same-day Eugene Morning Register brief states the Pacific Highway outlet south from Oregon City was closed for paving operations on one of the last remaining highway gaps.

This is strong evidence that by October 1925 the new Oregon City–Canemah grade was an established traffic route. Because the documented route crossed beneath the railroad at Fifth Street, the Fifth Street undercrossing must have been functionally in place by then, even though the final concrete paving was only beginning.

## Photograph observation
In the supplied photograph, the railroad corridor at the apparent Fifth Street alignment is visible, but no confidently identifiable developed Fifth Street roadway/undercrossing continuation has yet been resolved from the image. The project steward specifically noted that Fifth Street appears not to cross beneath the railroad and continue toward the hill/Canemah.

This is potentially important, but perspective and railroad equipment/foreground structures may obscure the undercrossing. Therefore **absence in the photograph is not yet proof that the undercrossing did not exist**.

## Dating implication
If geometric registration against the 1925 Sanborn and a securely dated post-construction photograph confirms that the Fifth Street undercrossing should be plainly visible in this view, its apparent absence would favor a date before the undercrossing became operational. That would conflict with other current clues (1923 replacement Elks Lodge; possible later Kwality sign) and would require resolving which identification is wrong.

Conversely, if the undercrossing can be located but is simply hidden/foreshortened in this bluff view, it does not constrain the date.

For now classify the Fifth Street observation as **STRONG RESEARCH LEAD / UNRESOLVED VISUAL CONTROL**, not as a terminus ante quem.

## Important correction / separation
Do not conflate the 1923–25 Southern Pacific railroad undercrossing at Fifth Street, part of the Pacific Highway south-end route, with the later 1936–37 Fifth Street subway / Bluff Boulevard project. They are distinct construction episodes/features.
