# 1916 Isaac Tolpolar Main Street location check — 11 September 2026

## Question

Can I. Tolpolar / Isaac Tolpolar be associated with a numbered Main Street address in Oregon City during the late-1916 liquidation of his second-hand furniture/hardware store?

## Result

**A Main Street location is directly supported; an exact 1916 street number is not yet proven.**

The strongest late-1916 evidence is:

1. The 23 November 1916 *Oregon City Courier* liquidation advertisement identifies **I. Tolpolar** and places the store on **Main Street, next door to the First National Bank**. The advertisement says Tolpolar is quitting because of his health and is liquidating the stock, fixtures, and shelving.
2. The 21 December 1916 *Courier*, p. 2, visually shows a large advertisement headed **“I. Tolpolar’s Auction Sale”**. It says the doctor says he must quit and that the entire stock of furniture, hardware, carpets, kitchenware, etc. will be sold under the hammer. No street number is printed in this advertisement.
3. The 18 January 1917 *Courier* reports an explosion in the **Main street second hand store of I. Tolpolar**.
4. The 1 February 1917 *Courier* obituary identifies him as **Isaac Tolpolar**, says he had done business in his **store on Main street for almost 25 years**, and links his recent retirement from business to the explosion and his heart trouble.

These records support a late-1916/early-1917 identity chain: **I. Tolpolar = Isaac Tolpolar**, operator of a long-running Main Street second-hand store, with the November 1916 store described as next door to the First National Bank.

## 514 Main lead — not yet promoted to Isaac Tolpolar

A visually inspected 6 August 1914 *Courier* advertisement gives **514 Main Street** for **D. Tolpolar**. The original scan visibly prints the initial **D**, not I. The ad concerns furniture, hardware, cutlery, paints, glass, wall/building/roofing paper, graniteware, tinware, crockery, and glassware, and says D. Tolpolar will manage the store for creditors.

This is a **strong location/business-continuity lead** because the merchandise class closely resembles Isaac Tolpolar’s later second-hand/furniture/hardware business. However, the present evidence does **not** establish that D. Tolpolar and Isaac Tolpolar are the same person, nor that the late-1916 I. Tolpolar store still occupied 514 Main.

Therefore:

- **Do not assign Isaac Tolpolar to 514 Main as a verified fact.**
- Preserve **514 Main** as a candidate address requiring an identity/continuity bridge.
- Preserve the late-1916 store location as **Main Street, next door to First National Bank** until a contemporaneous numbered-address source is recovered.

## Additional context

Later evidence places the First National Bank at 512 Main Street (for example, a 1922 advertisement), making 514 Main geographically plausible as an adjacent-address hypothesis. That later address does not by itself prove the bank had the same number in November 1916 or that Tolpolar occupied 514 in 1916.

## Evidence handling

- Original 1914 page was visually inspected; the initial is visibly **D. Tolpolar**.
- Original 21 December 1916 p. 2 was visually inspected; the advertisement visibly reads **I. Tolpolar’s Auction Sale** and **I. TOLPOLAR**.
- Newspaper OCR/search text is discovery/support only; visual inspection controls where available.
- Business operation, business ownership, premises address, and property ownership remain separate.
