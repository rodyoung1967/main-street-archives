## 507 / Harding Building handling note

The **present 507-511 Main building is officially/inventory-named the Harding Building** and dates to 1968 after the 1 September 1967 fire.

There is also contemporary evidence for a **Harding Building in 1909-1913**, associated with George A. Harding / Harding Drug and with Main/Fifth-Sixth block descriptions.

However, the surviving 1911 sources do **not** directly print an exact number for the earlier Harding Building. One 1911 account says George Young moved one door north from a Harding Building premises into the Willamette Building; another describes a Harding Building on Sixth Street between Main and Railroad Avenue.

Therefore:

- The fact that today's 507-511 replacement is named **Harding Building** is historically significant and may reflect continuity of site/name/ownership.
- It is reasonable to keep **507 as a serious candidate for the historical Harding Building relationship**.
- But the modern building name must **not** by itself be used as proof that every 1909-1913 reference to "the Harding Building" means exactly 507 Main.
- The earlier reconstruction placing the Hogg Bros. 1913 Harding Building premises at 505 remains a **high-confidence reconstruction, not direct fact**, and should remain open to revision if deed, directory, Sanborn, assessor, or newspaper evidence proves the older Harding Building footprint differently.

Related evidence: `E-085-1911-harding-building-location-ambiguity.md` and `buildings/507-harding-building.md`.

## 501 Main handling note

Current archive evidence supports a stricter statement than some earlier working language:

> **The 1925 Sanborn map is the only recovered source that explicitly identifies a premises by the numbered address `501 Main`.**

Additional evidence exists for a physical/business location at the Fifth-and-Main corner:

- 1915-1917 newspaper ads place Holman/Randall/Holman undertaking operations at **Fifth and Main Streets**, but do not print `501 Main`.
- A distinct corner structure is visible in the 1936 aerial evidence and disappears by 1944, but aerial photography does not independently supply the street number.

Therefore the archive should not assign any pre-1925 person or business to **501 Main** merely because it occupied the Fifth-and-Main corner. Until another numbered directory, deed, tax, permit, or newspaper source is found, **501 Main as a numbered address is Sanborn-only evidence**.

Related file: `buildings/501-main.md`.

## 1913 conclusions worth carrying forward

1. **Post Office geography is substantially resolved.** The old Post Office remained in use throughout 1913 and moved to the courthouse-area replacement only on 28-29 March 1914. Multiple independent geographic clues converge on probable **513 Main** for the old location.
2. **Dunn → Lent succession is established.** F. G. Lent purchased M. E. Dunn's confectionery near/next to the old Post Office in September 1913. Probable **515 Main** remains reconstructed rather than directly numbered.
3. **505 changed during 1913.** Chicago Store is directly at 505 in January; Hogg Bros. occupies the Harding Building four doors south of the Post Office by May, strongly suggesting the same 505-area storefront under the current reconstruction.
4. **507/509/511 show strong continuity**, even where the recovered 1913 ads use landmark/building/telephone identifiers rather than printing exact numbers.
5. **503 remains unresolved.** Ellerd Bailey clearly operated a Main Street pool room in 1913, but no source recovered in this pass proves it was 503.
6. **527 remains a predecessor-building/address-layout problem.** Price Bros. and Bailey & Price were separate contemporary businesses; intersection and numbered-address evidence must not be forced into a modern one-storefront model.
7. **501 is not part of the proved 1913 numbered-address sequence.** No recovered 1913 evidence uses that number.

## Open questions carried forward — not blockers

- Exact 1913 occupant of 503 Main.
- Direct source printing old Post Office as 513 Main.
- Direct source printing Dunn/Lent as 515 Main.
- Exact footprint/address relationship of the pre-1967 Harding Building(s), Willamette Building, and modern 507-511 Harding Building.
- Whether Hogg Bros. can be directly tied to 505 Main rather than reconstructed there.
- Exact 1913 numbered occupancy/configuration at 527 and relationship between Bailey & Price, Price Bros., and the Grand Theatre geography.
- First non-Sanborn source explicitly using the numbered address 501 Main.
- Oregon City pages of the 1913-1914 Polk directory/gazetteer.

These questions remain active research leads and can be revisited when a new source appears. They do not justify holding the chronological project at 1913.
