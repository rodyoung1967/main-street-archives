# 1912 Oregon City Dye Works — separate cleaning/dyeing business at 319 Main

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED primary source; continuity to 1922 unresolved**

Purpose: document a separate Oregon City cleaning/dyeing concern so the July 1922 fire report's unnamed `cleaning-and-dyeing establishment` is not treated as if Pantorium were the city's only possible match.

## 3 March 1912 — Oregon City Dye Works at 319 Main

The actual scanned page of the **Morning Enterprise, 3 Mar 1912, p. 2** was visually inspected.

Under the classified heading **DYEING AND STEAM CLEANING**, the advertisement reads:

- **OREGON CITY DYE WORKS — 319 Main street**;
- French dry and steam cleaning;
- repairing, alterations and relining;
- ladies' and gentlemen's clothing cleaned, pressed and dyed;
- curtains, carpets, blankets, furs and auto covers;
- work called for and delivered;
- phone Main 389;
- proprietors/contacts printed as **Mrs. J. Tamblyn** and **Mrs. J. Frank Sipe** on the visually reviewed page.

Canonical PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-03/ed-1/seq-2.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Why this matters for the 1922 fire

The same-day **18 Jul 1922 Eugene Daily Guard** account says the Main Street ignition room had reportedly been occupied until only a few days before the fire by a **cleaning-and-dyeing establishment**.

Pantorium Cleaners is a legitimate 1922 candidate because it is independently proven active in the trade in 1922. However, this 1912 source proves that Oregon City had at least one **other named cleaning/dyeing business** in the preceding decade.

Therefore the archive must not reason from business type alone that the unnamed 1922 cleaner/dyer was Pantorium.

This source does **not** establish that Oregon City Dye Works survived until 1922, occupied 501/503/505 Main, or had any connection to Alex Schieremann, S. Wolf, Pantorium, or the fire-origin room.

## Later lead

A 23 Apr 1914 Oregon City Courier profile appears in OCR/text to place **Oregon City Dye Works at 210 Seventh Street**, suggesting the concern moved after the 319 Main period. That specific 1914 item has not yet been visually certified for this capture and remains a follow-up rather than a promoted fact here.

## Confidence

- Oregon City Dye Works at 319 Main on 3 Mar 1912: **Very High / visually verified**
- business performed cleaning, pressing, dyeing and French dry/steam cleaning: **Very High / visually verified**
- same business continued to 1914: **OCR/text lead pending visual review**
- same business continued to 1922: **Unproven**
- Oregon City Dye Works = July 1922 ignition-room cleaner/dyer: **No evidence / hypothesis not supported at present**
