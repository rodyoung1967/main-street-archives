# Frank D. Cox — 514 Jefferson residence and downtown billiards/cigar business

Status: **official historic-resource corroboration; business address still unresolved**

Oregon City's Historic Resource Survey Form for **514 Jefferson Street** provides a useful independent biographical/business anchor for Frank D. Cox.

The survey states that **Frank and Ollie Cox purchased the 514 Jefferson house in 1915** and owned it until 1931. It also states that **Cox operated a billiards room and cigar store in downtown Oregon City**.

Official City source:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/7953/514_Jefferson

This materially corroborates the newspaper evidence identifying **Frank D. Cox / Cox's pool hall** in 1915–1916. It confirms that billiards/cigar-store operation was Frank Cox's recognized occupation in Oregon City's later preservation research, rather than merely a label arising from the 1915 liquor-law court case.

## What this does NOT establish

The historic-resource survey excerpt recovered in the current pass does **not** print the street address of Cox's billiards/cigar business. It therefore does not establish that Cox operated at:

- 503 Main;
- 708 Main;
- or any other specific Main Street number.

The exact premises still require a directory, license/bond record, advertisement, or other direct address source.

## Relationship to Asay & Cox

A September 1910 newspaper source directly places **Asay & Cox, The Pool Hall, at 708 Main**. F. D. Cox is independently present in Oregon City records during the same month, making him a plausible candidate for the Cox partner, but that identity is still unproven.

If a future source proves Frank D. Cox was the Cox in Asay & Cox, the chronology would become especially important:

- c. May 1910 — Asay & Cox apparently opens;
- Sep. 1910 — Asay & Cox at 708 Main;
- Oct. 1912 — F. D. Cox receives an Oregon City pool/billiard license, address omitted;
- 1915 — official historic-resource research says Frank Cox operates a downtown billiards room/cigar store;
- Jun. 1915–Mar. 1916 — newspapers directly document Cox's Main Street pool hall.

That would create a strong question of address continuity or relocation from 708 Main, but it still would not itself place Cox at 503.

## Research target

Locate the source(s) used by the 514 Jefferson historic-resource survey for Frank Cox's occupation. The 1916–1917 Sohns & Woodbeck directory is a plausible source because Oregon City historic-resource research used that directory extensively, but this has not yet been proven for this specific survey statement. Recovering the survey bibliography or underlying research notes may expose Cox's business address directly.
