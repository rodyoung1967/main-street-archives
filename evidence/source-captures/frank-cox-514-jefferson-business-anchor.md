# Frank D. Cox — 514 Jefferson residence and downtown billiards/cigar business

Status: **official historic-resource corroboration; Frank D. Cox identity now strongly linked; business address still unresolved**

Oregon City's Historic Resource Survey Form for **514 Jefferson Street** provides a useful independent biographical/business anchor for Frank D. Cox.

The survey states that **Frank and Ollie Cox purchased the 514 Jefferson house in 1915** and owned it until 1931. It also states that **Cox operated a billiards room and cigar store in downtown Oregon City**.

Official City source:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/7953/514_Jefferson

## Identity link — the survey's Frank Cox is Frank D. Cox

A **10 November 1983 Sandy Post obituary for Ollie Jane Hugger Wilson** states that she married **Frank D. Cox** in Oregon City on **3 July 1907** and that the couple lived in Oregon City. The obituary says Frank D. Cox died in 1957.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2024240281/1983-11-10/ed-1/seq-3/

Read together with the City survey, this creates a strong identity chain:

1. Ollie Jane Hugger married **Frank D. Cox** in Oregon City in 1907.
2. The City survey says **Frank and Ollie Cox** bought 514 Jefferson in 1915.
3. The same survey says **Frank Cox operated a billiards room and cigar store in downtown Oregon City**.

Therefore the billiards/cigar-store operator described in the 514 Jefferson survey can now be identified with high confidence as **Frank D. Cox**, the same name appearing in Oregon City pool/billiard licensing records. This materially strengthens the link between the municipal **F. D. Cox** pool-hall evidence and the later preservation biography.

This does **not** by itself prove that every historical reference to a Frank Cox in Oregon City is the same man. In particular, the separate August 1916 social reference to a **Mrs. Frank Cox in Lyle, Washington** remains identity-ambiguous unless a direct family link is established.

This materially corroborates the newspaper evidence identifying **Frank D. Cox / Cox's pool hall** in 1915–1916. It confirms that billiards/cigar-store operation was Frank D. Cox's recognized occupation in Oregon City's later preservation research, rather than merely a label arising from the 1915 liquor-law court case.

## Source-chain advance — 2002 HPNW survey explicitly used the 1916 Oregon City directory

The companion **2002 McLoughlin Conservation District Re-Survey Final Report** explains the methodology used to prepare the individual historic-resource forms. It says consultants first reviewed the earlier 1982–1986 survey information and then conducted background research on individual resources using:

- Sanborn Fire Insurance Maps;
- city building-permit records;
- county deed records; and
- **city directories**.

The report's bibliography explicitly lists **Oregon City directories for 1891, 1897, 1909, 1916, 1933, 1941, 1947, 1953, 1958, 1960, 1962, 1964 and 1965**.

Official City report:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4359/2002-McLoughlin-Survey-Final-Report-PDF

The methodology and bibliography pages were visually inspected in the current research pass. This substantially strengthens the **1916 directory as a likely route to the underlying Cox business evidence**.

However, the final report does not provide source-by-source footnotes for the individual 514 Jefferson statement. Therefore it is **not yet proven that the sentence about Cox's billiards room and cigar store came specifically from the 1916 directory**. It could derive from the earlier 1982–86 survey, deeds plus directory data, another directory year, or a combination of sources.

The correct next step is to recover either:

1. the actual **1916 Oregon City directory entry for Frank D. Cox**, or
2. the earlier **1982–86 514 Jefferson survey/research worksheet** reviewed by HPNW.

Either could reveal the exact business address or the original source behind the occupation statement.

## Northwest Heritage Index check — useful only as a selective pointer

The Wilsonville Public Library's **Northwest Heritage Index with Canby obituaries** includes selected entries sourced from the 1916 Clackamas County directory. The Cox and Hylton alphabetical pages were visually inspected in the current pass. They do **not** contain a Frank D. Cox directory entry or the relevant L. Hylton business entry.

This is **not negative evidence about the original 1916 directory**. The Heritage Index is selective and plainly does not reproduce every directory entry. Its silence cannot be used to infer that Cox or Hylton were absent from the 1916 directory.

Source:
https://www.wilsonvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/library/page/26561/northwest_heritage_index_with_canby_obits.pdf

## What this does NOT establish

The historic-resource survey does **not** print the street address of Cox's billiards/cigar business. It therefore does not establish that Cox operated at:

- 503 Main;
- 708 Main;
- or any other specific Main Street number.

The exact premises still require a directory, license/bond record, advertisement, or other direct address source.

## Relationship to Asay & Cox

A September 1910 newspaper source directly places **Asay & Cox, The Pool Hall, at 708 Main**. F. D. Cox is independently present in Oregon City records during the same month, making him a plausible candidate for the Cox partner, but that identity is still unproven.

If a future source proves Frank D. Cox was the Cox in Asay & Cox, the chronology would become especially important:

- c. May 1910 — Asay & Cox apparently opens;
- Sep. 1910 — Asay & Cox at 708 Main;
- Oct. 1912 — F. D. Cox receives an Oregon City pool/billiard license, address omitted;
- 1915 — official historic-resource research says Frank D. Cox operates a downtown billiards room/cigar store;
- Jun. 1915–Mar. 1916 — newspapers directly document Cox's Main Street pool hall.

That would create a strong question of address continuity or relocation from 708 Main, but it still would not itself place Cox at 503.
