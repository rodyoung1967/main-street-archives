# 1922 — Miller's Shoe Store / Gilbert Miller continuity lead

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED primary advertisements + STRONG IDENTITY/CONTINUITY INFERENCE; exact pre-fire street number and business-name identity not yet directly proved**

Purpose: preserve the unusually tight trade and fire-continuity evidence connecting the pre-fire `Millers Shoe Store` advertisements to Gilbert Miller, while keeping the missing explicit pre-fire proprietor statement and exact pre-fire storefront unresolved.

## 23 February 1922 — Millers Shoe Store opposite Post Office

The actual scanned page of the **Banner-Courier, 23 Feb 1922, p. 3** has been visually inspected.

The advertisement reads substantially:

- **Millers Shoe Store**
- Men's and Boys' Shoes, Gloves, Laces
- Shoe Polish, Oils and Greases
- **EXPERT SHOE REPAIRING**
- **Main Street — Opposite Post Office**

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-02-23/ed-1/seq-3.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

The ad does **not** print a proprietor's first name, surname with apostrophe, or exact numbered street address. Therefore it does not by itself prove that Gilbert Miller operated the business or identify 501, 503, or 505 Main.

## 20 July 1922 — fire report names Gilbert Miller

The visually reviewed local fire report in the **Banner-Courier, 20 Jul 1922** identifies **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop** among the destroyed businesses and reports that his **stock of shoes** was destroyed.

Related capture:

This is especially important because it combines the same two functions seen in the pre-fire `Millers Shoe Store` ads: **shoe retail stock + shoe repairing**.

## 21 September 1922 — Gilbert Miller resumed shoe repair; correction to misassigned address

The original **Banner-Courier, 21 Sep 1922, p. 5** page was **visually re-inspected 15 November 2026** at https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-09-21/ed-1/seq-5.pdf.

The upper-right **Gilbert Miller** boxed advertisement says he sold out the hardware, has new workingmen's clothing/shoes/gloves, and his machinery is ready for the same shoe repairing as before the fire. The ad ends with **GILBERT MILLER** and **Formerly Goldman's Second-Hand Store**. **It does not state a street number.**

**CORRECTION / SUPERSEDED:** The previously recorded **209 Fifth Street** address belongs to the **separate Portland Overtire Co. advertisement directly below** Miller's ad, inside its own ruled box. The original prior interpretation incorrectly merged the neighboring advertisements. **209 Fifth is NOT direct evidence for Gilbert Miller.** This correction supersedes the earlier 209 Fifth attribution without deleting its history.

This scan is direct evidence for Miller resuming his pre-fire trade, **not** for an intermediate move. See `evidence/source-captures/2026-11-15-1922-miller-september21-visual-correction.md`.

## 26 October 1922 — Miller's Shoe Store at 419 Main

The actual scanned page of the **Banner-Courier, 26 Oct 1922, p. 2** has now been visually inspected.

A boxed advertisement clearly reads:

- **MILLER'S SHOE STORE**
- Men's every-day-wear shoes, rubbers, gloves
- **Expert Shoe Repairing**
- **419 Main St.**
- Oregon City

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-10-26/ed-1/seq-2.pdf

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

The verified post-fire **419 Main** location is documented on 24 August and 26 October. The 21 September advertisement gives no address and does not establish an intermediate move. The sequence materially strengthens the business-identity inference, but the October ad still does **not** print Gilbert Miller's first name. The archive therefore does not yet promote `Miller's Shoe Store = Gilbert Miller` from strong inference to direct proof.

## Current identity assessment

The combined evidence makes it a **strong inference** that the pre-fire **Millers Shoe Store** and the post-fire **Miller's Shoe Store** were Gilbert Miller's business:

1. the store name is `Millers` / `Miller's`;
2. it sold shoes and gloves while advertising expert shoe repairing;
3. the July fire report names **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop** and specifically mentions his **stock of shoes**;
4. the September signed Gilbert Miller ad says his shoe machinery was restored for the same shoe repairing he had done **before the fire** and again sells shoes/gloves;
5. by 26 October the `MILLER'S SHOE STORE` name reappears at **419 Main** with the same shoes/gloves + expert-repairing business pattern.

However, the project has **not yet recovered a source explicitly saying `Gilbert Miller, proprietor` of `Miller's/Millers Shoe Store`**. Under the archive standard, the identity therefore remains a **strong inference rather than proven fact**.

## Fire-map implication

The pre-fire Miller's Shoe Store was advertised on **Main Street opposite the Post Office**. Separate evidence places the fire-period Post Office on the east/even side of Main between Fifth and Sixth, within the later **504–514 Main** range, and Dr. Freeze at **505½ Main** also advertised `Opposite Postoffice`.

If the Gilbert-Miller/store identity is eventually directly proved, these advertisements would place Gilbert's pre-fire shop on the **odd-numbered west side of Main in the fire-area frontage**, but they still would **not by themselves identify 501, 503, or 505**.

Do not use this evidence to assign Miller's shop to a specific modern storefront until either:

- the pre-fire store receives an exact street number in a primary source; or
- the exact Post Office bay and a sufficiently precise spatial relationship independently establish the storefront.

The post-fire **419 Main** address is a later relocation and must not be backdated to the pre-fire shop.
