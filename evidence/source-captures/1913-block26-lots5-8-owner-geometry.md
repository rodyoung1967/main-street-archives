# 1913 Block 26 Lots 5–8 — owner and geometry reconstruction

Status: **1913 newspaper scan visually verified; official original plat visually verified; 1925 Sanborn visually verified; lot-to-storefront alignment remains a strong map inference, not cadastral proof**

Purpose: consolidate the original Block 26 Main-side lot sequence between Fifth and Sixth, correct the earlier 50-foot working model, and focus the July 1922 S. Wolf property search.

Related evidence: `E-064`, `E-094`
Related sources: `S-021`, `S-078`, `S-107`, `S-108`

## 23 October 1913 Morning Enterprise assessment notice — VISUALLY VERIFIED

The actual full-page scan of *Morning Enterprise*, 23 October 1913, page 8, was inspected at high resolution on 24 August 2026. The relevant assessment notice is legible on the page itself; this is no longer an OCR-only finding.

Canonical page PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

The notice describes the assessment boundary as running east along Fifth Street to the line between **Lots 4 and 5, Block 26**, then northerly along the line separating **Lots 1, 2, 3, 4** from **Lots 5, 6, 7, 8** to the south line of Sixth Street.

The same visually inspected notice lists the four lots in the Main-side row:

- **Lot 5 — Mary E. Barlow and Juliette E. David — $10.71**
- **Lot 6 — First National Bank — $10.71**
- **Lot 7 — Frank Jaggar — $21.42**
- **Lot 8 — Gambrinus Brewing Company — $32.13**

The archive text layer remains useful for discovery, but the claims above now rest on direct page-image inspection (`S-107`).

## Independent Lot 8 anchor

Separate 1906 primary newspaper / realty-transfer evidence registered as `E-064` ties the **William Tell Hotel at Sixth & Main** to **Lot 8, Block 26**. The reported improved property measured approximately **61 by 100 feet** and was sold to Gambrinus Brewing Company.

This independently fixes **Lot 8 at the Sixth/Main end** of the Main-facing Block 26 row. Combined with the 1913 boundary wording and the original-plat lot labels, the Main-side sequence from Fifth north toward Sixth is:

**Lot 5 → Lot 6 → Lot 7 → Lot 8**.

## Original Oregon City plat — VISUALLY VERIFIED

Oregon City's official Subdivision Plat Finder identifies **OREGON CITY**, county plat **2**, year **1850**. The linked two-page, image-only PDF was inspected directly on 24 August 2026.

Official finder:
https://www.orcity.org/401/Subdivision-Plat-Finder

Canonical plat PDF:
https://maps.orcity.org/plats/2.pdf

On the first page, the recorded survey narrative states that:

- the original plat scale is **240 feet to the inch**;
- the part of town between Washington Street and the river was surveyed by Jesse Applegate in spring 1844;
- lots in that Applegate-surveyed part are **at least 66 feet front and 100 feet back**;
- alleys in that part are **10 feet wide**;
- the later Robert R. Short section has a different 60-by-105-foot lot pattern.

The target Block 26 graphic lies in the Washington-to-river/Applegate portion and visibly depicts eight lots, with Lots 5–8 in the row relevant to Main Street. The 66-by-100-foot statement is therefore the controlling original-plat dimension context for this block (`S-108`).

## Correction to the earlier 50-foot model

The earlier version of this note used the City's modern McLoughlin Conservation District webpage, which describes original-city blocks **generally** as approximately 200 by 200 feet with 50-by-100-foot lots. That general planning summary is not a survey of Block 26 and conflicts with the dimensions written on the official original plat for the Applegate portion.

The earlier proportional hypothesis that **501 + 503 might fill a roughly 50-foot Lot 5 and 505 might begin near the Lot 5/Lot 6 boundary is superseded and withdrawn**. It is preserved here as a research correction, not silently deleted.

## 1925 Sanborn scale alignment — STRONG INFERENCE

Official May 1925 Sanborn Sheet 6 was inspected directly. It shows separate storefronts:

- **501 — store**
- **503 — restaurant**
- **505 — billiards/cigars**
- **507 — adjoining commercial space to the north**

Canonical sheet:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4866/1925-6-PDF

A proportional reading made against the printed scale on the same sheet gives the following result:

- the combined Main Street frontage from the Fifth Street edge through the 501, 503 and 505 storefronts is approximately **66–67 feet**;
- the first quarter of the Fifth-to-Sixth Block 26 frontage therefore falls **at or very near the wall separating 505 from 507**.

This is a **strong map/plat alignment**, because one original Applegate lot is at least 66 feet wide and the three storefronts together occupy almost exactly that distance. It supports the working reconstruction:

- **501, 503 and 505 lie approximately within original Lot 5**;
- **507 begins at or near original Lot 6**.

This is **not yet a deed, title, survey-overlay or modern legal-description proof**. Sanborn line weight, scan distortion, later parcel divisions and the unresolved original-Block-26/later-Block-4 conversion prevent promotion to proven cadastral fact.

## Implication for the July 1922 fire question

The geometry does not decide whether 501, 503 or 505 contained the vacant former Alex Schieremann tailor room. It does materially focus the ownership search:

- all three live ignition candidates now appear to derive from **original Lot 5**, whose 1913 assessed owners were **Mary E. Barlow and Juliette E. David**;
- the Lot 5 title, tax and improvement/building-ownership chain should therefore receive first priority for identifying **S. Wolf of Portland**;
- Lot 6 / First National Bank remains a secondary cross-check until a deed or survey fixes the boundary and the cadastral-system conversion.

The finding remains compatible with—but does not prove—the strongest occupancy reconstruction:

- **503 predecessor = ignition room**
- **505 = adjoining pool hall**

**501 remains a live alternative.** The alignment also does not prove that a single landowner owned every building/improvement on Lot 5.
