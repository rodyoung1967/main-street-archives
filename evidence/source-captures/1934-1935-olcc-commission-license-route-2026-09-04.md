## Official State Archives record-series finding

The Oregon Secretary of State / Oregon State Archives current special records schedule for the Oregon Liquor Control Commission identifies:

- **Commission Meeting Records, 1934–[ongoing]**;
- approximately **15 cubic feet** under the current schedule description;
- **minutes, agendas, and exhibits are retained permanently and transferred to the State Archives after 20 years**;
- the Commission meets monthly and considers, among other matters, license disputes and liquor-law matters.

Official source indexed by the Oregon Secretary of State:

`https://sos.oregon.gov/archives/Documents/recordsmgmt/sched/schedule-liquor-control.pdf`

The same schedule's Licensing Division section identifies current record series for:

- Applications Received Lists;
- License Count Lists;
- **Liquor License Records**.

The current schedule provides finite retention for ordinary licensing records (including 20-year treatment for current Liquor License Records), while the Commission minutes/agendas/exhibits are permanent.

### Temporal / survival boundary

The current retention schedule is **not evidence** that the same retention rules governed in 1934–1935 or that any particular historic application was destroyed. Do not infer destruction of an Edwin/Alice Farr or 505 Main file from the modern schedule.

Its high-value implication is narrower and positive: **the OLCC Commission meeting series beginning in 1934 is explicitly permanent**, making the 1934–1935 minutes, agendas, and surviving exhibits a defined archival source class that should be requested/inspected even if routine license files no longer survive.

The schedule PDF could be read through the official search-index extraction in the current environment, but direct PDF retrieval returned an error, so this capture remains **OFFICIAL SEARCH-INDEX/TEXT LEVEL** pending visual inspection of the schedule page.

## Why this matters for 505 Main

Oregon City's official historic-resource inventory identifies **Edwin F. and Alice Farr** as proprietors of **Farr's Pool Hall, 505 Main Street**, and says they acquired the 401 Third Street residence in **1934**. The residence date is not an opening date for the pool hall.

Because OLCC Commission records begin in 1934, the first two years of that permanent state series overlap the earliest securely dated biographical context currently associated with Edwin/Alice Farr. The state series therefore offers a source-independent way to test whether the pool hall appears in early post-Prohibition licensing material.

No claim is made that Farr necessarily sold beer/liquor or held an OLCC license in 1934–1935. That is the question to test.
