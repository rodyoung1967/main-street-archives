All **8/8 archive sequences** were visually inspected. The archive/printed-page ordering is not assumed from sequence number alone; sequence 2 visibly carries a printed page number 8.

### Target-frontage screening

### Fred Steiner / rural identity-control finding

Archive sequence 2 contains a local-news column headed **CLEAR CREEK** which directly prints **“F. Steiner, of this place”** and then refers to him as **Fred**, describing a trip to Scappoose to buy a full-blooded bull calf and comments about dairy cows.

Standing alone, the heading could have been misread as locating Fred Steiner in Clear Creek. The next issue corrects that interpretation.

## 8 March 1912 issue

Canonical issue: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-03-08/ed-1/

All **8/8 archive sequences** were visually inspected. Direct-PDF opening initially failed for sequences 1, 2 and 5; each was recovered through its archive page and PDF link and then visually inspected.

### Beaver Creek correction / Fred Steiner

Archive sequence 2 explicitly says:

> “Last week the Courier made a mistake and printed the Beaver Creek items under the Clear Creek heading.”

The corrected **BEAVER CREEK** column then reports **Fred Steiner** had been sick for several weeks with a severe cold and separately refers to **Steiner Bros. of this place**.

Therefore the 1 March “F. Steiner … Fred” item belongs to the **Beaver Creek local-news stream**, not to Clear Creek despite the erroneous heading.

This is direct contemporary identity/location context for a rural Fred Steiner. It materially strengthens the project's rule that the probable **Fred Steiner** tenant transcription at **503½ Main / Barber Shop** must not be automatically merged with the Beaver Creek Fred Steiner. It does **not** prove they are different men: a Beaver Creek resident could theoretically have worked or operated a business in Oregon City. A direct occupational/address/biographical bridge is still required.

### 1914 independent rural control

For identity separation, the 26 February 1914 *Oregon City Courier* p.7 was also visually inspected directly. It states **“Mr. and Mrs. Fred Steiner, residents of Beaver Creek, were Oregon City visitors Saturday.”** The 5 March 1914 p.5 scan likewise lists **Fred Steiner, of Beaver Creek** among Oregon City visitors.

These later direct controls confirm a Fred Steiner was a Beaver Creek resident in 1914, overlapping the period of the 1914–1915 water ledger in which the faint 503½ tenant line is now read, steward-led, as Fred Steiner.

Again: overlapping date/name does not establish identity. Keep the rural-resident record and the 503½ barber-shop tenant hypothesis separate until independent evidence joins them.

### Other March 8 screening controls

- Page 1 discusses the prospective federal post-office building and argues over general location (Main Street/riverfront versus heights) but supplies no exact target premises or selected site.
- Page 3 is dominated by J. Levitt's sale at **corner 7th & Main**, outside the target block.
- Page 5 reports **Merrill & Cave moved their shoe store to Eighth Street, into a room built for them**. This is a useful non-target shoe-store control during the broader C. Schoenheinz/503 investigation; it is not evidence for 503.
- Page 5 also says Bannon Bros. were preparing to open their store April 1; no target number is printed in that item.
- No direct target-frontage billiard/barber/property/building event was identified.
