# 1989–2014 Lake Oswego surveys — direct-use provenance route for the 1947–1948 Clackamas County Directory

Date reviewed: 5 September 2026

Status: **OFFICIAL MUNICIPAL SURVEY PDF TEXT / DIRECT DIRECTORY-USE PROVENANCE — TARGET OREGON CITY PAGES NOT INSPECTED; NO FARR/HARR/505 OCCUPANCY CLAIM PROMOTED.**

Purpose: preserve a newly verified institutional route to the exact **Pacific Directory Service, 1947–1948 Directory of Clackamas County** needed for the unresolved Farr's Pool Hall → Harr's Recreation Center transition at 505 Main. This route is stronger than a bibliography-only citation because Lake Oswego historic-resource research used information from the directory in both the original 1989 inventory and the 2014 update.

## Direct-use evidence — original 1988–1989 469 Seventh Street inventory

City of Lake Oswego, **Cultural Resources Inventory Field Form 1988–1989, Rosentreter House, 469 Seventh Avenue**, recorded January 1989 by Koler/Morrison:

`https://www.ci.oswego.or.us/sites/default/files/fileattachments/boc_hrab/webpage/18341/rosentreter.pdf`

The original 1989 statement of significance says that Charles D. and Kay Needham, owners of Needham's Sporting Goods on State Street, are listed in the **1947–48 Clackamas County Directory** as residing at **“First and C.”**

This is direct evidence that the target directory's contents were being consulted during the original Lake Oswego cultural-resource inventory project by **1989**, not merely added as a bibliography item by the 2014 update.

The same 1989 form identifies **Koler/Morrison** as recorder and its bibliography includes Lake Oswego Library material and **Ticor Title Co., Oregon City**. The bibliography does not separately identify where the directory itself was physically consulted, so do not infer from those lines alone that either Lake Oswego Library or Ticor held the directory.

### 1989 project methodology / researchers

Lake Oswego's 1989 Historic Resource Protection Plan identifies the project team as **Jane Morrison, Julie Koler, and Jinx Faulkner of Koler/Morrison Historic Preservation and Planning Consultants**, with Morrison as project manager and Koler as project historian. The project ran from September 1988 through August 1989 and included literature search, survey/inventory, evaluation, and preparation of inventory forms/final documentation, with extensive citizen-volunteer research involvement.

Official report:

`https://www.ci.oswego.or.us/bids/sites/default/files/fileattachments/historicresourceprotectionplan1989.pdf`

This creates an older project-file provenance target: retained **1988–1989 cultural-resource inventory research files, bibliographic worksheets, directory notes, volunteer research files, and source-copy records** may identify where the target directory was consulted.

## Direct-use evidence — 2014 469 Seventh Street survey update

City of Lake Oswego, **Oregon Inventory of Historic Properties Historic Resource Survey Form, 469 Seventh Street**, recorded August 2014:

`https://www.ci.oswego.or.us/sites/default/files/fileattachments/boc_hrab/webpage/18341/104_469_seventh_street_ils_0.pdf`

On survey-form page 3 of 8, the statement of significance again discusses Charles D. and Kay Needham. It contrasts the older inventory's 1948 purchase date with the **1947–1948 Clackamas County Directory**, which places the Needhams at **“First and C”** and uses that entry to suggest that they purchased or rented the property a year earlier.

This confirms continuing use of the directory evidence in the 2014 update. It also shows that the directory citation was not a generic bibliography item detached from the historical narrative.

The 2014 form's research-source checklist marks **City Directories** and identifies **Lake Oswego Public Library** as the local library used for the project. The bibliography identifies:

**Pacific Directory Service. 1947–1948. _Directory of Clackamas County_. Portland, Oregon.**

Other 2014 Lake Oswego intensive-level survey forms carry the same Pacific Directory Service 1947–1948 bibliography entry, supporting project-wide access to the source rather than a one-property anomaly.

The form identifies:

- **City of Lake Oswego**, 380 A Avenue, Lake Oswego;

A March 2014 City notice confirms that Lake Oswego hired **SWCA** to update 40 Intensive Level Survey forms, with support from State Historic Preservation Office / National Park Service grant funding.

Official City notice:

`https://www.ci.oswego.or.us/sites/default/files/fileattachments/publicaffairs/webpage/11904/march_2014_hellolo.pdf`

This creates a concrete provenance chain for the target directory in addition to GFO / CCHS / CCFHS:

1. **City of Lake Oswego Planning / Historic Preservation project files** for both the 1988–1989 inventory and 2014 intensive-level survey update;
2. **Lake Oswego Public Library** local-history/research materials and source-copy provenance used by the survey projects;
3. retained **Koler/Morrison 1988–1989 research files**, if transferred to or retained by the City;
4. retained **SWCA 2014 project research files**, if transferred to or retained by the City;
5. Erin O'Rourke-Meadors / Lake Oswego preservation research files as a secondary provenance lead.

Do not assume any of these custodians still holds a full directory or scan until confirmed.

If the City/library does not retain the source copy, ask where the Koler/Morrison and SWCA teams consulted the directory and whether either project file identifies the holding library/archive.
