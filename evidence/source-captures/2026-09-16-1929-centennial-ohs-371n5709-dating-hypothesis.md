# 1929 Oregon City centennial / OHS 371N5706–5709 dating hypothesis

Date: 16 September 2026  
Status: **NEW WORKING HYPOTHESIS; NOT A PROVED DATE FOR 371N5709**

Purpose: test whether the Oregon Journal negative sequence containing OHS `371N5706`, `371N5707`, and `371N5709` may have been created in connection with Oregon City's 1929 centennial coverage, potentially explaining Oregon Encyclopedia's secondary `c. 1930` date for the same 371N5709 exposure.

## Directly established controls

### 1. OHS 371N5706 and 371N5709 are explicitly related

Oregon Historical Society catalogs:

- **371N5706 — Hawley Pulp and Paper mill, Oregon City, Oregon**
  - institutional creation range: **1923–1936**
  - number **4** written on the negative
  - OHS explicitly says: **“This photograph may be related to image No. 371N5709.”**
  - source: https://staff.digitalcollections.ohs.org/hawley-pulp-and-paper-mill-oregon-city-oregon

- **371N5709 — View of downtown Oregon City, Oregon**
  - institutional creation range: **1923–1936**
  - number **5** written on the negative
  - OHS explicitly says it may be related to **371N5706**
  - source: https://digitalcollections.ohs.org/oregon-city

The reciprocal OHS relationship plus visible negative numbers **4** and **5** supports treating 5706 and 5709 as a likely same-assignment or same-series pair. It does **not** establish the assignment date.

### 2. OHS 371N5707 is another Oregon City subject between the two identifiers

OHS `371N5707` is **McLoughlin House, Oregon City, Oregon**, institutional range **1923–1936**. The negative carries the text “McLoughlin House.”

Source:
https://digitalcollections.ohs.org/oregon-city

OHS does not, in the reviewed catalog wording, explicitly state that 5707 is related to 5706/5709. Identifier adjacency alone is therefore **not proof** of a shared assignment.

### 3. Oregon Encyclopedia dates all three Oregon City subjects around 1930

Oregon Encyclopedia's Oregon City article reproduces:
- **Hawley Pulp and Paper Mill, Oregon City, c.1930** — credit `Journal, 371A5706`
- **Oregon City, c. 1930** — the same exposure as OHS `371N5709`, credited `Journal, 371A5709`
- **John McLoughlin House, Oregon City, c. 1930** — credit `Oregon Journal, 371N5707`

Source:
https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/oregon-city/

This is a meaningful **secondary dating cluster**, but not an institutional OHS creation date. Preserve the Encyclopedia's A/N identifier discrepancy for 5706 and 5709 rather than silently normalizing it.

### 4. A November 1929 newspaper directly confirms an Oregon City centennial special edition

The **Eugene Register**, 10 November 1929, page 6, editorial **“Oregon City and McLoughlin,”** states that the centennial of McLoughlin's founding of Oregon City had **“just”** been observed by the Enterprise with a **“splendid special edition.”**

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088119/1929-11-10/ed-1/seq-6/

This directly establishes:
- Oregon City's **1929 founding centennial** was being commemorated;
- an **Enterprise special edition** devoted to that centennial existed shortly before 10 November 1929.

It does **not** identify the exact Enterprise issue date, title variant, page count, illustrations, photographer, or any Oregon Journal participation.

### 5. Enterprise-family 1929 microfilm survives locally

Oregon City Public Library currently lists:
- **Oregon City Enterprise Weekly** through 25 December 1942;
- **Oregon City Enterprise** from 8 January 1911 through 28 February 1950.

The existing project title-control work distinguishes the weekly **Oregon City Enterprise** from the daily **Morning Enterprise** in this era.

Source:
https://www.orcity.org/490/Microfilm-Scanning-System

Therefore the 1929 centennial special edition is a **surviving manual microfilm target**, even though this research pass has not recovered it online. No outreach has been sent.

## Why the centennial hypothesis is plausible

The OHS/Oregon Encyclopedia subject cluster is unusually compatible with a city-history feature:
- Hawley paper mill / industrial Oregon City;
- McLoughlin House / founder-history subject;
- broad downtown Oregon City cityscape.

The Eugene Register independently proves that a major Oregon City centennial special edition was issued in late 1929. Oregon Encyclopedia independently dates the same OHS images approximately **1930**.

Together, those facts make the following hypothesis worth testing:

> **WORKING HYPOTHESIS:** OHS 371N5706 and 371N5709 — and possibly 371N5707 — may have been made for, near, or in the context of Oregon City's late-1929 centennial publicity or a related Oregon Journal feature.

## Why the hypothesis is not yet evidence of date

Do **not** promote 371N5709 to 1929 from this pattern alone.

Missing bridge:
- no recovered Oregon Journal page publishes 371N5706/5709 in 1929;
- no OHS sleeve/catalog note gives a 1929 assignment date;
- no recovered Enterprise centennial issue contains or credits these images;
- OHS itself retains **1923–1936**;
- neighboring negative identifier `371N5708` is exactly dated **31 March 1928** and belongs to the Al Fausett falls-jump event, showing that adjacent archive identifiers are not safely chronological assignment groups by themselves;
- `371N5704` is an exact **1932** Mount Hood Loop aerial, further warning against dating solely from nearby identifier numbers.

Therefore the centennial link remains **UNVERIFIED / WORKING HYPOTHESIS**.

## Highest-value next checks

1. Recover the exact **Enterprise centennial special edition** issued before 10 November 1929 and inspect every page for the downtown, Hawley mill, or McLoughlin House images and photo credits.
2. Search the **Oregon Daily Journal** in approximately late October–November 1929 for an Oregon City / McLoughlin centennial feature, especially one using a numbered photo sequence.
3. Recover OHS original sleeve/envelope metadata for `371N5706`, `371N5707`, and `371N5709`.
4. Test whether the written **4** and **5** on 5706/5709 are publication-layout numbers, assignment-sequence numbers, or later archival marks.
5. Keep Oregon Encyclopedia's **c.1930** as the strongest presently recovered specific secondary date clue unless/until a primary dated publication or original-negative record improves it.
