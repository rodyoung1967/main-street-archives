# 26 April 1912 — Oregon City Shoe Store, 508 Main, opposite Post Office

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED primary newspaper advertisement**

Research date: 9 September 2026

Purpose: provide a direct exact-address / relative-location anchor for the **earlier circa-1912 Oregon City Post Office**, while keeping that earlier Post Office distinct from the later 1919–1922 Fifth/Main Post Office used in the July 1922 fire reconstruction.

## Source

**Oregon City Courier, 26 Apr 1912, p. 8**

Canonical PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-04-26/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

The actual scanned newspaper page was visually inspected at full-page scale on 9 September 2026.

## Direct reading

At the lower left of the page, a large shoe-sale advertisement identifies:

- **Oregon City Shoe Store**;
- **508 Main St.**;
- **Opposite Postoffice**.

The same advertisement promotes a special shoe sale for Booster Day and describes the store as a first-class shoe store carrying high-grade shoes.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## What this proves

On **26 April 1912**, a business at the exact even-side address **508 Main Street** publicly described itself as **opposite the Oregon City Post Office**.

This is independent direct evidence that the **circa-1912 Post Office stood across Main Street from 508**, therefore on the odd/west side of Main.

It strengthens the archive's existing earlier-Post-Office reconstruction based on:

- I. Tolpolar later documented at 514 Main and advertising opposite the Post Office;
- Chicago Store at 505 Main describing itself as just below the Post Office;
- other businesses using the Post Office as a near/next-door landmark.

## What this does NOT prove

Do not infer from this advertisement that:

- the Post Office's exact numbered address was **507**, **509**, **511**, **513**, or another particular odd number;
- **513 Main** is now proven as the Post Office address;
- `opposite` means directly perpendicular to one exact storefront bay rather than generally across the street from the building/entrance;
- the 1912 Post Office occupied the same location as the **1919–1922 Post Office**.

The archive's **513 Main** reconstruction remains a **working candidate / strong spatial hypothesis only**.

## Chronology warning — essential for the 1922 fire project

This source belongs to the **earlier** Post Office location.

The later chronology is independently established:

1. in 1913 the Weinhard Estate planned a new Post Office at Eighth & Main;
2. by the 1917–1918 period the Post Office operated opposite the courthouse in that Eighth/Main context;
3. the Post Office moved again on **15 April 1919** to **Fifth & Main on the east/even side of Main**, using two combined store rooms;
4. therefore the **July 1922 fire-period Post Office** was on the even/east side, not the 1912 odd/west side.

Thus this 508 Main advertisement is valuable for reconstructing the **1912** address geography, but it must not be used to reinterpret the 1922 `Opposite Post Office` evidence.

## Research consequence

This new exact control narrows the circa-1912 Post Office question from a general side-of-street inference to a directly documented cross-street relationship:

> **508 Main was opposite the Post Office on 26 April 1912.**

The next useful step is to combine this with exact odd-side address/business anchors and any surviving Post Office lease, directory, building, or postal record to determine which odd-side bay actually contained the Post Office.
