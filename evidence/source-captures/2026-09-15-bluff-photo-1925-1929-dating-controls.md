# Ralph Eddy / CCHS bluff photograph — 1925–1929 dating controls

Date researched: 2026-09-15

## Scope

Follow-up dating analysis of the project-steward-supplied CCHS-watermarked photograph captioned `BUSINESS SECTION OF OREGON CITY, OREGON FROM THE BLUFFS`, with apparent `Eddy` credit. The photograph binary was re-uploaded in the conversation on 2026-09-15. This note does not claim an exact catalog identity for the exposure.

## Existing lower bound retained

The bridge-adjacent building remains strongly consistent in position and massing with the 1923 replacement Oregon City Elks Lodge. Lodge history states the prior lodge burned 1922-12-02, the replacement was completed in 1923, and the first meeting in the new lodge occurred 1923-12-07. This supports late 1923 or later as the practical lower bound, but the exact exterior-completion date remains a qualification.

## New 1925 lower-bound control: municipal buildings

The 1925 Oregon City Municipal Activities Report states that the new City Hall and Municipal Building were completed in April 1925 and that City offices moved from the Masonic Building to the Municipal Building on May 1, 1925. The downtown historic-resource synthesis separately dates the Old City Hall / McCald Building at 712 Main to circa 1925.

The bluff photograph contains the developed 700-block civic/commercial context, but an exact visual identification of the McCald Building from this oblique/rearward angle has not yet been made strongly enough to use May 1925 as a hard terminus post quem. This is a high-value next visual comparison.

Evidence state: DOCUMENTED for the 1925 completion/move; UNRESOLVED VISUAL MATCH for whether the new building is positively identifiable in the supplied exposure.

## Strong new upper-bound candidate: Railroad Avenue paving, 1929

The 1929 Municipal Activities Report states that the Engineering Department completed an improvement of **Railroad Avenue from 5th Street to 7th Street**. The work consisted of grading the full 40-foot width, placing an 8-foot concrete sidewalk and curb, and a 32-foot-wide six-inch concrete roadway, plus 300 feet of six-inch sewer. Cost: $7,593.73.

The supplied bluff photograph gives an unusually clear view of Railroad Avenue alongside the railroad between the Fifth/Seventh Street area. The visible roadway in the relevant foreground/center-right stretch appears **unimproved/unpaved**, without the broad 32-foot concrete roadway and 8-foot concrete sidewalk/curb described as completed in 1929.

If the roadway segment has been geographically identified correctly, this is potentially a much stronger upper bound than the old courthouse: the photograph would predate completion of the 1929 Railroad Avenue 5th–7th improvement. Because the annual report does not in the currently reviewed passage give the exact completion day/month, the safe formulation is **before completion of the 1929 project**, not automatically before January 1, 1929.

Evidence state: DOCUMENTED PRIMARY for the 1929 completed project; STRONG VISUAL/SPATIAL INFERENCE for the supplied photo showing the pre-improvement roadway. Exact road-segment mapping should be checked against the 1925 Sanborn and a post-1929 photograph before upgrading.

## Independent 1929 visual control: Main Street ornamental lights

The same 1929 report states that 28 ornamental street lights were installed on Main Street from 5th to 9th at a cost of $5,600. The supplied photograph does not obviously show this completed ornamental-light system in the visible 5th–9th Main Street corridor. This is supporting evidence for a pre-/early-1929 exposure, but the image resolution and viewing angle make it weaker than the Railroad Avenue paving control.

Evidence state: DOCUMENTED PRIMARY for 1929 installation; SUPPORTING VISUAL INFERENCE only for absence in photo.

## Circa-1928 Depot Barber Building control

The City of Oregon City downtown historic-resource synthesis dates the **Depot Barber Building**, 216–218 7th Street / 615–619 Railroad Avenue, to circa 1928. This location lies directly in the high-value 7th/Railroad area visible in the photograph. The supplied image appears to show a substantially open/low-built condition near the Railroad Avenue/7th Street corner rather than an obvious later two-story commercial mass, but exact parcel/footprint registration has not yet been completed.

If a 1928 or contemporary map/photo confirms the Depot Barber Building footprint and it is demonstrably absent from the supplied exposure, the upper bound could move from “before completion of the 1929 Railroad Avenue work” to approximately **before the circa-1928 Depot Barber construction**. Do not use this as a hard bound yet because the construction year is itself `c.1928` and the exact footprint must be visually registered.

Evidence state: OFFICIAL SECONDARY for c.1928 construction date; PROMISING BUT UNRESOLVED visual absence test.

## Current dating assessment

The strongest current working window is now substantially narrower than the prior late-1923–1935/early-1936 bracket:

- Lower: late 1923 from the replacement Elks Lodge, with a possible move to spring 1925 if the new municipal building / McCald Building can be positively matched in the exposure.
- Upper: likely before completion of the 1929 Railroad Avenue 5th–7th concrete paving project; potentially before c.1928 if the Depot Barber Building absence is confirmed.

A **mid-1920s date (roughly 1925–1928)** is therefore now a leading hypothesis, not yet a final documented date.

## Kwality sign caution

The project steward's visual reading of the blurry 503-area sign as approximately `KWALITY CAFE / COCKTAILS` remains a useful hypothesis. Do not conflate this with the separate `Quality Cafe` known on the 400 block. The 1922 `QUALITY CAFE` OCR lead must remain separate from 503/Kwality unless direct evidence bridges them.
