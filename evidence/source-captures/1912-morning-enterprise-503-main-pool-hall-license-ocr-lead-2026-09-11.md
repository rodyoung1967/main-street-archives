## OCR-visible notice

University of Oregon Historic Oregon Newspapers OCR for page 2 contains a notice headed approximately **“Notice of Application for Pool Hall License.”** The OCR reads that the applicant would apply at the next regular city-council meeting for a license to run and regulate a pool hall at **“my place of business 503 Main Street”** for a period of three months.

The OCR renders the signature as **“H .H. SMTHI.”** This is very likely an OCR corruption of a surname such as **Smith**, but the exact applicant name must not be normalized until the original page image is visually inspected.

Canonical OCR page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-24/ed-1/seq-2/ocr/`

## Why this matters

If visually confirmed, this would be direct contemporary evidence that a pool-hall business operated or sought licensing at **503 Main Street** in March 1912. It would materially extend the known pre-Kwality occupancy history of 503 and would also provide an important early pool-hall/recreation-use control separate from later 505/505½ pool-hall evidence.

Do **not** yet:

- treat the applicant name as H. H. Smith without visual verification;
- infer ownership of the building from a business/license notice;
- merge this operator with any later Smith, Farr, Harr, Leland, Raasch, or other pool-hall person without independent identity evidence;
- treat the OCR notice as visually verified evidence.
