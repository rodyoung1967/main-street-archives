# 1920 Fifth–Sixth Main pool-hall visual verification

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED TARGETED SOURCE SET**
Review date: 2026-08-31
Annual research status: **IN PROGRESS** — this targeted review is not a page-by-page annual certification.

## Purpose

This capture promotes three 1920 Oregon City Enterprise pages that were preserved during the 1919–1920 501/503/505 construction search checkpoint. OCR was used only to locate the items; the claims below are based on direct page-image inspection.

The central question remains whether the narrow 1911 storefronts at 501, 503, and 505 Main were demolished, enlarged, or reconstructed before the changed 1925 Sanborn footprint. These pages provide business/address anchors but do **not** date construction of the surviving 503 or 505 buildings.

## 13 February 1920 — Clem Dollar pool hall

Source: *Oregon City Enterprise*, 13 February 1920, p. 5.

Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1920-02-13/ed-1/seq-5.pdf

Repository PDF: `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1920-02-13-page-5.pdf`

Visual reading: Under the headline **“POOL HALL PROPRIETOR AND BOYS PAY FINES”**, the article identifies **Clem Dollar** as proprietor of **a pool hall on Main, between Fifth and Sixth streets**. It reports that Dollar was fined $20 for permitting minors in the resort and that Homer Selby and Raymond Hermann, who were playing pool, were each fined $10.

What this proves:
- Clem Dollar was described as proprietor of a pool hall somewhere on Main between Fifth and Sixth by 13 February 1920.
- The item is a block-location anchor.

What it does not prove:
- no numbered address is printed;
- no building owner, lease, construction, demolition, or remodeling event is stated;
- it does not establish that Dollar's hall was the later A. Leland premises at 505½ Main.

## 4 June 1920 — A. Leland at 505½ Main

Source: *Oregon City Enterprise*, 4 June 1920, p. 3.

Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1920-06-04/ed-1/seq-3.pdf

Repository PDF: `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1920-06-04-page-3.pdf`

Visual reading: In the report of the Wednesday evening city-council meeting, the paper states: **“The application of A. Leland to operate a pool hall at 505½ Main street and to erect an electric street sign was granted.”**

What this proves:
- by the council meeting reported 4 June 1920, **A. Leland** had been granted permission to operate a pool hall at the exact address **505½ Main Street**;
- the same municipal action granted permission for an electric street sign;
- this is a direct exact-address/use anchor between Wolf & Miller at 505 Main in October 1918 (`E-027`) and Dr. Freeze at 505/505½ in 1921–1922 (`E-028`).

What it does not prove:
- the premises or surviving 505 building was newly constructed in 1920;
- the relationship between 505 and 505½ as physical rooms/storefronts;
- property ownership;
- that A. Leland and O. H. Leland are the same person;
- that A. Leland succeeded Clem Dollar or operated the same pool hall.

## 3 December 1920 — block-located pool hall

Source: *Oregon City Enterprise*, 3 December 1920, p. 1.

Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1920-12-03/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

Repository PDF: `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1920-12-03-page-1.pdf`

Visual reading: In the article **“Krassig Some ‘Detec’ / Bluff Is Worth $97 / And Man Leaves Town”**, the narrative refers to **“the pool hall between 5th and 6th streets on Main.”**

What this proves:
- a pool hall was again described in the Fifth–Sixth Main block in December 1920.

What it does not prove:
- no operator or numbered address is given;
- it does not establish identity with the February Clem Dollar hall or June A. Leland 505½ premises.

## Cross-source interpretation

The verified 505 chronology is now tighter:

1. October 1918 — Wolf & Miller at **505 Main** (`E-027`).
2. 4 June 1920 report — A. Leland granted a pool-hall operation/sign application at **505½ Main**.
3. 1921–1922 — Dr. Freeze advertises at **505/505½ Main** (`E-028`).
4. 1925 — Sanborn Sheet 6 shows **505** used for billiards/cigars (`E-020`).

This is meaningful occupancy/use continuity around the City's approximate c.1920 construction estimate, but it is **not** a construction-date chain. The surviving building may predate, postdate, or encompass alteration across some of these business uses; a permit, assessor card, deed/improvement record, or direct construction report is still needed.

## Municipal-record follow-up

The City of Oregon City historical meeting archive indicates that Commission records exist for the 1920s. The underlying **Wednesday, 2 June 1920** meeting record corresponding to the Enterprise report has not yet been recovered in the present web/tool environment. That is a retrieval gap/manual follow-up, not negative evidence.

Priority follow-up:
- recover the 2 June 1920 Commission minutes and inspect the full action on A. Leland;
- search late-1918 through June-1920 sale, lease, license, sign, remodel, demolition, or relocation notices involving Wolf & Miller, Clem Dollar, A. Leland, 505/505½ Main, and the Fifth–Sixth block;
- identify A. Leland independently before considering any relationship to O. H. Leland (`E-126`);
- continue construction-date work using primary permit/assessor/property records.

## Integrity / uncertainty rule

Do not convert the absence of a construction notice in the bounded OCR/targeted search into proof that no construction occurred. Do not merge Clem Dollar, A. Leland, O. H. Leland, Timms Pool Hall, or Leland & Little without an independent bridge.
