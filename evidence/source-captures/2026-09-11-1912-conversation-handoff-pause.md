### 2. Smith pool-room / pool-hall sequence at 503 Main

Primary evidence:
- **5 January 1912, Courier:** H. N. Smith, pool room, **503 Main**.
- **24 March 1912, Morning Enterprise:** legal notice signed **H. H. Smith** applying for a three-month pool-hall license at **his place of business, 503 Main Street**.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063701/1912-03-24/ed-1/seq-2/
- **12 July 1912, Courier:** H. H. Smith, who had conducted a pool hall at **Fifth and Main streets**, left for Aurora to open a pool hall there.
  - https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-07-12/ed-1/seq-5/

Conclusion:
- 503 Main had a pool-room/pool-hall use in early 1912.
- H. H. Smith's Oregon City operation ended or changed by about 10 July 1912.
- Preserve the **H. N. Smith vs H. H. Smith initials conflict**. Chronology/business/address overlap suggests a relationship or possible newspaper initial error, but do not silently merge identities without another source.
- The July Fifth/Main wording supports a close geographic relationship for 503 but does not independently prove the precise corner/lot footprint.

### 3. Gambrinus Saloon at Sixth & Main

**24 March 1912 Morning Enterprise** identifies the **Gambrinus Saloon at Sixth and Main streets** and names **William Trudell** as proprietor of “The Gambrinus.”

This extends the Gambrinus property chronology:
- 1906 — William Tell Hotel / lot 8, Block 26 sold to Gambrinus.
- 1910 — new two-story Gambrinus business block under construction at Sixth & Main.
- 1911 — second story occupied as Electric Hotel annex.
- 1912 — Gambrinus Saloon operating at Sixth & Main under William Trudell.

Do not assign the saloon to 507/509/511 or another number solely from intersection wording.

### 4. Confirmed Main Street demolition/rebuilding in May 1912

**10 May 1912 Courier** editorial states, in present tense, that Oregon City was **tearing down buildings on Main Street and building new buildings in their places**.

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-05-10/ed-1/seq-1/

This proves **active Main Street building replacement in 1912**.

Critical limitation:
- no block
- no owner
- no business
- no numbered address
- no building name

Therefore it does **not** prove demolition/replacement of 501, 503, 505, 507, the Harding/Willamette premises, or any specific Fifth–Sixth building. Preserve this as citywide Main Street structural-change evidence until another source identifies the buildings.

### 5. Schrader bakery transfer

**20 December 1912 Courier** reports that **Herman Schrader** sold his two Oregon City bakeries—one at Seventh and John Adams and the other **on Main Street**—to **Wemmo & Haas**.

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1912-12-20/ed-1/seq-8.pdf

January advertising placed Schrader's Bakery **near the Post Office**, but no exact Main Street number is yet proven.

This is a business-transfer event, not evidence of a building sale/demolition/relocation.

## Harding / Willamette implication

The January 1912 page directly places **G. H. Young at 507 Main**.

In March 1911 Young was reported moving **one door north into the Willamette Building** from the Harding Building on Main between Fifth and Sixth.

The 1912 exact 507 address therefore strengthens the hypothesis:
- Willamette Building may have been 507.
- the former Harding premises may have been immediately south.

But continuity from March 1911 to January 1912 is not directly proven. **Do not declare Willamette = 507 or Harding = 505** without a continuity/address source.

Geo. A. Harding's own business at **511 Main** on 5 January 1912 reinforces the rule that Harding's personal/store address and the separately named Harding Building must remain separate until mapped.
