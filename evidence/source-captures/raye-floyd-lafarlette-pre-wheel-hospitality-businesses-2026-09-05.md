# Raye and Floyd LaFarlette — pre-Wheel hospitality/business chronology

Date of research pass: **5 September 2026**  
Chronology reorganized: **6 September 2026**

Purpose: preserve and separate the emerging pre-503 business history of Raye LaFarlette / Raye Grisham (`P-006`) and Floyd LaFarlette (`P-022`) without retroactively using it as evidence for the later Wheel transaction.

## Organization rule

This capture is organized by the **date of the historical event or state being reconstructed**, not by the order in which sources were discovered. A later retrospective source may therefore appear under an earlier event date when it is the source establishing that earlier event. Publication dates, event dates, visual-verification status, and evidence classifications remain explicit so the chronology does not turn a retrospective statement into a contemporaneous record.

## Project-steward family provenance

Rodney Young recalls that Raye's later ownership of The Wheel was definitely **not her first business venture** and that she also ran some kind of **nightclub or hotel in Myrtle Point, Oregon**.

On 5 September 2026 Rodney added a second, more specific family-memory lead: **Raye may have worked at the Wheel Cafe in Morton, Washington, and the people who owned the Morton Wheel may later have owned The Wheel in Oregon City before Raye bought the Oregon City business from them.** Rodney explicitly cautioned that this is memory and may be partly wrong.

On 6 September 2026 Rodney's brother **Mitch Young independently recalled that the LaFarlettes were in Morton before Coquille, while Sheila was below school age, and that as far as he knew they came to Morton from Chicago**. This supersedes the earlier working placement of Morton after Myrtle Point. The older placement is retained here only as superseded research history; it is no longer the active chronology.

Rodney further states from family knowledge that **Sheila LaFarlette was born in Chicago**. The published obituary supplies Sheila's birth date, **13 June 1944**, but does not state a birthplace; therefore Chicago is retained here specifically as **PROJECT-STEWARD / FAMILY PROVENANCE**, not documentary birth-place proof. If accurate, it establishes that the family was still in Chicago at Sheila's birth and makes any remembered Chicago → Morton move necessarily **after 13 June 1944**.

# Chronological evidence sequence

## Before 1944 — Chicago rooming-house business

A visually reviewed **11 March 1954 Coquille Valley Sentinel** profile, “Mrs. Floyd LaFarlette Once Liked Noise Of Bustling City -- Now Coquille,” identifies Mrs. Floyd LaFarlette as **Raye** and retrospectively says the family had a **rooming-house business in Chicago**.

Repository source: `S-271` / `E-245`  
Preserved page: `newspapers/coquille-valley-sentinel/lafarlette-primary-sources/1954-03-11-p1-raye-lafarlette-profile.pdf`

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED** for Raye's published 1954 account that the family had the Chicago rooming-house business. The source is retrospective; it does not provide the exact start or end date of the Chicago business.

## 1944 — Raye's published Chicago → Coquille move account

The same **11 March 1954** profile says the family first came to Coos County in **1944** on a vacation trip, returned to their **home in Chicago**, sold the rooming-house business, and moved to **Coquille**. The profile separately says the decision to move to Coquille had been made about ten years earlier.

Repository source: `S-271` / `E-245`  
Preserved page: `newspapers/coquille-valley-sentinel/lafarlette-primary-sources/1954-03-11-p1-raye-lafarlette-profile.pdf`

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED** for Raye's published account; **UNRESOLVED CONFLICT** with the family recollection of a Morton stop between Chicago and Coquille.

## Approximately 1944–1947 — Morton, Washington hypothesis

The active family-memory chronology now places the possible Morton stop **after Chicago and before Coquille**, not after Myrtle Point.

Mitch Young independently recalled on 6 September 2026 that:

- Morton was **before Coquille**;
- Sheila “wasn't of school age”;
- as far as he knew, the family came to Morton **from Chicago**.

Rodney independently remembers Raye working at the **Wheel Cafe in Morton**, and remembers a possible later chain in which the Morton Wheel owners became connected to the Oregon City Wheel before selling it to Raye.

Sheila's published obituary reports a birth date of **13 June 1944**. Floyd is independently anchored in the Coos County business record by **7 August 1947**. Accordingly, if the family recollection is substantially correct, **13 June 1944 through 7 August 1947** is the highest-value working search bracket for a Morton interval, with Sheila approximately age 0–3.

Classification: **FAMILY / ORAL-HISTORY LEAD / WORKING SEARCH BRACKET / UNRESOLVED CONFLICT**. No contemporary source yet recovered establishes a Morton residence, Raye's employment at the Morton Wheel, the proprietor of the Morton Wheel in this period, or a Morton→Oregon City ownership bridge.

Related chronology capture: `evidence/source-captures/lafarlette-morton-chronology-controls-2026-09-06.md`.

The **11 March 1954 Coquille Valley Sentinel** profile says Raye was working at the **Coquille Hotel coffee shop** and had been employed at the hotel for several years.

## 18 October 1951 — Floyd becomes a co-owner of The Myrtle Lodge

The actual scanned **18 October 1951 Coquille Valley Sentinel** page contains a large advertisement stating, in substance:

- “OPENING FRIDAY”;
- “Under the New Ownership of **Charlie Elkins and Floyd LaFarlette**”;
- **The Myrtle Lodge**;
- steaks, seafoods and chicken;
- dancing from **10 to 1 A.M. Saturday nights**.

Repository source: `S-269` / `E-243`  
Preserved page: `newspapers/coquille-valley-sentinel/lafarlette-primary-sources/1951-10-18-p4-myrtle-lodge-new-owners.pdf`

This directly establishes Floyd LaFarlette as a co-owner of a dining/dancing establishment by October 1951. Earlier Myrtle Lodge advertising shows the venue predated this ownership change, so the advertisement documents an ownership transition rather than creation of the establishment.

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED** for Floyd's 1951 co-ownership and the venue's dining/dancing character.

### Geographic caution

The Myrtle Lodge must **not** automatically be identified as Rodney's remembered Myrtle Point hotel/nightclub. Contemporary advertisements place the Myrtle Lodge on Highway 101 **between Coquille and Coos Bay**, not in Myrtle Point.

## By 30 November 1960 — Oregon City Wheel already exists

The repository's visually preserved **30 November 1960 Oregonian** item (`E-044`) names the **Wheel Cafe at Fifth and Main in Oregon City**. Thus, if the remembered Morton-owner chain is substantially correct, those people would have acquired an **already-existing Oregon City Wheel** rather than necessarily originating the Oregon City trade name.

This creates two distinct questions:

1. **Did Raye work at the Morton Wheel Cafe, and who owned/operated it during the possible 1944–1947 interval?**
2. **Did one or more of those Morton owners/operators later acquire the already-existing Oregon City Wheel and then sell it to Raye?**

Classification: the Oregon City Wheel's existence is **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT**; the Morton→Oregon City ownership bridge remains **UNVERIFIED**.

# Current interpretation

The chronology presently supports these bounded conclusions:

1. **Before 1944:** Raye and Floyd had a Chicago rooming-house business according to Raye's 1954 published account.
2. **1944:** Raye's published account describes Chicago → Coquille, while family oral history independently inserts a possible Morton stop.
3. **Approx. 1944–1947:** Morton is the active working interval to test; it is not documented.
4. **7 Aug. 1947:** Anderson and LaFarlette are in the Coos County business record.
5. **By the early 1950s:** Raye is in the Coquille Hotel employment environment.
6. **18 Oct. 1951:** Floyd is a documented new co-owner of **The Myrtle Lodge**.
7. **11 Jun. 1953:** Raye and Floyd are documented hosts at a Myrtle Lodge banquet/dance.
8. **30 Nov. 1960:** an Oregon City Wheel Cafe already exists at Fifth and Main.
9. **1962–1965:** OCR/indexed material strongly associates Floyd and, in 1963, Mr. and Mrs. Floyd LaFarlette with the **Myrtle Hotel / Myrtle Coffee Shop / Club Lehua** cluster in Myrtle Point; scans remain retrieval gaps.
10. **Later:** Raye acquired The Wheel business at **503 Main Street**; seller and acquisition date remain unresolved.

The evidence therefore supports significant pre-Wheel hospitality/business experience, but it does **not** yet prove the Morton employment story or the Morton-owner → Oregon City-owner → Raye sale sequence.
