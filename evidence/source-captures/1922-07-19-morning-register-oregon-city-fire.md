# 19 July 1922 — Morning Register reports on Oregon City fire

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED AT PAGE LEVEL**. This verifies the two fire reports on the scanned page, not the full issue page-by-page.

## Source

**Morning Register (Eugene, Oregon), 19 July 1922, page 1**

The original full-page scan was opened and visually inspected on 24 August 2026. A high-resolution crop of the longer fire item was also visually checked to distinguish actual printed wording from OCR errors.

## The page contains two different fire reports

The same page prints **two separate Oregon City fire items** with materially different damage estimates and somewhat different detail.

### 1. “OREGON CITY HAS BLAZE” — $50,000

A center-column report headed **“OREGON CITY HAS BLAZE”** and subheaded **“FIRE DAMAGE IS ESTIMATED TO BE $50,000”** states that firemen intimated incendiary origin, that the fire swept away half a block of the older portion of the city, and that the Oregon City Laundry alone sustained a $30,000 loss.

The report visually prints the following business/ownership wording:

- frame garage of **May & Washburne**, with two automobiles;
- **Fifth street restaurant, owned by Ed Gage of Portland and operated by J. Blake**;
- Miller shoe repair shop;
- H. Ketchner home and confectionery, **owned by George Harding**.

The last two ownership/operator statements conflict with the local *Banner-Courier* report and are preserved exactly as printed; see below.

### 2. “Oregon City Has $30,000 Fire” — $30,000

A separate short lower-right-column item reports that:

- fire was discovered early July 18 in an **unoccupied building on Main street in the older business section**;
- damage was estimated at **$30,000**;
- the **Oregon City Laundry plant**, **May & Washburne's garage** with two automobiles, the **Fifth street restaurant**, the **Miller shoe shop**, and the **home and confectionery store of H. Ketchner** were destroyed.

This shorter report does not give the disputed owner/operator names.

## Significance

This is an independent contemporary newspaper published the day after the fire. Collectively the two reports corroborate several core elements of the more detailed 20 July 1922 *Banner-Courier* account:

1. the fire involved the **older business section**;
2. one version explicitly places discovery/origin in an **unoccupied Main Street building**;
3. the Oregon City Laundry, May & Washburn(e) garage, Fifth Street restaurant, Miller shoe business, and Ketchner confectionery/home were part of the same loss cluster.

Neither *Morning Register* item identifies Alex Schieremann, S. Wolf, the adjoining pool hall, or the exact Main Street number. They therefore do not distinguish 501, 503, or 505.

## Same-page damage-estimate conflict

The *Morning Register* itself prints two incompatible estimates on the same page:

- **$50,000** in the longer “OREGON CITY HAS BLAZE” item;
- **$30,000** in the separate “Oregon City Has $30,000 Fire” brief.

The local **20 July 1922 Banner-Courier** also reports **$50,000 total damage**, including approximately **$30,000 loss to the Oregon City Laundry alone**.

Preserve the $30,000/$50,000 discrepancy as a contemporary-source conflict. Do not silently normalize the figures.

## Blake / Gage / Harding conflict

The visually inspected *Morning Register* $50,000 item genuinely prints:

- Fifth Street restaurant **owned by Ed Gage of Portland and operated by J. Blake**;
- H. Ketchner's home/confectionery **owned by George Harding**.

By contrast, the local **20 July Banner-Courier** reports:

- Fifth Street restaurant **owned by Ed. Blake**;
- Ketchner home/confectionery **owned by George Gage of Portland and operated by J. Harding**.

The *Register* wording looks structurally like names may have been transposed or garbled during transmission/typesetting, but that is an **inference**, not a correction the archive may make silently. Preserve both contemporary readings until property/business records resolve them.

This conflict is especially important because it shows that apparently precise owner/operator assignments in syndicated or non-local fire reporting can be corrupted even when the broad fire geography is accurate.

## Naming caution — May & Washburn(e)

The high-resolution visual check shows the longer *Morning Register* item prints **May & Washburne**. The short report also reads May & Washburne in the original page. This aligns substantially with the local *Banner-Courier* form. Retain spelling variants where sources differ.
