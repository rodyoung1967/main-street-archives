## Printed report

A short lower-right-column item headed **“Oregon City Has $30,000 Fire”** reports from Oregon City on July 18 that:

- fire was discovered early that day in an **unoccupied building on Main street in the older business section**;
- damage was estimated at **$30,000**;
- the **Oregon City Laundry plant**, **May's Warehouse garage** with two automobiles, the **Fifth street restaurant**, the **Miller shoe shop**, and the **home confectionery store of H. Ketchner** were destroyed.

## Significance

This is an independent contemporary report published the day after the fire. It corroborates several core elements of the more detailed 20 July 1922 *Banner-Courier* account:

1. the fire began in an **unoccupied Main Street building**;
2. the origin was in the **older business section**;
3. the Oregon City Laundry, a garage, Fifth Street restaurant, Miller shoe business, and Ketchner confectionery/home were part of the same loss cluster.

The brief does **not** identify Alex Schieremann, S. Wolf, the adjoining pool hall, or the exact Main Street number. It therefore does not distinguish 501, 503, or 505.

## Contemporary damage-estimate conflict

The *Morning Register* headline/body estimates damage at **$30,000**. The local **20 July 1922 Banner-Courier** reports total damage of **$50,000**, including approximately **$30,000 loss to the Oregon City Laundry alone**.

Preserve these as a contemporary-source conflict. Do not silently replace one estimate with the other or reinterpret the *Register* figure as laundry-only damage; its wording presents $30,000 as the damage caused by the fire.

## Naming caution

The *Morning Register* calls the garage **“May's Warehouse garage.”** The local *Banner-Courier* calls it the frame garage of **May & Washburn(e)**. The project already has independent May/Washburn business chronology. Preserve the *Register* wording as printed rather than treating it as proof of a different garage or owner.
