# 1919 `Fesznecker` / Pesznecker locality correction — Estacada, not Oregon City Fifth/Main

Research date: **9 September 2026**  
Status: **CORRECTION — prior Oregon City spatial interpretation withdrawn; Estacada identity/locality strongly established by contemporary cross-source evidence**

Purpose: correct the archive's earlier use of the 12 Sep 1919 *Oregon City Enterprise* item about `S. Fesznecker` moving a tin/repair shop from Broadway to a building just south of the post office. That item was previously propagated as an Oregon City Fifth/Main Post Office spatial anchor. New contemporary context shows that interpretation is wrong.

## Original 12 September 1919 source wording remains preserved

The project's previously visually reviewed **Oregon City Enterprise, 12 Sep 1919, p. 2** scan states:

> **S. Fesznecker has moved his tin and repair shop from Broadway to the building just south of the post office.**

Source ID: `S-297`  
Evidence ID: `E-271`

The visual transcription itself is retained. What changes is the **locality/identity interpretation**.

## 19 September 1919 — next-week Enterprise context identifies Estacada

The **Oregon City Enterprise, 19 Sep 1919, p. 2** places a follow-up item inside the page's clearly labeled **`Estacada`** correspondence section. It states that a new building was being erected just north of **S. Pesznecker's residence**, which Pesznecker would use for his **machine and plumbing shop** and auto-livery business.

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1919-09-19/ed-1/seq-2/

This is the same trade cluster as the 12 Sep item: tin/repair → machine/plumbing work. The spelling `Fesznecker` in the 12 Sep transcription is therefore best treated as a newspaper/spelling variant or misreading of **Pesznecker**, not as a separate Oregon City operator.

## Independent Estacada identity controls

Contemporary sources independently place **S. P. / Stephen Pesznecker** in Estacada and in the same mechanical trades:

- **21 Jun 1917 Eastern Clackamas News** — `CASCADE GARAGE — S. P. Pesznecker — Estacada, Oregon`.
- **7 Oct 1917 Sunday Oregonian** automobile registration list — **Stephen Pesznecker, Estacada, Or.**, Dodge.
- **21 Aug 1919 Eastern Clackamas News** — `S. P. PESZNECKER, Manager` in an Estacada business context.
- **12 Feb 1920 Eastern Clackamas News** — local Estacada column states **S. Pesznecker made a business trip to Portland**.
- **2 Dec 1920 Eastern Clackamas News** — advertisement for **S. P. PESZNECKER Machine Shop**, plumbing, tinning, electric wiring and repairing, **Estacada, Oregon**.
- **29 Jun 1922 Eastern Clackamas News** — Estacada advertising again directs readers to **S. P. Pesznecker**.

These sources establish a long-running Estacada Pesznecker mechanical/repair business that fits the 12/19 Sep 1919 descriptions closely.

## Broadway and post-office wording no longer belongs to Oregon City's Fifth/Main map

The former archive interpretation assumed:

1. `Broadway` meant Oregon City Broadway; and
2. `the post office` meant the new Oregon City Fifth/Main Post Office opened in April 1919.

That inference is withdrawn.

The next-week Enterprise item explicitly situates Pesznecker in **Estacada**, and independent Estacada sources show he was operating there. Estacada also had a Broadway commercial street and its own post office. Therefore the 12 Sep `Broadway` → `building just south of the post office` move belongs to the **Estacada business geography**, not to the Oregon City Fifth/Main storefront reconstruction.

## Consequence for the 503/505 project

`S-297` / `E-271` must **not** be used as evidence for:

- the Oregon City Post Office's exact bay or neighboring storefronts;
- a building immediately south of the Oregon City Fifth/Main Post Office;
- 501 / 503 / 505 Main geometry;
- the July/August 1919 Oregon City cleaner/dyer's position relative to the Post Office;
- any Oregon City Main Street tenant or building.

The earlier comparison between the cleaner/dyer (`two doors south of new post office`) and `Fesznecker` (`just south of the post office`) is withdrawn because the two references concern **different towns**.

## Identity classification

- `S. Fesznecker` in the visually reviewed 12 Sep 1919 Enterprise wording = **source spelling/transcription retained**.
- Same person/business as **S. P. Pesznecker of Estacada** = **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE IDENTITY INFERENCE** based on next-week same-trade follow-up and the independent Estacada business record.
- Expansion to **Stephen Pesznecker** = **strongly supported** by the 1917 automobile-registration entry and Estacada continuity, but the 12 Sep item itself prints only `S.` and should not be rewritten as though the full name appeared there.

## Archive repair rule

Preserve IDs `S-297`, `E-271`, `P-398`, and `BUS-107`; do not renumber or delete them. Update their interpretation/locality to Estacada and mark prior Oregon City Fifth/Main spatial use as superseded.

## Why this correction matters

Removing the false `Fesznecker` anchor prevents an unrelated Estacada business from being used to infer the exact 1919–1922 Oregon City Post Office bay. The Post Office reconstruction should return to the actual Oregon City controls:

- 12 Sep 1918 final east-side Main between Fifth/Sixth lease;
- 15 Apr 1919 actual move to Fifth/Main into two combined store rooms;
- 1919 cleaner/dyer `two doors south of new post office` wording;
- 8 Jun 1922 Dr. Freeze at 505½ `Opposite Postoffice`;
- 1922 Miller's Shoe Store `Opposite Post Office`;
- property/address evidence for the east/even Main frontage.

The exact Post Office bay remains unresolved.
