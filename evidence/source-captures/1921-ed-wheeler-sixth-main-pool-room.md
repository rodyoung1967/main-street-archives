# 1921–1922 Ed Wheeler pool room — Sixth & Main

Status: **1921 location visually verified; August 1922 continued Wheeler name textually verified**

Purpose: preserve an exact-address pool-room anchor near the target block and prevent later 1922 pool-hall references from being collapsed into a single business without operator/address proof.

## 18 November 1921 — exact location

**Oregon City Enterprise, 18 Nov 1921, p. 1**

Source page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1921-11-18/ed-1/seq-1/

The actual scanned newspaper page was visually inspected.

A front-page report on an attempted safe burglary states that:

- the target was the **Ed Wheeler pool room**;
- the pool room was at **Sixth and Main streets**;
- Charles Schram, manager of the **Grand Theatre**, was in the **same building** and heard the explosion;
- the report repeatedly calls the premises a pool room / pool hall.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## 3 August 1922 — Wheeler Pool Hall name still in use after the Fifth/Main fire

The **Banner-Courier, 3 Aug 1922, p. 1** reports on the arrest/confession of George Martin, alias George Sticker. In describing earlier Oregon City robberies, the article says Martin confessed to attempted robberies of the **Grand Theater and Wheeler Pool Hall safes**.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-08-03/ed-1/seq-1/

This article is currently supported by the archive's page text and source-page rendering; the exact 3 August scan should still be visually inspected before marking this specific item VISUALLY VERIFIED.

The reference is important because it shows that the **Wheeler Pool Hall name remained recognizable in Oregon City after the 18 July Fifth/Main fire**. Combined with the November 1921 exact location, the strongest current reading is that Wheeler represents a distinct Sixth/Main pool-hall chain unless later evidence documents a move.

## Why this matters for the 1922 Fifth/Main fire

The July 1922 fire report describes a pool hall adjoining the vacant former Alex Schieremann tailor shop, but does not name the hall. The Ed Wheeler source proves that another pool room was operating on Main Street at **Sixth & Main** only about eight months earlier, and the Wheeler name was still being used in August 1922.

Therefore:

- multiple Main Street pool-hall/pool-room possibilities existed in the immediate era;
- the unnamed fire-adjacent pool hall cannot be assigned to 505 Main merely because the 1925 Sanborn later shows billiards/cigars at 505;
- Wheeler should not be equated with Timms or Leland & Little without a documented transfer/move;
- any Timms / Leland & Little / 505 connection still requires exact-address evidence.

The Ed Wheeler hall appears associated with the Grand Theatre building at Sixth & Main, which is spatially different from the Fifth/Main corner. Unless a later move/transfer is found, this Wheeler pool room is **not itself evidence for the fire-adjacent hall**.

## June 1922 Wheeler-name caution

The visually reviewed 8 Jun 1922 Buyers' Carnival roster includes **A. D. Wheeler and Co.** as a separate merchant entry.

Source:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-06-08/ed-1/seq-10/

Do not equate `A. D. Wheeler and Co.` with **Ed Wheeler's pool room** without additional evidence. The initials/name differ, and the June roster does not describe A. D. Wheeler and Co.'s business type or address.
