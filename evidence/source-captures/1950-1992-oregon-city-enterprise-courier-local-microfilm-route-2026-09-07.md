# Oregon City Enterprise Courier — local microfilm route for 1953–56 505 Main transition

Date reviewed: **7 September 2026**

Status: **OFFICIAL LOCAL-LIBRARY HOLDINGS / DIRECT MANUAL RETRIEVAL ROUTE; NO NEW 505 OCCUPANCY CLAIM.**

Purpose: preserve a concrete local source-access route for the unresolved **Harr's Recreation Center → Well's Recreation → post-November-1955 505 Main** business/operator transition.

## Official holding

The Oregon City Public Library's current **Microfilm Scanning System** page states that its Carnegie Center holds:

- **Oregon City Enterprise Courier: March 1, 1950 to April 8, 1992**.

Official page:

`https://www.orcity.org/490/Microfilm-Scanning-System`

The same page says the microfilm/microfiche reader is a **ScanPro 3000** capable of scanning 16mm and 35mm film at 500 dpi optical resolution; users may print images and save scans to USB. The library instructs researchers with questions about specific publication dates to contact its Reference Department.

Classification: **CURRENT OFFICIAL LOCAL-LIBRARY HOLDINGS / ACCESS ROUTE**. This establishes physical microfilm coverage and scanning capability, not newspaper content or a 505 Main historical fact.

## Why this materially changes the 505 research plan

The physically held Enterprise Courier run begins **1 March 1950**, so it directly covers the unresolved 505 chronology:

- late 1948: Carl R. Harr / Harr's Recreation Center directly documented at 505 in City liquor-license text;
- 1950 Sanborn: billiards / cigars / beer at 505;
- 1953 Salisbury directory synthesis: **Well's Recreation** at 505, proprietor and original page still unresolved;
- 20 November 1955: regional classified advertises the long-established beer/pool tavern at **505 Main** for sale;
- 1956 and later: likely successor / rename / transfer period before the later 505 Tavern evidence.

The Enterprise Courier is the local newspaper most likely to contain business advertisements, pool-tournament notices, liquor-license notices, sale/transfer announcements, proprietor names, telephone numbers, reopening notices, and social/local-business references that are not exposed in the current online Historic Oregon Newspapers corpus.

## Highest-priority inspection windows

When the microfilm is inspected, prioritize:

1. **January–December 1953**
   - `Well's Recreation`, `Wells Recreation`, `Well`, `Wells`, `Welle`, `Welsh`;
   - `505 Main`, `505 Main Street`;
   - Carl R. Harr / Harr's Recreation Center;
   - pool / billiards / recreation / beer / tavern advertising;
   - telephone-number listings and classified/business-directory advertisements.

2. **October–December 1955**
   - local versions or companion notices for the 20 Nov. 1955 505 sale advertisement;
   - proprietor/seller name;
   - visually verified contact phone **`9179`** from the original 20 Nov. 1955 regional ad; search that number for subscriber, proprietor, sale, transfer, and reopening references;
   - change-of-ownership, closing, sale-completed, liquor-license or reopening language.

3. **January–December 1956**
   - first named successor at 505;
   - new liquor-license applicant/operator;
   - first use of `505 Tavern` or any intervening trade name;
   - ads/notices preserving prior operator or transfer language.

4. **Late 1950–1952**, as time permits
   - earliest departure from Harr's name;
   - first Well's/variant appearance;
   - continuity between the 1950 Sanborn use and the 1953 Salisbury entry.

## Search/inspection rule

This is a microfilm/manual route. Do not treat absence from a sampled issue or an unsuccessful keyword-style manual pass as evidence that a business was absent. For a transition claim, preserve the exact issue date, page, scan, printed wording, and surrounding context.

If a decisive item is found, visually capture the original microfilm page and synchronize the relevant source/evidence registers, `BUS-014`, `BUS-015`, `timelines/505-main.md`, and any operator profile only to the level actually supported by the printed source.
