The extracted Title 5 definition text provides ordinance-history annotations including:

- **Auctioneer** — `(Ord. 1090; 3-3-26)`;
- **Bowling Alley** — `(Ord. 1090; 3-3-26)`;
- **Billiard Room** — `(Ord. 1250; 3-7-34)`;
- **Public Dance Hall** — `(Ord. 1110; 4-28-26)`;
- separate indexed text from the same historic code also attributes terms including **Laundry Wagon**, **Peddler**, and **Shooting Gallery** to Ordinance 1090.

### Evidentiary consequence

The 1963 compilation is useful as a **source-history / ordinance-locator lead** for Ordinance 1090, adopted **3 March 1926**.

It must **not** be used as evidence that Ordinance 1090 specifically created or governed the 1925/1926 **505 Main billiards/pool-room** use. The compilation expressly attributes its later `Billiard Room` definition to **Ordinance 1250 of 7 March 1934**, not Ordinance 1090.

That distinction is important disconfirming evidence against treating a generic 1926 business-license ordinance as a direct proxy for the unnamed May 1925 Sanborn billiards/cigars occupant.

No target business, proprietor, address, or 503/505 premises is named in the extracted Ordinance-1090 definition snippets.

## 3. General-license record-structure lead — later code only

A prior extracted-text pass through the 1963 code suggested that the City's historical general-license scheme used written applications routed through the Recorder/Commission, required licenses to be displayed/available for inspection, and contemplated transfers upon changes in ownership or location.

Because the actual 1963 scan pages containing those provisions were not visually rendered in the current environment, preserve this only as **OCR/TEXT-ONLY RECORD-STRUCTURE LEAD**. Do not represent those later-code procedures as proven unchanged rules in 1925–1927 until the original ordinance(s) or visually verified historic-code pages are recovered.

### Research value

If the older application/transfer language traces to a 1920s ordinance, the underlying record series could be unusually high-value because an individual license application or transfer may state fields that annual revenue totals do not, potentially including:

- applicant/licensee name;
- business class/trade;
- premises or location;
- date;
- transfer/change-of-location information;
- ownership-change wording;
- license or receipt number.

This reinforces the existing **MF-017 + MF-024** strategy to recover detailed 1925–1926 license receipts/register/ledger/application material rather than trying to infer occupants from annual aggregate revenue lines.

It does **not** prove that every such field existed in 1925 or that any target application survives.
