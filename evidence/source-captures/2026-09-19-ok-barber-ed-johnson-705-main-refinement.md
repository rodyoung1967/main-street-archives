# Oregon City O.K. Barber Shop / Ed Johnson address refinement — 2026-09-19

## Research question

Can the `O. K. Barber Shop` named in the visually/retrievably preserved 8 June 1922 *Banner-Courier* business list be identified with the unnamed barber adjoining Gilbert Miller in the George A. Harding Fifth/Main property group?

## New primary-source control: Ed Johnson was an Oregon City barber by 1916

The *Oregon City Courier*, 17 February 1916, p.9, carries a business advertisement reading, in substance:

- Sanitary Service
- Skilled Barbers
- Clean Baths
- **ED. JOHNSON — Prop.**

Public scan:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-02-17/ed-1/seq-9/

The same Ed Johnson barber advertisement is also recoverable in the *Oregon City Courier*, 23 November 1916, p.8:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-11-23/ed-1/seq-8/

Evidence state: **PRIMARY NEWSPAPER / DIRECT for Ed Johnson operating an Oregon City barber business in 1916.** The advertisement itself does not print the shop name or street address.

## O.K. Barber Shop building/address lead

The Historic Downtown Oregon City Association marker preserved by the Historical Marker Database identifies the **OK Barber Shop** as a building **built by Ed Johnson** as a four-chair full-service barber shop with bathing facilities. HMDB locates the marker at or near **705 Main Street**. A separate photographic transcription of the physical marker records the heading **“OK Barber Shop — Circa 1907.”**

Sources:
- https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=8746
- Flickr search/index result for the former OK Barber Shop / 705 Main, transcribing the marker as “Circa 1907”: https://www.flickr.com/photos/tags/oregoncitymasoniclodge/

Evidence state: **SECONDARY HISTORICAL-MARKER / BUILDING-LOCATION EVIDENCE.** It is materially stronger than the earlier generic “near 7th” lead because it supplies the specific modern address 705 Main and a circa-1907 marker date, but the circa date and address still require contemporary-source confirmation before being treated as a 1922 directory address.

## June 1922 control

The *Banner-Courier*, 8 June 1922, p.10, names **O. K. Barber Shop** among Oregon City businesses, separately from **Gilbert Miller**:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-06-08/ed-1/seq-10/

This directly establishes an Oregon City business using the O.K. Barber Shop trade name in June 1922, but that page does not print its proprietor or address.

## Current inference

The combined chain is now:

1. Ed Johnson directly operated an Oregon City barber business with baths by February 1916.
2. Local historical-marker evidence identifies the OK Barber Shop building as built by Ed Johnson, at/near modern **705 Main**, circa **1907**.
3. An **O. K. Barber Shop** is independently named in Oregon City in June 1922.

This creates a **STRONG LOCATION/IDENTITY HYPOTHESIS** that the June 1922 O.K. Barber Shop was the long-standing Ed Johnson shop at/near 705 Main, rather than the separate barber adjoining Gilbert Miller in the Fifth/Main Harding property group.

**Do not promote this to documented exclusion yet.** A contemporary 1921–July 1922 source explicitly joining O.K. Barber Shop or Ed Johnson to 705 Main / Seventh & Main is still needed. Trade-name continuity and renumbering/building-address continuity must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## Consequence for the Fifth/Main barber problem

The O.K. Barber Shop should no longer be treated as an equally plausible default identity for the Harding-property barber. It remains a candidate only until the contemporary-address bridge is closed. The 1914–1915 water-ledger control `503½ / Barber Shop` remains independently important and should be pursued as a separate business/proprietor identity.

## Next targets

## Further continuation — Johnson shop continuity back to 1901–1903

A fresh Historic Oregon Newspapers pass recovered much earlier primary references to **Johnson's barber shop** in Oregon City:

- *Oregon City Enterprise*, **4 October 1901**, p.8: a Troy Laundry notice instructs customers to leave orders at **Johnson's barber shop**.
- *Oregon City Enterprise*, **1 November 1901**, p.8: the same laundry notice again directs orders to **Johnson's barber shop**.
- *Clackamas County Record*, **21 May 1903**, p.1: a cleaning/dyeing advertisement uses **Johnson's Barber Shop** as its Oregon City order location.
- *Clackamas County Record*, **23 July 1903**, p.1: another cleaning/dyeing advertisement again uses **Johnson's Barber Shop**.

Public scans:
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1901-10-04/ed-1/seq-8/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063700/1901-11-01/ed-1/seq-8/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063710/1903-05-21/ed-1/seq-1/
- https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063710/1903-07-23/ed-1/seq-1/

These are **PRIMARY NEWSPAPER / DIRECT** for an Oregon City business called Johnson's barber shop in 1901–1903. They do not print a street address or Ed Johnson's given name on the recovered text, so the identity link to the later Ed Johnson / O.K. Barber Shop remains a **strong continuity hypothesis**, not direct proof.

### Effect on the O.K. hypothesis

The marker's circa-1907 date is no longer the earliest indication of a Johnson barber business in Oregon City. A Johnson-named barber shop was operating by 1901. Combined with the 1916 direct Ed Johnson barber advertisement and the 1922 O.K. Barber Shop listing, this strengthens the possibility of a long-lived Johnson barber operation near Seventh/Main. It does **not** by itself prove that the same premises/trade name persisted continuously or that Johnson's 1901 shop occupied modern 705 Main.

### 503½ search result

Targeted public-index searches for `503½`, `503 1/2`, `503-1/2`, `503 Main` + barber, and Miller + barber in the 1916–1922 period did not expose a contemporary source naming the 503½ barber proprietor. Treat this as a **public-index retrieval boundary only**, not evidence of absence.

The next highest-value route for the Fifth/Main barber is therefore the repository's address-based primary material (water ledgers and any directory/license records) rather than further generic web searching.
