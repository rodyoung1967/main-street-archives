# Red Front clothing store location search — pass 2 — 14 September 2026

## Objective

Continue the targeted search for the exact July 1922 Oregon City location of **L. Wolf's Red Front clothing store**, while testing the proposed continuity from **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main (1918)** to the later separate Miller shoe and Wolf clothing businesses.

This pass is discovery research. OCR/search text is not promoted to VISUALLY VERIFIED status unless the original scan was already visually verified elsewhere in the archive.

## New online-search result

A targeted Historic Oregon Newspapers search did **not** recover an exact-address advertisement or notice for the 1922 Red Front clothing store. Searches for combinations of `Red Front`, `Oregon City`, `clothing`, `Wolf`, `L. Wolf`, and exact Portland-address variants were heavily contaminated by the unrelated **Red Front Barn / Red Front Livery** at Sixth & Water and by older unrelated uses of the generic `Red Front Store` name.

Accordingly, the exact numbered address of the July 1922 Red Front clothing store remains **UNRESOLVED**. Search absence is not evidence that Red Front lacked a numbered address or advertisement.

## Important identity/address caution: 33 North Third Street, Portland

The visually verified 21 July 1922 Oregon City Enterprise fire account gives **L. Wolf, 33 N. 3rd St., Portland** in connection with Red Front and the recently purchased shoe-store/fire-origin building.

A new Portland-newspaper search shows that **33 North Third Street was publicly advertised in 1921–1922 as a Christian Science reading room**. This creates a significant caution about interpreting the Enterprise wording. The printed `33 N. 3rd St.` may have been a mailing/business contact address, a misprint, OCR/transcription issue, or may involve a multi-use building; it should **not automatically be treated as Wolf's residential address** without a Portland directory or other independent record.

This does not weaken the Enterprise identification of **L. Wolf** as Red Front owner; it only changes how the Portland address should be used in identity research.

Discovery sources:
- Sunday Oregonian, 22 Jan 1922, section 5 p.2: Christian Science reading room at 33 North Third Street.
- Sunday Oregonian, 30 Jul 1922, section 5 p.2: same address continuing as a reading room.

These pages were found through online newspaper search in this pass; visual-verification credit should be assigned only after original-page inspection under archive standards.

## Wolf & Miller continuity — strengthened context, still not proof of L. Wolf identity

The already visually verified 10 October 1918 Oregon City Courier item remains the strongest local continuity control:

- **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main**;
- shoes for the family;
- men's clothing and furnishing goods;
- an up-to-date shoe-repairing department;
- after the sale, **one member of the firm would go to Idaho and the other would re-stock the store and continue the business**.

Online discovery also reconfirmed the August 1918 Eugene Daily Guard Springfield report that **C. E. Miller, of the Wolf and Miller Department store**, left Springfield for Oregon City after closing out stock, joining Mrs. Miller and family. This remains subject to the archive's C. E. Miller/Gilbert Miller identity control and cannot by itself identify the Wolf partner.

The business-category fit remains striking: the 1918 partnership combined **shoes + shoe repair + men's clothing/furnishings**, while by July 1922 the fire accounts separately identify **Gilbert Miller's shoe business** and **L. Wolf's Red Front clothing store** within the same small property/fire group. Combined with the 1918 statement that one partner would leave and one would continue, this continues to support the archive's **PROBABLE / WORKING** post-partnership division hypothesis.

It is still not direct proof that L. Wolf was the Wolf partner, nor direct proof of how inventory/assets were divided.

## Red Front spatial constraints after this pass

Current evidence should be kept in this form:

1. Red Front was a **distinct business-loss line** from Gilbert Miller's shoe shop in July 1922.
2. L. Wolf owned Red Front according to the visually verified Enterprise account.
3. L. Wolf also had recently purchased the shoe-store/fire-origin building according to that account.
4. The project now reconstructs that fire-origin/Miller/former-Schiermann building as **503 Main** by strong cross-source inference.
5. The adjoining pool/billiard premises at **505 Main** survived the fire with water and roof damage, with the northern structure protected by fireproof construction.
6. These facts do **not yet locate Red Front**. Ownership of both a clothing business and the 503 shoe-store building does not prove the clothing business occupied that same building.
7. The 13 July Harding sale report remains critical because it groups the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller shoe store, adjoining barber shop and Red Front in the Harding property transaction. The missing discriminator is the storefront order / parcel dimensions / exact tenant address.

## Highest-value next discriminators

- Search the original 1922 Banner-Courier and Enterprise pages immediately before the fire for display ads headed `Red Front`, `Wolf`, or clothing/furnishings, including visually checking pages where OCR may omit display-ad text.
- Search 1918–1921 Oregon City papers for the **continuing business after the 10 October 1918 Wolf & Miller sale**, looking for a successor name and proprietor.
- Identify the Wolf & Miller Wolf partner by full name from Springfield/Oregon City directories, assumed-business-name records, partnership notices or legal notices.
- Re-check the Enterprise scan itself around `33 N. 3rd St.` to ensure punctuation/number/initial were read correctly before using that address as an identity key.
- Retrieve the Harding → Wolf/Gates deed package (`MF-006`) and OHS Block 4 Lots 3–4 abstract (`MF-007`) when an authorized accessible route is available; dimensions, improvements or tenant descriptions could settle Red Front's location.
- Search for the **adjoining barber shop** by proprietor/address. Because the 13 July sale report explicitly calls it adjoining Miller, fixing the barber's numbered address may indirectly fix Red Front's position even if Red Front never advertised its own number.
