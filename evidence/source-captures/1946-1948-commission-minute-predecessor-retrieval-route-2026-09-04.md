# 1946–1948 Oregon City Commission minute predecessor retrieval route

Date reviewed: 4 September 2026

Status: **OFFICIAL MUNICIPAL PDF TEXT + CURRENT CITY RECORDS-ACCESS ROUTE + TARGETED PREDECESSOR-PAGE RETRIEVAL GAP. NO NEW FARR→HARR TRANSITION DATE ESTABLISHED.**

Purpose: pursue the two highest-value municipal gaps in the Farr's Pool Hall → Harr's Recreation Center chronology: the Commission minutes immediately preceding municipal page 318 in 1946 and municipal page 474 in 1948. Preserve exact page boundaries, current archive-access routes, unsuccessful retrieval methods, and a narrowly scoped records request so future work does not repeat broad searching.

## Evidence boundary

This pass does not treat failed public search as evidence that Farr, Harr, 505 Main, or a liquor-license action is absent from the missing pages. The target predecessor pages were not recovered from the public web-search layer.

Keep **C. P. Farr** separate from **Edwin F. Farr / Edwin Franklin Farr**. The March 1946 and March 1948 municipal claim OCR contains a C. P. Farr row, but no source recovered here connects that person to Edwin F. Farr, Farr's Pool Hall, or 505 Main.

## 1946 hard boundary — municipal page 318

Official Oregon Records Management Solution / Oregon City WebDrawer record:

`https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4723048/File/document`

The recovered five-page PDF begins at municipal page **318**. A closer reading corrects the earlier shorthand that page 318 simply begins the 6 March meeting.

Municipal page 318 first contains the **tail end of the previous Commission meeting**, including a Clackamas County Court letter concerning Seventeenth Street. The letter is signed by County Judge E. L. Pope and Commissioners C. W. Kruse and George G. Randall. The preceding meeting then adjourns.

Only after that adjournment, on the same municipal page 318, appears the header:

> Oregon City, Oregon, March 6, 1946  
> REGULAR MEETING

The March 6 roll call names:

- George Woodward — Mayor;
- Raymond P. Caufield — Commissioner;
- W. G. H. Krueger — Commissioner;
- J. L. Franzen — City Manager.

The March 6 record states that the minutes of the previous meeting were read and approved and then proceeds into February claims.

### Retrieval consequence

Because page 318 physically closes the prior meeting before starting March 6, the missing predecessor record is not merely a generic earlier-month target. The exact manual target is:

- municipal pages **immediately preceding p.318**, provisionally **pp.314–317 or enough earlier pages to include that meeting's opening header**;
- capture the entire preceding meeting, not only the page containing a possible license list;
- search that complete meeting for liquor, beer, license, renewal, endorsement, recommendation, transfer, relocation, pool, billiard, Farr, Harr, and 505 Main;
- if any applicant list appears, preserve its complete applicant universe and exact Commission action.

The public OCR/index searches did not expose pp.314–317 or the prior meeting header. Searches by likely February 1946 dates, commissioners' names, municipal page numbers, liquor-license vocabulary, and stable later licensees returned either the March 6 record or unrelated later records. This remains a **retrieval gap**, not a negative finding.

### Visual-verification status

The official PDF text was retrievable and searchable. A screenshot request for PDF page 0 failed with a WebDrawer cache-miss error on 4 September 2026. Therefore the page-boundary statements above are **OFFICIAL PDF TEXT / NOT SCAN-CERTIFIED IN THIS PASS**.

## 1948 hard boundary — municipal page 474

Official Oregon Records Management Solution / Oregon City WebDrawer record:

`https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4723051/File/document`

The recovered six-page PDF begins cleanly at municipal page **474** with:

> Oregon City, Oregon, February 11, 1948  
> SPECIAL MEETING

Roll call:

- Raymond P. Caufield — Mayor;
- W. G. H. Krueger — Commissioner;
- George Woodward — Commissioner;
- Alden Miller — City Attorney;
- Robert A. Finlayson — City Manager.

The same page independently preserves the already-known pool-hall proceeding:

> Messrs. Harr and McDougald appeared before the Commission requesting permission to allow card playing in the pool halls.

No address is printed in that line. The Commission took no action at that time.

The same PDF then begins the 3 March 1948 regular meeting on page 474 and continues through its claims.

### Retrieval consequence

The exact missing predecessor target is:

- municipal pages **immediately preceding p.474**, provisionally **pp.470–473 or enough earlier pages to include the preceding meeting header(s)**;
- priority date span: **December 1947 through 10 February 1948**;
- preserve every annual or small-batch liquor/beer-license action, renewal, endorsement, recommendation, transfer, relocation or ownership change;
- if a list is recovered, preserve its complete applicant universe, not merely Harr/Farr/505 entries.

Targeted public searches for p.470–473, likely December 1947 / January-February 1948 meeting dates, the commissioners' names, and standard liquor-license wording did not recover the predecessor pages. This remains a **retrieval gap**, not absence evidence.

### Visual-verification status

The official PDF text was retrievable. Screenshot attempts for municipal p.474 and the claims pages again failed with WebDrawer cache-miss errors on 4 September 2026. The February 11 wording therefore remains **OFFICIAL PDF TEXT / NOT SCAN-CERTIFIED IN THIS PASS**.

## Current Oregon City archive transition matters

Oregon City's current **Meeting Document Archive** states that the City participates in the Oregon Records Management Solution and provides a direct decade link for **1940 to 1949 City Commission Agenda Packets and Minutes**:

`https://www.orcity.org/1844/Meeting-Document-Archive`

The underlying 1940s WebDrawer query exposed by that official link is:

`https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record?q=webdrawercode%3A%22%2A023%2A%22+And+recTitle%3A%22%2A194%2A+city+commission%2A%22+And+recContainer%3A3AAgenda+Packets+and+Minutes&sortBy=recTypedTitle-`

In the current research environment the decade query itself returned a cache miss, while individual known record PDFs remained text-retrievable.

Separately, Oregon City's current **Public Records Self-Service Portal** states that the City is transitioning to a new electronic records-management system and warns that there may be delays accessing records during the transition. It says Oregon City now uses **Laserfiche** and that documents continue to be added to the E-Vault:

`https://www.orcity.org/596/Public-Records-Self-Service-Portal`

This system transition is a plausible access explanation for why older WebDrawer decade navigation and predecessor records are currently difficult to expose. It is an access condition, not historical evidence.

## Ready-to-submit targeted records request

Suggested request title:

**Historical City Commission minute pages immediately preceding pp.318 and 474 (1946 / 1948)**

Suggested request text:

> I am conducting historical research using Oregon City's digitized City Commission minutes and need two small missing page ranges that are not currently discoverable through the public WebDrawer/Laserfiche search.
>
> **1. 1946 minute book:** Please provide clear digital scans of the municipal minute pages immediately preceding page 318, preferably pages 314–317 and any additional preceding page necessary to include the opening header/date of the meeting that ends at the top of page 318. Public WebDrawer record 4723048 begins at page 318 with the end of that prior meeting and then starts the March 6, 1946 regular meeting on the same page.
>
> **2. 1947–48 minute book:** Please provide clear digital scans of the municipal minute pages immediately preceding page 474, preferably pages 470–473 and any additional preceding page necessary to include the complete meeting header/date(s). Public WebDrawer record 4723051 begins at page 474 with the February 11, 1948 special meeting.
>
> For both ranges I am specifically trying to identify any City Commission action on liquor or beer license applications, renewals, endorsements, recommendations, transfers, relocations, or changes of ownership. If the requested page ranges do not contain the complete relevant meeting, please include enough adjacent pages to preserve the full meeting and any complete applicant list.
>
> Electronic PDF or image copies are sufficient. Existing archival scans are preferred; no new transcription is necessary.
>
> Known public record references: WebDrawer record 4723048 (municipal p.318, March 1946) and WebDrawer record 4723051 (municipal p.474, February-March 1948).

### Why this request is deliberately narrow

The goal is to obtain existing scans rather than commission open-ended staff research. The requested anchors already identify the exact minute book and adjacent public pages. If the City can supply the predecessor images directly, they can be visually reviewed for the complete annual license lists without further staff interpretation.

## What to capture when the pages arrive

For every liquor/beer action in the supplied pages record:

1. exact meeting date and meeting type;
2. municipal page number;
3. Commission action verb: approved, endorsed, recommended, denied, tabled, etc.;
4. every applicant/licensee in the action, preserving the complete universe;
5. trade/business name;
6. premises/address;
7. license class if printed;
8. renewal / new application / transfer / relocation / ownership wording;
9. exact Farr, Harr, McDougald or 505/505½ wording;
10. scan image or PDF page sufficient for visual certification.

Do not infer absence from a list until the list is established as complete. Do not merge C. P. Farr with Edwin F. Farr unless a contemporary source supplies the connection.

## Current historical conclusion

This retrieval pass **does not narrow the Farr → Harr transition interval**. It does, however, replace two broad manual targets with precise municipal page requests and documents a current City records-system transition that explains why the public predecessor scans are not readily navigable.

The strongest unresolved municipal targets are now:

- the meeting ending immediately before / at the top of **p.318** in early 1946;
- the meeting(s) immediately before **p.474** in late 1947 / early 1948.
