# 2000 Downtown Final Report — 503 / 505 Main visual verification

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED original PDF page**

Source:
https://www.orcity.org/DocumentCenter/View/4025/Downtown-Final-Report---2000-PDF

In the current research session, the original City of Oregon City PDF was opened and the relevant appendix page was inspected visually rather than relying only on extracted text or OCR.

## Visually verified entries

Appendix B, page 30, under the historic-resource inventory directly lists:

- **503 Main St. — Kwality Cafe — c.1919**
- **505 Main St. — Unknown — c.1920**

These are now accepted as **visually verified statements of what the 2000 city inventory reports**.

## Interpretation limits

The c.1919 and c.1920 dates remain **historic-inventory estimates**, not contemporaneous construction permits or proof of the exact construction mechanics. They may represent original construction, rebuilding, substantial alteration, or an estimate developed by later survey researchers.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

1. **address continuity** — 503 and 505 were active numbered commercial addresses before 1919/1920; and
2. **surviving-building chronology** — later survey work dates the resources at approximately 1919 and 1920.

Earlier exact-address evidence proves predecessor-premises use:

- **503 Main**: Henry Brightbill in 1908; H. H. Smith pool-hall application in 1912; L. Hylton in 1914.
- **505 Main**: Chicago Store in 1911–1913; Wolf & Miller in 1918.

Thus the survey dates must not be used to erase or reinterpret the earlier address history.

## 1925 Sanborn cross-check

The correct **1925 Sanborn Sheet 6** has also been visually inspected in the project. It shows separate modern-style address labels including **503** and **505**:

- **503** — restaurant
- **505** — billiards / cigars

This proves a distinct 505 commercial premises existed by 1925. It makes the later 2021 cultural-resources report's **1940** build date incompatible with a simple reading that the entire 505 premises was first constructed in 1940.

However, the 1940 date is not simply discarded. It could reflect a later rebuild, major alteration, facade replacement, assessor convention, or another phase of the surviving structure. Primary permits, assessor records, tax improvement records, deeds, and building-fabric comparison are still needed.

## Current construction-date handling rule

- Treat **c.1919 (503)** and **c.1920 (505)** as visually verified **2000 City inventory estimates**.
- Treat **1940 (505)** as a conflicting later survey estimate requiring explanation.
- Do not call any survey date definitive original construction without primary structural evidence.
- Continue comparing pre-1925 and post-1925 footprints/materials to determine predecessor vs. surviving-building continuity.
