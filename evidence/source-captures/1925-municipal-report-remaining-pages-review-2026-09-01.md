## E-141 — license-revenue and police-recordkeeping context

### What the scans establish

1. **Printed pp. 31–32 (PDF 16–17):** both consolidated and general-fund operating statements report **Franchises and Licenses: $6,576.91 for 1925**, versus **$7,897.49 for 1924**, a **$1,320.58 decrease**. These are two presentations of the same combined revenue category, not two independent license totals. They do not separate franchise revenue from license revenue or name payers, license types, numbers, terms, businesses or premises. Neither the amount nor the decrease proves a business count, closure, vacancy, or a change at 503/505.
2. **Printed p. 11 (PDF 6):** the Police Department says a system of permanent records had been installed for arrests and important complaints, with identification records as well. The annual table reports **two "Selling without license" cases**. No people, businesses, addresses or case dates are supplied. Do not associate these cases with Farr, a pool hall, the 503 restaurant, or any Main/Fifth business.

### Inferences and limits

The revenue schedules make the underlying **1925 franchise/license account detail and receipts** a more specific retrieval target; the police statement identifies a separate possible **arrest/complaint record series**. Those original records might supply names or premises, but this report does **not** establish that they survive, are online, or contain a relevant case. Business receipts and restaurant-inspection returns remain higher-priority occupant leads than an undirected police search.

The financial appendix's buildings, rents, street improvements, water plant, bonds and warrants are municipal account categories. They do not date private construction at 501, 503 or 505. In particular, printed p. 24's City Hall/municipal-building credit and p. 35's bond dates must not be read as target-property construction or permit evidence.
