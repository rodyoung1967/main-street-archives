## Spatial relationship to 501/503/505

Project-steward context places this Post Office on the same side of Main Street as 501/503/505. Existing archive map evidence independently resolves the geometry more precisely: the 1950 Sanborn shows the **U.S. Post Office at the southeast corner of Fifth and Main, marked BUILT 1933** (`E-102`). The 501/503/505 frontage is on the **east side of Main north of Fifth**. Therefore the Post Office was on the **same east side of Main**, but on the **Fourth-to-Fifth block immediately across Fifth Street from 501**; it was not part of the 501/503/505 block.

The steward also described the building as being "at the end of 4th street". That wording is preserved as supplied context but is not used as the controlling spatial statement because the CCHS title and Sanborn geometry directly anchor the building at **Fifth and Main**.

## Historical significance and limit

This photograph is valuable fixed-landmark evidence for the south side of Fifth Street and for understanding the immediate setting opposite 501. CCHS's construction-year wording independently agrees with the Sanborn `BUILT 1933` notation. The image should **not** be assigned a demolition year until a dated demolition, redevelopment, city, newspaper, or photographic record establishes when the building was removed.
