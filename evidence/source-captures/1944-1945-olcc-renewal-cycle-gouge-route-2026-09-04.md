# 1944–1945 OLCC renewal cycle — judicially documented records route

Date reviewed: 4 September 2026

Status: **AUTHORITATIVE JUDICIAL RECONSTRUCTION OF CONTEMPORARY OLCC RENEWAL PROCEDURE — SOURCE-SELECTION / DATE-WINDOW FINDING ONLY; NO FARR/HARR/505 LICENSE IDENTIFIED.**

Purpose: tighten the checkpoint-directed 1945–1947 liquor-license search for the unresolved Farr's Pool Hall → Harr's Recreation Center transition at 505 Main. This source does not establish any Farr or Harr license. Its value is that the Oregon Supreme Court reproduced a detailed 1944–1945 OLCC renewal chronology from Commission files and minutes, allowing the 505 search to target the correct months rather than treating each calendar year as a single undifferentiated block.

## Source

Oregon Supreme Court, **Gouge v. David et al.**, 185 Or. 437, 202 P.2d 489 (1949).

Accessible case-text route used in this pass:

`https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914a11fadd7b04934684123/amp`

The opinion identifies the Oregon Liquor Control Act as Oregon Laws 1933, Second Special Session, chapter 17, and reconstructs the renewal history for Leonard Gouge / Kelly's Place in Silverton from OLCC applications and Commission minutes.

This is a judicial opinion quoting and summarizing primary OLCC records. The original OLCC application and minute pages still remain preferable for visual inspection when accessible.

## Documented 1945 renewal sequence

For Gouge's 1945 retail beer/package licenses, the opinion records the following sequence:

1. **6 December 1944** — OLCC received the applications and required fees for licenses covering the period ending 31 December 1945.
2. **17–19 January 1945 regular monthly meeting** — Commission considered the renewal but deferred action while seeking written confirmation of the Silverton City Council's refusal to endorse it.
3. **14–16 February 1945 regular monthly meeting** — Commission considered the matter again and continued it for further investigation.
4. **23 February 1945 special meeting** — Commission refused the renewal after receiving the local council's written non-endorsement.
5. **14–16 March 1945 monthly meeting** — after a hearing, the Commission reconsidered and granted the licenses.

The opinion also quotes the governing statute allowing the Commission to require a written recommendation from the relevant city council and to consider that recommendation when granting or refusing a license.

It further states that Commission licenses expired at midnight on **31 December** of each year.

## Research consequence for 505 Main

This materially narrows the 1945 Farr/Harr search window.

A license intended to cover calendar year 1945 may have generated records **before 1 January 1945**, including:

- renewal application and fee receipt in **December 1944**;
- Oregon City Commission / council endorsement or recommendation in **December 1944–January 1945**;
- OLCC License Supervisor correspondence;
- January–March 1945 OLCC meeting-minute consideration, deferral, approval, refusal, or reconsideration;
- a temporary-operation or pending-renewal notation if final action lagged beyond 1 January.

Therefore the active 505 search must start no later than **December 1944**, not January 1945.

For a potential Farr → Harr transition, inspect the equivalent renewal cycle for **1945, 1946, 1947 and 1948**, always beginning in the preceding December. Record every Oregon City pool hall/tavern/recreation-premises licensee rather than searching only Farr or Harr.

## Evidence boundary

The Gouge case documents OLCC procedure in another Oregon city and for another licensee. It does **not** prove that Farr or Harr applied on the same exact day, held the same license classes, received the same treatment, or operated at 505 in any particular year.

No 505 occupancy, Farr/Harr business succession, or transition date is promoted from this finding. It is a targeted records-route refinement only.
