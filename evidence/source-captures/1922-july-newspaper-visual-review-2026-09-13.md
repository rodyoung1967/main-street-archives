## Direct historical evidence visually confirmed

### 13 July 1922 Banner-Courier, page 2 / sequence 2 — Dr. Freeze relocation

The rendered page was inspected at full size. A Dr. Freeze advertisement states **“New Location / New Equipment”** and gives his address as **207–8 Masonic Bldg., Oregon City, Ore.**

This is a direct dated post-505½ location control. Together with the visually confirmed 8 June 1922 advertisement at **505½ Main St.**, it brackets Dr. Freeze's move away from 505½ Main to the Masonic Building between those dated advertisements.

This is business/office-location evidence only. It does not establish who owned the 505½ real estate or when a physical building was demolished or rebuilt.

Source path: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07/1922-07-13-seq-02.pdf`

### 20 July 1922 Banner-Courier, page 1 / sequence 1 — Fifth/Main fire

The rendered front page was inspected at full size. The article **“BIG FIRE DESTROYS QUARTER BLOCK IN OREGON CITY”** directly reports the fire that began early Tuesday morning, 18 July 1922.

The page directly states, in substance:

- the fire caused roughly $50,000 damage and swept a half block in the older part of the city;
- the Oregon City Laundry was the largest loss;
- other losses included the frame garage of May & Washburne, the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller shoe-repair shop, and the Ketchner home/confectionery context;
- the flames were discovered about 2 a.m. in a **vacant store room formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop**;
- billiard tables in a **pool hall adjoining the tailoring establishment** were ruined by water and the roof of that building was partly burned;
- the building in which the fire started was **owned by S. Wolf of Portland** and had been used until recently as a tailoring shop.

This visually re-confirms an important structural distinction: the article describes the adjoining pool hall as water-damaged with a partly burned roof; it does **not** explicitly say that the adjoining pool-hall building was destroyed. It also does **not** state an exact street number for that pool hall. The project therefore must not equate the adjoining pool hall with 503, 505, or 505½ solely from this article.

Source path: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07/1922-07-20-seq-01.pdf`

## July interpretation controls

The July visual review is consistent with, but does not by itself resolve, the existing 501/503/505 fire-and-rebuild models.

Keep these propositions separate:

1. S. Wolf owned the fire-origin building described in the 20 July report.
2. Alex Schieremann had recently occupied the fire-origin room as a tailor shop.
3. A pool hall adjoined that tailoring establishment and sustained water/roof damage.
4. The article does not provide the pool hall's exact numbered address.
5. Dr. Freeze had moved from 505½ Main to the Masonic Building by 13 July, before the fire.
6. None of those facts alone establishes the post-fire construction date of the surviving 503 or 505 buildings.

The corrected November 1922 P-9594 photographic interpretation remains a separate structural control: 501 at left, apparent open/empty space at 503, and a shadowed structure farther right at 505. That photograph must continue to be reconciled with the July reporting, deeds/assessment records, later rebuilding reports, and the 1925 Sanborn without assuming same-address equals same-building continuity.
