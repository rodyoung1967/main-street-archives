# 1922 adjoining barber + Wolf & Miller identity online pass

Date: 2026-09-14 (Pacific)

## Scope

Follow-up to `2026-09-14-1922-fire-frontage-red-front-conversation-handoff.md` and `2026-09-14-miller-209-fifth-visual-correction-and-barber-lead.md`.

Targets:

1. identify the barber shop described as adjoining Gilbert Miller's shoe store in the 13 July 1922 Harding property-sale report;
2. test whether the older exact `503½ / Barber Shop` water-ledger control can be bridged into 1922;
3. resolve or narrow the identity of the `Wolf` in **Wolf & Miller**, 505 Main (1918), and the 1922 **S. Wolf / L. Wolf** discrepancy.

No institutional/manual request was sent.

## Barber findings

### 8 June 1922 Banner-Courier

The issue's buy-at-home/business list includes both **Gilbert Miller** and **O. K. Barber Shop** among Oregon City businesses. The page does not print a street number for O. K. Barber Shop and does not state that it adjoined Miller.

Evidence state: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT for the O. K. Barber Shop name being active in Oregon City on 8 June 1922; UNVERIFIED LEAD for identity with Harding's July 13 adjoining barber.**

### 503½ control

The archive's 1914–1915 Oregon City water ledger directly classifies **503½ / Barber Shop**, alongside `503 / Store`. This remains important spatial evidence that a barber sub-premises/half-address existed within the 503 frontage before 1922.

The current online search did **not** recover a 1921–July 1922 source explicitly printing `O. K. Barber Shop, 503½ Main`, `503 1/2 Main`, or another exact-number bridge.

### Current barber conclusion

The strongest defensible reconstruction remains:

- July 13, 1922 directly says **Miller's shoe store + adjoining barber shop** within Harding's named property group.
- Earlier exact evidence proves a **503½ barber-shop sub-premises** existed in this frontage.
- The fire reconstruction independently places **Miller/former-Schiermann at 503** and the surviving pool hall at **505**.

Therefore a 503/503½ internal-bay arrangement is **plausible and spatially economical**, but the July 1922 barber is **not yet proved** to be O. K. Barber Shop or 503½. Do not collapse the lead into fact.

## Wolf & Miller findings

### 10 October 1918 Oregon City Courier p.4

The page directly identifies **Wolf & Miller, 505 Main**. The business description says it was selling shoes, men's clothing and furnishings at reduced prices, had an up-to-date shoe-repair department with Landis electric machinery, and that after the sale **one of the firm was going to Idaho and the other would restock the store and continue the business**.

This is strong direct evidence of a partnership split/continuation event, but the item does **not name which partner left** and does **not identify either partner by first name/initial**.

### Gilbert Miller family-network lead

A web-accessible compiled genealogy identifies Oregon City's **Gilbert Miller (1878–1934)** and says his first wife was **Clara Rose Wolf (1879–1921)**, daughter of Henry and Elizabeth (Conrad) Wolf. This is a potentially important family-network lead but is a secondary compiled genealogy, not sufficient to identify the `Wolf` partner in Wolf & Miller or the S./L. Wolf purchaser/business owner of 1922.

Classification: **UNVERIFIED / SECONDARY LEAD** pending primary genealogy, directory, deed, probate, marriage, obituary or business evidence.

### S. Wolf / L. Wolf remains unresolved

Current primary-source pattern remains:

- **13 July 1922 Banner-Courier:** `S. Wolf` purchases Harding's remainder with C. Gates taking the restaurant location.
- **20 July 1922 Banner-Courier:** `S. Wolf of Portland` owns the fire-origin building.
- **21 July 1922 Oregon City Enterprise:** `L. Wolf` owns Red Front and is described as recent purchaser of the shoe-store building.

The online pass did not recover a decisive primary record proving whether `S. Wolf` and `L. Wolf` are the same person, a typographical/initial error, relatives, or distinct parties.

Likewise, no decisive source yet proves that `L. Wolf` is the Wolf partner from **Wolf & Miller**. The continuity remains a **PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS**, strengthened circumstantially by the 1918 clothing/shoes/shoe-repair mix and the later Miller-shoe / Wolf-clothing pattern, but not proven.

## Research implications

1. The **barber question is narrowed but not solved**. Exact-address/direct proprietor evidence remains needed.
2. The 1918 Wolf & Miller item directly supports a partner split/continuation but does not tell us who continued.
3. The Gilbert Miller–Clara Rose Wolf marriage creates a potentially useful Wolf-family route; it must not be used to identify S. or L. Wolf without primary bridging evidence.
4. The best decisive sources remain Portland/Oregon City directories around 1918–1922, Harding→Wolf/Gates deeds (`MF-006`), OHS title abstract (`MF-007`), probate/genealogy records, and exact-address newspaper advertisements.

## Negative-search discipline

Failure to recover an exact 1922 O. K. Barber address or a full-name Wolf identity in this pass is a coverage result only, not proof that the connection did not exist.
