# 1924 target-frontage photograph hunt — checkpoint

Review date: **9 September 2026**

Scope: targeted search for photographs from **1923–1925** that may show **501, 503, or 505 Main Street, Oregon City**, including images cataloged under bluff views, bridge views, downtown streetscapes, nearby landmarks, and other non-address descriptions.

## Confirmed repository control: CCHS P-267

Clackamas County Historical Society photograph **P-267**, **“Oregon City and the Arch Bridge as Seen from Bluff,”** is already preserved in the repository as `IMG-0770`.

Institutional metadata dates the photograph **1924–1928**. The project steward identifies the later-numbered **501/503/505 target row** in the image; direct visual comparison supports a materially changed post-1918 frontage state. The street numbers themselves are not readable in the photograph, and the CCHS date is a range rather than an exact exposure year.

Therefore:

- it is **possible** that P-267 was taken in **1924**;
- it is **not proven** that the exposure year was 1924;
- it is currently the strongest known photograph in the project that both falls within a date range beginning in 1924 and visibly includes the target frontage;
- it must not be described as an exact-1924 target-frontage photograph without narrower dating evidence.

Canonical source/evidence/media: `S-312` / `E-284` / `IMG-0770`.

## Exact-1924 external lead: Ralph Eddy negative No. 157

A public Old Oregon Photos catalog page describes **“Upper Oregon City — 1924”**, item **CC0002**, from an original **Ralph Eddy** negative held by the Clackamas County Historical Society, photographer's negative **No. 157**.

Public page reviewed: https://www.oldoregonphotos.com/upper-oregon-city.html

The public description explicitly dates the photograph **1924** and notes upper Oregon City, the new Arch Bridge, and the Atkinson Memorial Church under construction.

Current evidence status: **BIBLIOGRAPHIC / IMAGE LEAD ONLY for 501/503/505**.

The available public web-sized image has not yielded a defensible identification of the 501/503/505 target row. Do not claim that those buildings appear in the photograph until a higher-resolution institutional derivative or negative scan is visually inspected and the target block is mapped with adequate confidence.

## OHS downtown-from-bluff lead: 371N5709

Oregon Historical Society Digital Collections has a record titled **“View of downtown Oregon City, Oregon”**, identifier **371N5709**, an Oregon Journal negative taken from the bluff with downtown Oregon City and the Arch Bridge in view.

Public record reviewed: https://digitalcollections.ohs.org/cityscapes-oregon-oregon-city

The current catalog dating is **1923–1936**, so this source cannot presently be treated as a 1924 photograph. Its viewpoint makes it a potentially useful target-frontage lead, but the broad date range substantially limits chronological value unless it can be narrowed independently.

Current evidence status: **BIBLIOGRAPHIC / IMAGE LEAD ONLY**.

### Follow-up

- Recover the highest-resolution OHS image available for 371N5709.
- Determine whether 501/503/505 can be mapped in the frame.

## Current conclusion

As of this checkpoint, the project does **not** have a photograph proven to have been exposed specifically in **1924** that also positively identifies 501/503/505 Main Street.

The strongest present photographic sequence is:

1. **1918** — CCHS bluff photograph documenting the predecessor target-frontage state.
2. **1924** — Ralph Eddy No. 157 / CC0002, exact-year Oregon City photograph; target-row visibility **unproven**.
3. **1924–1928** — CCHS P-267 / `IMG-0770`, target row spatially identified; exact exposure year **unresolved**.
4. **1923–1936** — OHS 371N5709, downtown-from-bluff candidate; target visibility and exact year **unresolved**.
5. **1925** — Sanborn map remains the tighter independent by-1925 building-layout control, but is not photographic evidence.

## Research priority

Highest-value next step: attempt to **narrow the exposure date of CCHS P-267** and obtain/inspect the full-resolution **Ralph Eddy No. 157** image. Either route could materially tighten the chronology of the frontage replacement/reconfiguration between the documented 1918 state and the 1925 Sanborn state.

## Evidence limits

- Catalog date ranges are not exact exposure dates.
- A photograph's broad downtown coverage does not prove that a particular storefront is visible.
- Target-bay identification based on established map geometry must remain distinct from readable address-number evidence.
- Failed retrieval or inability to resolve a storefront in a low-resolution web image is a **retrieval/inspection gap**, not negative evidence that the storefront is absent.
