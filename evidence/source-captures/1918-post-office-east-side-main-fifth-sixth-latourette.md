# 12 Sep 1918 — Oregon City Post Office lease on east side of Main between Fifth and Sixth

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**

Purpose: establish the actual location chosen for the Oregon City Post Office lease beginning in 1919, because 1921–1922 advertisements repeatedly use `opposite Post Office` / `near Post Office` as relative-address language around the 503/505 fire-research area.

## Primary source

**Oregon City Courier, 12 Sep 1918, p. 1**

Canonical page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-09-12/ed-1/seq-1/

PDF:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1918-09-12/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

The actual scanned newspaper page was visually inspected. In the lower-right portion of page 1, a short item headed **`Location of Post Office Settled`** states that, despite a petition opposing the change, the Post Office Department had decided to relocate the Oregon City post office and had entered a **ten-year lease** for:

- a building on the **east side of Main Street**;
- **between Fifth and Sixth streets**;
- owned by **M. D. Latourette**;
- at **$1,800 annual rent**.

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Earlier August proposal was different — now visually verified

The actual scanned page of the **8 Aug 1918 Oregon City Courier, p. 1** was successfully recovered and visually inspected on 9 Sep 2026. The item headed `UNOFFICIAL REPORT SAYS POST-OFFICE MAY MOVE` directly says the Post Office might move from its then-current location opposite the courthouse to a new site **at Fourth and Main streets**. It says First National Bank and Barlow & David had offered a location occupied by **Klemsen's grocery store and a furniture store**, on **Main Street between Fourth and Fifth**.

## Why this matters for the 1922 fire reconstruction

The archive has visually reviewed 1921–1922 advertisements placing **Dr. Freeze at 505½ Main** with the wording **`Opposite Post Office`**. A June 1922 `Miller's Shoe Store` advertisement also uses **`Main Street — Opposite Post Office`** wording and advertises expert shoe repairing.

The September 1918 lease source fixes the Post Office on the **east side of Main between Fifth and Sixth**. Therefore businesses described as `opposite Post Office` in the 1921–1922 Fifth/Sixth block context are naturally on the **west side of Main**, the same side containing the odd-numbered 501/503/505/507 frontage.

This is an important spatial constraint, but it does **not** by itself prove an exact street number for the Post Office, Dr. Freeze's ground-floor neighbor, Miller's Shoe Store, or the July 1922 ignition room.

## Research consequence

The strongest next step is to determine the **exact numbered address of the fire-period Post Office rooms**. Once that is known, relative-address phrases such as:

- `505½ Main — Opposite Post Office`;
- `Miller's Shoe Store — Main Street opposite Post Office`;
- `near Post Office`;

can potentially be converted into tighter storefront placements.

This may be particularly useful for testing whether `Miller's Shoe Store` was Gilbert Miller's pre-fire shop and whether that shop was at/near 505, 503, or another west-side Main storefront.

## What is NOT established

Do not currently claim that:

- the Post Office itself had any particular 5xx Main number;
- `Miller's Shoe Store` was Gilbert Miller without a direct identity bridge;
- every business saying `opposite Post Office` occupied 505;
- the post-office lease building and any earlier Post Office building were the same premises;
- the August 1918 Fourth/Fifth proposed site was the site ultimately chosen.

## April 1919 follow-up — actual move and conflicting ownership description
<!-- 1919 April Enterprise Courier visual review — 2026-09-07 -->

`S-292` / `E-266` visually verifies the **18 Apr. 1919 Enterprise p1** report that the Post Office actually moved on **15 April 1919 to Fifth and Main**, into two store buildings combined into one large room. The article says one room was owned by **First National Bank** and the other by the **Barlow-David heirs**; First National Bank rented the latter and sublet the combined quarters to the federal government. It identifies **M. D. Latourette as vice president of First National Bank** and credits his efforts in securing the location.

This materially qualifies this capture's earlier statement that the September 1918 source proved Latourette ownership of the leased building. The **1918 source does directly print that ownership description**, but the **1919 source directly conflicts with it**. Current classification: **UNRESOLVED CONTEMPORARY OWNERSHIP-DESCRIPTION CONFLICT**. Possible agency, building-name, ownership-date or reporting differences remain hypotheses until lease/deed/property records resolve them.

## September 1919 locality correction — former `Fesznecker` corroboration withdrawn
<!-- 1919 Pesznecker locality correction — 2026-09-09 -->

The 12 Sep 1919 Enterprise item previously treated here as an Oregon City post-office-neighborhood anchor is **withdrawn from this reconstruction**. The paper printed `S. Fesznecker` moving a tin/repair shop from Broadway to a building just south of a post office, but a next-week 19 Sep item in the explicitly labeled **Estacada** correspondence section describes **S. Pesznecker** and the same machine/plumbing trade there. Independent contemporary records repeatedly place S. P. / Stephen Pesznecker in Estacada.

Current classification: the 12 Sep source wording remains visually verified, but its earlier **Oregon City Fifth/Main locality interpretation was wrong**. It belongs to Estacada business geography and must not be used to locate the Oregon City Post Office, cleaner/dyer, or 501/503/505 frontage.
