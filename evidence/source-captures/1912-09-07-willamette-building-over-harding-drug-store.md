## Printed advertisement

A Spirella Corsets advertisement for **Mrs. Adalyn Davis, Spirella Corsetiere**, appearing on the upper-left portion of page 3, prints:

- **SATURDAYS**
- **Willamette Bldg., Room 4, Over Harding Drug Store.**
- Phone Main 3552.

The wording is visually confirmed on the scan; it is not being promoted from OCR alone.

Search indexing also shows the same wording in the 14 and 28 September 1912 issues, but those repeat pages have not been credited as visually reviewed in this record.

## Why this matters

This adds a direct 1912 physical/vertical relationship between a named **Willamette Building** room and **Harding Drug Store**: Room 4 of the Willamette Building was described as **over Harding Drug Store**.

It must be reconciled with other already verified evidence:

- `E-085`: in March 1911 George Young moved **one door north into the Willamette Building** from a Harding Building premises on Main between Fifth and Sixth; neither building is numbered in that article.
- `E-092`: on 5 January 1912 the exact Main Street ladder visually places **G. H. Young at 507 Main**, **H. P. Brightbill at 509 Main**, and **Geo. A. Harding at 511 Main**. Young's March-1911 Willamette Building continuity to 507 is a hypothesis, not a proved identity.
- `E-137`: in March 1921 Dr. Freeze was said to be locating **over Hardings Drug Store**, followed by exact ads at **507½ Main**; Harding-at-numbered-507 in 1921 is retained only as a strong inference pending a direct numbered Harding source.

## Interpretation and conflict rule

The 1912 Spirella advertisement **does not justify equating the Willamette Building with 507 alone**, nor does it justify changing Harding's directly printed January 1912 business address of **511 Main**.

Plausible models to test include:

1. the named Willamette Building encompassed more than one numbered Main Street bay, potentially including space over Harding's 511 storefront;
2. George Young's 1911 Willamette Building premises and his January 1912 507 address were not continuously the same room/building identity;
3. contemporary building names, storefront numbers, and upstairs-room addresses did not map one-to-one;
4. one of the named-building descriptions was broader or less precise than the numbered business advertisements.

None of these models is selected by this source alone.

## Significance for 503/505 reconstruction

The result is relevant because it warns against reconstructing the Fifth–Sixth Main frontage as a simple one-building-per-number ladder. Named buildings could contain or relate to multiple storefront/upstairs spaces. That is important when interpreting half-numbers such as **505½** and **507½**, the 1911→1925 Sanborn footprint change, and later descriptions of offices located “over” neighboring businesses.

It does **not** date demolition/reconstruction of 503 or 505 and does not prove that the surviving 505 structure existed in September 1912.
