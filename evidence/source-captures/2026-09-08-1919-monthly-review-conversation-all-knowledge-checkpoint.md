## January 1919 — useful neighboring-frontage evidence

The complete January batch was **70 pages**: 40 Enterprise + 30 Courier.

### Direct controls

- **2 Jan. 1919 Courier p.3:** Pantorium Cleaners is directly at **500 Main Street**. An earlier visual misreading of this as `510` was corrected before integration; the original scan and OCR both support **500**.
- **2 Jan. 1919 and repeated January Enterprise/Courier advertising:** Ohio Painless Dentists / Ohio Dentists at **507½ Main Street, over Harding's Drug Store**.
- **Dr. J. G. Nash** is identified as manager in the January Ohio Dentists advertising; integrated as `P-397` with business `BUS-106`.

### Interpretation limit

Earlier direct controls put Harding Drug Store at **511 Main** (including 1911/1915). Therefore the January 1919 wording is evidence that the neighboring address/spatial configuration had changed by early 1919, but the ads do **not** print `Harding Drug Store, 507 Main`. Do not convert `507½ over Harding's` into an exact lower-floor 507 address without an independent source.

### Target result

No source-explicit **501, 503, or 505 Main** occupant and no target construction/demolition/rebuild/remodel event was recovered in January. This is bounded coverage only, not vacancy/non-use evidence.

A source quirk is preserved in the January capture: the genuine **30 Jan. 1919 Courier** issue has p.1 correctly dated 1919 while interior running headers on pp.2–6 print **1918**. Content confirms they belong to the 1919 issue; this is treated as a printed-header error, not page substitution.

## February 1919 — Cox billiard-parlor sale

The complete February batch was **56/56 pages**.

### Direct sequence

- **6 Feb. Courier:** F. D. Cox received a billiard-hall license for the year.
- **13 Feb. Courier:** reports that **Cox Billiard Parlors were sold** to a man from Ocean Falls, British Columbia, named **Mr. Rohrberg** in that report.
- **14 Feb. Enterprise:** independently confirms the purchase and says the buyer would assume charge Wednesday morning, corresponding to **12 Feb. 1919**.

The February sources do **not** print the billiard-parlor address. Cox's 1915 hall had been between Seventh and Eighth, but that older location is not silently carried forward to 1919, and the February sale is not assigned to 503/505.

Ohio Dentists / Dr. Nash continue at **507½ over Harding's** through February; Theroux Music House is directly at **210 Seventh Street**. Farr Brothers appearances remain unnumbered and are not bridged to Farr's Pool Hall at 505.

## April 1919 — Post Office move / parcel-geometry breakthrough

The complete April batch was **56/56 pages**.

### Direct Post Office event

**18 Apr. 1919 Enterprise p.1** reports that the Oregon City Post Office actually moved on **15 Apr. 1919** to **Fifth and Main**. The new quarters consisted of **two store buildings combined into one large room**:

- one owned by **First National Bank**;
- one owned by the **Barlow-David heirs**;
- First National Bank rented the Barlow-David room and sublet the combined quarters to the federal government.

### Cross-source geometry and conflict

The owner names strongly recur in the independent 1913 Block 26 lot controls (Barlow/David on Lot 5; First National Bank on Lot 6). This is a **strong cross-source spatial inference**, not a source-explicit numbered storefront assignment.

A **12 Sep. 1918 Courier** report had instead described the proposed leased building as owned by **M. D. Latourette**. April 1919 identifies Latourette as the bank vice president involved in securing the quarters while naming First National Bank and the Barlow-David heirs as the store-room owners. Preserve this as an **unresolved contemporary ownership-description conflict**; do not silently choose one version.

No exact 501/503/505 occupant or target structural event was established by the April batch.
