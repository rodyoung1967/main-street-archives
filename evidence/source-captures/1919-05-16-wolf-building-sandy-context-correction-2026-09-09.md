# 16 May 1919 “Wolf building” — Sandy Department context correction

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED CONTEXT CORRECTION — FALSE OREGON CITY / 503–505 LEAD CLOSED**  
Review date: **9 September 2026**  
Existing archive IDs: source `S-293`; evidence `E-267`; timeline `T-100`.

## Source inspected

- *Oregon City Enterprise*, 16 May 1919, p. 2.
- Repository original: `newspapers/oregon-city-enterprise/1919-may/1919-05-16-seq-02.pdf`.
- Item heading: **NEW CONFECTIONERY OPEN.**

The item says alterations to the `Wolf building` were nearing completion and describes a full-length rustic front porch, a rustic side pergola, and a blue-and-white interior. Those words remain directly documented.

## Page-layout finding

Visual inspection of the complete original page establishes context that detached OCR text does not preserve:

- the rightmost page section has the large boxed heading **SANDY DEPARTMENT**;
- the `NEW CONFECTIONERY OPEN.` item appears inside that section, below other Sandy subheads and immediately above the department's **LOCALS** subhead;
- the department identifies Mrs. Blanche R. Shelley as its representative.

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT** that the item is Sandy Department news. The article does not print a numbered address, Wolf's identity, or the confectionery proprietor, so those details remain unresolved **within Sandy**.

## Effect on the 503/505 project

The item is not an unlocated Oregon City building-alteration lead. It is a Sandy-area item and supplies no evidence about:

- 501, 503, 505, or 505½ Main Street in Oregon City;
- the surviving 503/505 building's construction or remodeling date;
- Wolf & Miller (`BUS-012`);
- S. Wolf of Portland (`P-035`) or the 1922 fire-property sequence.

The earlier same-name lead was appropriately kept unmerged, but its geographic status is now resolved. No search for a 1919 Oregon City confectionery should use this article as its premise.

## Method note

This correction demonstrates why the archive requires page-level visual inspection. OCR recovered the small article but separated it from the large departmental heading and surrounding column structure, making an Oregon City reading appear possible. The complete scan rules that reading out.
