## Purpose

This continuation tests the period immediately after the visually verified **20 November 1955** classified advertisement offering the long-established beer-and-pool business at **505 Main Street, Oregon City** for sale (`S-037` / `E-039`). The unresolved question is whether an Oregon City Commission liquor-license action can identify the purchaser or successor operator between the 1953 official-secondary **Well's Recreation** point and the later **505 Tavern** chronology.

The pass also tests the procedural assumption that a buyer/change could appear in a single in-year Commission action rather than an annual renewal roster.

## New procedural control — 19 July 1956 special meeting

Official Oregon City Commission WebDrawer record **`4751849`**:

`https://ormswd.synergydcs.com/HPRMWebDrawer/Record/4751849/File/document`

The official searchable municipal text contains a **19 July 1956 special meeting** of the City Commission. It records that the Commission considered and unanimously approved the liquor license of **Carlise Grocery, 102 Molalla Avenue**.

This is **not a 505 Main record** and creates no relationship between Carlise Grocery and Farr's Pool Hall, Harr's Recreation Center, Well's Recreation, or the unnamed business offered for sale at 505 in November 1955.

Its value is procedural: Oregon City was still handling a **single named business's liquor-license approval in an isolated special meeting in July 1956**, eight months after the 505 sale advertisement. A post-sale 505 buyer/licensee therefore could plausibly appear in a similarly small action outside a large annual renewal list.
