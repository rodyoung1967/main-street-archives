## Project-steward oral history — chronology revised 6 September 2026

Rodney Young initially recalled, with uncertainty, that Morton may have fallen after the later Myrtle Point period and before Oregon City. On **6 September 2026**, he revised that recollection and said he now thinks **Morton came right after Chicago and before Coquille**.

The current family-memory hypothesis is therefore:

1. Raye and Floyd were in **Chicago** and had the rooming-house business;
2. the family may then have spent time in **Morton, Washington**;
3. Raye may have worked at **The Wheel Cafe in Morton**;
4. the family then moved to **Coquille / Coos County, Oregon**;
5. the Morton Wheel owners may later have owned **The Wheel at 503 Main, Oregon City**;
6. Raye may later have bought the Oregon City Wheel from those owners.

Rodney explicitly cautioned that these details are remembered family history and may be partly wrong.

Rodney also corrected the remembered telephone numbers during this pass:

- **656-5505** — probably Raye's home line, not the Wheel business line;
- **656-9717** — remembered as **The Wheel's business telephone** during the later family-ownership period.

Classification: **FAMILY / ORAL-HISTORY LEAD**. Do not use the proposed Morton chronology, employment, ownership bridge, or either telephone number as a dated business fact until matched to a contemporary directory, advertisement, license, newspaper, or other dated record.

## Direct contemporary control — Raye's 1954 account conflicts with a substantial Morton stop

The visually reviewed **11 March 1954 Coquille Valley Sentinel** profile of Raye is unusually specific about the family's move west. It says Raye first came to Coos County with Floyd and the children on a vacation trip in **1944**; the family then returned to their **home in Chicago**, sold their **rooming-house business**, and moved to **Coquille**. The article separately says the LaFarlettes' decision to move to Coquille had been made **10 years earlier**.

### Interpretation

Rodney's revised **Chicago → Morton → Coquille** memory and Raye's 1954 **Chicago → Coquille** account are now an **UNRESOLVED CHRONOLOGY CONFLICT**. The contemporary profile weighs strongly against a long, separate Morton residence after Chicago and before Coquille, but it does not rule out:

- a brief stop in Morton omitted from a short newspaper profile;
- temporary work or travel rather than formal residence;
- a move sequence simplified by the reporter;
- a family-memory chronology that has shifted over time.

Do not choose between these explanations without a Morton or Coos County record from the 1944–1947 interval.

## Coos County control — Floyd is independently in the local business record by August 1947

A visually inspected **7 August 1947 Coquille Valley Sentinel**, p. 13, reports that the trucking firm **Anderson and LaFarlette** had been awarded the new **Coos Bay–Powers mail contract**. This directly demonstrates a LaFarlette business presence in southern Coos County by August 1947.

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088222/1947-08-07/ed-1/seq-13.pdf

Classification: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — VISUALLY VERIFIED** for the Anderson and LaFarlette trucking firm and its Coos Bay–Powers mail contract by 7 August 1947.

A **29 January 1948 Coquille Valley Sentinel** publication of Coos County Court proceedings for December 1947 also includes an **Anderson-LaFarlette, Freight** charge. Later 1948 advertisements directly place **Anderson & LaFarlette Motor Freight** in service between North Bend, Coos Bay, Coquille, Myrtle Point, Broadbent and Powers. On **23 June 1949**, the Sentinel reported that Carl A. Anderson purchased partner **Floyd LaFarlette's** interest in the freight line.

These later items confirm that the 1947 business is the Floyd LaFarlette partnership rather than merely a same-surname coincidence.

### Chronological consequence

If the revised Morton memory is substantially correct, the strongest present search window is now **after the Chicago rooming-house sale and before the documented Coos County business presence of August 1947**, with **1944–1947** as the working bracket. Raye's 1954 account suggests the actual interval, if any, may have been much shorter and centered around **1944**.

## Morton, Washington — Wheel Cafe chronology

### Business identity

Later public listings and local-history material identify **The Wheel Cafe**, later **Wheel Cafe & Jubilee Room**, at **145 Main Street, Morton, Washington**. These later sources establish the existence/location of the Morton business but do not identify its 1940s–1960s proprietor.

### Earliest present existence evidence is not yet early enough for the revised family chronology

A later obituary for **Faye Edith (Hunt) Kaiser** says she worked as a waitress at **The Wheel Cafe in Morton** before her **1952 marriage**. The obituary is secondary retrospective evidence, so it does not establish the exact beginning/end of her employment, but it places the Morton Wheel in the community by the early 1950s.

Indexed text for the **11 December 1957 Centralia Daily Chronicle** also includes the Wheel Cafe in a list of Morton businesses; the page image was not recovered in this research environment.

Classification: **SECONDARY BIOGRAPHICAL EVIDENCE** for existence by the early 1950s; **OCR/INDEX TEXT ONLY / RETRIEVAL GAP** for the 1957 mention.

**Critical boundary:** no source recovered yet proves that the Morton Wheel Cafe existed in **1944–1947**. That is now a decisive question. If Raye worked there before the family's Coquille move, the restaurant must be documented earlier than the project's present early-1950s existence anchor.
