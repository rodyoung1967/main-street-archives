# 1916 — 501 Main restaurant / Fifth Street Restaurant / Holman Fifth & Main location caution

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED LEDGER + PRIMARY NEWSPAPER OCR/TEXT; IDENTITY BETWEEN THE TWO RESTAURANT REFERENCES UNPROVEN**

Purpose: preserve a new cross-source constraint on the early history of **501 Main** and prevent the archive from treating the undertaking business advertised at **Fifth & Main** as the default occupant of 501 without a numbered-address bridge.

## 1. Official 1916–1917 water ledger — 501 Main classified as Restaurant

The relevant pages of Oregon City's official **1916–1917 Water Rent Record Book 4** were visually reviewed from user-supplied screenshots of the official City scan on 23 Aug 2026.

The odd-numbered Main Street sequence includes:

**511 → 509 → 507 → 503 → 503½ → 501 Main**

The **501 Main** entry contains a handwritten use/classification notation that reads **`Restaurant`** with high confidence. A nearby notation appears to read **`5th`**, but its meaning remains unresolved and must not be expanded into a street-address claim.

## 2. 31 Aug 1916 Oregon City Courier — a named Fifth Street Restaurant existed

The **31 Aug 1916 Oregon City Courier**, p. 3, prints a state dairy-and-food-commissioner inspection report covering Oregon City hotels and restaurants.

The Oregon City list includes a business explicitly called:

- **Fifth Street Restaurant** — inspection score **75.3**.

Primary archive page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-08-31/ed-1/seq-3/ocr/

Verification state: **PRIMARY OCR/TEXT ONLY in this research pass**. The archive page is accessible, but the PDF/page-image endpoint could not be brought into the screenshot-capable viewer during this pass. Do not mark this item visually certified until the actual scan is inspected.

This proves at the primary-text level that a business styled **Fifth Street Restaurant** existed in Oregon City during the same ledger period in which 501 Main is classified as a restaurant.

## 3. The tempting identification is NOT yet proven

The contemporaneous combination is highly suggestive:

- official ledger: **501 Main — Restaurant**;
- local newspaper: **Fifth Street Restaurant** active in Oregon City;
- 501 is the first odd-numbered Main storefront at/near the Fifth/Main corner in the later 1925 Sanborn sequence.

However, no recovered 1916 source yet says:

- `Fifth Street Restaurant, 501 Main`;
- `Fifth Street Restaurant, Fifth & Main`;
- a proprietor name that can be matched to the 501 ledger tenant line.

Therefore the correct status is:

> **Fifth Street Restaurant = the 501 Main restaurant is a plausible cross-source hypothesis, not a proven identity.**

This is nevertheless relevant to the July 1922 fire because the fire destroyed a business again called the **Fifth Street restaurant**. The 1916 evidence establishes that this business name/use existed at least six years earlier, but does **not** yet prove uninterrupted continuity or a 1922 501 address.

## 4. Conflict with the existing Holman-at-501 hypothesis

Separate primary newspaper advertisements place **R. L. Holman / Holman & Randall undertaking parlors at `Fifth and Main`** during 1914–1917.

For example, a **13 Apr 1916 Oregon City Courier** advertisement prints:

- R. L. Holman and T. P. Randall;
- Leading Undertakers;
- **Fifth and Main St.**

Primary page:
https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1916-04-13/ed-1/seq-2.pdf

The project has correctly treated `Holman at Fifth & Main → 501 Main` as a hypothesis rather than a proven numbered address. The water-ledger evidence now gives a concrete reason to **downgrade that mapping further**:

- during the same 1916 period, the official water ledger explicitly labels **501 Main as Restaurant**, not undertaking/funeral use;
- no recovered Holman advertisement prints `501 Main`;
- an intersection description such as `Fifth and Main` can refer to another corner, another entrance, a building spanning multiple spaces, or a premises whose numbered relationship differs from the later 1925 map.

Accordingly, **do not use Holman's Fifth & Main advertisements as evidence that 501 Main was an undertaking establishment in 1916** unless a numbered-address or map/property bridge is recovered.

## 5. Possible implication for the 1922 fire geometry

The **20 Jul 1922 Banner-Courier** says the fire destroyed the **Fifth Street restaurant**. The exact address of that restaurant remains unresolved.

The 1916 pairing now makes **501 Main a more substantive location candidate for the Fifth Street Restaurant business/use** than a generic corner guess because 501 is independently documented as a restaurant in the official ledger during the same year the named Fifth Street Restaurant appears in the newspaper.

But six years separate the sources, and no continuity chain has yet been established. Therefore:

- **501 Main restaurant in 1916:** proven from visually reviewed ledger;
- **Fifth Street Restaurant existed in 1916:** strong primary-text evidence, scan visual certification pending;
- **1916 Fifth Street Restaurant = 501 Main:** plausible hypothesis;
- **1922 Fifth Street Restaurant = same business/location as 1916:** unresolved;
- **1922 Fifth Street Restaurant = 501 Main:** unresolved.

If future evidence does place the 1922 Fifth Street Restaurant at 501, that would materially constrain the ignition-room reconstruction because the fire report separately describes an **unoccupied Main Street room** as the origin and a **pool hall adjoining the former tailor room**.

## What is NOT established

Do not claim that:

- Holman never occupied any portion of the Fifth/Main corner area;
- the ledger's nearby `5th` notation is a street address;
- the Fifth Street Restaurant definitely occupied 501 Main in 1916;
- the Fifth Street Restaurant stayed in one location continuously from 1916 to 1922;
- the 1922 restaurant was 501 Main;
- this resolves whether the ignition room was 501, 503 or 505.
