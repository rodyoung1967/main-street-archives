# 503 Main post-fire demolition — online route pass — 14 September 2026

Status: **ONLINE-FIRST ROUTE PASS; NO DIRECT DEMOLITION RECORD FOUND. STRONG WORKING HYPOTHESIS REMAINS THAT 503 WAS REMOVED AFTER THE JULY 1922 FIRE AND BEFORE THE NOVEMBER 1922 PHOTO.**

## Purpose

Test online sources outside the already-completed 1922 newspaper visual sweep for evidence explaining the apparent disappearance of the 503 Main storefront/building between the July 18, 1922 Fifth/Main fire and the November 1922 Fifth/Main bridge photograph.

This pass specifically tested municipal-record access, deed/tax-record availability, and additional online photographic collections. It also re-read the controlling July 20, 1922 Banner-Courier fire account so that later demolition reasoning remains anchored to the contemporary wording.

## Controlling fire wording rechecked

The July 20, 1922 *Banner-Courier* report says the fire "swept away a half block" and says there was practically no insurance on the "other buildings destroyed." It separately says:

- the fire was discovered in a vacant storeroom formerly occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop;
- the building in which the fire started was owned by S. Wolf of Portland;
- the pool hall adjoining the tailoring establishment suffered water-damaged billiard tables and a roof that was partly burned off;
- Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop was destroyed.

The article does **not** expressly say that the Wolf-owned Schieremann storeroom itself was destroyed, nor does it expressly say that it remained standing. Therefore it does not resolve whether the 503-position structure survived the fire and was later razed, or was structurally lost during the fire event.

Project-steward photo interpretation continues to favor the former: the 503-position structure existed at the fire origin in July and was subsequently removed before the November photograph. Preserve that as a strong working reconstruction until a direct demolition/permit/tax/photo source is recovered.

## Deed / property-record route

The Oregon Secretary of State Clackamas County records inventory confirms that the deed series covering 1922 survives, including:

- Deed Books through the relevant period;
- direct and indirect deed indexes covering the period;
- Clackamas County Archives deed records covering 1916-1959;
- State Archives Clackamas deeds through 1957.

However, the online inventory is a holdings guide, not a public page-image system for the 1922 deeds. No publicly exposed online image of the June-August 1922 Harding -> Wolf/Gates deed(s) was recovered in this pass.

This confirms that `MF-006` remains one of the most valuable direct property-geometry routes: the deed legal descriptions may establish exactly what frontage Gates received and what Wolf retained immediately before the fire.

## Assessment / tax route

The Oregon State Archives land-records guide confirms **Clackamas County Assessment and Tax Rolls, 1850-1949** and an **Assessment and Tax Rolls Index, 1901-1949** survive.

The modern Clackamas County online assessment/tax site does not expose 1922 parcel assessment images; its public web material is oriented to current/recent tax data. No public online image of the 1922-1925 target assessment pages was recovered in this pass.

Therefore `MF-019` remains a high-value manual route. A 1922-to-1923 improvement-value change for the target Block 4 lots could materially test whether a structure was removed after the fire.

These collections confirm additional 1922 Oregon City photography survives, but the indexed online descriptions reviewed in this pass did not identify a securely dated July-October 1922 view that directly resolves the 501/503/505 frontage.

OHS does have a later bluff view of downtown Oregon City (`371N5709`) dated broadly **1923-1936**, but that range is too wide to establish the immediate post-fire transition.

The photo route therefore remains worth targeted visual inspection where image angle/date permit, especially ODOT/CCHS bridge-construction negatives that may incidentally include the Fifth/Main frontage.

## Result

No new online source in this pass directly says `503 Main was razed` or gives a demolition date.

The strongest current structural reconstruction remains:

1. the 503-position structure existed immediately before the July 1922 fire and is the archive's best-supported location for the Wolf-owned former Schieremann storeroom;
2. the adjoining 505-position pool-hall premises survived at least to the extent described as water/roof damaged rather than explicitly destroyed;
3. project-steward reading of the November 1922 photograph shows the 503 position open/empty while 505 remains present;
4. therefore **post-fire removal/demolition of the 503-position structure between July and November 1922 is a strong working hypothesis**, but the exact date and reason remain undocumented.

Possible reasons remain open and must not be promoted without a source: structural instability/fire damage, owner-directed demolition, condemnation, parcel redevelopment, preparation for new construction, or another post-fire decision.
