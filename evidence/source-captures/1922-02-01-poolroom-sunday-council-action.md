# 1 February 1922 Oregon City council — poolrooms Sunday action

Status: **VISUALLY VERIFIED newspaper report; council-minute packet still to retrieve**

Purpose: establish the exact city-council meeting date at which Oregon City acted on Sunday operation of poolrooms, creating a high-value archival target for the 1922 pool-hall address reconstruction.

## Source

**Banner-Courier, 2 Feb 1922, p. 10**

Verification state: **VISUALLY VERIFIED**.

## Directly supported facts

The item states that the **Oregon City council “last night” voted for open poolrooms on Sunday** and that, by order of Mayor Shannon, they had been closed during the preceding month.

Because the newspaper is dated **Thursday, 2 February 1922**, the reported council action occurred on **Wednesday, 1 February 1922**.

The short newspaper item does **not** identify individual pool halls, proprietors, or premises.

## Relationship to 20 January ordinance evidence

A visually reviewed / primary-page Oregon City Enterprise item dated **20 Jan 1922** discusses a new pool-hall ordinance then approaching final council consideration. It says the draft's Sunday-closing clause had been struck by unanimous council consent at the prior meeting and that petitions seeking Sunday closure were expected at the next regular session.

Together, the January and February reports sharply identify the municipal-record target:

- January 1922 — new pool-hall ordinance under active consideration;
- **1 Feb 1922 — council votes for Sunday opening of poolrooms**.

The corresponding 1 February council minutes, ordinance text, license applications, petitions, or related clerk records may name the existing halls or the particular premises they occupied.

## Why this matters to the Fifth/Main fire problem

The July 1922 fire began in a Main Street room adjoining an unnamed pool hall. Current candidates include separate chains for:

- Ed Wheeler / Wheeler Pool Hall;
- Timms Pool Hall;
- Leland & Little.

A contemporary city pool-hall license or permit naming the **particular place** could directly establish whether Timms or Leland & Little occupied 505 Main before the fire and thereby materially constrain whether the ignition room was 503 or 501.

## What is NOT established

Do not infer from this newspaper item that:

- Timms Pool Hall attended or was specifically affected by the 1 February action;
- Leland & Little was then a pool hall;
- any pool hall was at 505 Main;
- the ordinance/license records necessarily survive;
- the Sunday-opening vote itself granted or renewed a particular business license.
