# E-061 — Formal Oregon City Street Numbering, 1906

Type: Primary newspaper / municipal-context evidence  
Date: May–August 1906  
Sources: `S-075`, `S-169`  
Related evidence: `E-060`  
Related research lead: `RL-025`

## Claims Supported

Oregon City moved from the preliminary numbering effort reported in 1905 to a formal municipal numbering program in 1906:

- **25 May 1906:** Postmaster T. P. Randall told the City Council that free mail delivery required houses to be regularly numbered and street names posted. The Council referred the matter to the Streets and Public Property committee, which was to prepare ordinances.
- **6 July 1906:** the *Oregon City Enterprise* reported that Mayor Caufield had approved the street-numbering ordinance and that a **plat designating the correct numbering** had been filed with Recorder Dimick. Property owners could consult the plat for the **true number** of a home or place of business.
- **13 July 1906:** council expenses included Bruce C. Curry for work “in re house numbering” and R. B. McAlpin for “numbering plats.” Councilman Rands reported finding duplicate street names while drawing maps for house numbering, and a special committee was appointed to oversee the work.
- **17 August 1906:** street signs had arrived for posting at intersections and the completed numbering plat remained at Recorder Dimick’s office. Residents were urged to obtain and post their numbers so federal inspection for free mail delivery could proceed.
- The same 17 August issue advertises Lerow’s Glass Blowers at **504 Main Street**, showing that exact numbered Main Street addresses were already appearing in print during the implementation period.
- **24 August 1906:** a legacy-branch *Courier* council report, retained as `S-169`, says an ordinance was passed “Providing for numbering of houses.” The retained endpoint is OCR/TEXT ONLY in this consolidation and is corroborating context, not a replacement for the visually/independently established ordinance-and-plat trail.
- **3 October 1913:** a City Recorder notice says that a **plat and plan showing the proper street number for all property in Oregon City** remained on file in the recorder's office and quotes Section 5 of **Ordinance No. 357** requiring assigned numbers on buildings (`E-105` / `S-119`).

## Significance for 501 / 503 / 505 / 507 Main

The **1906 numbering plat is a high-value missing source**. If recovered, it may directly connect pre-1906 storefront descriptions and the 1900 Sanborn frontage with the later numbered sequence 501 / 503 / 505 / 507.

This also changes how pre-1906 newspaper evidence should be handled. A business described only as “Main Street,” “Fifth and Main,” or relative to the Post Office should not automatically be assigned a later street number simply because that number existed by 1908–1912.

## Search result for exact target addresses

Targeted 1906 searches for **501 Main**, **503 Main**, **505 Main**, and **507 Main** did not recover a contemporaneous 1906 source directly identifying an occupant of those later-numbered addresses. This is a research-status statement, not evidence that the storefronts were vacant or absent.

## Confidence

**Very High** for formal ordinance/plat implementation in 1906 and for a filed municipal numbering plat.  
**Very High** that the recorder-held plat/plan remained an operational municipal record in October 1913.
**Unresolved** for the actual 1906 plat contents and exact mapping of the target storefronts.
