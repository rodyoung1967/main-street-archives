# E-092 — 503 / 507 / 509 / 511 Main Address Ladder, 1912

Type: Primary newspaper advertising / exact-address reconstruction evidence  
Date: 5 January 1912  
Source: `S-102`  
Related evidence: `E-050`, `E-051`, `E-085`, `E-089`  
Related research leads: `RL-025`, `RL-026`, `RL-029`, `RL-035`

## Claims Supported

The **5 January 1912 Oregon City Courier** business page provides an unusually dense exact-address sequence on Main Street. Visual inspection of the original page image establishes:

- **H. N. Smith — Pool Room — 503 Main Street**;
- **G. H. Young — The Curio Man — 507 Main Street**;
- **H. P. Brightbill — 509 Main St.**;
- **Geo. A. Harding — 511 Main St.**

The page OCR contains important number errors: it reads Smith as 603, Brightbill as 609, and garbles Harding's number. The page image itself clearly reads **503, 509, and 511** respectively. Existing `E-050` already preserves the Young/Brightbill address evidence; this record emphasizes the full visual address ladder and its implications for reconstruction.

The same page also places The Chicago Store **on Main Street just below the Post Office**, M. E. Dunn and Schrader's Bakery **near the Post Office**, J. Tolpolar **opposite the Post Office**, and Corbett & Company in the **Post Office Building**. Those relative descriptions are not converted to numbered addresses.

## Significance for Harding / Willamette Mapping

`E-085` establishes that George Young moved **one door north into the Willamette Building** from the Harding Building in March 1911. By January 1912 he is directly documented at **507 Main**. This strengthens the hypothesis that his Willamette Building premises may have been 507, but it does **not** prove that he remained continuously in the same premises from March 1911 to January 1912.

Geo. A. Harding's own business is directly at **511 Main** on this page. This is further evidence that **George Harding's business address must be kept separate from the location of the named Harding Building**; the archive must not derive the building's number merely from Harding's personal/store listing.

## 505 Gap

The page prints 503, 507, 509 and 511 but does not give an exact `505 Main` advertisement. The Chicago Store's relative wording on the same page cannot be converted into 505 despite its documented 505 Main address in spring 1911.

Absence of 505 from this particular business page is not evidence of vacancy.

## Confidence

**Very High** for the visually verified 503 / 507 / 509 / 511 address sequence on 5 January 1912.  
**High** that the sequence materially constrains the Harding/Willamette reconstruction.  
**Unresolved** for Willamette=507, Harding's exact building number, and the 505 occupant on this date.

## September 1912 building-name constraint
A later visually verified 7 September 1912 advertisement (`E-138` / `S-154`) states **“Willamette Bldg., Room 4, Over Harding Drug Store.”** It prints no number. That wording does not alter the exact January ladder on this page—Young 507, Brightbill 509, Harding 511—but it prevents treating the hypothesis “Young's Willamette Building = 507” as a proved one-building/one-number identity. The named Willamette Building may have encompassed multiple numbered bays, Young may have changed premises between March 1911 and January 1912, or the building naming may have been broader than storefront numbering. Those alternatives remain unresolved.
