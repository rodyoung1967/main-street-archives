# 1911-1913 reconstruction — old Harding Building, 505 Main, and the old Post Office

This note records a converging reconstruction linking the **old Harding Building**, **505 Main**, Hogg Bros.' first Oregon City store, and the old Oregon City Post Office used through March 1914.

## Bottom line

The current evidence supports the following **high-confidence reconstruction**:

- The **old Harding Building** referred to in 1911 and 1913 was very likely the storefront later numbered **505 Main**.
- **Hogg Bros.** very likely occupied that 505 storefront when opening in May-June 1913.
- The old Post Office was therefore very likely **513 Main**.

However, the archive still lacks a primary source that literally prints either **"Hogg Bros., 505 Main"** in 1913 or **"Post Office, 513 Main."** For that reason, these should be labeled **high-confidence reconstructed addresses**, not direct-address transcriptions.

This early **Harding Building** must also be kept distinct from the later replacement **Harding Building at 507-511 Main**, dated 1968 in modern historic-resource records.

## 1. George Young moves one door north — 10 March 1911

*Oregon City Courier*, 10 March 1911, reports that **George Young moved his second-hand store one door north into the Willamette Building**. The article says he had formerly occupied the **Harding Building on Main Street between Fifth and Sixth Streets**.

Primary-source page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1911-03-10/ed-1/`

This establishes a direct storefront relationship:

**old Harding Building → one door north → George Young in the Willamette Building**.

## 2. George Young is directly at 507 Main in 1912

The *Oregon City Courier* of 5 January 1912 directly advertises **G. H. Young, "The Curio Man," at 507 Main Street**.

See:
- `S-061`
- `E-052`
- `newspapers/oregon-city-courier/1912-01-05-page-6-transcription.md`

Absent evidence of another move between March 1911 and January 1912, this strongly identifies Young's "one door north" Willamette Building storefront as **507 Main**.

That places the old Harding Building immediately south of 507. In the established odd-numbered storefront sequence, the immediately preceding storefront is **505 Main**.

### Confidence

**High-confidence inference:** old Harding Building = **505 Main**.

It is not yet a direct-address transcription because the March 1911 article does not print "505."

## 3. Independent evidence places the Chicago Store at 505

The Chicago Store is directly documented at **505 Main** in 1911-1913 advertising, including a 26 January 1913 *Morning Enterprise* advertisement.

This means 505 was still occupied by the Chicago Store in late January 1913.

The current search did not recover a later spring 1913 Chicago Store advertisement or a direct sale/move notice explaining its departure. The transition from Chicago Store to Hogg Bros. therefore remains bounded rather than fully documented.

## 4. Hogg Bros. rents the Harding Building — 16 May 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 16 May 1913, reports that **Hogg Brothers** had rented the **Harding Building on Main Street, four doors south of the Post Office**, for a new furniture/hardware/building-material store. The opening was planned for about 20 May.

Primary-source page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-05-16/ed-1/seq-1/`

This is the same early-building name that George Young had vacated in 1911.

Given the 1911→1912 storefront chain above, Hogg Bros.' May 1913 Harding Building is very likely **505 Main**.

### 1913 505 transition bracket

- **26 Jan 1913:** Chicago Store directly at **505 Main**.
- **16 May 1913:** Hogg Bros. has rented the **old Harding Building**, strongly reconstructed as 505.

Thus the evidence strongly suggests a **Chicago Store → Hogg Bros. transition at 505 Main between late January and mid-May 1913**.

No exact transaction or move date has yet been recovered.

## 5. Hogg Bros. opens "just below the postoffice" — 13 June 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 13 June 1913, reports that Hogg Bros.' new Main Street store was open and describes it as **"just below the postoffice."**

Primary-source page:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-06-13/ed-1/seq-1/`

Because the replacement Post Office opposite the courthouse did not open until 28-29 March 1914, this June 1913 landmark is the **old Fifth-to-Sixth-block Post Office**.

## 6. Four-door count points to 513 Main

The 16 May article gives a particularly useful relationship:

> Hogg Bros. / old Harding Building = **four doors south of the Post Office**.

If the old Harding Building is 505, the northward odd-side storefront sequence is:

1. **507** — first door north
2. **509** — second
3. **511** — third
4. **513** — fourth

That independently reconstructs the old Post Office as **513 Main**.

This is especially persuasive because it agrees with several independent clues already in the archive:

- **505 Main Chicago Store** described itself in January 1912 as **"just below Postoffice."**
- **I. Tolpolar**, later directly at **514 Main**, advertised **opposite the Post Office**.
- **George A. Harding** was directly at **511 Main** in January 1912.
- **M. E. Dunn** was repeatedly described as **next door to the P.O.**; if P.O. = 513, and Harding occupies 511, **515 becomes the plausible Dunn side**.
- 1913 evidence independently constrains the old Post Office to the **Main Street block between Fifth and Sixth Streets**.

## Confidence upgrade for 513 Main

Earlier archive language treated **513 Main** as merely a working candidate derived mainly from Tolpolar's "opposite" description.

The Hogg/Harding-Building doorway chain provides a **second, independent geometric reconstruction** that lands on the same number.

Recommended handling from this point forward:

> **Old Oregon City Post Office: 513 Main — high-confidence reconstructed address; exact numbered-address primary source still sought.**

Do not describe 513 as directly documented until a directory, lease, postal record, Sanborn annotation, deed, advertisement, or other primary source actually prints the number.

## 7. Willamette Building continuity strengthens the chain

George A. Harding's July 1913 advertising identifies his drug business as being in the **Willamette Building**. Harding is directly documented at **511 Main** in January 1912.

Together with George Young at 507, this suggests the Willamette Building encompassed or was associated with multiple storefronts in the 507-511 range. That makes the 1911 wording — Young moving **one door north** out of the separate old Harding Building into the Willamette Building — still more consistent with an old Harding Building storefront at 505.

This should remain architectural/business-location evidence, not be confused with the later 1968 structure currently known as the Harding Building at 507-511.

## 8. Hogg Bros. later relocates north

By early 1914, Hogg Bros. was described as **next door to the Oregon City Courier office**. The Courier office was at **Eighth and Main**. By April 1914 Hogg Bros. is directly listed at **806 Main**.

Therefore Hogg Bros.' first 1913 Harding-Building location and its later 804/806 Main-area location should not be conflated.

The exact date of the relocation from the old Fifth-Sixth-block storefront to the Eighth/Main district remains open.

## Research priorities

1. Find a source literally printing **Hogg Bros. at 505 Main** during May-1913 or the exact Chicago Store→Hogg transition.
2. Find a source literally printing **the old Post Office at 513 Main**.
3. Recover the **1913-1914 Polk Oregon City directory/gazetteer pages**.
4. Identify the owner/lessor of the old Harding Building and determine why it carried that name while George A. Harding's drug store was in the separate Willamette Building.
5. Determine the footprint and storefront range of the early Willamette Building.
6. Establish the exact Hogg Bros. relocation date to the Eighth/Main-area store.
7. Keep the early old Harding Building separate from the **1968 Harding Building at 507-511 Main** in all archive records.
