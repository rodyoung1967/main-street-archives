# E-090 — Active Main Street Demolition and Rebuilding, May 1912

Type: Primary newspaper editorial / contemporary construction context  
Date: 10 May 1912  
Source: `S-105`  
Related research leads: `RL-024`, `RL-038`

## Claims Supported

On **10 May 1912**, the *Oregon City Courier* published an editorial about growth and replacement of infrastructure. In discussing Oregon City's rapid change, it stated that the city was **then tearing down buildings on Main Street and building new buildings in their places**.

The statement is contemporaneous and written in the present tense, making 1912 a confirmed year of active demolition/replacement somewhere along Main Street.

## Significance

This is unusually important to the archive's standing construction question because later historic-resource sources date the surviving 503 building to about 1919 and place the surviving 505 building later than the 1911 predecessor occupancy evidence. The May 1912 statement demonstrates that Main Street's built fabric was actively being replaced during this broader transition era.

## Limitation

The editorial gives **no block, owner, business, street number, or building name** for the structures being demolished and replaced. It therefore does **not** prove that 501, 503, 505, 507, the Harding/Willamette premises, or another Fifth–Sixth building was involved.

Targeted follow-up searches during the 1912 pass found multiple construction projects elsewhere in Oregon City but did not recover an independent source tying this specific teardown statement to the Fifth–Sixth target block.

The archive must therefore record this as **citywide Main Street structural-change evidence**, not as a target-building demolition claim.

## Confidence

**Very High** that active building demolition/replacement was occurring on Oregon City Main Street in May 1912.  
**Unresolved** for the identities, addresses, blocks, and footprints of the buildings involved.
