# E-060 — Preliminary Oregon City Street-Numbering System, 1905

Type: Primary newspaper / municipal-context evidence  
Date: 30 June 1905  
Source: `S-074`

## Claim Supported

The 30 June 1905 *Oregon City Enterprise* reported that Oregon City was pursuing free mail delivery and that a federal inspector would check whether streets and houses were properly named and numbered. The article specifically urged residents to acquaint themselves with a **system of numbering their residences that had recently been introduced** and to number their properties accordingly.

## Significance

This is important to the early Main Street address chronology. It shows that an address-numbering system was being introduced by mid-1905, before the more formal 1906 city ordinance, numbering plat, street signs, and mandatory numbering campaign documented in later sources.

It helps explain why many 1904–1905 business advertisements identify only **Main Street** or a relative landmark rather than a numbered street address.

## Limitation / relationship to 1906

The 1905 article does **not** reproduce the numbering scheme, a Main Street address plat, or a numbered list of businesses. Later 1906 sources show the City Council still formulating formal numbering ordinances in May 1906, Mayor Caufield approving an ordinance and a numbering plat in July 1906, and street signs / numbering implementation being pushed in August 1906 for free mail delivery.

Accordingly, treat 1905 as evidence of a **preliminary or recently introduced numbering system**, not proof that the later 501/503/505/507 sequence was already uniformly assigned or in common use.

## Confidence

**Very High** that a street/residence numbering system had been introduced by 30 June 1905.  
**Unresolved** whether the modern 501/503/505/507 Main sequence was already assigned exactly as later used.
