# E-066 — Main Street Track, Paving, and Grade Reconstruction, 1907

Type: Primary newspaper / public-works and railway evidence  
Date: April–August 1907  
Source: `S-080`  
Related research lead: `RL-024`

## Claims Supported

In 1907 the Oregon Water Power & Railway Company and Oregon City undertook major reconstruction along paved Main Street:

- On 1 April the *Morning Oregonian* reported plans to rebuild the railway line along Main Street **from Eleventh Street to the Basin**, effectively making a new roadbed through the paved business district.
- In May heavy new rails were delivered for the Main Street track. Contemporary reports differ on the stated rail weight (one report says 90-pound rail; another describes 86-pound-per-yard standard city rail), so the archive preserves the source discrepancy rather than selecting one value.
- The track was to be placed in the center of Main Street, with pavement disturbed in limited sections while work proceeded.
- On 16 August the *Oregon City Courier* reported active reconstruction: brick paving west of the track was being removed and relaid, the company was repairing the east side, and work was proceeding southward.
- Crucially, the newspaper states that the **street was being raised to meet the new track, which was about six inches higher than the old track**.

## Significance for Fifth–Sixth Main

Because the documented project extended from Eleventh Street south to the Basin, it necessarily passed through the **Fifth-to-Sixth Main Street block**. This is strong evidence of a meaningful 1907 change in street elevation, track construction, and paving immediately in front of the target storefronts.

The roughly six-inch grade increase may matter when interpreting historic photographs, storefront thresholds, sidewalk relationships, basement/window elevations, later remodeling, and apparent building heights.

## Limitation

No recovered 1907 source states that the predecessor buildings later numbered 501, 503, 505, or 507 were demolished, moved, structurally rebuilt, or replaced as part of this project. The evidence establishes a **street/track/grade event**, not a building replacement.

## Confidence

**Very High** for the corridor reconstruction and approximately six-inch track/street elevation change.  
**Unresolved** for its physical effect on individual storefront structures.
