# 1913-1914 Oregon City Post Office relocation

This evidence note separates the **old Main Street Post Office used throughout 1913** from the replacement building constructed opposite the courthouse in late 1913 and occupied at the end of March 1914.

## Finding

The strongest current reconstruction is:

- The **old Post Office** used during 1913 was in the **Main Street block between Fifth and Sixth Streets**. Its exact numbered address remains unproven; **513 Main is a working candidate only**.
- The **replacement Post Office** was constructed **opposite the courthouse** in the Eighth/Main-area courthouse district during late 1913.
- The Post Office **did not move into the replacement until 28-29 March 1914**.

Therefore 1913 advertisements describing businesses as near, below, next to, or opposite "the Post Office" should be interpreted relative to the **old Fifth-Sixth block Post Office**, not the replacement under construction.

## Old Post Office site evidence

### 28 February 1913 — alley north of Post Office

*Oregon City Courier*, 28 February 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-02-28/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper describes an alley **immediately north of the postoffice building** that ran through to **Water Street**, plus a gateway from the alley into ground **behind the postoffice building**.

This is a direct physical-site description, though it gives no street number.

## Willamette Hall adjacency

### 20 November 1913 — Hall just south of Post Office

*Oregon City Courier*, 20 November 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-20/ed-1/seq-1/`

A meeting notice identifies **Willamette Hall as just south of the post office**.

### 19 November 1914 — Hall explicitly between Fifth and Sixth

*Morning Enterprise*, 19 November 1914:
Willamette Hall is explicitly described as being on **Main Street, between Fifth and Sixth Streets**.

Combined, these reports strongly constrain the old Post Office to the same Fifth-Sixth Main Street block area.

## Consistency with 1912 business geography

The block reconstruction fits existing primary-source clues:

- **505 Main — Chicago Store:** "Main St. just below Postoffice."
- **514 Main — I. Tolpolar:** advertised opposite the Post Office.
- **511 Main — George A. Harding:** directly documented there on 5 January 1912.
- **M. E. Dunn:** repeatedly advertised next door to the P.O.

These clues make **513 Main** plausible, but not proven. Do not convert the geographic fit into a factual numbered address without a source that prints the number.

## Old lease expiring in 1913

### 13 June 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 13 June 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-06-13/ed-1/seq-1/`

A government inspector criticized the existing Post Office quarters. The article says the **present lease of ten years would expire in December 1913** and urges provision of better quarters.

The same issue describes **Hogg Bros.** as having a Main Street store **just below the postoffice**. Because the replacement did not open until March 1914, this June 1913 wording refers to the old Fifth-Sixth-block Post Office.

## Replacement building chronology

### 23 October 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 23 October 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-10-23/ed-1/seq-1/`

A new Post Office building is reported planned **opposite the courthouse**.

### 6 November 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 6 November 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-06/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper says **private parties** were beginning construction of a Post Office building under a **five-year renting contract with the government**. This distinguishes the leased replacement from the separate effort to obtain a federally owned Post Office building.

### 20 November 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 20 November 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-11-20/ed-1/seq-1/`

David Williams had purchased the old house standing on the new Post Office site and was moving it away.

### 18 December 1913

*Oregon City Courier*, 18 December 1913, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1913-12-18/ed-1/seq-1/`

The paper reports that the replacement building's **cement foundation walls were in** and wall construction was underway.

## Occupancy date — 28-29 March 1914

### 26 March 1914

*Oregon City Courier*, 26 March 1914, p. 1:
`https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063698/1914-03-26/ed-1/seq-1/`

The headline **"Postoffice Will Move Saturday"** reports that the Post Office would move into its **new building opposite the courthouse on Saturday and Sunday**, with the building complete and newly equipped.

Since 26 March 1914 was Thursday, the scheduled move dates were **Saturday-Sunday, 28-29 March 1914**.

This is the controlling date for interpreting 1913 Post Office landmark references.

## Courthouse / Eighth and Main context

Contemporary 1913 advertising for **Dillman & Howland** associates the business with **Eighth and Main** and describes it as **opposite the courthouse**. That provides a contemporary geographic link between the courthouse and the Eighth/Main district.

Current evidence is sufficient to distinguish the replacement Post Office district from the old Fifth-Sixth-block Post Office, but it is **not sufficient to assign a precise numbered address to the replacement building**.

## Evidence handling rules

1. **Do not call 513 Main the Post Office as fact.** It remains a strong candidate.
2. **Do not interpret 1913 Post Office landmarks relative to the later building opposite the courthouse.** The relocation happened in March 1914.
3. Keep the privately leased 1913-1914 replacement distinct from proposals for a federally owned Post Office building.
4. Seek the 1913-1914 Polk gazetteer/business directory and municipal/property records for exact numbered addresses.

## Open questions

- Exact numbered address of the old Post Office.
- Owner/lessor of the old ten-year Post Office lease.
- Exact numbered address and parcel of the replacement building opposite the courthouse.
- Whether surviving Sanborn, directory, assessor, deed, or postal records explicitly identify either building.
