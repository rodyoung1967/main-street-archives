## Post Office cluster

Contemporary advertisements use the Oregon City Post Office as a landmark:

- **The Chicago Store**, independently documented at **505 Main**, says **"Main St. just below Postoffice"** on 5 January 1912. This is a strong same-date anchor, but "just below" must not be converted into an exact numbered address without corroboration.
- **I. Tolpolar** advertised **opposite the Post Office**. Later evidence places Tolpolar at **514 Main**, making an odd-numbered Post Office location a strong working constraint, not an exact-address proof.
- **M. E. Dunn** operated a confectionery selling cigars, candies, Wilhoit water and soft drinks. The 5 January *Courier* places Dunn near the Post Office, and *Morning Enterprise* advertising in 1912 repeatedly describes the business as **next door to the P.O.** Exact street number remains unproven.
- **Schrader's Bakery** is printed on the 5 January page as **near the Postoffice**. A 20 December 1912 *Courier* report states that Herman Schrader sold two bakeries — one at Seventh and John Adams and the other on Main Street — to **Wemmo & Haas**. The exact Main Street number remains unproven, and the two bakery locations must not be conflated.
- **Pioneer Transfer Co.** advertised an office **in the Postoffice Building**, further establishing the Post Office Building as a commercial landmark.

## Current address hypothesis — NOT established fact

Earlier reconstruction made **513 Main** a strong candidate for the Post Office because Tolpolar was opposite it at 514. The documented **505 Main Chicago Store = "just below Postoffice"** statement adds another constraint but does not establish a number. The meaning and distance implied by "just below" in 1912 Oregon City usage still require corroboration.

If 513 ultimately proves to be the Post Office, Dunn's "next door" location would likely be 511 or 515; because Harding is directly documented at 511 on 5 January 1912, **515 becomes a plausible Dunn candidate**. This remains inference and must not be promoted to fact.

Do **not** assign Schrader's Bakery to 503, 505, or another exact number without direct evidence.

## 1912 closeout status

The **1912 year pass is archival-complete for the evidence presently recovered**: the primary page evidence is normalized, the 503/507/509/511/527 assignments are explicit, the H. N. / H. H. Smith discrepancy is preserved rather than silently reconciled, and the December Schrader-to-Wemmo-&-Haas transition is recorded.

The following are open research questions, not blockers to moving to 1913:

1. Find a primary source explicitly giving the numbered address of the Oregon City Post Office / Postoffice Building circa 1908-1912.
2. Find a numbered address for M. E. Dunn, especially 1911-1913.
3. Find a numbered address for Schrader's Main Street bakery / Wemmo & Haas.
4. Determine whether **H. N. Smith** (January) and **H. H. Smith** (March) are the same person, a newspaper error, or two operators.
5. Determine exactly what "just below Postoffice" meant geographically in the Chicago Store advertisement.
6. Continue the broader 503-529 Main storefront reconstruction without treating inferred adjacency as proof.
