# E-103 — Fifth/Main Fire Origin, S. Wolf Ownership and Pool-Hall Adjacency

## Evidence type

Primary local newspaper fire-origin, ownership, adjacency and damage evidence; direct visual inspection.

## Source

- `S-117` — *Banner-Courier*, 20 July 1922, page 1, “Big Fire Destroys Quarter Block in Oregon City” and the separate same-page item “Misfortune Calls.”
- Repository PDF: `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07-20-page-1-fifth-main-fire.pdf`
- Canonical PDF: https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn00063699/1922-07-20/ed-1/seq-1.pdf

Detailed research note: `evidence/source-captures/1922-fire-s-wolf-main-street-property-lead.md`

## Visually verified facts

1. The fire began early on 18 July 1922 in a vacant Main Street storeroom.
2. The room had formerly been occupied by Alex Schieremann as a tailor shop.
3. The report names S. Wolf of Portland as owner of the building in which the fire began.
4. A pool hall adjoined the former tailor premises.
5. The report separately names the Oregon City Laundry, May & Washburn garage, Fifth Street Restaurant, Gilbert Miller shoe-repair shop, and Ketchner home/confectionery within the affected cluster.
6. The separate “Misfortune Calls” item places Ketchner's confectionery next to the laundry.

## Relationship to the July property sale

Five days before the fire, `S-111` / `E-096` reported George A. Harding's named Main Street store-property group sold to S. Wolf and C. Gates of Portland. Gates received the Fifth Street Restaurant location and Wolf the remainder. The matching S. Wolf residence wording, dates, and business cluster create a strong property-chain inference that Wolf's newly reported remainder included the ignition building. The newspaper reports are not deeds and do not establish legal boundaries or the effective date of title transfer.

## Conflicts and limits

- The page gives no numbered address for the ignition room, former tailor shop, or adjoining pool hall.
- It does not decide among modern 501, 503, and 505 Main.
- It does not identify the pool-hall operator; do not automatically merge the adjoining hall with Timms Pool Hall or Leland & Little.
- It does not establish whether S. Wolf owned the land, the building/improvement, or both.
- It does not identify S. Wolf by full name or connect him to Wolf & Nat, Wolf & Miller, Samuel Wolf, Simon Wolf, Victor Wolf, Gilbert Miller's relatives, or later Wolf-family ownership without a direct bridge.
- Independent Eugene/Morning Register reporting carries conflicting damage estimates and scrambled owner/operator names. Those conflicts remain preserved separately and are not harmonized into the local account.
- Absence of a tenant at the moment of the fire does not establish how long the room had been vacant or why Schieremann left.

## Current storefront assessment

The report remains compatible with the working reconstruction—**503 predecessor as ignition room and 505 as adjoining pool hall**—but it does not prove it. **501 remains a live alternative.** Later 1925 Sanborn uses may guide hypothesis formation but cannot be backdated to July 1922.

The [28 August model test](source-captures/1922-task-7-fire-origin-model-test-2026-08-28.md) qualifies the earlier “strongest” wording: it was a working research preference, not a demonstrated ranking over the Gates-501+503 alternative. Neither model is selected, neither is exhaustive, and a property boundary alone would not identify the particular ignition room. Source wording and conflicts are unchanged.

## Confidence

- **Very High** for the visually verified local report's origin, former tailor use, S. Wolf ownership wording, pool-hall adjacency, and named damage cluster.
- **High / strong inference** for continuity with the Harding-to-Wolf property remainder reported five days earlier.
- **Unresolved** for the exact modern storefront, S. Wolf's identity and legal ownership mechanism, and the adjoining pool hall's operator.
