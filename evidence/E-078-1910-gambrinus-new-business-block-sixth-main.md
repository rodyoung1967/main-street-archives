# E-078 — Gambrinus New Business Block at Sixth & Main, 1910

Type: Primary municipal record + primary newspaper construction evidence  
Date: 29 June–26 August 1910  
Source: `S-092`  
Related evidence: `E-064`, `E-075`, `E-076`  
Related research leads: `RL-024`, `RL-026`, `RL-031`

## Claims Supported

1910 provides direct evidence of **new building construction on the Gambrinus property at Sixth and Main Streets**.

- **29 June 1910:** at a special Oregon City Council meeting, contractor **J. G. Killgreen** was granted permission to use ten feet of Main Street and ten feet of Sixth Street **abutting the Gambrinus property at Sixth and Main Streets** for placing building material for **construction of a new building at that location**.
- **26 August 1910:** the *Oregon City Courier* reported that **basement work was completed for a two-story business block to be erected by the Gambrinus Brewing Company on Main Street at Sixth**. The paper described the planned building as two stories and modern in every way.

## Significance for Block 26

This is the first direct new-construction evidence recovered for the Gambrinus Sixth/Main property after the 1906 sale of the historic three-story frame William Tell Hotel property to Gambrinus (`E-064`). It materially narrows the structural-change window at the north end of Block 26.

The sequence now established is:

1. October 1906 — William Tell Hotel, a three-story frame building at Sixth & Main, sold to Gambrinus; matching transfer identifies lot 8, Block 26 (`E-064`).
2. 1909 — Carl G. Hodes operates a saloon at Sixth & Main (`E-076`); Philip Street has a building at the intersection under repair (`E-075`).
3. June 1910 — City authorizes construction staging specifically for a **new building on the Gambrinus property at Sixth & Main**.
4. August 1910 — basement complete for the new two-story Gambrinus business block.

## Limitation

The recovered 1910 records do **not** explicitly say that the William Tell Hotel was demolished to make way for the new building, nor do they identify a demolition date. They also do not prove that the Hodes saloon or Philip Street repair occupied the same structure.

Because later lot/address relationships are still being reconstructed, the new Gambrinus block must not yet be assigned to 507, 509, 511, or another numbered Main Street address without direct mapping evidence.

## Confidence

**Very High** that a new two-story Gambrinus business block was being constructed at the Gambrinus property at Sixth & Main in summer 1910.  
**High** that this is part of the structural succession of the 1906 Gambrinus/William Tell property; **Unresolved** for the exact demolition/replacement mechanics and later street number.
