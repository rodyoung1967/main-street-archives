## Corrected locality

The visually reviewed 12 Sep 1919 *Oregon City Enterprise* source states that `S. Fesznecker` moved his tin and repair shop from **Broadway** to **the building just south of the post office**.

The archive formerly interpreted that wording as Oregon City geography. That interpretation is **withdrawn**.

A next-week 19 Sep 1919 *Enterprise* follow-up, in the explicitly labeled **Estacada** section, describes a new building beside **S. Pesznecker's residence** for his machine/plumbing shop and auto-livery work. Independent contemporary sources repeatedly place S. P. Pesznecker in Estacada, including Cascade Garage and later machine-shop activity.

Accordingly the 12 Sep move belongs to the **Estacada business geography**, not to Oregon City's Fifth/Main storefronts.

## Location chronology — corrected

- Before 12 Sep 1919: **Broadway, Estacada** — exact number unresolved.
- By 12 Sep 1919: **building just south of the Estacada post office** — exact street/number/parcel unresolved.
- 19 Sep 1919: new building reported just north of Pesznecker's Estacada residence for his machine/plumbing shop and auto-livery business.
- Dec 1920: independent *Eastern Clackamas News* advertising identifies **S. P. Pesznecker Machine Shop, Estacada, Oregon**.

Do **not** compare this relative-location wording to the Oregon City cleaner/dyer's `two doors south of new post office` wording (`E-136`) as though both referred to one Post Office. They concern different towns.
