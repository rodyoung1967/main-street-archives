# 1922 Fifth/Main Fire Frontage — Current Integrated Timeline

Status: **authoritative September 2026 synthesis for the July 1922 fire-frontage question**. This timeline supersedes older model-test language that treated 503-origin / 505-pool as an unranked hypothesis, while preserving those older records as research history.

## Before July 1922

- **22 September 1921 — Red Front Clothing Store:** project-steward visual reading of an *Aurora Observer* p.3 advertisement directly prints **507 Main Street**. Evidence state: **DOCUMENTED / DIRECT — STEWARD VISUALLY VERIFIED**; archive independent scan preservation/review still desired.
- **1921–1922 — Dr. Harvey Freeze:** 507½ Main in spring 1921, then 505/505½ Main by May 1921; moved to the Masonic Building around 1 July 1922. This establishes half-address/sub-premises use but does not by itself locate the fire-origin room.
- **1918 Wolf & Miller:** directly documented at 505 Main selling men's clothing, shoes and shoe-repair services. October 1918 wording says one partner would go to Idaho and the other would restock/continue. A later connection to L. Wolf/Red Front is probable but unproved.

## 13 July 1922 — Harding property group sold

The visually verified *Banner-Courier*, 13 July 1922, p.1, “Property Changes Hands,” states that George A. Harding's Main Street property included stores occupied by:

- Fifth Street Restaurant;
- Miller's shoe store;
- **the barber shop adjoining**;
- Red Front Clothing Store.

The property was sold to **S. Wolf and C. Gates, both of Portland**. **C. Gates took the location where the Fifth Street Restaurant then stood; S. Wolf took the remainder.** The original page is already preserved at `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07/1922-07-13-seq-01.pdf` (`E-096` / `S-111`).

This directly establishes the reported division of Harding's named property group. The exact deed dimensions and legal parcel boundaries remain unretrieved, so the newspaper allocation must not be expanded beyond what it says.

## 18 July 1922 — fire event

### 501 Main — Fifth Street Restaurant / Gates portion destroyed

The project now treats **501 Main as the Fifth Street Restaurant corner building destroyed in the fire**. This is a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE** from the established 501→503→505→507 frontage, the July 13 Harding sale allocation, the fire losses, and Gates's immediate post-fire rebuilding at Fifth/Main. Contemporary reporting identifies the restaurant building/property with **C. P. / C. Gates**; wording involving **Blakesley** concerns the restaurant business/operator side and is not treated as real-estate title.

The 501 building burned / the restaurant was a total loss. In August 1922 Gates rebuilt at Fifth/Main with a two-story hollow-tile replacement containing two ground-floor storerooms. The 17–18 August reports projected the building would be **ready for occupancy about 1 October 1922**; that is a projected readiness date, not proof of actual occupancy on that date. The later numbered footprint of both replacement rooms remains a separate deed/plan question.

### 503 Main — Miller / former Schiermann fire-origin building

The 20 July *Banner-Courier* says the fire began in a vacant storeroom formerly occupied by **Alex Schiermann** as a tailor and names **Gilbert Miller's shoe-repair shop** among the destroyed businesses. It says a pool hall adjoined the former tailor premises.

The 21 July *Oregon City Enterprise* says the blaze broke out in a **vacant half** formerly occupied by Schiermann and also describes the origin structure as **the shoe-store building in which the fire started**. This reconciles the apparently vacant tailor room with Miller's active shoe business as separate portions/halves of the same structure.

With 501 destroyed to the south and the adjoining 505 pool-hall position fixed to the north, the project treats **503 Main as the Miller / former-Schiermann fire-origin building** as a **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**. No recovered pre-fire ad yet literally prints Miller or Schiermann at 503, so this remains inferred rather than DOCUMENTED / DIRECT.

### 505 Main — adjoining pool/billiard premises survived

The 20 July report says billiard tables in the pool hall adjoining the tailoring establishment were damaged by water and part of the roof burned. The 21 July Enterprise says the building immediately north of the fire was **saved by fireproof construction**.

Current conclusion: **505 Main was not razed**. It suffered water damage and some roof burning, but fireproof construction is directly credited with saving the building immediately north of the origin. The later 1925 Sanborn billiards/cigars use is consistent with survival but is not the sole basis for the conclusion.

### 507 Main — Red Front Clothing Store

Red Front is directly documented at **507 Main on 22 September 1921**. The 13 July 1922 sale report still names Red Front within Harding's property group, and the fire accounts continue to name Red Front as a distinct loss/damage business. The 21 July Enterprise identifies **L. Wolf** as Red Front's owner.

Current evidence state for **Red Front at 507 during the July 1922 fire:** **STRONG CROSS-SOURCE INFERENCE**. A July 1922 source printing the number remains desirable, but 507 is now the clear leading location.

## Ownership controls

- **George A. Harding** — seller of the named Main Street property group on 13 July 1922.
- **C. Gates / C. P. Gates** — buyer of the Fifth Street Restaurant location, reconstructed as the **501 Main** portion; contemporary reporting associates Gates with the restaurant building/property and the immediate post-fire replacement construction.
- **S. Wolf** — buyer of Harding's **remainder** according to the July 13 article; the remainder is described through the occupied stores including Miller's shoe store, adjoining barber shop and Red Front. Exact legal parcel geometry still requires deeds.
- **20 July fire account:** origin building reported owned by **S. Wolf of Portland**.
- **21 July Enterprise:** **L. Wolf** identified as Red Front owner and as recent purchaser of the shoe-store building where the fire started.
- **1918 Wolf & Miller:** unidentified Wolf partner in the clothing/shoes/shoe-repair business at 505 Main.

A working hypothesis that **L. Wolf = the Wolf of Wolf & Miller** is reasonable and research-useful, especially given the 1918 partner split/continuation language and the later separation into Miller shoe and Wolf clothing lines. It remains **PROBABLE / WORKING HYPOTHESIS**, not documented identity. The **S. Wolf / L. Wolf** initial conflict must remain explicit until resolved.

Do not merge business ownership, operation, tenancy and real-estate ownership. In particular, L. Wolf's ownership of Red Front is not by itself proof that L. Wolf personally held title to 507, and the July 13 newspaper sale report is not a substitute for the unretrieved deeds.
