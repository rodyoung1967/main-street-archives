## July 1922 ownership and fire interpretation

The controlling source is the visually verified **Banner-Courier, 13 July 1922, p.1**, `newspapers/banner-courier/1922-07/1922-07-13-seq-01.pdf`. It reports that George A. Harding's Main Street property included the Fifth Street Restaurant, Miller's shoe store, adjoining barber shop, and Red Front Clothing Store. It was sold to **S. Wolf and C. Gates**; **Gates took the restaurant location and Wolf took the remainder**.

The fire followed five days later. Contemporary reporting says the Fifth Street Restaurant was destroyed. The integrated frontage reconstruction places the restaurant/corner loss at **501 Main**, the Miller/former-Schiermann origin structure at **503**, the surviving adjoining pool/billiard premises at **505**, and Red Front at **507**. The 501 assignment is therefore no longer carried as an equal unresolved alternative to the 503 ignition model. It is a **strong cross-source reconstruction**, while the unretrieved deed remains the desired legal-property proof.

Keep these roles separate:

- **George A. Harding** — seller of the named property group immediately before the fire.
- **C. Gates / C. P. Gates** — purchaser of the Fifth Street Restaurant location; contemporary reporting associates Gates with the restaurant building/property and immediate replacement construction.
- **Blakesley** — restaurant business/operator ownership wording in fire reporting; this is not treated as real-estate title.
- **S. Wolf** — purchaser of Harding's remainder, not the restaurant location according to the July 13 article.
